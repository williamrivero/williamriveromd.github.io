Proper cleaning habits are very important every single day. 1 Always wipe front to back After using the toilet, always wipe from front (urethra) to back (anus). Never wipe back to front. This prevents bacteria from the bowel from entering the urinary opening. 2 Wash the perineal area gently twice daily Use clean lukewarm water and mild unscented soap (Dove sensitive or Johnson’s baby soap). Clean front to back. Rinse well and pat completely dry with a clean towel. 3 Avoid harsh products Do not use feminine washes, vaginal sprays, powder, or scented soap in the perineal area. These disrupt natural protection and increase infection risk. 4 Wear loose cotton underwear, changed daily Avoid tight or synthetic fabrics that trap moisture. If you use incontinence pads, change them frequently — never sit in a wet pad. 5 Urinate regularly — do not hold Try to urinate every 3–4 hours even without urgency. Emptying the bladder regularly flushes bacteria out. Urinate before sleeping. 6 Keep the area dry After washing, pat the area completely dry. Moisture allows bacteria to grow. Powder (unscented, like cornstarch-based) may be used to keep the area dry between washes. 7 Caregiver hand hygiene Anyone assisting with personal care must wash hands thoroughly with soap and water before and after helping. Use gloves if available. ⚠ RETURN TO THE CLINIC IMMEDIATELY IF: • Burning or pain during urination that is getting worse despite medicines • Urine that is bloody, very dark, or foul-smelling • Fever above 38°C with chills or shaking • Pain in the back or sides below the ribs • Confusion, extreme weakness, or inability to urinate ☕ Hydration How much to drink each day Drink 6–8 glasses (1.5 to 2 liters) of water daily.
