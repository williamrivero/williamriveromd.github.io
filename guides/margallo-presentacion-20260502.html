Right Foot Skin Care

How to care for the healing skin on your right foot and ankle

What the doctor found today

The right foot and ankle show redness with dry, peeling, and flaking skin — this is the skin healing after the previous cellulitis (skin infection) that has now resolved. There is no active infection, no open wounds, and no pus. The left foot has mild redness only. This is expected and will continue to improve.
  • Apply moisturizer twice daily — use petroleum jelly (Vaseline) or a urea-based cream (e.g., Carmel, Eucerin) over the dry, peeling areas morning and evening after cleaning. This prevents cracking and further skin breakdown.
  • Gentle washing only — wash the foot and ankle once daily with mild soap and lukewarm water. Pat dry gently — do not rub. Make sure the skin between the toes is dry.
  • Do not peel or scratch — the flaking skin will fall off naturally. Peeling it by force can open the skin and cause a new infection.
  • Wear clean, soft socks — cotton socks protect the healing skin from friction and dirt. Change daily.
  • Elevate feet when resting — prop your feet up on a pillow or cushion when sitting or lying down to improve circulation and reduce any residual swelling.
  • Check feet daily — a family member should inspect both feet each day for new redness, warmth, swelling, blisters, or open sores. This is especially important because of diabetes.

⚠ Go to the clinic or emergency room immediately if:

  • •Redness spreads rapidly up the leg
  • •The skin becomes hot, very painful, or swollen suddenly
  • •Pus, yellow or green discharge, or open wounds appear
  • •Fever above 38°C with chills and worsening leg redness
  • •A red line spreading up the leg from the ankle — seek emergency care immediately

Perineal Hygiene Guide

How to clean properly to prevent UTI from coming back

Because you have diabetes and kidney disease, you are more prone to urinary tract infections. Proper cleaning habits are very important every single day.

1

Always wipe front to back

After using the toilet, always wipe from front (urethra) to back (anus). Never wipe back to front. This prevents bacteria from the bowel from entering the urinary opening.

2

Wash the perineal area gently twice daily

Use clean lukewarm water and mild unscented soap (Dove sensitive or Johnson’s baby soap). Clean front to back. Rinse well and pat completely dry with a clean towel.

3

Avoid harsh products

Do not use feminine washes, vaginal sprays, powder, or scented soap in the perineal area. These disrupt natural protection and increase infection risk.

4

Wear loose cotton underwear, changed daily

Avoid tight or synthetic fabrics that trap moisture. If you use incontinence pads, change them frequently — never sit in a wet pad.

5

Urinate regularly — do not hold

Try to urinate every 3–4 hours even without urgency. Emptying the bladder regularly flushes bacteria out. Urinate before sleeping.

6

Keep the area dry

After washing, pat the area completely dry. Moisture allows bacteria to grow. Powder (unscented, like cornstarch-based) may be used to keep the area dry between washes.

7

Caregiver hand hygiene

Anyone assisting with personal care must wash hands thoroughly with soap and water before and after helping. Use gloves if available.

⚠ Return to the clinic immediately if:

  • •Burning or pain during urination that is getting worse despite medicines
  • •Urine that is bloody, very dark, or foul-smelling
  • •Fever above 38°C with chills or shaking
  • •Pain in the back or sides below the ribs
  • •Confusion, extreme weakness, or inability to urinate

How much to drink each day

Drink 6–8 glasses (1.5 to 2 liters) of water daily. Good hydration flushes bacteria from the urinary tract and supports your kidneys.

= 6–8 glasses per day
