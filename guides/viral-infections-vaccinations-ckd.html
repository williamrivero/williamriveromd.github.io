Viral Infections & Vaccinations in CKD Mga Viral na Impeksyon at Bakuna sa CKD Mga Viral nga Impeksyon ug Bakuna sa CKD

CKD profoundly impairs the immune system — making viral infections more severe, more frequent, and more likely to drive acute kidney injury and permanent disease progression. Vaccination is one of the most powerful and underutilized tools in CKD care. This guide covers what you need to be protected from, why vaccines work differently in kidney patients, and the complete Philippine vaccination schedule for CKD. Ang CKD ay lubos na nagpapahina ng immune system — na nagpapabigat, nagpapalala at nagpapataas ng panganib ng acute kidney injury mula sa mga viral na impeksyon. Ang pagbabakuna ay isa sa pinaka-mahalagang tool sa pag-aalaga ng CKD. Ang CKD nagpapahuyangon sa immune system — nagpabug-at, nagpapalala ug nagpadugang sa risk sa acute kidney injury gikan sa mga viral nga impeksyon. Ang pagbabakuna usa sa labing importante nga himan sa pag-atiman sa CKD.

Immunology of CKD

Why Viral Infections Hit Harder in Kidney Disease

The immune system of a CKD patient is profoundly and specifically impaired — not simply "weakened" in a general sense. Uremic toxins that accumulate as kidney function declines directly interfere with neutrophil chemotaxis, T-lymphocyte activation, natural killer cell cytotoxicity, and B-cell antibody production. The result is a state of uremic immunoparalysis — the patient is simultaneously prone to infections they cannot fight and, paradoxically, prone to inflammatory conditions driven by dysregulated cytokine release.

This matters enormously for vaccination: vaccines produce weaker and shorter-lived antibody responses in CKD patients — requiring higher doses, more booster doses, and earlier timing than in healthy adults. A vaccine given too late (e.g., after dialysis initiation) may produce only half the antibody titre of the same vaccine given in CKD Stage 2–3. Timing is part of the treatment.

Uremic immunoparalysis is not a single defect — it is simultaneous failure across five arms of immunity. This is why viral infections that a healthy person clears in 5–7 days can trigger AKI, CKD progression, or death in a dialysis or advanced CKD patient.

The vaccine timing paradox

CKD patients need vaccines most urgently — but respond to them least effectively. The practical implication: vaccinate early, while eGFR is still above 30 mL/min. Seroconversion rates for hepatitis B vaccine are 95% in healthy adults, 80% in CKD Stage 3, 60% in CKD Stage 4–5, and only 50–60% in dialysis patients. Every month of delay is lost immunogenicity. Vaccines given before dialysis initiation are measurably more effective than those given after.

AKI from viral infection — the kidney's secondary injury

Viral infections cause AKI in CKD patients through four simultaneous mechanisms: (1) direct tubular cytopathic effect of the virus; (2) sepsis-mediated renal vasoconstriction and ischaemia; (3) rhabdomyolysis from severe myalgia; (4) immune complex deposition in the glomerulus triggering glomerulonephritis. Each hospitalization for viral illness in a CKD patient accelerates the rate of eGFR decline measurably — further emphasizing that prevention is treatment.

Viral Threat Profiles

The Six Viral Threats Every CKD Patient Must Know

Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Critical risk
Hepadnavirus · Bloodborne + sexual transmission · Vaccine-preventable
Why CKD patients are especially vulnerable
  • Dialysis blood circuit = repeated blood exposure risk
  • Shared needles at dialysis centers historically — strict universal precautions required
  • CKD patients → fulminant hepatic failure at 10× higher rate than general population
  • HBV reactivation with immunosuppression (transplant) can be rapidly fatal
  • Carrier state (chronic HBV) → cirrhosis + hepatocellular carcinoma over years
CKD-specific management

All CKD patients must be screened for HBsAg, anti-HBs, and anti-HBc at diagnosis. All seronegative patients vaccinated immediately — double-dose, extended schedule (see HBV section). Dialysis patients: monthly HBsAg surveillance. Anti-HBV antivirals (tenofovir, entecavir) require dose adjustment in CKD — adefovir is no longer recommended. Active HBV + CKD Stage 4–5 requires specialist co-management (hepatology + nephrology).

Influenza A & B Critical risk
Orthomyxovirus · Droplet transmission · Annual vaccine available
Why it hits CKD harder
  • Influenza pneumonia 5× more lethal in ESKD than in healthy adults
  • Secondary bacterial pneumonia (Streptococcus, Staphylococcus) = leading cause of death post-influenza in dialysis
  • Myositis-related rhabdomyolysis → AKI superimposed on CKD
  • Fluid shifts from fever and poor intake → dangerous in fluid-restricted patients
  • Antiviral oseltamivir (Tamiflu): dose must be halved in eGFR <30 mL/min
Prevention and treatment

Annual inactivated influenza vaccination is the highest-priority, lowest-risk intervention in CKD — safe in all CKD stages, all dialysis modalities, and post-transplant (inactivated form). High-dose or adjuvanted influenza vaccine (Fluzone HD, Fluad) produces superior antibody response in immunocompromised patients — preferred when available. Neuraminidase inhibitors (oseltamivir 75 mg → 30 mg in eGFR <30) should be started within 48 hours of symptom onset.

SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) Critical risk
Betacoronavirus · Airborne + droplet · mRNA and protein-subunit vaccines available
CKD-specific COVID burden
  • Dialysis patients: 30-day COVID mortality 20–25% in early pandemic (USRDS 2021)
  • CKD Stage 3–5 non-dialysis: 2–3× higher ICU admission than general population
  • Direct viral ACE2 receptor-mediated tubular injury → AKI in 20–40% of hospitalized COVID patients
  • Post-COVID accelerated CKD progression documented at 12 months even after mild infection
  • Immunosuppressed transplant patients: prolonged viral shedding for weeks to months
Vaccination and antivirals

COVID vaccination significantly reduces hospitalization and mortality in CKD even with attenuated antibody response. Primary series + booster doses strongly recommended. Antivirals: nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid) requires dose reduction if eGFR 30–60 mL/min; contraindicated if eGFR <30. Remdesivir IV: use with caution in eGFR <30 (excipient accumulation). Molnupiravir: no dose adjustment needed in CKD. Start antivirals within 5 days of symptom onset.

Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV) — Chickenpox & Shingles High risk
Herpesvirus · Airborne (primary) · Reactivation (zoster) · Vaccine-preventable
The unique CKD danger
  • Primary varicella (chickenpox) in adult CKD patients → visceral dissemination, hepatitis, pneumonia
  • Herpes zoster (shingles): 2–4× higher incidence in CKD; disseminated zoster in transplant patients can be fatal
  • Acyclovir: dose reduction mandatory in CKD (crystalline nephropathy risk at standard doses)
  • Valacyclovir: preferred oral antiviral — dose adjusted by eGFR (see prescribing)
  • Zoster ophthalmicus (eye involvement): urgent ophthalmology referral
Vaccination strategy

Recombinant zoster vaccine (Shingrix) — non-live, safe in CKD and immunocompromised patients — is the preferred zoster vaccine. Two doses 2–6 months apart. Recommended for all CKD patients ≥50 years and all transplant candidates regardless of age (ideally before transplant). The older live Zostavax vaccine is contraindicated post-transplant. Shingrix is not yet universally available in all Philippine centers — inquire at your center or travel medicine clinic.

Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) High risk · now curable
Hepacivirus · Bloodborne · No vaccine — but highly effective antivirals exist
HCV in CKD
  • HCV prevalence in Philippine dialysis centers: 10–20% (vs <1% in general population)
  • HCV causes membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis — direct kidney disease
  • HCV + CKD → accelerated hepatic fibrosis from uremia
  • Cryoglobulinemic vasculitis from HCV → membranous nephropathy
  • No vaccine exists — prevention = universal precautions at dialysis
Treatment in CKD — now highly effective

Direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) achieve >95% SVR12 (sustained viral response = functional cure) even in CKD and dialysis. Glecaprevir/pibrentasvir (Maviret) — safe in all CKD stages including eGFR <30 — is preferred for CKD. Sofosbuvir-based regimens: avoid if eGFR <30 (renal excretion of GS-331007 metabolite). 8–12 week courses. Treatment dramatically slows CKD progression and is now a standard-of-care recommendation before kidney transplantation.

BK Polyomavirus & CMV High risk — transplant-specific
Polyomavirus / Herpesvirus · Reactivation under immunosuppression
BK virus nephropathy
  • Latent in 80% of healthy adults; reactivates under tacrolimus + mycophenolate
  • BK nephropathy in kidney transplant: 5–10% incidence; can cause graft loss
  • Monitored via plasma BK viral load (PCR) — quarterly in first 2 years
  • Treatment: immunosuppression reduction (paradoxically) — no specific antiviral
  • Cidofovir and leflunomide used off-label with limited evidence
CMV in transplant

CMV (cytomegalovirus) is the most common opportunistic viral infection after kidney transplantation. CMV disease (pneumonitis, colitis, retinitis) is life-threatening in immunosuppressed patients. All D+/R− transplants (donor positive, recipient negative) receive valganciclovir prophylaxis for 6 months. CMV viral load monitoring by PCR every 1–2 weeks in first 100 days. Ganciclovir / valganciclovir doses must be adjusted for eGFR — consult transplant nephrology.

Priority Vaccine #1

Hepatitis B Vaccination in CKD — The Most Critical Vaccine

🚨

Screen before vaccinate — mandatory in CKD

Before administering any hepatitis B vaccine, check: HBsAg (active infection — do not vaccinate), anti-HBs (immunity level), anti-HBc total (past exposure). Only vaccinate patients who are HBsAg-negative and anti-HBs <10 IU/L. Vaccinating an HBsAg-positive patient does no harm but wastes the vaccine. Missing an HBsAg-positive patient and failing to refer them appropriately is a serious clinical error.

Hepatitis B Vaccine — Double-Dose Protocol for CKD
All CKD patients who are HBsAg-negative and anti-HBs <10 IU/L
Critical
Standard dose40 mcg IM (double the normal 20 mcg adult dose) — Recombivax HB 40 mcg or Engerix-B 20 mcg × 2 vials given at same visit
Schedule0, 1, 2, and 6 months — 4-dose extended schedule (vs 3-dose for healthy adults). All 4 doses required for maximum protection in CKD.
SiteDeltoid muscle IM — never gluteal (poor immunogenicity). In dialysis patients: non-fistula arm only.
Post-vaccination serologyCheck anti-HBs titre 1–2 months after completing the 4-dose series. Target: anti-HBs ≥100 IU/L in CKD (higher threshold than standard ≥10 IU/L — wanes faster).
If non-responderAnti-HBs <10 after primary series: administer 3 additional double doses. Re-check anti-HBs 1–2 months after. If still <10: patient is a true non-responder — counsel strict universal precautions and HBsAg annual surveillance.
BoosterCheck anti-HBs annually in dialysis patients. If falls below 10 IU/L: give booster dose. In pre-dialysis CKD: check every 2–3 years.
Philippine brandsEngerix-B (GSK) · Hepavax-Gene (Berna Biotech) · Euvax B (LG Chem) — all available at Philippine hospital pharmacies and DOH centers
seroconversion ratesHealthy adults: 95% · CKD Stage 3: ~80% · CKD Stage 4–5: ~60% · Dialysis: 50–60% — this is why double-dose and 4-dose schedules are required
💡

Adjuvanted HBV vaccine — superior option when available

HEPLISAV-B (Dynavax) — a CpG-adjuvanted recombinant HBV vaccine — achieves significantly higher seroconversion rates in immunocompromised patients in a 2-dose schedule (0 and 1 month). Not yet widely available in the Philippines but registered in the US, EU, and Australia. For patients who can access it through international pharmacies or travel medicine clinics, it is the preferred option for CKD patients with known poor HBV vaccine response history.

Priority Vaccine #2

Influenza Vaccination in CKD — Annual, Every Year, No Exceptions

Inactivated Influenza Vaccine (IIV)
All CKD patients — all stages, all modalities, all ages ≥6 months
Annual · Critical
DoseStandard dose: 0.5 mL IM once annually. High-dose (Fluzone HD 60 mcg) or MF59-adjuvanted (Fluad) preferred in CKD Stage 3b–5 and dialysis when available — significantly better antibody response.
TimingBefore influenza season (July–October in Philippines corresponds to rainy season peak). Do not wait for the "worst" season — give at first opportunity each year.
In dialysisAdminister during the first half of a HD session when possible — post-dialysis fatigue reduces immune response to vaccination. For PD: standard IM timing.
ContraindicationsSevere egg allergy (anaphylaxis to egg): use egg-free recombinant vaccine (Flublok) if available. Minor egg allergy: standard IIV is safe. Active febrile illness: defer 2 weeks. Live attenuated nasal spray (LAIV): contraindicated in CKD and immunocompromised.
Household contactsVaccinate all household contacts — cocooning strategy. A vaccinated household reduces the CKD patient's exposure from within the home, where most transmission occurs. Household members should use inactivated (not live nasal) vaccine.
Philippine brandsVaxigrip Tetra (Sanofi) · Influvac Tetra (Abbott) · Fluarix Quadrivalent (GSK) — quadrivalent (4-strain) coverage preferred over trivalent
Priority Vaccine #3

COVID-19 Vaccination in CKD — Boosters Required

COVID-19 Vaccines — mRNA and Protein-Subunit
All CKD patients — primary series + updated boosters as recommended
Critical
Primary seriesComplete primary series regardless of prior infection. mRNA vaccines (Pfizer BNT162b2, Moderna mRNA-1273) produce superior antibody response compared to viral vector or inactivated vaccines in immunocompromised patients.
BoostersCKD and dialysis patients: booster doses recommended every 6–12 months or as updated by DOH/WHO guidelines for immunocompromised. Antibody waning is faster in CKD — do not wait for national recommendations designed for healthy adults.
Antibody responseDialysis patients achieve only 60–70% of the antibody titre of healthy adults after primary series. Booster dose brings response closer to healthy adult levels. Antibody titre testing (anti-spike IgG) can guide timing of boosters in immunocompromised — discuss with nephrologist.
Timing with dialysisOn a non-dialysis day if possible — avoids rapid clearance of activated lymphocytes through the HD circuit. If unavoidable, administer in first hour of HD session.
Post-transplantDefer vaccination 3–6 months after transplant if possible (immunosuppression highest early). After stable engraftment: complete primary series and boosters. mRNA vaccines are safe — no live virus component.
COVID antiviral in CKDNirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid): dose reduce if eGFR 30–60; contraindicated eGFR <30. Remdesivir: use with caution eGFR <30. Molnupiravir: no adjustment. Start within 5 days of symptoms.
Priority Vaccine #4

Varicella & Zoster Vaccination — Before Immunosuppression Begins

Varicella Vaccine (Chickenpox)
VZV-seronegative CKD patients who have never had chickenpox
Recommended
Vaccine typeLive attenuated (Varivax, Varilrix) — 2 doses 4–8 weeks apart
TimingMust be given ≥4 weeks before any immunosuppression (transplant, high-dose steroids). Cannot be given post-transplant or when eGFR <30 with active uremia. Give while eGFR still allows immune response.
Serology firstCheck VZV IgG. If already immune (prior infection or old vaccine): no vaccine needed. Screen all adult CKD patients without documented history.
Safety in CKDSafe in CKD Stage 1–3b non-immunosuppressed. Avoid in eGFR <30 or active immunosuppression.
Recombinant Zoster Vaccine (Shingrix)
All CKD patients ≥50 yrs; transplant candidates of any age
High priority
Vaccine typeNon-live recombinant subunit — safe in all CKD stages including post-transplant
Schedule2 doses, 2–6 months apart. Can give at any time — no restriction related to immunosuppression (non-live).
Efficacy in CKD91% efficacy in immunocompetent adults; ~68–87% in immunocompromised patients in RCTs — still strongly protective. Reduces postherpetic neuralgia (the most debilitating zoster complication) by 90%.
Philippine accessAvailable at select travel medicine clinics and private hospital pharmacies. Not yet in DOH EPI — out-of-pocket cost. Inquire at your nephrology center.
🚫

Live attenuated zoster vaccine (Zostavax) — contraindicated post-transplant

Zostavax contains live attenuated VZV and is absolutely contraindicated in transplant recipients and any patient on significant immunosuppression. Even minimal residual vaccine virus can cause disseminated varicella in the immunocompromised. If a patient received Zostavax before transplant: they are protected but the live vaccine cannot be re-administered post-transplant. Use Shingrix instead for all post-transplant patients requiring zoster protection.

Additional Recommended Vaccines

Other Vaccines Recommended for CKD Patients

Pneumococcal Vaccines (PCV15 / PCV20 / PPSV23)
All CKD Stage 3–5 and dialysis patients
High priority
Why in CKDStreptococcus pneumoniae causes post-viral bacterial pneumonia — the most common infectious death trigger in CKD patients post-influenza. Secondary pneumococcal pneumonia after influenza is 20–40× more common in dialysis patients than healthy adults.
Preferred schedulePCV15 or PCV20 (conjugate, broader coverage) FIRST, followed by PPSV23 (polysaccharide) 8 weeks later if PCV20 was not used. The sequential schedule enhances immunogenicity over PPSV23 alone.
PPSV23 boosterRepeat PPSV23 once after 5 years in CKD. In dialysis patients: every 5 years is recommended (faster antibody waning).
Philippine brandsPrevenar 20 (Pfizer) · Vaxneuvance (MSD) · Pneumovax 23 (MSD) — available at private hospitals and pharmacies
Hepatitis A Vaccine
All seronegative CKD patients; mandatory pre-transplant
Recommended
Why in CKDHAV infection in patients with underlying liver disease (HBV/HCV co-infection common in CKD) causes fulminant hepatic failure. HAV vaccination is simple, effective, and mandatory before kidney transplantation in most Philippine transplant centers.
Schedule2 doses: 0 and 6–12 months. Inactivated — safe in all CKD stages including post-transplant. Check anti-HAV IgG first (many Filipinos have natural immunity from childhood exposure).
Philippine brandsHavrix (GSK) · Vaqta (MSD) · Avaxim (Sanofi)
Tetanus-Diphtheria-Pertussis (Tdap)
All CKD patients not updated in last 10 years
Recommended
Why in CKDWound infections in CKD patients with AV fistula or PD catheter can introduce Clostridium tetani. Tetanus immunity wanes in uremia faster than in healthy adults.
ScheduleTdap once (if no prior adult Tdap), then Td booster every 10 years. Safe in all CKD stages.
Philippine brandsBoostrix (GSK) · Adacel (Sanofi) — available at DOH centers and private hospitals
Meningococcal Vaccine (MenACWY)
Transplant candidates; CKD patients with asplenia
Conditional
Indication in CKDMandatory pre-transplant at most Philippine centers. Also recommended for CKD patients with functional or anatomical asplenia (post-splenectomy, sickle cell disease). Complement deficiency from systemic lupus (common CKD cause) also increases meningococcal risk.
Schedule2 doses 8 weeks apart for immunocompromised. Booster every 5 years if risk persists.
Philippine brandsMenveo (GSK) · Menactra (Sanofi) — available at travel medicine clinics and major private hospitals
Human Papillomavirus (HPV) Vaccine
CKD patients 9–45 years (all genders); transplant candidates
Recommended
Why in CKDTransplant recipients have dramatically higher rates of HPV-related cancers (cervical, oropharyngeal, anal, penile) due to long-term immunosuppression. Vaccination before transplant provides the strongest protection. Post-transplant HPV vaccination is safe (inactivated) but produces lower antibody response.
Schedule3-dose series (0, 2, 6 months) for immunocompromised. Gardasil 9 covers 9 HPV strains. Ideally completed before kidney disease reaches advanced stages and before any immunosuppression.
Philippine brandsGardasil 9 (MSD) — available at DOH (ages 9–14 in school program) and private hospitals

Safety Rules

Live Vaccines in CKD — The Rules That Cannot Be Broken

Live attenuated vaccines contain weakened but replicating viral or bacterial particles. In healthy people, the immune system controls these attenuated pathogens and builds lasting immunity. In immunocompromised CKD patients — especially those post-transplant or on high-dose immunosuppression — the attenuated pathogen can replicate unchecked and cause the disease it was meant to prevent. Understanding which vaccines are live and which rules apply is a matter of patient safety, not preference.

Live vaccines — absolute contraindications

  • Post-transplant (any time) — live vaccines are contraindicated indefinitely after solid organ transplant due to ongoing immunosuppression
  • eGFR <20 mL/min with active uremia — immune response cannot contain live attenuated virus
  • High-dose corticosteroids — prednisolone ≥20mg/day for ≥2 weeks
  • Biological agents — rituximab, mycophenolate, tacrolimus, cyclosporine
  • Active malignancy on chemotherapy

Live vaccines affected: Varicella (Varivax), MMR, Zostavax (old zoster), LAIV (nasal flu), Yellow fever, Oral typhoid (Vivotif), Oral polio (not used in PH)

Safe window for live vaccines — timing rules

  • Give live vaccines ≥4 weeks before transplant or starting immunosuppression
  • Wait ≥3 months after stopping high-dose steroids or immunosuppressants before administering live vaccines
  • Wait ≥6–12 months after rituximab before live vaccines (B-cell depletion persists)
  • Household members of immunocompromised patients CAN receive live vaccines — the vaccinated person does not transmit vaccine virus significantly (exception: oral polio — avoid, but not used in Philippines)
  • If a post-transplant patient urgently needs varicella protection: use varicella-zoster immune globulin (VZIG) post-exposure instead of vaccine
Special Population

The Post-Transplant Vaccination Timeline

Kidney transplant recipients require a complete vaccination reassessment — pre-transplant vaccines may need to be repeated (immunosuppression reduces existing immunity), and a strict timeline governs when each vaccine can safely be administered post-transplant.

All time points are minimum waiting periods. The immunosuppression protocol, rejection episodes, and overall clinical stability influence the actual timing — always confirm with your transplant nephrology team before each vaccine.

📋

Pre-transplant vaccination checklist — complete these before surgery

  • Hepatitis B: Complete 4-dose double-dose series; confirm anti-HBs ≥100 IU/L; if not achieved, additional doses or HEPLISAV-B
  • Hepatitis A: 2-dose series if seronegative; check anti-HAV IgG first
  • Influenza: Current season dose
  • COVID-19: Primary series complete + booster within last 6 months
  • Pneumococcal: PCV20 + PPSV23
  • Varicella (live): If VZV-seronegative — must give ≥4 weeks before transplant
  • MMR (live): If not immune — must give ≥4 weeks before transplant
  • Shingrix: 2 doses if ≥50 years or if time permits
  • HPV: 3-dose series if age-eligible and not complete
  • Meningococcal: 2 doses
  • Tdap: If not updated in 10 years
Important: This guide reflects current KDIGO 2024, IDSA, and WHO vaccination recommendations for CKD patients as of 2025. Vaccine availability, scheduling, and specific indications are subject to change with updated guidelines and Philippine DOH EPI program updates. Always confirm your individual vaccination plan with your nephrologist and infectious disease specialist. Never self-administer vaccines without confirming your current immune status and CKD stage.
