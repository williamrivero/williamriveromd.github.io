The Basics

The Connection

How CKD Affects Your ThyroidPaano Nakakaapekto ang CKD sa Iyong ThyroidUnsaon Pakaapekto sa CKD sa Imong ThyroidPapanung Nakakaapekto ing CKD king Thyroid Mu

CKD affects the thyroid in several ways. As kidney function declines, waste products build up in the blood — a state called uremia. Uremia suppresses the thyroid gland and reduces the body's ability to convert T4 into the active form T3. The kidneys also retain too much iodine in CKD, which can slow hormone production further.Nakakaapekto ang CKD sa thyroid sa maraming paraan. Habang bumababa ang paggana ng bato, nag-iipon ang mga basura sa dugo — isang kondisyong tinatawag na uremia. Pinipigilan ng uremia ang thyroid gland at binabawasan ang kakayahan ng katawan na gawing T3 ang T4. Nananatili rin ang masyadong maraming yodo sa mga bato sa CKD, na maaaring magpabagal ng produksyon ng hormone.Nakaapekto ang CKD sa thyroid sa daghang paagi. Samtang mokunhod ang function sa bato, nag-ipon ang mga basura sa dugo — usa ka kondisyon nga gitawag uremia. Gipugngan sa uremia ang thyroid gland ug gipakunhod ang abilidad sa lawas nga himoong T3 ang T4. Nagpabilin usab ang sobrang yodo sa mga bato sa CKD, nga makapahinay pa sa produksyon sa hormone.Nakakaapekto ing CKD king thyroid king daramihang paraan. Habang bumababa ing paggana ning batu, nag-iipon ing mga basura king dugo — metung a kondisyon a tinatawag na uremia. Pinipigilan ning uremia ing thyroid gland at binabawasan ing kakayahan ning katawan na gawing T3 ing T4. Nananatili naman ing masyadong daming yodo king mga batu king CKD, a makapagpabagal pa ning produksyon ning hormone.

Can treating my thyroid improve my kidney function?Maaari bang mapabuti ng paggamot sa aking thyroid ang function ng aking bato?Makapaayo ba ang pagtambal sa akong thyroid sa function sa akong bato?Maaari bang mapabuti ning paggamot king thyroid ko ing function ning batu ko? +

Sometimes, yes. Hypothyroidism reduces blood flow to the kidneys, which can lower your eGFR (kidney function number). Treating hypothyroidism with levothyroxine can improve eGFR by 5–10 mL/min/1.73m² in some patients. Epidemiology

Prevalence & Risk Factors

Thyroid dysfunction affects 20–44% of CKD/ESRD patients, predominantly hypothyroidism. A 2025 prospective cross-sectional study at SIUT, Pakistan (n=131 HD patients) identified a 20.6% hypothyroidism prevalence, with 66.7% of those cases being overt. Independent risk factors included female sex, longer dialysis duration, and elevated serum urea and creatinine. [PMID 39897291]

A 2025 cross-sectional analysis of 350 DKD patients (Lahore General Hospital) demonstrated that thyroid dysfunction prevalence increases with KDIGO risk stage. FT3 declined linearly from 4.15 pmol/L (low risk) to 3.66 pmol/L (very high risk). FT3 correlated positively with eGFR (r=0.277, p<0.001) and inversely with ACR and serum creatinine. [PMID 40330362]

Mechanisms

Pathophysiology of Thyroid Dysfunction in CKD

1
Iodine retention

Reduced renal iodine clearance → elevated intrathyroidal iodine → Wolff-Chaikoff effect → transient or sustained thyroid suppression.

2
Uremia-induced cellular resistance

Uremic toxins impair intracellular T3 binding and nuclear receptor activation → functional hypothyroidism at the tissue level despite near-normal circulating levels.

3
↓ Peripheral T4→T3 conversion

Decreased 5'-deiodinase activity in CKD → low circulating T3 (euthyroid sick pattern) — the most universal thyroid finding in ESRD.

4
Nephrotic syndrome: TBG and TSH loss

Heavy proteinuria causes urinary loss of TBG (thyroxine-binding globulin) and TSH → spuriously low total T4, low total T3, and occasionally low TSH — mimicking hyperthyroidism or subclinical disease.

5
Protein-energy wasting

Malnutrition reduces TBG synthesis → low total T3/T4 without true hypothyroidism. Free T4 (not total) is the reliable index.

Treatment Decision

Subclinical Hypothyroidism in CKD — When to Treat

Subclinical hypothyroidism (SCHT: elevated TSH with normal Free T4) is more prevalent in CKD/ESKD than the general population and independently increases cardiovascular disease risk via vascular calcification, dyslipidemia, and endothelial dysfunction. 🔑

eGFR re-staging after levothyroxine initiation

Hypothyroidism independently reduces renal blood flow and GFR. Levothyroxine treatment can improve eGFR by 5–10 mL/min/1.73m². Always recheck serum creatinine and eGFR 8–12 weeks after initiating or dose-adjusting levothyroxine before re-staging CKD — a patient may move from G4 to G3b with treatment alone.

Non-Thyroidal Illness

Low T3 Syndrome / Euthyroid Sick Syndrome in ESRD

Low FT3 in CKD/ESRD represents uremia-driven suppression of peripheral T4→T3 conversion — not a primary thyroid disorder. Low FT3 is a strong independent predictor of cardiovascular mortality and all-cause mortality in dialysis patients, but treatment with exogenous T3 has not demonstrated benefit in RCTs and carries risk of cardiac harm (tachyarrhythmia, increased cardiac oxygen demand).

✗
Do NOT treat low T3 in CKD with T3 replacement

No RCT evidence of benefit. Cardiac harm risk. Document as non-thyroidal illness / euthyroid sick syndrome.

✓
Use low FT3 as a prognostic marker

Declining FT3 correlates with worsening KDIGO risk stage and increased CV mortality. Optimize dialysis adequacy, nutritional status, and manage underlying CKD progression.

→
Distinguish from true hypothyroidism

In true hypothyroidism: TSH >4 mIU/L + low Free T4. In euthyroid sick: low T3/FT3, TSH normal or mildly elevated, Free T4 often normal or low-normal. Context matters: acute illness, malnutrition, hospitalization all shift the picture.

Contrast & Iodine

Iodine Exposure & Dialysis Access Considerations

💉 Iodinated contrast (CT, angiography, fistulography)

Large iodine loads can trigger hypothyroidism in pre-existing autoimmune thyroid disease or hyperthyroidism (Jod-Basedow) in nodular goiter patients. Check TSH + Free T4 at 4–8 weeks post-exposure in at-risk patients (known nodular goiter, borderline TSH, autoimmune thyroid history).

🩺 Povidone-iodine dialysis access care

Repeated povidone-iodine application for dialysis catheter care is a significant cumulative iodine source. Consider chlorhexidine-based alternatives in patients with known thyroid disease or nodular goiter on long-term CVC access.

💊 Amiodarone

Amiodarone contains ~37% iodine by weight and causes thyroid dysfunction in up to 15–20% of treated patients — either hypothyroidism (more common in iodine-replete areas) or hyperthyroidism (Amiodarone-Induced Thyrotoxicosis, AIT). Check TFTs before initiation, at 3 months, then every 6 months. CKD patients on amiodarone for AF require heightened surveillance given overlapping symptoms with uremia.

Transplant Nephrology

Thyroid Disease in the Kidney Transplant Setting

Pre-transplant thyroid malignancy — rethinking the waiting period

Analysis of the UNOS database (2003–2019, n=86 pre-transplant thyroid malignancy patients) found that pretransplant thyroid malignancy does not significantly affect patient or graft survival (HR 0.66, p=0.31 for patient survival; HR 0.32, p=0.11 for graft survival). However, mean waitlist duration for these patients was 1,444 vs 438 days — an excess of >1,000 dialysis-days with no survival benefit. [PMID 34303544]

📋

Recommendation: individualized approach

Avoid blanket application of 2–5 year cancer-free waiting periods for pre-transplant thyroid malignancy. Consider tumor biology, treatment response, and patient-specific risk. Multidisciplinary discussion with endocrine surgery is advised.
