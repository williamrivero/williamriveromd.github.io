Understanding Your Lab Results

Getting Started

How to Read a Laboratory Report

Bring ALL your recent lab results to every consultation — not just the abnormal ones. Trends over time matter as much as individual values in CKD monitoring.

Laboratory results have three parts: the test name, your result, and the reference range. A result outside the reference range does not automatically mean something is seriously wrong — context, trends over time, and your clinical picture matter far more than any single number.

Trend matters more than a single result

A creatinine of 1.8 mg/dL that has been stable for 3 years is very different from a creatinine of 1.8 that was 1.2 three months ago. Always bring your previous results to compare. Your doctor reads patterns, not isolated numbers.

Reference ranges vary by laboratory

Different laboratories use different machines and reference populations. A result flagged "H" (high) at one lab may be within range at another. Always compare results from the same laboratory when tracking trends.

Kidney Function

Kidney Tests — What They Measure

🫘

Creatinine & eGFR

The primary measure of kidney filtering capacity

TestWhat it isNormal rangeWhat to watch
Serum CreatinineWaste product from muscle metabolism — rises when kidneys filter less efficiently0.6–1.1 mg/dL (F) / 0.7–1.3 (M)A rising creatinine over time is more important than a single high value
eGFR (CKD-EPI 2021)Estimated filtration rate calculated from creatinine, age, and sex. Think of it as % of kidney function remaining> 90 mL/min/1.73m²60–89 = mild decline · <60 = CKD confirmed
BUN (Blood Urea Nitrogen)Another waste product filtered by kidneys; also affected by protein intake, hydration, and liver function7–20 mg/dLBUN:Creatinine ratio >20 suggests dehydration or upper GI bleeding
BUN:Creatinine RatioHelps distinguish kidney causes of elevated creatinine from dehydration10:1 to 20:1>20:1 = likely dehydration or reduced kidney blood flow

Urine Albumin & Protein

The earliest and most sensitive marker of kidney damage

TestWhat it isNormal rangeWhat to watch
UACR (Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio)Measures albumin leaking into urine — the filtration membrane's "leak test." Abnormal years before eGFR declines.<30 mg/g30–300 = microalbuminuria · >300 = macroalbuminuria — active kidney damage
Spot Urine Protein-to-Creatinine Ratio (UPCR)Estimates 24-hour protein excretion from a single urine sample<0.2 g/g>0.5 significant; >3.5 = nephrotic-range proteinuria
24-Hour Urine ProteinGold standard for quantifying protein loss; requires collecting all urine for 24 hours<150 mg/day>300 mg/day = confirmed significant proteinuria requiring investigation
Complete Blood Count (CBC)

Blood Count — What Each Cell Measures

🩸

Red Blood Cells & Anemia Tests

Oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood

TestWhat it isNormal rangeWhat to watch
Hemoglobin (Hgb)The protein inside red blood cells that carries oxygen. Low hemoglobin = anemia = fatigue, shortness of breath120–160 g/L (F) / 130–170 (M)CKD target: 100–115 g/L. <90 = symptomatic anemia requiring treatment
Hematocrit (Hct)Percentage of blood volume made up of red blood cells — mirrors hemoglobin0.36–0.46 (F) / 0.40–0.50 (M)Runs roughly 3× the hemoglobin value in g/dL
MCV (Mean Corpuscular Volume)Size of red blood cells — helps identify the type of anemia80–100 fL<80 = iron deficiency or thalassemia · >100 = B12/folate deficiency
Reticulocyte CountImmature red blood cells — measures how fast the bone marrow is producing new RBCs0.5–2.5%Low reticulocytes + anemia = bone marrow not responding. High = active blood loss or recovery
Serum FerritinIron storage protein — best overall marker of iron stores12–150 ng/mL (F) / 12–300 (M)CKD/dialysis target: >200 ng/mL. >500 with inflammation may be falsely elevated
TSAT (Transferrin Saturation)Percentage of transferrin (iron transport protein) bound to iron — functional iron availability20–50%CKD target: >30%. <20% = iron-deficient erythropoiesis even if ferritin is normal
🛡️

White Blood Cells & Platelets

Immune defense and clotting

TestWhat it isNormal rangeWhat to watch
WBC (White Blood Cell Count)Total immune cell count — elevated in infection or inflammation, low in some medications or bone marrow conditions4.5–11.0 × 10⁹/L>15 suggests significant infection · <3.0 = leukopenia — may require medication review
Neutrophils (Segs)First-responder immune cells — elevated in bacterial infection50–70%>80% with high WBC = likely active bacterial infection
LymphocytesVirus-fighting cells — low in early viral infections, malnutrition, or immunosuppression20–40%<15% in CKD may indicate uremia-related immune suppression
PlateletsClotting cells — too low causes bleeding risk, too high can cause clotting150–400 × 10⁹/L<100 = increased bleeding risk — review anticoagulants · >600 = thrombocytosis, evaluate
Electrolytes & Chemistry

Metabolic Panel — Electrolytes and Organ Function

⚡

Electrolytes

Minerals that control fluid balance, nerve and muscle function

TestWhat it isNormal rangeWhat to watch
Sodium (Na)Controls fluid balance and nerve function. Low sodium causes confusion and seizures; high causes dehydration136–145 mEq/L<130 or >150 = symptomatic — requires urgent correction
Potassium (K)Critical for heart rhythm. Elevated in CKD because kidneys cannot excrete it. Life-threatening if too high.3.5–5.0 mEq/LCKD target: 3.5–5.5 pre-HD. >6.0 = cardiac emergency. <3.0 = hypokalemia — also dangerous
Bicarbonate (HCO₃)Acid-base buffer — low in metabolic acidosis, which accelerates muscle and bone breakdown in CKD22–29 mEq/LCKD target: 22–26. <18 = significant acidosis — often requires sodium bicarbonate treatment
Chloride (Cl)Works with sodium to maintain fluid balance — usually interpreted alongside sodium and bicarbonate98–107 mEq/LRarely interpreted alone; high chloride + low bicarbonate = metabolic acidosis pattern
Magnesium (Mg)Important for muscle, nerve, and heart function. Low in patients on SGLT2 inhibitors or with poor intake0.75–1.0 mmol/L (1.8–2.4 mg/dL)<0.7 = supplementation needed. Low Mg worsens potassium and calcium control

Liver & Protein Tests

Organ function and nutritional status

TestWhat it isNormal rangeWhat to watch
AlbuminMain blood protein — marker of nutritional status and liver function. Low albumin = protein malnutrition or liver disease3.5–5.0 g/dLCKD/dialysis target: ≥4.0 g/dL. <3.5 = malnutrition — requires nutritional intervention
ALT / SGPTLiver enzyme — elevated when liver cells are damaged (hepatitis, fatty liver, medications)<40 U/L (F) / <56 U/L (M)1–3× upper limit = mild elevation, monitor · >3× upper limit = significant liver stress
AST / SGOTEnzyme found in liver and muscles — less specific than ALT, also elevated in heart muscle damage<35 U/L (F) / <40 U/L (M)AST:ALT ratio >2:1 suggests alcoholic liver disease
Uric AcidBreakdown product of purines — elevated in gout, CKD, metabolic syndrome2.5–6.0 mg/dL (F) / 3.5–7.2 (M)CKD target: <6.0 mg/dL. >8.0 with symptoms = gout treatment indicated

Lipid Panel — Cholesterol and Heart Risk

Fasting Lipid Profile

LDL Cholesterol"Bad" cholesterol — the primary driver of atherosclerotic plaque formation. The most important lipid target.<100 mg/dLTarget <55 mg/dL for CKD + DM or prior ASCVD event
HDL Cholesterol"Good" cholesterol — removes cholesterol from arteries. Higher is better.>50 mg/dL (F) / >40 (M)Cannot be directly raised by medication — lifestyle (exercise, omega-3) helps

Glucose & Diabetes

Diabetes Tests — Blood Sugar Control

🍬

Glucose & HbA1c

Immediate and long-term blood sugar control

TestWhat it isNormal / TargetWhat to watch
Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS)Blood glucose after 8 hours of fasting — direct snapshot of current glucose control70–100 mg/dL (normal) / 80–130 (DM target)>126 on two occasions = diabetes · <70 = hypoglycemia — treat immediately
HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin)Average blood sugar over 90 days. Sugar attaches to hemoglobin over its lifespan — the more sugar, the higher the percentage.<5.7% (normal) / <6.5% (pre-DM threshold)DM target: 7–8% for CKD patients. >9% = poor control, high organ damage risk. <6.5% in CKD = hypoglycemia risk
Fasting Insulin & HOMA-IRMeasures insulin resistance — the root of Type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndromeHOMA-IR <2.02.0–3.0 = insulin resistance · >3.0 = significant IR, metabolic syndrome likely
C-PeptideMeasures how much insulin your pancreas is still producing — helps distinguish Type 1 from Type 2 DM0.5–2.0 ng/mL (fasting)Low C-peptide = pancreas producing little insulin. Helps guide insulin therapy decisions in advanced CKD

Urine Tests — What Your Urine Reveals

🔬

Complete Urinalysis (UA)

A window into kidney health and urinary tract status

ParameterWhat it meansNormalAbnormal significance
Protein (dipstick)Protein normally absent in urine — presence indicates kidney filter damageNegativeTrace = borderline · 1+ or higher = significant proteinuria — confirm with UACR or UPCR
Blood (hematuria)Red blood cells in urine — from kidney, ureter, bladder, or prostateNegative or 0–2 RBC/HPF3–10/HPF = microscopic hematuria, evaluate · Visible red urine = gross hematuria, urgent evaluation
Pus cells / WBCWhite blood cells in urine — sign of urinary tract infection or kidney inflammation0–5/HPF>10/HPF = pyuria — likely UTI or kidney inflammation. Culture and sensitivity needed.
GlucoseSugar in urine — normally absent as kidneys reabsorb it allNegativePositive without high blood sugar = SGLT2 inhibitor effect (expected) or renal glycosuria. High blood sugar + glucosuria = uncontrolled diabetes.
Specific GravityConcentration of the urine — reflects kidney's ability to concentrate or dilute1.005–1.030Fixed at 1.010 regardless of intake = loss of concentrating ability — marker of CKD. Very low = over-hydrated or diabetes insipidus.
CastsCylindrical structures formed in tubules — their type identifies the source of kidney pathologyAbsent or rare hyaline castsRBC casts = glomerulonephritis · WBC casts = pyelonephritis or interstitial nephritis · Granular/muddy brown casts = acute tubular necrosis

Bone & Mineral Tests

🦴

Calcium, Phosphorus & Parathyroid

KDIGO 2024 targets — critical in CKD Stage 3 and beyond

TestWhat it isKDIGO 2024 Target (CKD/ESKD)What to watch
Serum CalciumControlled by PTH and vitamin D. Low in CKD due to vitamin D deficiency and phosphate retention.8.4–10.2 mg/dL<7.5 = symptomatic hypocalcemia (cramps, tetany) · >10.5 = hypercalcemia — may indicate vitamin D toxicity
Serum PhosphorusRetained as kidneys fail. High phosphorus binds calcium, triggers PTH, and causes vessel calcification.3.5–5.5 mg/dL>5.5 = requires dietary restriction and/or phosphate binders. CaxP product >55 = vascular calcification risk
iPTH (Intact Parathyroid Hormone)Rises in CKD as a response to low calcium and high phosphorus — causes bone disease and cardiovascular damageDialysis: 130–600 pg/mL>600 = secondary hyperparathyroidism — requires active vitamin D or calcimimetic therapy
25-OH Vitamin DStorage form of vitamin D — commonly deficient in Filipino patients due to sun avoidance and CKD30–60 ng/mL (optimal)<20 = deficiency — requires supplementation · <30 = insufficiency
Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)Elevated in bone disease and liver disease — rises when bone turnover is high (hyperparathyroidism)44–147 U/LHigh ALP + high PTH = active bone disease from secondary hyperparathyroidism
Important: This guide is for educational purposes only. Reference ranges and targets vary by laboratory, patient age, sex, and clinical condition. Never interpret lab results in isolation — always discuss your results with your physician.
