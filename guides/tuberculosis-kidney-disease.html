PhilCAT/PSMID 2021 / NTP 6th MOP — DOTS & Standard Regimens

The 2021 Philippine TB CPG (PhilCAT/PSMID/PCCP) updated 17 recommendations using the GRADE framework — the most significant change being the discontinuation of Category II empiric retreatment. The NTP delivers all first-line drugs free under DOTS, mainly as fixed-dose combinations (FDCs). Rapid molecular testing (Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra now preferred over original Xpert) is the front-line diagnostic, establishing rifampicin susceptibility before treatment begins.

▣

2021 key update — DST before retreatment

Xpert MTB/RIF (or Ultra) is mandatory before initiating retreatment. If rifampicin-susceptible: start 2HRZE/4HR (Regimen 1). If rifampicin-resistant: refer immediately to PMDT — do not give Category II empirically. DST for other agents (beyond RIF) should guide PMDT regimen construction.

DOTS essentials

Every dose during the intensive phase is swallowed under observation by a treatment supporter or DOTS facility. NTP provides drugs, monitoring smears, and contact investigation at no cost. Document a treatment supporter and a fixed facility before starting.

What changes in CKD

The drugs are the same, but dosing intervals change: isoniazid and rifampicin are unchanged, whereas pyrazinamide and ethambutol must move to thrice-weekly dosing once CrCl < 30 mL/min or on dialysis (see the CKD-Stage Matrix). Daily (not intermittent) dosing of H/R is preferred in advanced immunosuppression.

Clinician Section 2

LTBI Screening in CKD — IGRA Preference & Pre-Transplant Workup

Most TB in CKD is reactivation of latent infection. The 2021 CPG (Q13, Weak recommendation) states that either TST or IGRA may be used for LTBI screening in non-HIV close contacts — cost and availability guide the choice. In nephrology practice, uremia causes cutaneous anergy, making TST unreliable in advanced CKD and dialysis; IGRA is the preferred test in this population wherever available.

Whom to screen

All CKD G4–G5 and dialysis patients; all kidney-transplant candidates and living donors; before starting biologics, high-dose or prolonged corticosteroids, or other potent immunosuppression. Pre-transplant workup

Additive QT risk

Bedaquiline, clofazimine, and the fluoroquinolone together produce additive QT prolongation. Obtain a baseline ECG and serial ECGs, keep K⁺ > 4.0 and Mg²⁺ normal (electrolyte shifts are routine in dialysis), and review concomitant QT-prolonging drugs (ondansetron, azoles, macrolides).

Transplant strategy

In kidney-transplant recipients with active TB, substitute rifabutin for rifampicin (weaker inducer), pre-empt CNI dose increases, monitor levels 2–3×/week, and co-manage with transplant ID. Re-titrate every induced drug when the rifamycin is eventually stopped.

Clinician Section 5

Hepatotoxicity Monitoring — LFT-Based Decision Tree

Isoniazid, rifampicin, and pyrazinamide are all hepatotoxic. Obtain baseline LFTs in every patient and monitor more closely (2-weekly) in CKD, the elderly, alcohol use, viral hepatitis, or baseline derangement.

⛔

Stop H / R / Z if any threshold is met (ATS / NTP)

  • ALT ≥ 3× ULN with symptoms (nausea, vomiting, RUQ pain, jaundice), or
  • ALT ≥ 5× ULN regardless of symptoms, or
  • Total bilirubin ≥ 2× ULN.
1

Baseline & monitor

Check ALT/AST, bilirubin, ALP before therapy; risk-stratify. Educate on warning symptoms and recheck on schedule or at any new symptom.

2

Threshold met → STOP hepatotoxic drugs

Hold isoniazid, rifampicin, and pyrazinamide. If smear-positive or clinically unstable and treatment cannot wait, bridge with a non-hepatotoxic regimen: ethambutol + levofloxacin + an aminoglycoside (renally dosed).

3

Investigate other causes

Exclude viral hepatitis (A/B/C/E), alcohol, biliary obstruction, and other hepatotoxins before attributing solely to ATT.

4

Wait for recovery

Resume only once ALT < 2× ULN and symptoms resolve, with bilirubin normalizing.

5

Sequential rechallenge

Reintroduce rifampicin first (least hepatotoxic, most essential), then isoniazid after 3–7 days, rechecking LFTs at each step. Reintroduce pyrazinamide last — or omit it if DILI was severe, extending the regimen (e.g., 2HRE / 7HR = 9 months).

6

Recurrence on rechallenge

If ALT rises again, identify and permanently drop the culprit, then redesign the regimen (often pyrazinamide-sparing) with ID input.
