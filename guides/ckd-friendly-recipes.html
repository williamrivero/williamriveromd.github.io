Before You Cook

How to Use These Recipes

The leaching method removes 30–50% of potassium from vegetables: peel → dice small → soak in warm water 2 hours → boil → drain. Never use the boiling water as soup stock.

These recipes are designed to be flavorful, affordable, and kidney-safe using ingredients available at any palengke or supermarket. Each recipe is tagged for pre-dialysis (CKD Stage 1–4) or dialysis (hemodialysis/peritoneal dialysis) patients — some are safe for both. Always confirm portions with your nephrologist based on your current lab results.

PRE-DIALYSIS — Lower protein, stricter potassium/phosphorus limits
DIALYSIS (HD/PD) — Higher protein needed; stricter fluid and potassium control
BOTH — Safe for all CKD patients with appropriate portion adjustments

General cooking rules

Cook fresh — never from cans or processed packs. Use calamansi, suka (vinegar), bawang (garlic), luya (ginger), and sibuyas (onion) for flavor instead of patis, toyo, or salt. Boil and discard water for high-potassium vegetables before adding to dishes.

Portion awareness

Recipes are based on 1 serving for a 60 kg patient. Adjust protein portions proportionally to your actual weight. Phosphorus and potassium estimates are approximate — lab results guide individual adjustments, not recipes alone.

⚠

Take your phosphate binder WITH every meal

If your doctor has prescribed a phosphate binder (calcium carbonate, sevelamer), it must be taken at the START of every meal — not before or after. The binder works by trapping phosphorus from the food you are currently eating. Skipping it even once allows excess phosphorus to be absorbed.

Soups & Broth Dishes

Soups are a Filipino staple. For CKD patients, use low-sodium broth, discard vegetable boiling water, and avoid adding patis or salt at the table.

Kidney-Safe Tinola

Classic chicken ginger broth with sayote — naturally low in potassium and phosphorus

Prep 10 min Cook 30 min Serves 4
Ingredients
  • 300 g chicken breast (skin removed), cut into pieces
  • 2 medium sayote (chayote), peeled and cubed
  • 1 thumb-size luya (ginger), sliced
  • 3 cloves bawang (garlic), crushed
  • 1 sibuyas (onion), sliced
  • 1 tbsp canola oil
  • 5 cups water
  • Pepper to taste — NO patis or salt
Steps
  1. Sauté garlic, onion, and ginger in oil until fragrant.
  2. Add chicken and cook until lightly browned on all sides.
  3. Pour in water, bring to a boil. Skim foam from surface.
  4. Reduce heat, simmer 20 minutes until chicken is tender.
  5. Add sayote, cook 5–7 minutes until just tender.
  6. Season with pepper only. Serve hot with white rice.
Why it works
  • Sayote is among the lowest-potassium vegetables available
  • Chicken breast = complete protein, low phosphorus additives
  • No salt → sodium stays low
  • HD modification: Add 1 cup extra chicken and reduce sayote to 1 piece

Modified Sinigang na Bangus

Low-sodium sinigang using calamansi instead of commercial sinigang mix

Prep 15 min Cook 25 min Serves 4
Ingredients
  • 400 g bangus (milkfish) belly, cleaned, cut into portions
  • Juice of 6–8 calamansi (for sourness — replaces sinigang mix)
  • 1 medium radish (labanos), sliced — low potassium
  • 1 cup sitaw (string beans), cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 1 medium tomato, quartered
  • 1 sibuyas, quartered
  • 5 cups water
  • Pepper to taste — NO patis, NO salt, NO sinigang mix
Steps
  1. Bring water to a boil with tomato and onion.
  2. Add bangus pieces, simmer 10 minutes.
  3. Add labanos and sitaw, cook 8 minutes more.
  4. Squeeze in calamansi juice, stir gently.
  5. Taste and add more calamansi for sourness. Pepper only.
  6. Serve immediately — do not reduce/over-boil.
Why it works
  • Calamansi gives authentic sour flavor — zero sodium
  • Commercial sinigang mix = ~900 mg sodium per pack — avoided entirely
  • Labanos and sitaw are low-potassium vegetables
  • Avoid: kangkong, camote tops in sinigang — high potassium

Upo at Itlog na Puti Soup

Gentle bottle gourd soup with egg whites — ultra-low potassium and phosphorus

Prep 10 min Cook 20 min Serves 2
Ingredients
  • 2 cups upo (bottle gourd), peeled and cubed
  • 3 egg whites (itlog puti lamang)
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp canola oil
  • 3 cups water or unsalted chicken broth
  • Pepper and a pinch of dried herbs
Steps
  1. Sauté garlic in oil until golden.
  2. Add upo, stir for 2 minutes.
  3. Pour in water or broth, bring to boil.
  4. Simmer 12 minutes until upo is soft and translucent.
  5. Slowly pour egg whites in a thin stream while stirring — creates soft egg ribbons.
  6. Season with pepper only. Serve with white rice.
Why it works
  • Upo is among the absolute lowest-potassium vegetables
  • Egg whites = complete protein with virtually no phosphorus (yolk removed)
  • Ideal for Stage 3–4 CKD patients on protein restriction

Chicken Congee (Goto-Style)

High-protein congee using chicken breast — goto without the organ meat risk

Prep 10 min Cook 40 min Serves 2
Ingredients
  • ½ cup white rice (malagkit if desired for texture)
  • 200 g chicken breast, shredded
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 thumb luya, julienned
  • 3 cloves garlic, fried crispy in oil
  • 2 stalks dahon ng sibuyas (green onion tops), sliced thin
  • Calamansi halves for serving
  • Pepper to taste — NO patis
Steps
  1. Boil chicken breast in 4 cups water with ginger, 20 minutes.
  2. Remove chicken, shred finely. Reserve broth.
  3. Add rice to broth, simmer on low 25–30 minutes, stirring often, until porridge consistency.
  4. Return shredded chicken, stir through.
  5. Serve topped with fried garlic bits, green onion, and calamansi.
Why it works
  • High protein for HD patients — chicken breast, no organ meat
  • Traditional goto uses tripe (tripas) — extremely high in phosphorus
  • Rice dilutes phosphorus and potassium concentration
  • Calamansi replaces patis perfectly

Inihaw na Tilapia

Grilled tilapia with calamansi-garlic dip — no marinade needed

Prep 5 min Cook 15 min Serves 2
Ingredients
  • 2 medium tilapia, whole (cleaned) or fillets
  • 4 calamansi, halved
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp canola oil
  • Banana leaves or foil for grilling
  • Pepper only — NO toyo, NO patis
Steps
  1. Score fish with 2–3 diagonal cuts on each side.
  2. Rub with pepper and a light brush of oil.
  3. Grill on banana leaves or foil over medium heat, 7 minutes per side.
  4. Mix minced garlic with calamansi juice for dipping sauce.
  5. Serve immediately with white rice and a simple vegetable side.
Why it works
  • Grilling requires no added sodium — natural flavor shines
  • Tilapia has moderate protein and natural phosphorus only (well-absorbed but manageable)
  • Avoid soy sauce-based marinades — 1 tbsp toyo = ~900 mg sodium

Torta ng Itlog Puti at Sayote

Egg white omelette with grated sayote — kidney-safe comfort food

Prep 8 min Cook 10 min Serves 1
Ingredients
  • 4 egg whites (no yolks)
  • ½ cup sayote, finely grated or diced small
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp sibuyas, minced
  • 1 tbsp canola oil
  • Pepper to taste — NO salt
Steps
  1. Beat egg whites until slightly frothy. Add pepper.
  2. Sauté garlic and onion in oil until soft.
  3. Add grated sayote, cook 3 minutes, stirring.
  4. Pour egg whites over vegetables. Do not stir.
  5. Cook on medium-low until bottom is set (3 min), then flip carefully.
  6. Cook 2 more minutes. Serve with white rice and calamansi.
Why it works
  • Egg whites = near-zero phosphorus (yolk removed) + complete protein
  • Sayote = lowest-potassium vegetable available in the Philippines
  • Inexpensive, filling, and quick to prepare

Steamed Chicken with Ginger-Calamansi Sauce

High-protein steamed chicken — HD patients need 1.0–1.4 g/kg protein daily

Prep 10 min Cook 25 min Serves 2
Ingredients
  • 300 g chicken breast or thigh (skin removed)
  • 1 thumb luya (ginger), sliced thin
  • 3 stalks tanglad (lemongrass), bruised
  • Juice of 5 calamansi
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced (for sauce)
  • 1 tsp sesame oil (for sauce only — not cooking)
  • Pepper to taste
Steps
  1. Place chicken pieces on a steaming rack over boiling water.
  2. Top with ginger slices and lemongrass stalks.
  3. Cover and steam 20–25 minutes until fully cooked through.
  4. Mix calamansi juice, minced garlic, sesame oil, and pepper for dipping sauce.
  5. Slice chicken, serve with sauce alongside white rice.
Why it works
  • Steaming preserves protein without adding sodium
  • Lemongrass and ginger provide complex flavor with zero sodium
  • Large portions appropriate for HD protein requirements

Paksiw na Isda (Low-Sodium)

Vinegar-braised fish — using cane vinegar replaces all the patis

Prep 5 min Cook 20 min Serves 2
Ingredients
  • 300 g galunggong or bangus, cut into pieces
  • 3 tbsp suka (cane vinegar or white vinegar)
  • ½ cup water
  • 4 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 thumb ginger, sliced
  • ½ tsp whole black pepper
  • 2 pcs siling haba (finger chili) — optional
  • NO patis, NO toyo, NO salt
Steps
  1. Arrange fish in a shallow pan.
  2. Add vinegar, water, garlic, ginger, pepper, and chili.
  3. Bring to a boil uncovered for 5 minutes (to cook off vinegar sharpness).
  4. Cover, reduce heat, simmer 15 minutes.
  5. Serve with white rice. The natural fish flavor and vinegar provide all the taste needed.
Why it works
  • Vinegar = zero sodium natural preservative and flavor agent
  • Traditional paksiw uses patis — this version has <100 mg sodium
  • Galunggong = budget-friendly, lower phosphorus than canned sardines

Ginisang Sayote at Sitaw

Sautéed chayote and string beans — the kidney patient's everyday gulay

Prep 10 min Cook 12 min Serves 2
Ingredients
  • 2 medium sayote, peeled and sliced thin
  • 1 cup sitaw, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 100 g chicken breast, thinly sliced (optional — adds protein for HD)
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 sibuyas, sliced
  • 1 tbsp canola oil
  • Pepper — NO patis or salt
Steps
  1. Sauté garlic and onion in oil until soft.
  2. Add chicken (if using), cook until white all around.
  3. Add sayote and sitaw, toss to coat in oil.
  4. Add 2–3 tbsp water, cover, steam-sauté 8 minutes.
  5. Uncover, toss, season with pepper. Serve.

Ginisang Repolyo

Sautéed cabbage — one of the safest, most affordable kidney-friendly vegetables

Prep 5 min Cook 10 min Serves 2
Ingredients
  • ½ head repolyo (cabbage), thinly sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 sibuyas, sliced
  • 1 medium tomato, diced
  • 1 tbsp canola oil
  • Calamansi juice (squeeze over before serving)
  • Pepper to taste
Steps
  1. Sauté garlic and onion until soft and fragrant.
  2. Add tomato, cook until softened, 3 minutes.
  3. Add cabbage, toss to mix well.
  4. Cover, cook 5 minutes until wilted but still slightly crisp.
  5. Squeeze calamansi over, add pepper. Serve immediately.

Ampalaya at Itlog Puti

Bitter melon with egg whites — anti-inflammatory and kidney-protective

Prep 15 min Cook 12 min Serves 2
Ingredients
  • 2 medium ampalaya, deseeded and sliced thin
  • 3 egg whites
  • 1 medium tomato, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 sibuyas, sliced
  • 1 tbsp canola oil — Pepper to taste
Reducing bitterness
  • Slice ampalaya thin, sprinkle with 1 tsp salt, massage, let sit 10 min
  • Rinse thoroughly under running water — removes bitterness AND reduces potassium
  • Squeeze gently and drain before cooking
Steps
  1. Sauté garlic, onion, tomato until soft.
  2. Add prepared ampalaya, toss well, cook 5 minutes.
  3. Push to sides, pour egg whites in center, scramble gently.
  4. Fold ampalaya back in, toss together. Season with pepper.

Sinangag (Garlic Rice)

Classic garlic fried rice — day-old rice keeps phosphorus absorption lower

Prep 2 min Cook 8 min Serves 1
Ingredients
  • 1 cup cold cooked white rice (day-old is best)
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp canola oil
  • Pepper to taste — NO salt, NO toyo
Steps
  1. Heat oil in a wok or pan over medium-high heat.
  2. Add garlic, fry until golden and crispy — do not burn.
  3. Add cold rice, breaking up clumps with a spatula.
  4. Toss and fry 5 minutes until each grain is coated and slightly crispy.
  5. Season with pepper. Top with extra fried garlic bits.
Kidney notes
  • White rice = lowest phosphorus of all staple grains
  • Day-old rice has slightly lower glycemic index
  • Avoid adding toyo for color — use turmeric powder instead (natural color, anti-inflammatory)

Fresh Pineapple with Crackers

One of the few fruits kidney patients can enjoy freely — sweet, acidic, low potassium

Prep 5 min Cook None Serves 1
Ingredients
  • ½ cup fresh pineapple chunks (not canned in syrup)
  • 4–6 unsalted crackers or rice cakes
  • Optional: small drizzle of honey
Kidney notes
  • Pineapple: only ~180 mg potassium per cup — safe for most CKD patients
  • Naturally high in Vitamin C — helps iron absorption
  • Avoid canned pineapple — syrup adds sugar; rinse with water if using canned
  • Unsalted crackers: no sodium. Avoid regular crackers or Skyflakes (salted)
  • Grapes (½ cup) and apple slices are equally good low-potassium alternatives

Kitchen Techniques

CKD Cooking Tips That Make a Real Difference

The leaching method — reduce potassium by up to 50%

Peel and cut vegetable into small pieces → soak in large amount of warm water for 2 hours (change water once) → drain → boil in fresh water → drain again and discard water. Works for kamote, potato, pechay, kangkong, sitaw. Never use the boiling water as sabaw.

Replace patis with calamansi + pepper

1 tbsp patis = ~1,500 mg sodium. Calamansi juice provides similar umami brightness with zero sodium. For dishes needing depth, use a combination of calamansi + garlic + black pepper + a drop of sesame oil (for flavor, not cooking).

Grilling, steaming, and poaching over frying

Grilling and steaming require no breading (which adds phosphate additives) and no sauces (which add sodium). They also reduce fat content — important for CKD patients with concurrent dyslipidemia. Reserve frying for small amounts of garlic bits and onion for topping.

Avoid phosphate-laden processed foods entirely

Hotdog, tocino, longganisa, processed cheese, instant noodles, and canned goods all contain inorganic phosphate preservatives absorbed at 90–100%. One piece of hotdog can contain 400 mg phosphorus — nearly half the daily limit. Cook fresh every day when possible.

🧂

Flavor without salt — your new pantry essentials

  • Calamansi / suka — brightens any dish, replaces patis and toyo
  • Luya (ginger) — warming, anti-inflammatory, flavor depth
  • Tanglad (lemongrass) — fragrant, zero sodium, great for fish and chicken
  • Bawang (garlic) — most versatile flavor base; anti-inflammatory
  • Pandan leaves — natural sweetness and aroma for rice dishes
  • Black pepper — replaces salt's role in bringing flavors forward
  • Turmeric (dilaw) — anti-inflammatory, gives color without toyo
