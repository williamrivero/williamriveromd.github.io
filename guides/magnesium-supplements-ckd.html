If you have chronic kidney disease, are on dialysis, have had a kidney transplant, or you simply do not know your kidney function, ask your kidney clinician or pharmacist before using any magnesium supplement, laxative, antacid, powder, or electrolyte product. Your kidneys are what clear extra magnesium out of your body. When that clearance is impaired, magnesium can build up — and the result, called hypermagnesemia, ranges from nausea and drowsiness to dangerously slow breathing and abnormal heart rhythm. Your estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) is the number that tells your clinician how much clearance you still have.Kung mayroon kayong chronic kidney disease, nasa dialysis, sumailalim sa kidney transplant, o hindi ninyo alam ang paggana ng inyong bato, magtanong sa inyong doktor sa bato o parmasyutiko bago gumamit ng anumang magnesium supplement, laxative, antacid, pulbos, o produktong electrolyte. Terms

Bioavailability
The share of a swallowed dose that actually reaches the bloodstream. In magnesium studies it is usually inferred from how much extra magnesium appears in the urine — a marker of uptake, not of benefit.
Blood–brain barrier
The selective lining of brain blood vessels that controls which substances reach brain tissue. Central to the L-threonate marketing claim, and not settled for the intact compound in humans.
Bowel preparation
The purgative regimen taken before a colonoscopy. Magnesium-based preparations deliver a very large magnesium load over a few hours.
Chelate
A mineral bound to an organic molecule such as an amino acid — bisglycinate is a chelate. Chelation changes solubility and tolerability, not the amount of magnesium your kidneys must eventually clear.
Elemental magnesium
The weight of actual magnesium in a compound, as opposed to the weight of the whole salt. The clinically meaningful number, and the one on the Supplement Facts panel rather than the front of the bottle.
Hypermagnesemia
A dangerously high blood magnesium level. In reduced kidney function it can follow ordinary over-the-counter products, and it causes nausea, flushing, weakness, low blood pressure, slowed breathing, and abnormal heart rhythm.
Hypomagnesemia
A low blood magnesium level. Causes include chronic diarrhea, malabsorption, diabetes, alcohol use, diuretics, and prolonged PPI therapy.
Malabsorption
Impaired absorption of nutrients from the gut — a genuine and often overlooked cause of real magnesium depletion.
Osmotic laxative
An agent that draws water into the bowel, softening stool and prompting a bowel movement. This is how unabsorbed magnesium salts cause diarrhea — a drug effect, not evidence of nutritional benefit.
Serum magnesium
The magnesium measured in blood. Only a small fraction of body magnesium circulates, so a normal value does not fully exclude depleted stores — but the test still reliably detects both low and dangerously high levels.
Transdermal
Absorbed through the skin. The evidence that clinically meaningful magnesium crosses intact skin from sprays, oils, or Epsom-salt baths is weak.
