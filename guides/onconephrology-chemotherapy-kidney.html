Summary

Evidence base. The 34th Acute Disease Quality Initiative (ADQI) report on conventional cytotoxics (Lentini et al., 2026) covers cisplatin, ifosfamide, methotrexate, pemetrexed, gemcitabine, melphalan and mitomycin C. It offers expert-based narrative synthesis without graded recommendations.

Practice. Recognize that the cancer can injure the kidney before the drug does, choose the kidney-function measure that sets each dose, apply agent-specific prevention and rescue, adjust around dialysis, and watch checkpoint inhibitors, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)-pathway drugs and tumor lysis.

Think of the kidney as both the exit door and a possible target. Many chemotherapy medicines leave the body through the kidneys. On the way out, a few of them collect inside the very kidney cells that are pushing them out.Isipin ang bato bilang pintuang labasan at posibleng target. Each kidney has about a million filtering units. Blood is first filtered at the glomerulus, a tiny ball of blood vessels. The filtered fluid then flows through a long tube, the tubule. The first part of that tube, the proximal tubule, works like a busy pump station. Its cells use protein "doors" (transporters) to pull certain medicines from the blood and push them into the urine. This is how the body clears drugs such as cisplatin, methotrexate, and pemetrexed.May humigit-kumulang isang milyong pansala ang bawat bato. Unang sinasala ang dugo sa glomerulus, isang maliit na bola ng mga daluyan ng dugo. Dumadaloy ang nasalang likido sa isang mahabang tubo, ang tubule. Ang unang bahagi nito, ang proximal tubule, ay parang abalang pumping station. Gumagamit ang mga cell nito ng mga protinang "pinto" (transporter) para hilahin ang ilang gamot mula sa dugo at itulak sa ihi. Ganito inaalis ng katawan ang mga gamot gaya ng cisplatin, methotrexate, at pemetrexed.Adunay mga usa ka milyon nga pansala ang matag bato. Una nga gisala ang dugo sa glomerulus, usa ka gamay nga bola sa mga ugat sa dugo. Modagayday ang nasala nga likido sa usa ka taas nga tubo, ang tubule. Ang unang bahin niini, ang proximal tubule, sama sa busy nga pumping station. Mogamit ang mga cell niini og protina nga "pultahan" (transporter) aron bitlingon ang pipila ka tambal gikan sa dugo ug iduso sa ihi. Mao kini ang paagi sa lawas sa pagkuha sa mga tambal sama sa cisplatin, methotrexate, ug pemetrexed.Atin intang metung a milyung pansala ing balang batu. The trouble starts when a drug comes in faster than it can leave. Cisplatin is a good example: the cell pulls it in efficiently through an entry door called organic cation transporter 2 (OCT2), but the exit pump, multidrug and toxin extrusion protein 1 (MATE1), is slower. The drug builds up inside the cell, damages its energy factories (mitochondria), and the injured cells leak salt, potassium, and especially magnesium into the urine. If injury is heavier, the kidney filters less and creatinine rises. Kapag mas mabigat ang pinsala, mas kaunti ang nasasala ng bato at tumataas ang creatinine. Why a "normal" creatinine can misleadBakit maaaring nakalilinlang ang "normal" na creatinineNganong makapahisalaag ang "normal" nga creatinineNung bakit malyaring makapanlinlang ing "normal" a creatinine

Creatinine comes from muscle. People who are thin, older, bedridden, or who have lost weight from cancer make less creatinine, so their creatinine looks "normal" even when the kidneys filter less. The estimate then overstates kidney function, and a dose based on it may be too high.     Evidence Base

    ADQI 34: what the consensus is, and what it is not

    The 34th Acute Disease Quality Initiative (ADQI) conference met in Charlottesville, Virginia, from September 26 to 29, 2024, with nephrologists, oncologists, hematologists, intensivists, and pharmacists divided into four workgroups. The conventional cytotoxic workgroup report (Lentini et al., Kidney International 2026;109:1114–1124) covers seven agents: cisplatin, ifosfamide, methotrexate, pemetrexed, gemcitabine, melphalan, and mitomycin C. For each it synthesizes epidemiology, mechanism, risk factors, clinical phenotype, prevention, management, and a research agenda, organized as four tables (mechanisms; risk factors and prevention; pharmacology, dosing in reduced GFR, and toxicity management; knowledge gaps), a histopathologic pattern figure, a transport schematic, and a prevention-to-management algorithm.

    The cancer can injure the kidney before the drug does

    Measuring GFR

    Which kidney-function number should set an anticancer dose?

    Physiology first. Creatinine is generated by muscle; its serum level reflects generation divided by clearance. Cachexia, sarcopenia, amputation, paralysis, and prolonged bed rest lower generation, so creatinine-based estimates overstate GFR in exactly the patients most exposed to dose-related toxicity. Cystatin C is generated by nucleated cells and is largely independent of muscle, but malignancy, inflammation, high cell turnover, systemic glucocorticoids, obesity, and smoking raise it independently of GFR (thyroid dysfunction can move it either way), so cystatin C–based estimates can understate GFR and push toward underdosing. A 2026 commentary by Delanaye and Agarwal summarizes the directions of these errors and makes the bedside point plainly: even the best combined equations place only 85% to 90% of estimates within 30% of measured GFR, so at least one patient in ten is materially misestimated, and measured GFR should be considered when the decision matters.

    What the guidelines now say

    KDIGO 2024 holds that creatinine-based eGFR is appropriate for dosing most drugs, but that eGFR combining creatinine and cystatin C (eGFRcr-cys), or measured GFR, is preferred when a drug has a narrow therapeutic or toxic range, and that eGFR not indexed to body surface area (BSA) should be used at extremes of body size. These are practice points, not graded recommendations.

    The International Consensus Guideline for Anticancer Drug Dosing in Kidney Dysfunction (ADDIKD), built by 38 working-group members using GRADE appraisal of 59 drugs plus consensus voting and published as three papers in eClinicalMedicine in 2025, brings oncology into line with KDIGO:

    • Assess kidney function with eGFR from the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) equation rather than Cockcroft-Gault creatinine clearance (CrCl), and classify it by KDIGO categories. The main barrier identified was oncologists' familiarity with Cockcroft-Gault.
    • For dosing, convert eGFR to an absolute value (mL/min) by removing the 1.73 m² indexing (BSA-adjusted eGFR = eGFR × BSA ÷ 1.73), particularly when body size is far from average.
    • Use directly measured GFR (clearance of iohexol, iothalamate, 51Cr-ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid [EDTA], or 99mTc-diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid [DTPA], in mL/min) at baseline before high-dose methotrexate (a strong recommendation), before high-dose cisplatin, and whenever eGFR is unreliable (extremes of body composition, amputation, paraplegia, skeletal muscle disease).
    • For carboplatin, measured GFR is preferred in the Calvert formula, especially with curative intent or when eGFR is above 125 or at or below 45 mL/min/1.73 m²; if unavailable, use BSA-adjusted CKD-EPI eGFR, which predicts drug exposure (area under the curve, AUC) more accurately than Cockcroft-Gault.
    • eGFR is out of scope in AKI, on kidney replacement therapy (KRT), in pregnancy, and under 18 years. In non–steady-state AKI, a kinetic eGFR is a better bedside estimate than any static equation.

    Worked example: de-indexing (illustrative only)

    Cisplatin

    Mechanism. Cisplatin is taken up across the basolateral membrane of proximal tubular cells mainly by organic cation transporter 2 (OCT2) and copper transporter 1 (CTR1), with possible contributions from organic anion transporters 1 and 3, and is extruded apically by multidrug and toxin extrusion protein 1 (MATE1). Uptake that outpaces efflux concentrates the drug, which forms reactive thiol metabolites, cross-links nuclear and mitochondrial DNA, inhibits respiratory complexes (reactive oxygen species, adenosine triphosphate depletion), depletes glutathione, and triggers apoptotic and inflammatory signaling. The S3 segment in the outer medulla bears the brunt. Hypomagnesemia is both a consequence and, by enhancing tubular platinum handling, a risk factor.

    Incidence and prediction. AKI is reported in roughly 20% to 45% of patients depending on definition and preventive practice. A stricter endpoint gives a clearer picture: in 24,717 adults receiving a first dose of intravenous cisplatin at six U.S. cancer centers, severe cisplatin-associated AKI (doubling of creatinine or KRT within 14 days) occurred in 5.2% of the derivation and 3.3% of the validation cohort. A simple nine-item score built from routine variables (age, hypertension, diabetes, current or former smoking, hemoglobin, white blood cell count, serum albumin, serum magnesium, and cisplatin dose; smoking applies to the simple score only, per the published correction) predicted risk monotonically; the highest category carried about 24-fold higher odds than the lowest in derivation (17.9-fold in validation), and the full model had a C statistic of 0.75. This is a validated prognostic tool, not yet an intervention trial: no study has shown that acting on the score changes outcomes.

    Prevention, calibrated. Intravenous saline before and after the dose is standard and physiologically sound (higher tubular flow, lower luminal concentration). Magnesium supplementation is the best-supported adjunct: a meta-analysis of 15 observational studies (1,841 patients) found lower odds of cisplatin AKI with magnesium (odds ratio 0.24, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.19–0.32). Because these were nonrandomized, confounding by protocol era and patient selection cannot be excluded; the effect is associated, not demonstrated. Mannitol is not supported: a multicenter target trial emulation in more than 24,000 adults found no benefit and more AKI with mannitol, so routine use is hard to justify outside protocols. Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors reduce cisplatin uptake and nephrotoxicity in animal models; human evidence is lacking. Amifostine has some benefit but is limited by toxicity. Dose reduction, longer infusions, or switching to carboplatin can be considered in high-risk patients when oncologically acceptable.

    Long-term course. Among 821 adults who survived at least five years after cisplatin, AKI (above 25% creatinine rise) occurred in 31.5%, the median early eGFR decline was 10 mL/min/1.73 m², fewer than 3% ever fell below 29 mL/min/1.73 m², and none were known to require dialysis. Most survivors therefore carry a small, permanent decrement, which matters when later drugs are dosed and when CKD risk factors accumulate.

    Ifosfamide

    Ifosfamide enters proximal tubular cells via OCT2, where local metabolism yields chloroacetaldehyde, which inhibits mitochondrial complex I, depletes adenosine triphosphate, and impairs endocytosis, producing Fanconi syndrome (glycosuria with normal glucose, phosphaturia, aminoaciduria, bicarbonate and potassium wasting). Hepatic metabolism also yields acrolein, which causes hemorrhagic cystitis. Mesna neutralizes acrolein in the bladder but does not protect the kidney from chloroacetaldehyde. Fanconi syndrome occurs in up to about 30% of children. In adults, a prospective cohort of 259 patients showed a mean eGFR fall of about 15 mL/min/1.73 m² after initial exposure with continued gradual decline, and a French case series reported progression to kidney failure in 29%. There is no proven nephroprotectant; N-acetylcysteine helped in preclinical studies, but human data are inconclusive. Management is supportive: phosphate, bicarbonate, and potassium replacement, and dialysis for conventional indications (it does not remove the toxic metabolites). Ifosfamide encephalopathy is treated by stopping the infusion, with methylene blue with or without thiamine.

    Pemetrexed

    Pemetrexed is 70% to 90% renally excreted unchanged. It enters tubular cells basolaterally via the reduced folate carrier and apically via folate receptor-α, is polyglutamated and retained, and disrupts purine and pyrimidine synthesis. AKI occurs in about 21% of treated patients; 8.1% discontinued for nephrotoxicity in one cohort, and about half of patients on pemetrexed-bevacizumab maintenance in another stopped because of kidney injury. Risk rises with cumulative exposure, so this is a drug for which eGFR should be trended across cycles rather than checked as a pass/fail gate. ADQI Table 3 lists pemetrexed as contraindicated when CrCl is below 45 mL/min (the U.S. label gives no dose recommendation below this level); ADQI's text also cites eGFR below 30 as a contraindication, an internal inconsistency in the source that should be resolved toward the label threshold. Folate and vitamin B12 prophylaxis reduce hematologic toxicity; whether folinic acid rescues kidney injury is unproven. Pemetrexed is only partly removed by hemodialysis.

    Melphalan (high dose)

    High-dose melphalan before ASCT is associated with AKI in 7.7% to 29% (definitions vary), with dialysis in about 5%, mostly in myeloma and AL amyloidosis. Melphalan enters tubular cells via the L-amino acid transporter. Exposure is higher with reduced kidney function despite limited renal clearance, and baseline GFR below 60 mL/min is associated with delayed engraftment, more infections, diarrhea, and greater parenteral nutrition use; lower eGFR is also linked with more severe mucositis. IMWG considers high-dose therapy with melphalan 100–140 mg/m² feasible in renal impairment (grade C). Melphalan is not dialyzable, but administration should be separated from hemodialysis by at least 2 hours to reduce pharmacokinetic variability.

    High-Dose Methotrexate

    Crystals, clearance, and rescue

    Physiology. Methotrexate and its metabolite 7-hydroxymethotrexate are cleared by glomerular filtration and saturable tubular secretion (uptake via organic anion transporters and the reduced folate carrier; apical efflux via multidrug resistance proteins 2 and 4 and breast cancer resistance protein). Both are weak acids that are poorly soluble at low urine pH; raising urine pH from 6.0 to 7.0 increases solubility five- to eightfold. At doses above 500 mg/m², intratubular precipitation, direct tubular toxicity, oxidative stress, and afferent vasoconstriction combine to produce AKI, which then slows methotrexate clearance and prolongs systemic exposure: a self-reinforcing loop whose extrarenal consequences (mucositis, myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity) are what make it dangerous.

    Incidence. Reported AKI ranges from 2% to 50%; in lymphoma it was 21.6% and 15.6% in derivation and validation cohorts of a prediction model. Risk is raised by pre-existing kidney disease, volume depletion, urine pH below 7.0, third-space collections (pleural effusion, ascites), hypoalbuminemia, liver disease, variants in ABCC2, the gene encoding multidrug resistance protein 2 (MRP2), and drugs that impair secretion: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), penicillins, proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, amiodarone, probenecid, imatinib, and valacyclovir.

    High-dose methotrexate: kidney-focused checklist
    1
    Baseline: measured GFR where available (ADDIKD strong recommendation); drain or account for third-space fluid; stop interacting drugs; review albumin and liver tests. Dose reduction for GFR 30–60 mL/min and for liver dysfunction (bilirubin 3.1–5.0 mg/dL and transaminases above 3 times normal) is described in ADQI; ADDIKD allows full dose at eGFR 45–59 in curative settings such as primary central nervous system lymphoma with good performance status.
    2
    During infusion: aggressive IV hydration with sodium bicarbonate–based fluid before, during, and after; target urine pH above 7.1 (ADQI); check pH with every void.
    3
    Levels and rescue: plasma methotrexate every 24 hours; nomogram-guided leucovorin starting within 24–36 hours, every 3–6 hours until levels are below 0.05–0.1 μmol/L.
    4
    Delayed clearance with AKI: glucarpidase, ideally within 48–60 hours of the start of infusion, dosed and timed by levels. It lowers plasma methotrexate by up to 99% within 15 minutes, but intracellular drug leaks back (rebound). It also cleaves leucovorin: per the U.S. label, do not give leucovorin within 2 hours before or after glucarpidase, and keep pre-glucarpidase leucovorin dosing for 48 hours.
    5
    After glucarpidase: immunoassays cross-react with the inactive metabolite 4-deoxy-4-amino-N10-methylpteroic acid (DAMPA) and overestimate methotrexate; use high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) or mass spectrometry. Consider rechallenge once AKI resolves.

    Evidence for glucarpidase. In a sequential target trial emulation of 708 adults with methotrexate AKI at 28 U.S. cancer centers, 209 received glucarpidase within 4 days. Glucarpidase was associated with 2.70-fold higher adjusted odds of kidney recovery at discharge (95% CI 1.69–4.31), faster recovery (adjusted hazard ratio 1.88), and halved odds of grade 2 or higher neutropenia and transaminitis on day 7 (adjusted odds ratio 0.50 for each); time to death did not differ (adjusted hazard ratio 0.76, 95% CI 0.49–1.18). This is the strongest outcome evidence available, but it remains observational: the verb is associated, and residual confounding by indication is possible.

    Extracorporeal removal. Methotrexate is moderately dialyzable; 6 hours of high-flux hemodialysis lowers plasma levels by about 63%, but rapid redistribution from cells causes rebound, so repeated sessions are needed. The EXtracorporeal TReatments In Poisoning (EXTRIP) workgroup suggested against extracorporeal treatment when glucarpidase is not given, and recommended against it when glucarpidase is given or as a substitute for glucarpidase, all on very low quality evidence. Rationale: methotrexate is largely intracellular, dialysis also removes folinic acid, and glucarpidase lowers levels faster than any extracorporeal method. Dialysis remains appropriate for conventional AKI indications.

    Chemotherapy-Associated TMA

    Gemcitabine and mitomycin C

    Drug-induced TMA from cytotoxics is type 1: dose-dependent, direct endothelial toxicity (with possible complement amplification), presenting late and with frequently incomplete renal recovery. Gemcitabine TMA typically appears after 2 to 6 months (ADQI text; its Table 2 says 3 to 5 months), occasionally up to 12, usually after multiple cycles and more often after prior mitomycin C; incidence is about 0.3% to 1.4% (ADQI Table 2; the text cites 0.015% to 1.4%). Mitomycin C TMA (historically, mitomycin-associated hemolytic uremic syndrome [HUS]) is dose-related: under 3% below 50 mg/m² cumulative in one prospective cohort, rising to 28% at 70 mg/m² or more; ADQI's Table 2 cites an incidence of 4% to 15% in the context of high cumulative dose (it is not explicit whether this is the overall or the high-dose rate), with onset 8 to 12 weeks after dosing and rarely up to 12 months. Type 2 TMA, linked to vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) pathway inhibitors and proteasome inhibitors, is not dose-dependent and tends to appear earlier.

    Recognition. New or worsening hypertension, rising creatinine, hematuria, and proteinuria, with or without systemic microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (falling hemoglobin, schistocytes, high lactate dehydrogenase, low haptoglobin, thrombocytopenia). Renal-limited TMA is easy to miss when blood counts are confounded by chemotherapy itself; a kidney biopsy is diagnostic when safe.

    Management. Stop the drug, permanently for mitomycin C. Control blood pressure (renin-angiotensin blockade is reasonable on mechanistic grounds), treat AKI supportively, and dialyze for conventional indications. For gemcitabine TMA, ADQI states that corticosteroids and plasma exchange have no proven benefit. Eculizumab has been used in refractory cases; in reported gemcitabine-associated TMA series, hematologic remission exceeded 80%, with complete and partial renal remission in 17% and 67%. These are uncontrolled case series and the timing and true effect of complement blockade remain unknown. For mitomycin C, plasma exchange has been used empirically with variable results.

    Differential and complement work-up: Complement-mediated kidney disease: TMA.

    Beyond Cytotoxics

    Checkpoint inhibitors, VEGF-pathway drugs, and tumor lysis

    ADQI 34 deliberately excluded immunotherapy and targeted agents. Three points are essential enough to state briefly here, each from a separately verified source.

    Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) AKI. In a multicenter study of 138 patients with ICI-associated AKI (creatinine doubling or new dialysis) and 276 controls, lower baseline eGFR, proton pump inhibitor use, and combination ICI therapy were independently associated with AKI. Median onset was 14 weeks (interquartile range 6–37). Acute tubulointerstitial nephritis was the dominant lesion in 93% of 60 biopsies, and 69% of all 138 patients were also taking a drug known to cause interstitial nephritis. Most (86%) received corticosteroids; recovery was complete in 40%, partial in 45%, and absent in 15%, and failure to recover was associated with higher mortality. ICI rechallenge occurred in 22%, with recurrent AKI in 23% of those rechallenged. Reasonable practical implications: stop or switch PPIs where possible, consider biopsy when the diagnosis is uncertain, and use corticosteroids, which were associated with better renal prognosis.

    VEGF-pathway inhibitors (bevacizumab, VEGF-receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors [TKIs]) cause hypertension and proteinuria and can cause renal-limited type 2 TMA, mechanistically through loss of podocyte-derived VEGF signaling to the glomerular endothelium. Monitor blood pressure and urine protein at each cycle.

    Tumor lysis syndrome (TLS) results from rapid release of potassium, phosphate, and nucleic acids (metabolized to uric acid) from bulky, rapidly proliferating, chemosensitive tumors. AKI follows from uric acid and calcium phosphate precipitation and from other mechanisms. Prevention is risk-stratified: hydration for all at risk, allopurinol for intermediate risk, and rasburicase for high risk (contraindicated in glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency). Urine alkalinization is no longer routine because it promotes calcium phosphate deposition.

    Evidence and Uncertainty

    What is settled, what is open, and survivorship

    QuestionCurrent position
    Should cancer patients be dosed by Cockcroft-Gault CrCl?No, per ADDIKD and KDIGO: use eGFR (de-indexed for dosing) and measure GFR when the decision is high-stakes. Many labels and order sets still use CrCl.
    Does magnesium protect against cisplatin AKI?Associated with lower AKI in observational studies; no randomized trial.
    Do SGLT2 inhibitors prevent cisplatin nephrotoxicity?Mechanistic and animal data only; human trials needed.
    Does mannitol help?No benefit and more AKI in a large target trial emulation; not recommended routinely.
    Does glucarpidase improve outcomes in methotrexate AKI?Associated with better kidney recovery and less extrarenal toxicity in a large target trial emulation; no survival difference.
    Is there a role for dialysis to remove methotrexate?EXTRIP recommends against it when glucarpidase is used, and suggests against it otherwise (very low quality evidence).
    Are early biomarkers (neutrophil gelatinase–associated lipocalin [NGAL], kidney injury molecule-1 [KIM-1]) ready for routine use?Not yet; they may detect tubular injury earlier but are not validated to change management.
    Does eculizumab treat chemotherapy-associated TMA?Case series only; timing and benefit uncertain.

    ADQI's research agenda prioritizes a modular, drug-class–specific risk score embedded in electronic health records, validated oncology AKI prediction models, randomized trials of nephroprotectants (magnesium, cilastatin, SGLT2 inhibitors), genetic predisposition (OCT2 polymorphisms), and long-term outcome data, particularly for melphalan in AL amyloidosis and pre-existing CKD.

    Survivorship. Because cisplatin and ifosfamide leave many survivors with a permanent eGFR decrement and persistent tubular wasting, treat them as people with kidney risk: annual eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), blood pressure, and magnesium, more often after an AKI episode, with standard CKD care when criteria are met. AKI does not end at discharge outlines the 90-day pathway.

    Philippine Practice

    Making this work in the Philippines

    • Kidney function method. Many oncology order sets still dose by Cockcroft-Gault CrCl. Reporting a BSA-adjusted CKD-EPI eGFR alongside it, and requesting cystatin C in cachectic or bedridden patients where the laboratory offers it, is an inexpensive step toward ADDIKD practice. Measured GFR (for example, 99mTc-DTPA plasma clearance) is offered by some nuclear medicine units; ask before scheduling high-dose methotrexate or curative-intent carboplatin.
    • Methotrexate levels. Confirm before admission that 24-hourly plasma methotrexate levels with same-day results are available; high-dose methotrexate without timely levels removes the key safety signal.
    • Glucarpidase. As of November 2026 we could not find a Philippine Food and Drug Administration registration for glucarpidase. Access, if needed, is likely to require special importation, which takes longer than the 48–60 hour window. Check the current status on the FDA Philippines verification portal and with your hospital pharmacy before scheduling high-dose methotrexate. Prevention (hydration, alkalinization, drug-interaction review, levels, leucovorin) therefore carries more weight locally.
    • Magnesium and electrolytes. Intravenous magnesium sulfate and oral magnesium are widely available and inexpensive; build them into cisplatin order sets.
    • Over-the-counter nephrotoxins. Mefenamic acid and other NSAIDs, and herbal "detox" products, are easily bought without prescription. Ask about them explicitly at every cycle.
    • Financing. Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) Z Benefit packages cover selected cancers, and the Cancer Assistance Fund (Section 20 of the National Integrated Cancer Control Act, Republic Act 11215) supports medicines, treatment, and related diagnostics; the Department of Health and Department of Budget and Management implementing guidelines limit it to eight priority cancer types. Eligibility and package contents change, so confirm with the hospital's PhilHealth and social service offices. See PhilHealth Z packages.

    A protein doorway that moves substances into or out of a cell.Protinang pinto na naglilipat ng sustansya papasok o palabas ng cell.Protina nga pultahan nga mobalhin sa substansiya pasulod o pagawas sa cell.Protinang pasbul a manlipat substansya papasok o palwal ning cell.
    Fanconi syndrome
    A "leaky" proximal tubule that loses glucose, phosphate, amino acids, and bicarbonate into the urine.Tumatagas na proximal tubule na nawawalan ng glucose, phosphate, amino acid, at bicarbonate sa ihi.Motulo nga proximal tubule nga mawad-an og glucose, phosphate, amino acid, ug bicarbonate sa ihi.Tutulung proximal tubule a mawawalan glucose, phosphate, amino acid, at bicarbonate king ihi.
    Glucarpidase
    An enzyme given into the vein that rapidly breaks down methotrexate in the blood.Enzyme na itinuturok sa ugat na mabilis sumisira sa methotrexate sa dugo.Enzyme nga ituturok sa ugat nga paspas modaot sa methotrexate sa dugo.Enzyme a iturok king ugat a mabilis manira king methotrexate king daya.
    Leucovorin
    A form of folate given as "rescue" to protect normal cells from methotrexate.Anyo ng folate na ibinibigay bilang "rescue" para protektahan ang normal na cell mula sa methotrexate.Porma sa folate nga ihatag isip "rescue" aron panalipdan ang normal nga cell gikan sa methotrexate.Anyu ning folate a ibibie bilang "rescue" ban protektan ing normal a cell manibat king methotrexate.
    Mesna
    A medicine that protects the bladder lining during ifosfamide; it does not protect the kidney.Gamot na nagpoprotekta sa lining ng pantog habang may ifosfamide; hindi nito pinoprotektahan ang bato.Tambal nga manalipod sa lining sa pantog samtang adunay ifosfamide; dili kini manalipod sa bato.Gamut a protektan na ing lining ning pantug kabang atin ifosfamide; ali na protektan ing batu.
    Cumulative dose
    The total amount of a drug received over all cycles.Kabuuang dami ng gamot na natanggap sa lahat ng cycle.Kinatibuk-ang gidaghanon sa tambal nga nadawat sa tanang cycle.Kabuuang dakal ning gamut a tinanggap king eganagang cycle.
    De-indexed GFR
    GFR converted from mL/min/1.73 m² to mL/min using your own body surface area, used for drug dosing.GFR na kinonvert mula mL/min/1.73 m² patungong mL/min gamit ang sariling body surface area, para sa dosis ng gamot.GFR nga gi-convert gikan sa mL/min/1.73 m² ngadto sa mL/min gamit ang kaugalingong body surface area, para sa dosis sa tambal.GFR a kinonvert manibat mL/min/1.73 m² papunta mL/min gamit ing sariling body surface area, para king dosis ning gamut.
    Tumor lysis
    Rapid breakdown of cancer cells that floods the blood with potassium, phosphate, and uric acid.Mabilis na pagkasira ng cell ng kanser na pumupuno sa dugo ng potassium, phosphate, at uric acid.Paspas nga pagkadaot sa cell sa kanser nga mopuno sa dugo og potassium, phosphate, ug uric acid.Mabilis a pamakasira ning cell ning kanser a pupunu king daya potassium, phosphate, at uric acid.
    Target trial emulation
    An observational study designed to mimic a randomized trial; stronger than ordinary cohort analysis but still open to confounding.Observational na pag-aaral na ginaya ang randomized trial; mas matibay kaysa karaniwang cohort pero maaari pa ring may confounding.Observational nga pagtuon nga gisundog ang randomized trial; mas lig-on kaysa kasagarang cohort apan mahimo gihapong adunay confounding.Observational a pamanigaral a ginaya ing randomized trial; mas matibe kesa karaniwang cohort pero malyari pa muring atin confounding.
