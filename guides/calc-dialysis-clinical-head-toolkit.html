Dialysis Clinical Head Toolkit

Twelve working tools for the Renal Care Academy Dialysis Clinical Head track. They do arithmetic, not decisions. Clinical priorities belong to the attending nephrologist, technical releases to the technical leads, and limits to your unit’s approved protocols and the current regulations. Nothing you enter leaves this page.

1. Safe capacity and the limiting resource

Safe slots per shift are set by the narrowest gate: released stations, patients your cleared nurses can safely cover, physician coverage, and isolation needs (Modules 8 and 9).

2. Reorder point and days of cover

Reorder point = daily use × lead time + safety stock. Recalculate the day a supplier warns of delay (Modules 8 and 11).

3. Quality rate with a 95% confidence interval

A rate needs a denominator. Small counts swing widely; the exact Poisson interval shows how widely. Add a second period to compare (Modules 6 and 13).

4. Risk score

Teaching scale: likelihood 1–5 × severity 1–5. A score at or above the threshold, or any catastrophic consequence, goes to leadership. Use your facility’s approved scale and thresholds (Modules 2 and 6).

5. PhilHealth session tracker

Projects when a patient will reach the annual session allocation, counting sessions at every unit. Defaults reflect PhilHealth Circular 2024-0023 (156 sessions, 6,350 pesos); confirm the rules in force on the date of service (Module 3).

6. The dialysis clock

Hours since each patient’s last completed session, sorted longest first. Flags support, and never replace, the physician’s priority (Module 7). Use fictional or approved identifiers only.

7. Shortage cost comparison

Compares paying more for verified stock with the cost of sessions that fall in a supply gap (Module 11). A cheaper unqualified substitute is not an option until it passes its gates.

8. Water result check

Compares a dialysis-water result with the limits you enter. Defaults: ISO 23500-3 (2024) maximums of 100 CFU/mL and 0.25 EU/mL, with action levels at half. Enter the limits your unit adopted under DOH AO 2013-0003 (Module 5).

9. Absolute risk reduction and NNT

Turns a relative claim into an absolute one. Enter event proportions over the same follow-up (Module 14).

10. Single-pool Kt/V and URR

Daugirdas second-generation formula from valid pre- and post-dialysis urea samples. KDOQI 2015: target spKt/V 1.4 per session, minimum delivered 1.2, for thrice-weekly treatment (Module 4).

11. Vascular access mix and catheter infection rate

Catheters carry the highest bloodstream infection risk. A Philippine General Hospital cohort reported 6.72 catheter-related infections per 1,000 catheter-days; use it as context, not as a target (Module 5).

12. Missed treatments

Two views: missed sessions per 100 scheduled, and the share of patients who missed at least one in the period. In DOPPS, the 4-month share ranged from under 1% to 24% across countries (Modules 4 and 10).

Important: These tools do arithmetic, not decisions. They do not replace your unit’s protocols, current regulations, or the attending nephrologist, who is always asked for guidance on clinical priorities. Reviewed November 24, 2026.
