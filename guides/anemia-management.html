Anemia means your blood does not carry enough oxygen to meet your body's needs. Red blood cells contain hemoglobin — the protein that picks up oxygen in the lungs and delivers it to every organ. When hemoglobin falls too low, every organ suffers — especially the heart (which works harder) and the kidneys (which need oxygen to maintain filtration).Ang anemia ay nangangahulugang ang inyong dugo ay hindi nagdadala ng sapat na oxygen para matugunan ang pangangailangan ng katawan. Ang mga pulang selula ng dugo ay naglalaman ng hemoglobin — ang protina na kumukuha ng oxygen sa baga at naghahatid nito sa bawat organo. Kapag masyadong bumaba ang hemoglobin, nagdurusa ang bawat organo — lalo na ang puso (na nagtatrabaho nang mas malakas) at ang mga bato (na nangangailangan ng oxygen upang mapanatili ang pagsasala).Ang anemia nagpasabot nga ang inyong dugo dili magdala og igo nga oxygen aron matubag ang mga pangangailangan sa lawas. Quick Clinical Overview

Start with a complete blood count (CBC), reticulocyte count, ferritin, and transferrin saturation (TSAT). Adult anemia is Hb <13 g/dL in men or <12 g/dL in women; pediatric thresholds are age-specific. Kidney disease does not exclude another cause.

If the initial tests do not explain anemia, tailor further testing: smear, haptoglobin/lactate dehydrogenase, C-reactive protein, B12/folate, liver tests, thyroid-stimulating hormone, parathyroid hormone, and monoclonal-protein studies. Assess blood loss and nutrition. Ferritin <45 ng/mL, or unexplained microcytosis without a ferritin result or known genetic cause, warrants evaluation for bleeding and appropriate referral (practice points 1.2.1–1.2.3, S19–20).

ESA Hyporesponsiveness

Investigate iron deficiency or iron-restricted erythropoiesis, inflammation/infection, bleeding, hemolysis, nutritional deficiency, hyperparathyroidism, inadequate dialysis, adherence, and marrow disease before escalating an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent (ESA). A sudden Hb decline with severe reticulocytopenia raises concern for pure red cell aplasia and requires specialist evaluation.

The ESA resistance index (ERI) is weekly epoetin dose ÷ weight ÷ Hb in g/dL. It is a longitudinal exposure/response measure, not a stand-alone diagnosis or instruction to maintain a dose. KDIGO 2026 Table 10 summarizes heterogeneous definitions; ERI 10 is not a universal KDIGO cutoff. Darbepoetin-to-epoetin conversions are approximate and dose-dependent; do not apply a fixed 200-IU/µg factor to CERA as a validated conversion.

HIF Prolyl Hydroxylase Inhibitor (HIF-PHI) Therapy

After correcting reversible causes, an ESA is generally suggested before a hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor (HIF-PHI). A selected patient with ESA hyporesponsiveness may consider a HIF-PHI trial to relieve symptoms or avoid transfusion, with explicit discussion that evidence for clinical benefit and safety in this group is limited.

  • Do not combine an ESA and a HIF-PHI.
  • Review active malignancy, recent cardiovascular or thrombotic events, and the agent’s local authorization and contraindications.
  • Use the lowest effective dose and monitor Hb every 2–4 weeks after initiation or changes.
  • Discontinue a trial for hyporesponsiveness after 3–4 months without the desired erythropoietic response.

Correct documented nutritional deficiencies and manage CKD mineral/bone disease for their indications. Vitamin D or L-carnitine should not be presented as proven routine KDIGO anemia therapy; adjunct studies do not establish patient-important benefit for every hyporesponder.

Transfusion Strategy in CKD Anemia

For chronic anemia in CKD, base red-cell transfusion on symptoms and signs attributable to anemia and the individual balance of benefits and harms, rather than an arbitrary Hb threshold (practice point 4.4). Restrictive thresholds from other settings are context-specific and are not universal CKD orders.

Acute hemorrhage, unstable coronary disease, or urgent surgery may require rapid correction. Avoid transfusion when possible in transplant candidates to reduce allosensitization. Leukoreduction does not eliminate sensitization risk. When transfusion is appropriate, account for volume overload, potassium, iron burden, and reassess the response; stable patients are often managed one unit at a time under local protocols.

Patient handout: understanding your anemia

Your kidney team checks why hemoglobin (Hb) is low before changing treatment. Iron tests and symptoms matter together. IV iron is usually preferred in hemodialysis; oral or IV iron can be chosen in non-dialysis CKD and peritoneal dialysis.

For adults taking an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent (ESA/EPO), the Hb goal is individualized and below 11.5 g/dL (115 g/L). More EPO is not a treatment for every cause of fatigue. Do not change your dose yourself.

The team usually withholds routine iron if ferritin is >700 ng/mL or TSAT ≥40%, and considers pausing it during systemic infection. These are treatment-review limits, not tests that prove iron overload.
