Summary

Framework. Kidney resilience is the capacity to withstand a hemodynamic, toxic, inflammatory or obstructive stress and return toward baseline. For adults at risk of or recovering from acute kidney injury, it rests on four levers: characterize susceptibility, remove avoidable exposures, detect and respond early, and complete recovery.

Practice. Use medication stewardship with a sick-day plan, apply surgical bundles and contrast decisions with their evidence limits in view, treat alerts as detection rather than action, and finish with a 3-month review across the acute kidney injury (AKI)–acute kidney disease (AKD)–chronic kidney disease (CKD) continuum.

Think of your kidneys like a community water system with backup pumps. On an ordinary day only some pumps run. When there is a sudden demand — a fever, diarrhea, a hot day, an operation — the backups switch on. Resilience is how well that system withstands a shock and then returns to normal. Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a sudden fall in kidney function over hours to days. It usually happens when a vulnerable kidney meets a hit: low fluid or low blood pressure during an illness, an infection, a medicine that strains the kidney, a blocked urine flow, or a major operation. The same hit can pass harmlessly in one person and injure the kidney in another. Vomiting, diarrhea, high fever, and heavy sweating drain water and salt from the blood. The kidney responds by squeezing its own blood vessels to hold filtering pressure steady. On a dehydrated day, the same action can tip a strained kidney into injury. Pain relievers called NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) block the kidney's own emergency widening of its incoming vessels. Framework

Resilience as physiology: susceptibility, exposure, response, recovery

Kidney resilience is the capacity to withstand a hemodynamic, toxic, inflammatory, or obstructive stress and return toward baseline function. This guide treats it as four modifiable levers for adults at risk of, or recovering from, acute kidney injury (AKI): characterizing susceptibility, removing avoidable exposures, detecting and responding early, and completing recovery. It does not cover children, pregnancy, kidney transplant recipients, or patients on maintenance dialysis, who need individualized pathways.

Normal physiology. Glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is held relatively constant across a range of renal perfusion pressures by two mechanisms: myogenic afferent arteriolar tone and tubuloglomerular feedback, in which macula densa sensing of distal chloride delivery adjusts afferent resistance. When effective arterial volume falls, angiotensin II constricts the efferent arteriole to preserve glomerular capillary pressure, while afferent vasodilatory prostaglandins counter sympathetic and angiotensin-mediated afferent constriction. This is established physiology, and it explains the common medication vulnerabilities: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) remove afferent prostaglandin support, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEi) and angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB) remove efferent support, sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors restore tubuloglomerular feedback and lower intraglomerular pressure, and diuretics reduce the volume that the whole system is defending. Each is beneficial at steady state; in combination with volume depletion they narrow the autoregulatory margin.

Reserve. Renal functional reserve is the increment in GFR that healthy nephrons can recruit under a protein or amino acid load. It is a physiological concept, not a routine clinical measurement: a normal creatinine shows that filtration is adequate at rest, not how much headroom remains. Reserve is reduced by nephron loss from age, chronic kidney disease (CKD), prior AKI, and low nephron endowment, and its depletion is plausibly why the same insult produces AKI in one patient and not another. The 2024 PROTECTION trial offers indirect support that recruiting reserve can matter clinically (see Perioperative), but no bedside test of reserve is validated for risk stratification, and this guide does not offer one.

Injury and repair. Ischemic and septic AKI involve tubular epithelial injury, microvascular dysfunction, and inflammation. Surviving tubular cells dedifferentiate, proliferate, and redifferentiate over days to weeks. Repair is frequently incomplete: maladaptive repair with cell-cycle arrest, a profibrotic secretory phenotype, and capillary rarefaction plausibly links AKI to subsequent CKD. The mechanistic chain is well described in experimental models; in humans the AKI-to-CKD association is robust but partly confounded by shared risk factors, which is why the guide uses "is associated with" for long-term outcomes.

Susceptibility

Baseline estimated GFR (eGFR) and albuminuria, age and frailty, heart failure, cirrhosis, diabetes, prior AKI, low nephron endowment, polypharmacy.

Exposure

Volume depletion, sepsis, hypotension, nephrotoxins, obstruction, major surgery, heat, tropical infection.

Response

Detection against a credible baseline, cause-directed care, complication management, medication stewardship.

The fourth lever, recovery, closes the loop: documenting the episode, timed reassessment for acute kidney disease (AKD) or CKD, and long-term risk-factor control. Each lever is linked below to the strongest available human evidence; where only mechanism or association exists, the text says so.

Cause-directed response

History, examination, medication review, urinalysis with microscopy, and targeted imaging identify the cause faster than any biomarker. Separate four physiologic states because their treatments conflict: true volume depletion (give fluid), congestion with venous hypertension (decongest), sepsis-associated hypoperfusion (resuscitate to perfusion targets and reassess), and obstruction (decompress). Look for intrinsic disease — active sediment, new proteinuria, rash, or eosinophilia suggesting glomerulonephritis or acute interstitial nephritis.

Sepsis. The 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC) guideline suggests balanced crystalloids over 0.9% saline for initial resuscitation, upgraded to moderate certainty, with context-specific exceptions such as traumatic brain injury; it also suggests active fluid removal once the acute resuscitation phase has passed (very low certainty). Reassess perfusion and congestion repeatedly rather than following a fixed volume. Do not use low-dose dopamine for kidney protection (KDIGO 2012, 1A): in the Australian and New Zealand Intensive Care Society (ANZICS) randomized trial of 328 critically ill patients with early kidney dysfunction, peak creatinine was 245 versus 249 µmol/L with dopamine versus placebo, with no difference in KRT or survival. Loop diuretics treat fluid overload; they do not prevent or treat AKI itself (KDIGO 2012: 1B against diuretics to prevent AKI; 2C against diuretics to treat AKI except for volume overload).

Susceptibility

Characterizing the starting point

Record a recent stable baseline SCr and eGFR, CKD stage with albuminuria category, age and frailty, heart failure, cirrhosis, diabetes, prior AKI (date, cause, peak stage, dialysis), and current exposures. Kidney function can change over hours to days, so the baseline is a reference point, not today's function.

⚠️

Proteinuria measured during AKI is not a baseline

In a 2026 prospective paired cohort of 100 Thai inpatients, agreement between spot ratios and 24-hour collections was weaker during active AKI than after recovery: concordance 0.81 versus 0.89 for urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and 0.68 versus 0.83 for urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (UPCR), with spot ratios systematically overestimating 24-hour excretion. Document the collection phase, interpret ratios drawn during AKI in context, and repeat once function is stable to estimate baseline or treatment response. Urinalysis and protein quantification may still be needed during AKI for etiologic work-up. Single-center data; external validation is needed.

Avoid steady-state eGFR equations and the Kidney Failure Risk Equation (KFRE) while creatinine is changing; both assume steady state. Neither a questionnaire nor a single creatinine measures renal functional reserve.

Avoidable Exposures

Medication stewardship and the sick-day plan

The KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline frames medication stewardship as a core part of CKD care and addresses temporary interruption of selected agents during acute illness. The evidence that a generic sick-day protocol prevents AKI is weak, and the case for individualized, clinician-written plans rests on physiology, harms of blanket holds, and implementation lessons.

The "triple whammy." In a nested case-control analysis of 487,372 users of antihypertensive drugs in UK primary care, current use of an NSAID with both a diuretic and an ACEi or ARB was associated with a higher rate of AKI (rate ratio 1.31, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.12–1.53), highest in the first 30 days (1.82, 1.35–2.46); double combinations were not. Observational, but consistent with the physiology above.

What the sick-day trial showed. In a 6-month pragmatic randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 315 US veterans with CKD stages 3–5, a sick-day protocol delivered by interactive voice response did not reduce AKI or kidney function loss. True sick days were infrequent and the protocol was executed correctly on only 14 of 33 qualifying sick days. The trial was underpowered for clinical events and tested a delivery model, not the principle. A 2026 perspective argues for sick-day guidance as routine counseling while naming the barriers clinicians cite: unclear sick-day definitions, unclear restart criteria, and concern that repeated interruptions erode the long-term benefit of cardiorenal drugs.

✅

Practical standard

Write an individualized plan: what counts as a sick day for this patient, which drugs (by generic name) to continue, reduce, or hold, the exact restart condition, who owns the restart decision, and when labs are drawn. Use teach-back. Avoid blanket holds and generic fluid targets, especially in heart failure and advanced CKD. The patient section of this guide contains a blank card that records the team's instructions without generating any.

Environmental exposures in the Philippine setting

Heat. In a 2026 nationwide Japanese case-crossover study of 119,027 AKI emergency admissions, extreme heat (99th percentile) was associated with a cumulative relative risk of 2.21 (95% CI 2.00–2.43) over lags 0–13 days, and extreme cold with 1.24; heat susceptibility was highest in younger and middle-aged men. A 2026 review of heat-related kidney disease notes that individualized mitigation advice for people with CKD is still extrapolated from studies of healthy volunteers. Plausible mechanisms include volume depletion, rhabdomyolysis, and hyperuricosuria. No Philippine risk estimate exists, so these figures should not be transplanted to local patients.

Infection. A 2026 review of infection-associated AKI in resource-limited settings emphasizes enteric, zoonotic, and vector-borne causes acquired outside hospital, delayed recognition, and reserve silently depleted by recurrent insults. Leptospirosis after floodwater exposure is the archetype: kidney involvement is common in hospitalized cases. Ask about exposure explicitly. Prophylaxis and treatment are covered in Fever and Kidney Injury in the Tropics.

Perioperative

Major surgery: what a prevention bundle can and cannot do

BigpAK-2 (Lancet, 2025). An adaptive multinational RCT in 34 European hospitals randomized 1,180 adults undergoing major surgery who were at high risk by predefined clinical factors and elevated urinary tubular stress biomarkers. The intervention implemented the KDIGO bundle: advanced hemodynamic monitoring, optimization of volume status and hemodynamics, avoidance of nephrotoxic drugs and radiocontrast, and prevention of hyperglycemia. Moderate or severe AKI within 72 hours occurred in 84 of 584 patients (14.4%) versus 131 of 588 (22.3%): odds ratio 0.57 (95% CI 0.40–0.79), about 8 fewer cases per 100, trial-reported number needed to treat (NNT) 12 (7–33). Adverse events did not differ. Major adverse kidney events at 90 days (MAKE90) were similar (11.0% versus 10.6%).

🔬

Interpretation limits

BigpAK-2 tested a bundle in patients selected partly by a biomarker that is rarely available in Philippine hospitals; the effect of any single component, and of the bundle in unselected patients, is unknown. The primary endpoint is short-term and creatinine/urine-based; no benefit on 90-day kidney events or survival was shown. The abstract and Table 3 report different pooled denominators; arm denominators here follow Table 3. The single-center predecessor, PrevAKI (cardiac surgery, 2017), found AKI in 55.1% versus 71.7% (absolute reduction 16.6%), again without effects on longer-term outcomes. The bundle is low-risk and its components are good care, so applying them to clinically high-risk patients is an evidence-supported inference rather than a directly tested strategy.

Amino acids in cardiac surgery (PROTECTION, NEJM 2024). In a double-blind trial of 3,511 adults undergoing cardiopulmonary bypass at 22 centers, intravenous balanced amino acids (2 g/kg ideal body weight/day for up to 3 days) reduced AKI from 31.7% to 26.9% (relative risk 0.85, 95% CI 0.77–0.94; absolute difference 4.8 points, NNT about 21) and stage 3 AKI from 3.0% to 1.6%. KRT use (1.4% versus 1.9%) and mortality did not differ significantly. The rationale is recruitment of renal functional reserve and increased kidney perfusion. This is cardiac-surgery evidence from a single trial program; it should not be extrapolated to non-cardiac surgery or medical patients without further trials, and protocol adoption is a local anesthesia and surgery decision.

Contrast

Iodinated contrast: association versus causation

Contrast-associated AKI (CA-AKI) is a temporal association; contrast-induced AKI (CI-AKI) implies causation. Historical studies without adequate controls overstated causation, because illness severity drives both scanning and AKI. The American College of Radiology–National Kidney Foundation (ACR–NKF) consensus states that the risk from intravenous iodinated contrast with computed tomography (CT) has been overstated and that intravenous normal saline prophylaxis is indicated for patients with AKI or eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² who are not on maintenance dialysis, and may be considered in individual high-risk circumstances at eGFR 30–44 at the ordering clinician's discretion. The UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) NG148 guidance, updated in 2024, aligns with this risk-based approach. Follow local radiology protocol and preserve timely indicated imaging.

Detection Systems

Alerts and prediction models: detection is not action

Two 2026 studies argue against assuming that earlier detection improves outcomes on its own. A regression-discontinuity analysis of the nationwide Welsh AKI e-alert program (3.1 million adults) found no causal effect on mortality or admissions with passive or interruptive alerts, and little change in post-discharge proteinuria or blood-pressure checks. In a single-center RCT of 180 inpatients without creatinine-defined AKI but at high machine-learning–predicted risk (ESTOP-AKI), structured early nephrology consultation did not change the 7-day peak creatinine change (adjusted means 0.04 versus −0.03 mg/dL, P=.30) or the incidence of AKI. Only 41% of consultation recommendations were fully followed. Model discrimination and patient benefit are separate questions; an alert or a consult has value only if it changes what is done.

💡

Implication for units without electronic alerts

The bundle elements that work in trials are actions — volume and perfusion assessment, nephrotoxin review, glucose control, monitoring. A daily ward round habit of checking creatinine trend, urine output, and the medication chart in at-risk patients delivers these without software.

Next Risk

Reducing the next risk: the AKI–AKD–CKD continuum

The Acute Disease Quality Initiative (ADQI) 16 consensus defined AKD as kidney dysfunction persisting between 7 and 90 days after an initiating event; persistence beyond 90 days meets the time criterion for CKD, which still requires chronicity and clinical context to establish. Teach the three as a continuum: every further insult falls on a smaller reserve.

SGLT2 inhibitors. A 2022 collaborative meta-analysis of 13 large placebo-controlled trials (90,409 participants with diabetes, heart failure, or CKD) found that SGLT2 inhibitors reduced AKI by 23% (relative risk 0.77, 95% CI 0.70–0.84), alongside a 37% reduction in kidney disease progression, with similar effects with and without diabetes. AKI was a reported outcome rather than the trials' primary endpoint, but the direction is consistent across trials. For indicated patients this is strong evidence that one guideline-directed therapy also lowers AKI risk; it is not a reason to start an SGLT2 inhibitor outside its indications.

ACEi/ARB after AKI. In an Alberta propensity-matched cohort of 46,253 adults, ACEi or ARB use within 6 months after AKI was associated with lower 2-year mortality (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] 0.85, 0.81–0.89) but more hospitalizations for a kidney cause (1.28, 1.12–1.46), with no association with kidney failure. Residual confounding by indication is likely. Restart when indicated and the patient is hemodynamically stable, with a potassium and creatinine check planned.

Long-term care at a stable visit. Manage indicated CKD and cardiovascular risk factors per KDIGO 2024: blood pressure, glycemic control, albuminuria-lowering therapy where indicated, lipid management, and avoidance of recurrent nephrotoxin exposure. Flag the AKI episode in the problem list so future teams dose and monitor accordingly.

Life course. In a 2026 Danish birth cohort of 607,524 people followed to age 25, small-for-gestational-age (SGA) birth was associated with higher AKI risk among those without major birth defects (25-year risk 3.7% versus 3.2%; adjusted HR 1.20, 1.13–1.27) and more strongly among those with major birth defects (15.2% versus 9.9%; adjusted HR 1.45, 1.29–1.64). Observational, creatinine-based ascertainment, through young adulthood; no individual reserve score or Philippine estimate can be derived. See Born Small, Kidneys for Life.
