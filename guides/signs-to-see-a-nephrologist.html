Your kidneys can lose half their working power and still leave you feeling perfectly fine. By the time symptoms shout, a lot of quiet damage is already done. This guide is about the whispers — the small signals that mean it's time to check, while there's still the most to protect. Puwedeng mawala ang kalahati ng gana ng iyong mga bato at pakiramdam mo'y okay ka pa rin. Sa oras na sumigaw na ang mga sintomas, marami nang tahimik na pinsala ang naganap. Ang gabay na ito ay tungkol sa mga bulong — ang maliliit na senyales na nagsasabing panahon na para magpatingin, habang marami pa ring maaaring maligtas. Mahimong mawala ang katunga sa gahum sa imong mga kidney ug bation mo gihapon nga okay ra ka. Pag-abot sa panahon nga mosinggit na ang mga simtomas, daghan nang hilom nga kadaot ang nahitabo. Kini nga giya mahitungod sa mga hunghong — ang gagmay nga mga timailhan nga nagpasabot nga panahon na para magpatan-aw, samtang daghan pa gihapon ang maluwas. The Quiet Signals Ang mga Tahimik na Senyales Ang mga Hilom nga Timailhan Reng Malagung Tanda

Kidneys are quiet organs. They don't hurt when they're struggling, and they keep the blood tests looking "almost normal" for a long time by working the surviving filters harder. That's why the earliest signs are easy to shrug off. Here are eight of them. None on its own is proof of kidney disease — but each is a reason to check, not to wait. Tahimik na organ ang bato. Hindi ito sumasakit kahit nahihirapan na, at pinapanatili nitong halos normal ang resulta ng blood test sa mahabang panahon sa pamamagitan ng mas mabigat na pagtatrabaho ng mga natitirang filter. Kaya naman madaling balewalain ang mga unang senyales. Narito ang walo sa mga ito. Wala man sa mga ito ang sapat na patunay ng sakit sa bato — pero bawat isa ay dahilan para magpatingin, hindi para maghintay. Hilom nga organ ang kidney. Wala kini masakit bisan naglisod na, ug gipabilin niini nga daw normal ang resulta sa blood test sa dugay nga panahon pinaagi sa mas kusog nga pagtrabaho sa nahibiling mga filter. Mao nga sayon lang i-ignore ang unang mga timailhan. Ania ang walo niini. Wala niini ang igo nga pamatuod sa sakit sa kidney — apan ang matag usa hinungdan na mopatan-aw, dili maghulat. Malagung organ deng bato. Ali la sasakit anggang mumasakit na la, at pipilitan da lang ing resulta ning blood test a mamalting normal king matagal a panaun ali't lalu la mangasakit deng atyu keng surviving filters. Kanita mayadyad lang lawasan deng anyang tanda. Ati ilang walu karini. Alang metung karini ing pamaneknekan ning sakit ning bato — dapot balamu deng lahat rason la para pamialaus, e para pamitas.

Abbreviations

ACE-i
Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor — a blood pressure medicine that widens the outflow vessel of the kidney's filter.
ACR
Albumin-to-creatinine ratio — a urine test measuring how much protein is leaking.
ARB
Angiotensin receptor blocker — a blood pressure medicine with a similar effect to an ACE-inhibitor.
BP
Blood pressure.
EPO
Erythropoietin — the hormone the kidney makes to tell bone marrow to produce red blood cells.
eGFR
Estimated glomerular filtration rate — a blood test estimating how well the kidneys filter.
HbA1c
Glycated hemoglobin — a blood test showing average blood sugar control over roughly three months.
HMO
Health maintenance organization — a private health-insurance plan, separate from PhilHealth.
KDIGO
Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes — the nephrology guideline body.
NSAID
Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug — a common pain reliever (e.g. mefenamic acid, ibuprofen).
PhilHealth
The Philippine Health Insurance Corporation — the national health insurance program.
PPI
Proton pump inhibitor — a long-term acid-reducing medicine.
RAAS
Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system — the hormone chain that raises blood pressure; both driven by and damaging to the kidney.
SGLT2-inhibitor
Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor — a newer diabetes medicine that also lowers pressure inside the kidney's filters.

Terms

Afferent arteriole
The tiny artery carrying blood into the kidney's filter; NSAIDs constrict it, reducing blood flow.
AV fistula
A surgically joined artery and vein, usually in the arm, used for dialysis access — needs weeks to mature, so it is best planned before dialysis becomes an emergency.
Brown-bag review
Bringing every medicine, supplement, and herbal product you take — in their bottles — to a visit so a clinician can review the full list at once.
Glomerulus
The tiny cluster of blood vessels inside a nephron where blood is first filtered.
Interstitial nephritis
Inflammation of the kidney's filtering tissue, sometimes caused by long-term use of certain medicines.
Nephrologist
A doctor who specializes in kidney disease.
Nephron
The kidney's basic filtering unit; each kidney has roughly a million, and lost ones do not regrow.
Nocturia
Waking at night to urinate — often an early sign the kidney has lost some ability to concentrate urine.
Podocyte
A specialized cell that wraps around the glomerulus's blood vessels and forms the final barrier keeping protein in the blood.
Polypharmacy
Taking several medicines at once, prescribed or not — each additional one raises the chance of a kidney-harming interaction.
Triple whammy
The combination of an NSAID, an ACE-inhibitor or ARB, and a diuretic taken together, which can collapse kidney filtration.
Uremic
Relating to the build-up of waste products in the blood when the kidneys can no longer clear them efficiently.
