What Is Hemodiafiltration (HDF)? Ano ang Hemodiapiltrasyon (HDF)? Unsa ang Hemodiafiltrasyon (HDF)? Nanu ing Hemodiafiltrasyon (HDF)?

Regular dialysis (HD) cleans your blood mainly by pushing small waste particles across a filter using a concentration difference. It works well for small molecules like urea and potassium. But medium-sized waste products — called "middle molecules" — are too large to move through easily. They build up silently over years and cause damage. Hemodiafiltration (HDF) solves this by adding a second cleaning method: it flushes large volumes of fluid through your blood to physically sweep out those middle molecules. Ang regular na diyalisis (HD) ay naglilinis ng dugo sa pamamagitan ng pagtulak ng maliliit na basura sa pamamagitan ng filter. Gumagana ito nang maayos para sa maliliit na molekulo. Ngunit ang mga "gitnang molekulo" — mga katamtamang sukat na basura — ay masyadong malaki upang malabas nang madali. Ang Hemodiapiltrasyon (HDF) ay naglulutas nito sa pamamagitan ng pagdaragdag ng pangalawang paraan ng paglilinis. Ang regular nga diyalisis (HD) naglimpyo sa dugo pinaagi sa pagtulod sa gagmayng basura pinaagi sa filter. Maayo kini alang sa gagmayng molekulo. Apan ang "tungatunga nga molekulo" — dako kaayo para molabas. Ang Hemodiafiltrasyon (HDF) nagsulbad niini pinaagi sa pagdugang og ikaduhang paagi sa paglimpyo. Ing regular na diyalisis (HD) maglinig king dugu sa pamamagitan ning pagtulak ning malating basura king filter. Maayus iti para sa malating molekulo. The results were consistent: when patients received a high enough volume of treatment, HDF reduced the risk of dying. Section 1 · Mechanism

HDF vs HD: The Physiology of Clearance

Conventional hemodialysis (HD) achieves solute clearance by diffusion — concentration-gradient-driven transport across a semipermeable membrane. This is highly efficient for small solutes (urea 60 Da, creatinine 113 Da, K⁺) but progressively ineffective for molecules above ~500 Da. The middle molecule hypothesis posits that uremic toxins in the 500–15,000 Da range — not cleared by diffusion — accumulate and drive inflammation, immune dysfunction, and end-organ injury in dialysis patients.

Hemodiafiltration adds convective transport: high volumes of plasma water are ultrafiltered across a highly permeable membrane and replaced with sterile online-generated substitution fluid. This solvent drag physically sweeps middle molecules through the membrane pores regardless of concentration gradient.

Diffusion (HD)

Driven by concentration gradient. Efficient for: urea, creatinine, K⁺, phosphate (partial), small uremic acids. Inefficient for: β₂-microglobulin (11.8 kDa), FGF-23, AGEs, inflammatory cytokines.

Convection (HDF)

Driven by transmembrane pressure (solvent drag). Clears all of the above, plus middle molecules 500–15,000 Da. Requires: ultrapure water, online substitution fluid generation, HDF-capable machine, and ≥23 L/session convection volume for clinical benefit.

β₂-microglobulin clearance: Meta-analytic data demonstrate HDF achieves ~87 mL/min vs ~49 mL/min for high-flux HD — a 78% advantage. Chronic β₂M accumulation drives dialysis-related amyloidosis (carpal tunnel syndrome, destructive arthropathy, cystic bone disease), predominantly a disease of patients on conventional HD for >10 years.

Section 2 · Landmark Trials

The Four Landmark RCTs: What Each Taught Us

💡

The Central Lesson

CONTRAST and Turkish OL-HDF failed their primary ITT endpoints because most patients received sub-therapeutic convection volumes. ESHOL and CONVINCE consistently delivered high volumes — and showed unambiguous mortality benefit. The dose is the drug.

CONTRAST (Netherlands, 2012, n=714)

The Dutch Convective Transport Study randomized stable HD patients to OL-HDF vs HD. Primary endpoint (all-cause mortality) was not met in the ITT analysis (HR 0.95, NS). However, post-hoc analysis identified convection volumes >21.95 L/session as the threshold associated with lower all-cause mortality. This planted the dose hypothesis that drove the subsequent decade of research.

Turkish OL-HDF Study (2013, n=782)

Similar result to CONTRAST: no significant mortality difference in the ITT analysis. Post-hoc analysis suggested benefit above >17.4 L/session — a lower threshold, partially explained by the predominantly lower blood flows achievable in that patient population. The trial was underpowered for the full mortality question.

ESHOL (Spain, JASN 2013, n=906)

The first unambiguously positive trial. 906 patients, multicenter RCT (Maduell et al., DOI: 10.1681/ASN.2012080875):

  • All-cause mortality: HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.53–0.92), p=0.01 — 30% reduction
  • CV mortality: HR 0.67 (95% CI 0.44–1.02), p=0.06 — 33% reduction (borderline NS)
  • Infection-related mortality: HR 0.45 (95% CI 0.21–0.96) — 55% reduction
  • Intradialytic hypotension: lower in OL-HDF group
  • Median quarterly convection volume: 22.9–23.9 L/session — the highest of the three early trials
  • NNT: 8 patients switched from HD to HDF to prevent 1 annual death

CONVINCE (Multinational, NEJM 2023, n=1,360) — The Definitive Trial

The largest and most rigorously designed HDF RCT. Blankestijn et al., DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2304820.

  • Design: Pragmatic, multinational, open-label RCT; 683 HDF vs 677 HD
  • Median follow-up: 30 months (IQR 27–38)
  • Mean convection volume achieved: 25.3 L/session (97% protocol success rate)
  • Primary endpoint met: HR 0.77 (95% CI 0.65–0.93) — 23% reduction in all-cause mortality
  • Absolute: 118 (17.3%) deaths in HDF vs 148 (21.9%) in HD
  • CV mortality: HR 0.81 (95% CI 0.49–1.33) — NOT significant
  • Composite fatal/nonfatal CV events: HR 1.07 — NOT significant
  • Infection-related and COVID-related deaths: significantly reduced
  • QoL (QALY gains): significantly better in HDF (0.045–0.058/year)
Section 3 · IPD Meta-analysis

The Lancet IPD Meta-Analysis (2024): The Final Word

Vernooij et al. pooled individual patient data from all 5 major HDF RCTs (Lancet 2024, DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(24)01859-2):

  • 5 trials, n=4,153 patients (2,083 HDF vs 2,070 HD)
  • Median follow-up: 30 months (IQR 24–36)
  • All-cause mortality: HDF 23.3% vs HD 27.0%
  • HR 0.84 (95% CI 0.74–0.95) — 16% relative risk reduction
  • No differential effect across any predefined subgroup: age, sex, diabetes, dialysis vintage, prior CV disease, vascular access type — all null for interaction
  • Continuous graded dose-response: as convection volume increases, mortality risk decreases — no single hard threshold, but consistent benefit above ~23 L/session
✅

Signal Strength

This is the strongest mortality reduction signal produced by any dialysis modality intervention in nephrology history. Further mortality RCTs are unlikely to change these conclusions — the field has shifted toward implementation science.

Section 4 · Dose

The Dose Question: ≥23 L/Session

The ≥23 L/session threshold derives from ESHOL and CONVINCE operational targets and post-hoc analyses of CONTRAST and Turkish OL-HDF. The Lancet IPD data clarify this is a continuous dose-response gradient, not a binary cutoff:

  • <17 L: minimal to no mortality benefit signal
  • 17–23 L: partial, inconsistent benefit
  • ≥23 L: consistent, robust mortality reduction — the evidence-based target
  • CONVINCE mean achieved: 25.3 L/session with 97% success using optimization protocol

Real-World Feasibility

A 2025–2026 French prospective cohort study applying the CONVINCE optimization protocol to 67 unselected incident and prevalent patients achieved ≥23 L/session in 62.3% of patients (Lukoki-Beudin et al., Diseases 2026, DOI: 10.3390/diseases14010020):

  • Blood flow increased stepwise: 283 → 338 mL/min
  • Dialyzer ≥2 m² surface area used in 68% (up from 36%)
  • Kt/V improved: 1.22 → 1.60
  • Mean convection volume: 23.5 L/session, sustained through 6 months

Implication: High-volume HDF is not restricted to a select high-performance phenotype. With systematic optimization, the majority of unselected patients can achieve the therapeutic target.

Section 5 · Clinical Outcomes

What HDF Does — and Doesn't Do

✅ Proven Benefits

  • ↓ All-cause mortality 16–23% (5 RCTs, Lancet IPD)
  • ↓ Infection-related mortality (ESHOL 55%, CONVINCE signal)
  • ↑ β₂M and middle molecule clearance (+78%)
  • ↑ Hemodynamic stability (less intradialytic hypotension)
  • ↑ QoL / QALY gains (CONVINCE: 0.045–0.058/year)
  • ↑ Phosphate removal
  • ↓ Vascular calcification progression vs HDx (CARTOON RCT)

⚠️ Not Yet Proven / Neutral

  • CV mortality: HR 0.81 (CONVINCE) — NS
  • Composite fatal/nonfatal CV events: HR 1.07 — NS
  • Prevention of sudden cardiac death — insufficient evidence
  • Consistent intradialytic hypotension reduction — mixed evidence
  • Hospitalization reduction — not clearly demonstrated
Section 6 · The Cardiovascular Paradox

Why HDF Saves Lives But Doesn't Reduce CV Events

This is the most intellectually provocative finding in the HDF evidence base. HDF reduces all-cause mortality by 23% in CONVINCE — yet shows no significant reduction in composite cardiovascular events (HR 1.07) or cardiovascular mortality (HR 0.81, NS). This apparent contradiction is not unique to HDF:

  • Statins (4D, AURORA): No cardiovascular or survival benefit in dialysis patients despite robust benefit in the general population
  • Spironolactone (ACHIEVE, ALCHEMIST): No benefit on cardiovascular mortality or heart failure hospitalization
  • Potassium control (DIALIZE-Outcomes): No reduction in sudden cardiac death or arrhythmic events

Cardiovascular mortality in HD patients remains ~10%/year — driven by "non-traditional" mechanisms: systemic inflammation, vascular calcification, oxidative stress, intradialytic myocardial ischemia, and uremic cardiomyopathy. Classical atherosclerosis prevention strategies that work in the general population consistently fail in dialysis.

HDF's survival benefit appears to operate through immune competence restoration, inflammatory burden reduction, and infection prevention — not atherosclerotic plaque stabilization. The ESHOL 55% reduction in infection-related mortality and CONVINCE's COVID signal are the key mechanistic clues.

Section 7 · Uremic Toxins

The Uremic Toxin Picture

Toxin Class Examples MW HD Clearance HDF Advantage
Small solutes Urea, Creatinine, K⁺ <500 Da ✓ Good Equivalent
Middle molecules β₂M, FGF-23, AGEs 500–15,000 Da ✗ Limited +78% (β₂M)
Phosphate PO₄³⁻ 95 Da Partial Improved
Protein-bound IS, p-cresyl sulfate Variable ✗ Limited Partial (adsorption)

β₂-microglobulin is the prototypic middle molecule: chronic accumulation → dialysis-related amyloidosis, carpal tunnel syndrome, destructive spondyloarthropathy, cystic bone disease — predominantly after >10 years on conventional HD. HDF's sustained 78% better β₂M clearance is expected to reduce amyloid burden over time, though long-term observational data are still maturing.

Section 8 · Guidelines

Guidelines in 2025–2026

2025 EuDial Working Group Consensus (European Renal Association)

The ERA's EuDial consensus supports high-volume HDF (≥23 L/session) as superior to high-flux HD for overall and cardiovascular survival when high volumes are consistently achieved. The consensus stops short of designating HDF as the universal standard of care, citing insufficient evidence for:

  • Prevention of sudden cardiac death
  • Consistent intradialytic hypotension reduction (mixed trial evidence)
  • Clear reduction in hospitalization rates

KDIGO: Pending

No formal KDIGO guideline on HDF vs HD exists as of 2025–2026. The post-CONVINCE nephrology community (Strippoli et al., CKJ 2026, DOI: 10.1093/ckj/sfag115) actively advocates for world-class KDIGO-level guidelines rather than consensus or position statements, noting that nephrology has historically taken 10–15 years to implement evidence into practice.

The US Inflection Point: FDA Clearance 2024

In 2024, the FDA granted 510(k) clearance to a hemodialysis machine capable of delivering online high-volume HDF — the first such approval for the US market. The ~800,000 US dialysis patients have been treated exclusively with conventional HD while Europe, Japan, and parts of the Middle East have had HDF available for 15–20 years. Phased clinical introduction into US dialysis clinics began post-clearance.

Section 9 · HDF vs Expanded HD

HDF vs Expanded Hemodialysis (HDx): The Emerging Rivalry

Medium cutoff (MCO) membranes used in expanded hemodialysis (HDx) expand diffusive clearance of middle molecules without convection. Advantages: no water quality upgrade, standard machines, lower cost. Disadvantage: no large mortality RCT data.

CARTOON RCT (Korea, Lee et al., Sci Rep 2021, n=80, DOI: 10.1038/s41598-021-90311-6): HDx vs online-HDF — cardiovascular parameters (baPWV, echocardiography) did not differ significantly. However, coronary artery calcium (CAC) scores remained stable in the HDF group while showing an increasing trend in the HDx group (p=0.012) over 1 year. Mortality was similar but the trial was underpowered.

The clinical bottom line: HDx is a pragmatic, more accessible option with plausible mechanistic benefit. But the 4,153-patient mortality evidence base belongs entirely to HDF. HDx should not be positioned as equivalent to high-volume HDF until mortality data emerge.

Section 10 · Patient Selection

Patient Selection and Optimization Protocol

Candidate criteria (no formal exclusions per Lancet IPD):

  • Any dialysis patient who can consistently achieve ≥23 L/session
  • Blood flow rate ≥350 mL/min — AV fistula preferred
  • Center with HDF-capable machine + ultrapure water
  • No absolute contraindication (active bacteremia, unstable hemodynamics, recent access complication)

CONVINCE Optimization Protocol (stepwise):

  1. Set blood flow ≥350 mL/min — increase stepwise as tolerated
  2. Use dialyzer ≥2 m² membrane surface area
  3. Optimize session duration to maximize convective volume
  4. Reassess convection volume after 4 weeks
  5. If <23 L: troubleshoot access, dialyzer size, session duration before declaring failure

In the real-world French cohort (Lukoki-Beudin et al., 2026), 62.3% of unselected patients achieved ≥23 L/session using this protocol. The 37.7% who did not were not excluded — they continued at the highest achievable volume (partial-dose HDF or optimize to HDx).

Section 12 · Future Directions

The Post-CONVINCE Research Agenda

Further "does HDF work?" mortality trials are unlikely and unnecessary. The Strippoli et al. research agenda (CKJ 2026) identifies the following priorities:

Real-World Implementation

Target trial emulation methods, registry studies, health system implementation research — moving from RCT-eligible populations to all-comers.

Personalized HDF

Optimal dosing per patient phenotype. Digital twin technology for session-by-session convection volume optimization. Patient-reported outcomes as primary endpoints.

Formal Guidelines

KDIGO-level guideline development (not consensus/position statements). The nephrology community deserves the same guideline rigor applied to other major interventions.
