When to Use

When to Use This Calculator

Use this tool to estimate the risk of acute kidney injury (AKI) in any patient with confirmed or suspected dengue fever. It integrates six parameters available at the bedside or on a routine dengue workup: the illness day (febrile, critical, or recovery phase), platelet count, serum creatinine, hematocrit trend, pre-existing CKD stage, and WHO warning-sign burden. The score guides the urgency of hospitalization, nephrology referral, and serial creatinine monitoring.

✅

Appropriate population

  • Adults (≥12 years) presenting with dengue fever at any illness day, in any outpatient or emergency setting.
  • Patients in the critical phase (Days 4–6) with thrombocytopenia, capillary leak, or warning signs where early admission prevents AKI.
  • Dengue patients with known CKD, diabetes, or hypertension where the AKI threshold is lower and monitoring must be intensified.
  • Clinicians deciding between home management, clinic review in 24 hours, or immediate ER referral.
⚠️

Limitations — do not use as a diagnosis

  • This score has not been formally validated in a prospective cohort; it synthesizes published risk factors from observational literature and WHO dengue guidelines.
  • Not validated for pediatric patients under 12 years of age — use PICU or pediatric dengue severity scores instead.
  • A low score does not exclude AKI if the clinical picture is deteriorating. Always defer to physician evaluation.
  • Creatinine may be falsely low in patients with low muscle mass (elderly, malnourished) — a normal creatinine can mask significant GFR loss.
Why It Matters

Why Dengue AKI Matters

Dengue is the most common mosquito-borne viral illness in the Philippines, with hundreds of thousands of cases reported annually. Acute kidney injury complicates approximately 5–10% of hospitalized dengue cases and rises to 20–30% among patients with severe dengue or pre-existing CKD. The mechanisms are multifactorial: direct viral cytopathic injury to tubular epithelium, immune-complex deposition in the glomerulus, myoglobinuria from dengue myositis, and — critically — hemodynamic insult from capillary leak and plasma volume depletion during the critical phase.

The World Health Organization recognizes warning signs that predict the transition from non-severe to severe dengue: abdominal pain or tenderness, persistent vomiting, clinical fluid accumulation (ascites, pleural effusion), mucosal bleeding, lethargy or restlessness, liver enlargement >2 cm, and a rapid rise in hematocrit concurrent with a rapid decrease in platelet count. Each warning sign independently elevates AKI risk by signaling plasma leakage and hemodynamic stress.

Pre-existing CKD magnifies every dengue insult. A patient with CKD Stage 3 who sustains a relatively small dengue-associated volume contraction may cross the threshold for Stage 2 AKI, precipitating hyperkalemia, pulmonary edema on fluid resuscitation, or an abrupt need for dialysis. Identifying high-risk dengue patients early — before creatinine peaks — allows timely volume management, avoidance of nephrotoxic drugs (NSAIDs are contraindicated in dengue), and nephrology involvement at the right moment.

Interactive Tool

Dengue-Associated AKI Risk Estimator

Enter the six parameters below and press Estimate AKI Risk. The tool scores each parameter, sums the points, and returns a risk band with a plain-language recommendation. All computation runs locally in your browser — no data is stored or transmitted.

    This tool is a clinical decision-support aid only. It does not replace physician evaluation. Results should be interpreted alongside the full clinical picture. Not validated for pediatric patients (<12 years).

    How to Use

    Instructions

    1. Confirm dengue diagnosis or strong clinical suspicion before using this score. The tool is intended for dengue-specific AKI risk stratification, not for fever of unknown origin.
    2. Select the illness day category that matches the current day of fever onset. Day 1–3 is the febrile phase; Days 4–6 is the critical phase when plasma leakage and AKI risk peak; Day 7+ is the recovery phase.
    3. Enter the most recent platelet count and serum creatinine from the same blood draw, ideally taken within the past 12–24 hours. If creatinine is not yet available, leave it blank — the score will still calculate based on the remaining parameters.
    4. Select the hematocrit trend compared to the patient's known baseline or the value at illness onset. A rise >20% indicates severe plasma leakage (hemoconcentration) and warrants urgent fluid assessment.
    5. Select the patient's CKD stage and the worst WHO warning sign present at the time of assessment. If the patient has any severe feature (no urine output, spontaneous bleeding, hypotension), go directly to the ER — do not wait for the score result.

    The tool generates a total score and a risk band (Low / Moderate / High) with a plain-language recommendation. Re-run the tool at each reassessment visit as illness day and labs evolve.

    Evidence & References

    The scoring parameters and thresholds are derived from peer-reviewed observational studies examining determinants of AKI in dengue fever, WHO dengue management guidelines, and clinical reviews of dengue nephropathy. No single validated scoring system for dengue-AKI currently exists in the literature; this tool synthesizes the most consistently replicated independent predictors from published evidence.

    Pearls & Pitfalls

    Pearls & Pitfalls

    💡

    Critical phase (Days 4–6) is the highest-risk window

    Most dengue AKI occurs during the critical phase when plasma leakage is maximal and intravascular volume is depleted. Creatinine may still appear normal at the start of this phase — do not be reassured by a single value. Serial creatinine every 12–24 hours during Days 4–6 is the standard of care for hospitalized dengue patients with any risk factor.

    🔬

    Pre-existing CKD multiplies every dengue AKI risk

    A patient with CKD Stage 3 and dengue has the equivalent baseline renal reserve of someone functioning with roughly half their nephrons. A modest dengue-associated creatinine rise of 0.5 mg/dL that would be minor in a healthy adult may represent a 50% GFR decline in a patient whose baseline creatinine was already 1.6 mg/dL. All dengue patients with known CKD should be hospitalized from the onset of the critical phase regardless of initial platelet count.

    🚫

    Pitfalls to avoid

    • Never give NSAIDs in dengue. Ibuprofen, mefenamic acid, and diclofenac are all contraindicated — they impair platelet function, increase bleeding risk, and cause direct nephrotoxicity. Paracetamol is the only safe antipyretic.
    • Do not administer excess IV fluids in the recovery phase (Day 7+). As leaked plasma re-absorbs, aggressive fluid administration causes acute pulmonary edema and hyponatremia. Taper IV fluids and monitor urine output carefully once hematocrit starts falling.
    • Do not use forced diuresis to treat oliguria in the critical phase. Oliguria in severe dengue is often pre-renal; aggressive diuretics worsen volume depletion. Confirm adequate intravascular filling before considering furosemide.
    • Creatinine lag: Creatinine elevation lags behind actual tubular injury by 24–48 hours. A normal creatinine on Day 4 does not exclude early dengue AKI developing over the next 48 hours.
    Important: This calculator is an educational and clinical decision-support aid. It does not replace physician evaluation or hospital admission criteria. All dengue patients with warning signs, severe thrombocytopenia, or deteriorating clinical status require immediate emergency evaluation regardless of the score result.
