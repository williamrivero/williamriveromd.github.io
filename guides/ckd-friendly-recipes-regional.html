Central Luzon · Pampanga

Kapampangan Cuisine

🟠 Kapampangan — Culinary Capital of the Philippines

Kapampangan cooking is bold, savory, and heavily reliant on fermented condiments — bagoong, patis, and toyo are foundational. The challenge for CKD: these are extreme sodium sources. The solution: the underlying aromatics — garlic, ginger, onion, vinegar, and achuete — are all kidney-safe and carry the essential character of the cuisine without the salt burden.

Northern Luzon · Ilocos Region

Ilocano Cuisine

🔵 Ilocano — The Cuisine of Frugality and Fermentation

Ilocano cooking is famous for its simplicity, sour-fermented flavors, and resourceful use of vegetables. Pinakbet with bagoong, dinengdeng, and bagnet define the region. For CKD: the heavy bagoong use and high-sodium fermented ingredients are the main challenges, but the underlying vegetable-forward philosophy of Ilocano cooking is actually highly compatible with kidney-friendly eating once the salt sources are addressed.

  • Okra, halved100g
  • Ampalaya (bitter gourd) — 1 small, seeds removed, soaked in water 15 min (reduces bitterness + K⁺)½ small
  • Kamote (leached 2hrs, thin slices)½ small

  • Garlic, minced5 cloves
  • Ginger, sliced1 thumb
  • Calamansi juice3 tbsp
  • White vinegar1 tbsp
  • Canola oil1 tbsp

Method

  1. Render lean pork belly in a dry pan until fat is released and edges are slightly crisp. Drain excess fat. Set pork aside.
  2. In the same pan, sauté garlic and ginger until fragrant. Add tomato and cook until softened — this creates the base flavor that normally comes from bagoong.
  3. Return pork. Add calamansi and vinegar — let sizzle for 30 seconds.
  4. Layer vegetables in order of cooking time: kamote first (already leached), then sayote and upo, then talong and okra, ampalaya last.
  5. Add 2 tbsp water, cover, and steam-cook on medium heat 8–10 min. Do not over-stir — traditional pinakbet is lightly steamed, not stirred.
  6. Uncover, season with pepper and more calamansi to taste. The tomato and calamansi should provide sufficient savory-sour flavor.

Western Visayas · Iloilo & Bacolod

Ilonggo Cuisine

🔴 Ilonggo — Sweet, Sour, and Subtly Rich

Ilonggo food is characterized by its sweet-sour balance, seafood-forward cooking, and the iconic KBL (Kadyos, Baboy, Langka) and La Paz Batchoy. The major CKD challenges: kadyos (pigeon peas) are very high in potassium and phosphorus, batchoy uses organ meats and a pork-bone broth that is extremely high in phosphorus, and the sweet corn in many dishes adds potassium. The restructured recipes preserve the soul of Ilonggo cooking.

Central Visayas · Cebu

Cebuano Cuisine

🟢 Cebuano — Bold, Sweet-Sour, and Pork-Forward

Cebuano cooking is famous for lechon (the best in the Philippines, by many accounts), chorizo de Cebu, and a sweet-savory adobo tradition. The challenge: chorizo de Cebu contains high levels of phosphate preservatives and sodium; lechon skin is extremely high in phosphorus; and the sweet soy-sauce base in many Cebuano dishes accumulates sodium. The solutions preserve the bold Cebuano flavor profile.

Method

  • Siling labuyo (bird's eye chili), sliced2 pcs

Method

  4. Add grilled pork pieces. Mix everything together — the charred pork flavor against the fresh kinilaw is the sinuglaw character.
  5. Season with black pepper only — no salt, no patis. The calamansi and vinegar provide all the flavor needed.
  6. Serve immediately at room temperature. Best eaten fresh — do not refrigerate after assembling.

NOTE Sinuglaw is naturally low in sodium when made traditionally with calamansi and vinegar. CAUTION For CKD Stage 4–5 / dialysis: reduce tuna portion to 60g and increase pork or add tokwa to compensate for protein. Raw fish should be avoided in transplant patients (immunosuppression).

Colonial Heritage · Spanish Influence

Filipino-Spanish Cuisine

🟣 Fil-Spanish — The Festive Table

The 333-year Spanish colonial presence gave Philippine cuisine its most celebratory dishes: morcon, relleno, callos, and pochero.   2. In a wide flat pan (paellera or large skillet), heat olive oil. Brown chicken cubes over high heat — deep golden color, 3–4 min. Remove and set aside.
  3. Sauté garlic and onion until soft. Add crushed tomato and paprika. Cook 5 min into a thick sofrito base.
  4. Add rinsed rice. Stir into sofrito — coat every grain with the tomato base (2 min). This is the sauté step that gives paella its toasted-bottom character.
  5. Add saffron water and unsalted broth. Bring to a boil. Return chicken. Cook on medium-high heat uncovered 10 min — do not stir after this point (allows socarrat crust to form).
  6. Nestle shrimp, clams, bell pepper strips, and peas into the rice. Cover and cook 8–10 min on medium-low until seafood is cooked and rice has absorbed all liquid.
  7. Rest 5 min. Serve with fresh calamansi. Portion: ¾ cup cooked paella per serving.

KEY MOD Chorizo Bilbao completely omitted — replaced by paprika (pimentón), which provides the same smoky-red Spanish character that chorizo traditionally contributes to paella, without the phosphate preservatives and very high sodium. MOD Unsalted homemade broth only — commercial bouillon cubes are extremely high in sodium (800+ mg per cube). MOD Peas and bell pepper strictly limited by volume — present for authenticity and color, not as a main vegetable component. Shellfish naturally low in potassium.

Quick Reference

Universal Flavor Swaps for All Regional Cuisines

💡

The CKD flavor formula — works across all six cuisines

Savory depth: caramelized garlic + ginger base → builds umami without salt. Acid brightness: calamansi juice → replaces patis/soy as the primary flavor balancer. Sour complexity: white cane vinegar + 1 tsp coconut aminos → replicates the soy-vinegar foundation of Filipino cooking at 10% of the sodium. Richness: olive oil or a small amount of rendered lean fat (discarding the first boil water) → provides body and mouthfeel. Heat: black pepper + siling haba → zero sodium, amplifies perception of saltiness.

Ingredients to use freely (all regions)

  • Garlic, ginger, onion — build these heavy
  • All vinegars (cane, coco, sukà ng Iloko) — zero sodium
  • Calamansi juice — use generously
  • Achuete / annatto — natural color and mild flavor, safe
  • Black pepper, white pepper — amplifies savory perception
  • Siling haba (green finger chili) — adds heat without salt
  • Lemongrass (tanglad) — aromatics, no mineral concern
  • Turmeric — anti-inflammatory, safe in CKD
  • Bay leaves, oregano, coriander, cumin — all safe
  • Olive oil, canola oil — heart and kidney friendly
  • Dried mushrooms (small amount) — umami without salt

Ingredients to eliminate or micro-dose

  • Bagoong (any form): eliminate — use calamansi + vinegar base
  • Patis: eliminate — coconut aminos at ½–1 tsp maximum
  • Toyo (regular soy sauce): eliminate — coconut aminos 1–2 tsp only
  • Oyster sauce, hoisin: eliminate — coconut aminos + pinch of sugar
  • MSG (Vetsin/AJI-NO-MOTO): eliminate — garlic caramelization provides equivalent umami
  • Chorizo, longganisa, tocino: eliminate — use fresh seasoned meat
  • Processed cheese: eliminate — very high phosphate
  • Canned goods (sardines, corned beef): eliminate — replace with fresh
  • Legumes (kadyos, monggo, sitaw): leach if used; prefer sayote/upo as substitute
