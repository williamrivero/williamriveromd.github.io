Why Food Is Medicine

Why Diet Matters for Your Kidneys

Dietary restrictions become progressively stricter as CKD advances. Importantly, dialysis patients actually INCREASE protein intake — the opposite of pre-dialysis. Never apply dialysis-level restrictions to early CKD patients.

What you eat directly affects how fast your kidney disease progresses. Excess protein increases glomerular pressure. High sodium raises blood pressure and worsens proteinuria. Phosphorus and potassium build up when kidneys cannot clear them — damaging bones and the heart. The right diet is not a restriction — it is active treatment.

Renal nutrition is highly individualized — what is right for a Stage 2 CKD patient is very different from what a dialysis patient needs. This guide covers general principles and then breaks down recommendations by stage. Always confirm specifics with your nephrologist or renal dietitian.

Diet slows CKD progression

A low-protein diet (0.6–0.8 g/kg/day) in pre-dialysis CKD reduces hyperfiltration stress on surviving nephrons, decreases proteinuria, and lowers uremic toxin production. Studies show it can delay dialysis initiation by months to years.

Malnutrition is a real danger

Under-eating is as dangerous as over-eating in CKD. Protein-energy wasting (PEW) — muscle loss due to inadequate calories and protein — dramatically worsens outcomes. Do not restrict too aggressively without professional guidance.

Stage-Specific Guidance

Dietary Targets by CKD Stage

Your nutritional needs change as CKD progresses. The table below outlines how key dietary targets shift across stages.

Stage 3 (eGFR 30–59) 0.6–0.8 g/kg/day <2,000 mg Restrict if K >5.0 Begin monitoring; <1,000 mg 2 L/day (or per labs)

💡

On dialysis — protein needs INCREASE, not decrease

Many patients assume they should eat less protein once on dialysis. The opposite is true. Each hemodialysis session removes amino acids along with waste products. Dialysis patients need 1.0–1.4 g/kg/day of high-quality protein to prevent dangerous muscle wasting (protein-energy wasting). Low albumin on dialysis significantly increases mortality.

The Four Key Nutrients

Understanding What to Watch — and Why

Protein
0.6–1.4 g/kg/day
Lower pre-dialysis to reduce hyperfiltration. Higher on dialysis to replace losses. Quality matters — choose complete proteins (egg whites, fish, chicken).
Sodium
<2,000 mg/day
1 teaspoon of table salt = 2,300 mg sodium. High sodium raises BP, worsens proteinuria, and increases thirst — causing dangerous fluid overload on dialysis.
Potassium
<2,000 mg/day (if restricted)
Only restrict if your blood potassium is elevated (>5.0–5.5 mEq/L). High potassium causes life-threatening irregular heartbeat. Not all CKD patients need restriction.
Phosphorus
800–1,000 mg/day
Kidneys cannot excrete excess phosphorus efficiently. High phosphorus causes itching, bone loss, and vessel calcification. Take phosphate binders WITH every meal — not before or after.
Calories
30–35 kcal/kg/day
Adequate calories prevent the body from breaking down muscle for energy. This is critical in CKD — malnutrition is a major cause of poor outcomes. Do not skip meals.
Fluids
Individualized
Pre-dialysis: usually 2 L/day is fine. On HD: restrict to urine output + 500 mL. Count all liquids — soup, ice, softdrinks, fruit with high water content.
Filipino Food Guide

What to Eat, Limit, and Avoid

This guide is calibrated for the Filipino diet — using foods available in local markets (palengke), grocery stores, and carinderia. Restrictions depend on your stage and lab results — confirm with your doctor.

Eat freely — kidney-safe choices

  • White rice, puti na tinapay, pasta, bihon, sotanghon
  • Egg whites (itlog puti — very low phosphorus)
  • Bangus, tilapia, galunggong (moderate portions)
  • Chicken breast (without skin)
  • Cabbage (repolyo), green beans (sitaw), pechay
  • Sayote (chayote) — low potassium and phosphorus
  • Upo, patola, ampalaya (bitter melon)
  • Apple, grapes, pineapple, canned pears (in juice, drained)
  • Corn, unsalted crackers, rice cakes (puto, suman without salt)
  • Water, unsweetened calamansi juice, barley water

Limit — eat small portions only

  • Red meat (beef, pork) — maximum 1 palm-sized serving/day
  • Kamote (sweet potato) — leach before cooking
  • Kangkong, malunggay — boil and discard water
  • Saging (banana) — 1 small piece if K is controlled
  • Papaya, melon (limited — moderate K)
  • Whole eggs (yolk has high phosphorus)
  • Brown rice (higher phosphorus than white)
  • Mongo (mung beans) — small portions, well-cooked
  • Low-sodium soy sauce (use sparingly)

Avoid — high risk foods

  • Organ meats: liver, kidney, brain, goto, dinuguan
  • Bagoong, patis, toyo (fish/soy sauce) in large amounts
  • Instant noodles (Lucky Me, Nissin — very high sodium)
  • Coconut water (buko juice — very high potassium)
  • Avocado (high potassium)
  • Processed meats: longganisa, tocino, hotdog, SPAM
  • Dark colas (Coke, Pepsi — high phosphorus additives)
  • Nuts, peanut butter (high phosphorus and potassium)
  • Squash/kalabasa (high potassium)
  • Salt substitutes (contain potassium chloride — dangerous)

Kidney-protective foods — include regularly

  • Ampalaya (bitter melon) — low K/P, anti-inflammatory
  • Sayote — low calorie, low K/P, high fiber
  • Calamansi — Vitamin C helps iron absorption; low K
  • Ginger and garlic for flavor (replaces salt)
  • Olive oil or canola oil (heart-protective, no K/P)
  • Egg whites — highest quality protein, minimal phosphorus
  • Low-fat yogurt (if K is controlled) — supports gut-kidney axis
Sample Meal Plans

A Day of Eating Well with CKD (Pre-Dialysis, Stage 3)

Based on a 60 kg patient targeting 0.7 g/kg protein, <2,000 mg sodium, and approximately 1,800 kcal/day. Adjust portions to your actual weight and lab results.

Tagalog-style day

Breakfast
1 cup white rice · 2 egg whites scrambled with garlic and tomato (walang asin) · calamansi juice (unsweetened)
Snack
1 small apple · 4 unsalted crackers
Lunch
1 cup rice · 1 palm-size grilled bangus (walang toyo) · boiled sayote · small bowl of clear broth (low-sodium)
Snack
½ cup pineapple chunks · 2 rice cakes (puto — unsalted)
Dinner
1 cup rice · tinolang manok (chicken breast, sayote, walang patis) · boiled green beans · water

Budget-friendly day (approx. ₱150–200)

Breakfast
1 cup rice · 2 egg whites fried in oil (no salt) · sliced tomato · barley water
Snack
1 small pack of unsalted crackers · water
Lunch
1 cup rice · sinigang na tilapia (low-sodium, no patis) · boiled upo · clear soup
Snack
½ cup canned pears (drained, rinsed) · water
Dinner
1 cup rice · steamed chicken breast with calamansi and garlic · boiled sitaw · 1 glass water
⚠

If you are on hemodialysis, this meal plan changes significantly

Dialysis patients need more protein (1.0–1.4 g/kg/day) and must be stricter with potassium and fluids. The portions of fish and chicken above should be increased, and coconut water, banana, and squash must be strictly avoided. Ask your dialysis nurse or dietitian for an HD-specific meal plan.

Reading Food Labels

How to Read a Nutrition Label for Kidney Health

Check sodium first

Look at "Sodium" per serving. Anything above 400 mg per serving is high. Watch for multiple servings per pack — multiply the sodium by the number of servings you eat. Target <600 mg sodium per meal.

Phosphorus additives

Inorganic phosphorus (from additives) is absorbed 90–100% vs. 40–60% from natural foods. Look for ingredients ending in "-phosphate": calcium phosphate, sodium phosphate, disodium phosphate. These are in canned goods, processed cheese, instant noodles, and dark colas.

Potassium on labels

Philippine food labels do not always list potassium. For packaged foods, look for "potassium chloride" in the ingredients — this is used as a salt substitute and is very high in potassium. Avoid it entirely.

Cooking Technique

How to Leach Vegetables to Reduce Potassium

Leaching removes 30–50% of the potassium from most vegetables — making some higher-potassium vegetables safer to eat in small amounts.

🥬

Step-by-step leaching method

  • Step 1: Peel and cut vegetables into small, thin pieces — more surface area means more potassium released
  • Step 2: Soak in a large amount of warm water for at least 2 hours. For best results, change the water once during soaking
  • Step 3: Drain and discard the soaking water completely
  • Step 4: Boil in fresh water (at least 10 times the volume of the vegetable)
  • Step 5: Drain and discard the boiling water — do not use the broth for soup
  • Works best for: kamote, potato, pechay, kangkong, sitaw, squash (if permitted in small amounts)
⚠

Leaching does not eliminate potassium — it reduces it

Even after leaching, high-potassium vegetables should still be eaten in small portions. Leaching does not make them freely safe. And fruits cannot be leached — stick to low-potassium fruits (apple, grapes, pineapple, canned pears rinsed in water).

Practical Advice

Everyday Tips for Eating Well with CKD

Season without salt

Use calamansi, vinegar (suka), garlic, onion, ginger, pandan, and herbs (tanglad, dahon ng laurel) to add flavor without sodium. A squeeze of calamansi replaces the need for patis in most dishes.

Choose fresh over processed

Fresh bangus from the palengke has far less sodium and phosphorus than canned sardines. Fresh chicken has less sodium than processed longganisa. The fresher and less processed, the better for your kidneys.

Eat smaller, more frequent meals

Small, frequent meals (4–5 per day) are easier on the kidneys than two large meals. They also help maintain blood sugar, prevent excessive protein loading per meal, and keep energy levels stable throughout the day.

Plan ahead for carinderia

When eating out, ask for your viand to be cooked without patis or toyo and served with extra rice and vegetables. Sinigang without patis, grilled fish, and steamed vegetables are generally safe choices at most Filipino restaurants.

Weigh yourself daily

Daily morning weight (same scale, after toileting, before eating) tracks fluid changes. A gain of more than 0.5 kg overnight suggests excess fluid intake. Bring your weight log to every appointment.

Take binders with meals

Phosphate binders (calcium carbonate, sevelamer) must be taken with food — not before or after. They work by binding phosphorus in the food you are currently eating. Taking them at the wrong time makes them useless.
