Before you begin

How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Dialysis Technician series. Each sitting draws 40 questions at random from a bank of 258, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark32 of 40 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.
What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

How to get your certificate of completion

  1. Pass all 12 module quizzes. Each module quiz is passed at 80%, or at the pass mark the module states. The Certificate checklist above the examination shows which modules are done; each line links to that module's quiz.
  2. Use the same device and browser for the modules and this examination. Progress is saved in your browser, not in an account. On iPhone and iPad, the Home Screen app and Safari keep separate progress.
  3. Pass this examination (80%; where these instructions say so, every safety-critical question must also be correct). If you pass before finishing the modules, your pass is saved: finish the module quizzes, then come back here.
  4. Type your name the way you want it printed and tap Make my certificate. You can tick Add a preceptor sign-off line if your unit will sign the printed copy.
  5. Save or print your completion packet: two pages, the certificate and a record of your module results. Use Print or save as PDF to keep both pages together.
  6. Be online when you make it, so the certificate gets its QR code. Anyone can scan the code, or enter it at renalcarematters.com/verify, to confirm it.

Not finished yet? Use Print a progress record in the checklist to show a preceptor how far you have gone; it is clearly marked "not a certificate". The certificate is a self-study certificate of completion, not CPD units: competence is demonstrated on your own unit and signed off by a preceptor. To make the QR code work, your name, track, date and score are saved with the certificate code; to have them removed, use Report a correction on any guide.

Examination

End-of-course examination

Question 1Where should unopened supplies be staged?
A designated clean area separates supplies from used equipment and waste. Module 1.
Question 2Who authorizes a change to the dialysis prescription?
The prescribing clinician makes medical decisions about treatment parameters. Module 1.
Question 3Which pair can verify identity when approved by the facility?
Both identifiers are person-specific and must be checked against the approved source. Module 1.
Question 4What does the R in SBAR stand for?
This element states the action or review you are requesting. Module 1.
Question 5A station was cleaned but the machine failed a pre-use check. What does that mean for readiness?
Cleaning and technical readiness are separate prerequisites. Module 1.
Question 6Which note is objective?
It records the reported symptom, timing and action without speculation. Module 1.
Question 7Which response shows a check-back?
Repeating the instruction gives the recipient a chance to confirm or correct it. Module 1.
Question 8A wrong record is caught before setup. How should it be classified for safety review?
The problem was intercepted before it reached the patient. Module 1.
Question 9The name matches, but the stated birth date differs. What does that mean?
Both identifiers must be verified before relying on the record. Module 1.
Question 10The patient cannot state their identifiers. What should you do?
The alternate procedure supplies an authorized basis for verification. Module 1.
Question 11A caller who knows the surname asks for a patient’s schedule. What should you do?
A surname alone establishes neither identity nor authority to receive information. Module 1.
Question 12A patient reports new chest discomfort during a routine check. What comes first?
New chest discomfort warrants immediate clinical attention. Module 1.
Question 13A coworker asks you to do a task you have only observed. What should you do?
Observation gives exposure. The preceptor confirms competency and permitted practice. Module 1.
Question 14You recorded a check under the wrong machine ID. What should you do?
An auditable correction makes clear which machine was checked and limits reliance on an erroneous record. Module 1.
Question 15A patient asks to shorten treatment because of transport. What should you do?
The nurse can raise the barrier with the clinical team while treatment decisions stay within the authorized scope. Module 1.
Question 16Which wording is the correct way to ask a patient to confirm identity?
An open question makes the patient supply the identifiers; a yes can be given to any name. Module 1.
Question 17Jose Santos and Josefina Santos are scheduled on the same shift. What should happen?
Similar names are marked, kept apart where possible, and announced at every handoff. Module 1.
Question 18At 10:15 you realize you did not write down the 09:30 reading you took. What is correct?
A late entry is honest; backdating is falsification. Module 1.
Question 19When may cleaning and disinfection of a station begin?
The station is cleaned only once the patient has left it, and the next patient’s supplies come after cleaning. Module 1.
Question 20Which statement is a suitable Assessment in a technician’s SBAR report?
The technician reports observations and numbers, not a cause or a treatment. Module 1.
Question 21After treatment, who decides that the patient is fit to go home?
The technician measures and reports; the nurse assesses and decides. Module 1.
Question 22Which nephron structure first filters water and small solutes from blood?
The glomerulus is the nephron’s initial filtration site. Module 2.
Question 23Which structure carries urine from the kidney to the bladder?
The ureter carries urine from the renal pelvis toward the bladder. Module 2.
Question 24Which hormone signals the bone marrow to produce red blood cells?
Erythropoietin stimulates red blood cell production. Reduced kidney production can contribute to anemia. Module 2.
Question 25What minimum duration supports the definition of CKD when qualifying abnormalities persist?
chronic kidney disease (CKD) requires evidence of abnormalities present for at least three months. Module 2.
Question 26A diagram shows useful solutes moving from tubular fluid back into blood. What process does this represent?
Reabsorption returns selected filtered substances from the tubule to the circulation. Module 2.
Question 27A mock result shows low serum bicarbonate. Which kidney function is most directly related to this finding?
The kidneys excrete acid and conserve or regenerate bicarbonate. The clinician interprets the result in context. Module 2.
Question 28A patient with advanced CKD has reduced vitamin D activation and altered phosphate handling. What complication does this help explain?
Impaired phosphate handling and reduced activation of vitamin D can contribute to CKD-related mineral and bone disorder. Module 2.
Question 29After a recent illness, creatinine rises over two days in a patient already known to have CKD. How should you interpret the timeline?
Acute deterioration can occur in someone who already has chronic kidney disease. Report the trend for clinical evaluation. Module 2.
Question 30Why can a person with kidney failure still pass urine?
Residual urine production can persist even when overall filtration and regulation are severely impaired. Module 2.
Question 31Previous post-dialysis weight was 67.2 kg and current pre-dialysis weight is 69.4 kg. What should you report?
69.4 minus 67.2 equals 2.2 kg. Report the measured change. The fluid removal prescription is a separate decision. Module 2.
Question 32The same patient now reports new breathlessness at rest. What should you do first?
New breathlessness at rest needs prompt clinical assessment. Escalation takes priority over routine calculations. Module 2.
Question 33A verified potassium result is marked critical, but the patient says they feel well. What is the best action?
A critical potassium result requires timely clinical review even in the absence of symptoms. Module 2.
Question 34A new chart contains one low eGFR value. How should you communicate it?
A single abnormal estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) cannot establish chronicity. Reporting the result and missing history helps the clinical team investigate. Module 2.
Question 35The patient is tired and a previous hemoglobin is flagged low. Which handoff best fits your role?
This reports the symptom and available result objectively without diagnosing the cause or directing treatment. Module 2.
Question 36Today’s weight was entered as post-treatment, but you measured it at arrival. What should you do before it is used?
Treatment timing changes how the weight is interpreted. Correct it through the unit process and alert the team before it is relied upon. Module 2.
Question 37A patient’s last post-weight was 71.5 kg. Today’s pre-weight is 74.6 kg. What is the interdialytic weight gain?
74.6 minus 71.5 equals 3.1 kg, about 3.1 liters of fluid. Module 2.
Question 38A patient with a dry weight of 75.0 kg has gained 3.0 kg. What is the gain as a percent of dry weight?
3.0 divided by 75.0, times 100, equals 4.0 percent. Module 2.
Question 39A blood pressure cuff that is too small for the patient’s arm gives a reading that is:
A cuff that is too small overestimates blood pressure; one that is too large underestimates it. Module 2.
Question 40A patient in a wheelchair reads 92.6 kg on the platform scale. The empty wheelchair weighs 19.4 kg. What is the patient’s weight?
92.6 minus 19.4 equals 73.2 kg. Module 2.
Question 41A hepatitis B surface antigen result is newly reported as reactive for a patient due on the next shift. Why must the nurse know before setup?
Infection status decides where and on which machine the patient is treated. Module 2.
Question 42A patient’s pulse feels irregular. How should the rate be counted?
An irregular pulse is counted for a full minute and the irregularity is reported. Module 2.
Question 43Why can a dangerously high potassium be missed at the chair?
High potassium can be silent, which is why a flagged result is reported even when the patient feels well. Module 2.
Question 44What serves as the exchange membrane in peritoneal dialysis?
The lining of the abdomen provides the surface across which fluid and solutes move. Module 3.
Question 45Which option uses an extracorporeal dialyzer at home?
Home hemodialysis (HD) circulates blood through an external dialyzer using a home machine. Module 3.
Question 46Which device performs programmed exchanges in APD?
APD uses a cycler to carry out prescribed exchanges. Module 3.
Question 47Which option involves surgically placing a donor kidney?
Transplantation places a living- or deceased-donor kidney into the recipient. Module 3.
Question 48Which description best fits comprehensive conservative kidney management?
Conservative management is an active care pathway guided by symptoms, quality of life and patient goals. Module 3.
Question 49A patient wants to compare PD and home HD. Which equipment distinction is accurate?
The two therapies use different exchange surfaces and different types of access. Module 3.
Question 50A patient assumes a kidney transplant means all kidney-related follow-up ends. What needs clarification?
The transplant team monitors graft function and ongoing immunosuppression. Module 3.
Question 51A patient says home HD would make travel easier. Which additional point belongs in the discussion?
Travel is one factor. The home program evaluates training, setup, practical support and backup arrangements. Module 3.
Question 52Which patient statement best shows accurate teach-back after approved contact instructions?
A specific explanation in the patient’s own words shows what action they understood. Module 3.
Question 53Ms. Reyes says she may have no transport for tomorrow’s dialysis. What should you do first?
A potential missed treatment needs timely attention. The nurse and unit determine the safe plan. Module 3.
Question 54The patient asks whether her daughter must be present for every home HD treatment. What is the best response?
Care-partner requirements vary. The home program can assess suitability and explain its requirements. Module 3.
Question 55A patient wants to keep working but has rotating shifts. Which question best elicits what matters?
An open question identifies the patient’s priorities and gives the clinical team specific barriers to address. Module 3.
Question 56A patient on PD calls and reports cloudy drained fluid and new abdominal pain. What should you reinforce?
These symptoms can signal infection and require prompt assessment by the PD team. Module 3.
Question 57During teach-back the patient says they will wait silently if they cannot attend. What should you do next?
Teach-back shows where communication needs repair. Re-explain and check again. Module 3.
Question 58A relative offers to manage treatment discussions, but the patient has expressed a preference to speak privately. What should you do?
Shared decision-making centers the patient’s preferences and establishes who may join the discussion. Module 3.
Question 59A session is prescribed for 240 minutes. The patient ends 30 minutes early. What share of the session was delivered?
210 divided by 240, times 100, equals 87.5 percent. Module 3.
Question 60A patient with a 1,000 mL allowance has had one coffee (180 mL), one bowl of soup broth (200 mL), and two glasses of water (240 mL each). How much is left for the day?
180 + 200 + 480 = 860 mL, and 1,000 minus 860 leaves 140 mL. Module 3.
Question 61A patient mentions switching to a salt substitute to cut down on salt. Why should the nurse be told?
Potassium-based salt substitutes can raise blood potassium in a person with kidney failure. Module 3.
Question 62A patient says, “Take off an extra kilo today, I drank a lot.” Which reply is within the technician’s scope?
Fluid removal is a clinical decision; the technician passes on the information. Module 3.
Question 63A patient says he ate star fruit yesterday and now has hiccups that will not stop. What should you do?
Star fruit is harmful in kidney failure, and persistent hiccups can be an early sign. Module 3.
Question 64A patient quietly says, “Sometimes I think everyone would be better off if I were gone.” What is the correct response?
Talk of wanting to die is reported at once and is never kept secret. Module 3.
Question 65Which force primarily drives the diffusion of urea across a dialyzer membrane?
Diffusion moves permeable solutes down their concentration gradient. Urea generally moves from blood toward dialysate. Module 4.
Question 66Which term describes net water movement across the membrane driven by a pressure difference?
The pressure difference across the membrane drives water movement. Controlled net water removal is ultrafiltration. Module 4.
Question 67A dissolved solute crosses the membrane as it is carried along with moving water. Which process describes that solute movement?
Convection is the movement of permeable solutes with water through the membrane. Ultrafiltration describes the water movement itself. Module 4.
Question 68At one point in a teaching dialyzer, dialysate bicarbonate concentration is higher than blood bicarbonate concentration. In which direction does bicarbonate tend to diffuse at that point?
A permeable solute tends to diffuse from higher to lower concentration. The direction depends on the gradient for that solute. Module 4.
Question 69Why does countercurrent flow help sustain diffusion in a conventional dialyzer?
Opposing blood and dialysate flows help maintain usable concentration gradients over the dialyzer’s length. Module 4.
Question 70In a simulated session, the net ultrafiltration goal is zero while blood and fresh dialysate still flow. What can still happen?
Urea can still diffuse across the membrane when a concentration gradient is present. Net fluid removal and diffusive solute removal are distinct processes. Module 4.
Question 71What are the usual inputs for preparing conventional bicarbonate dialysate?
The system uses treated dialysis water with the correct matched concentrates. Verify the prescribed formulation and equipment compatibility under the facility’s protocol. Module 4.
Question 72In the conceptual expression Kt/V, what does t represent?
K represents urea clearance, t represents time, and V estimates the volume in which urea is distributed. Module 4.
Question 73A colleague says a urine output estimate is required to calculate the urea reduction ratio (URR) for this session. Which data are needed for the simple URR calculation?
URR is the percentage decrease between paired pre- and post-dialysis BUN values. The clinical team may consider residual kidney function separately when evaluating the overall prescription. Module 4.
Question 74During a simulation, the pre-use concentrate label disagrees with the patient's verified prescription even though the machine displays conductivity within range. What should you do?
Conductivity does not verify every ingredient or replace label and prescription checks. Resolve the mismatch through the approved process before proceeding. Module 4.
Question 75The two BUN results for a mock URR both have a pre-treatment timestamp. What should you do before using them?
URR requires verified pre- and post-treatment samples from the corresponding treatment. A mismatched time or label can invalidate the result. Module 4.
Question 76What is the difference between target weight and a prior post-treatment weight?
The goal and actual measured weight may differ. The clinical team reviews the target using current assessment and treatment history. Module 4.
Question 77The prior post-dialysis weight was 64.8 kg and today’s pre-dialysis weight is 67.1 kg. What is the measured interdialytic weight gain?
Subtract the prior measured post-treatment weight from today’s measured pre-treatment weight. Here 67.1 − 64.8 = 2.3 kg. Module 4.
Question 78In the same case, the target weight in the chart is 65.4 kg and the session’s prescribed ultrafiltration goal is unclear. What is the best next step?
The measured gain and the 1.7 kg difference from target answer different questions. Neither independently authorizes a machine setting. Module 4.
Question 79A simulation order lists 1,800 mL of fluid removal over 3 hours. Assuming the rate is constant, what is the average ultrafiltration rate in mL/hour?
Divide mock volume by the time over which it is removed. Here 1,800 mL ÷ 3 hours = 600 mL/hour. A calculated value is for verification within the trainee’s role. Module 4.
Question 80A patient’s pre-dialysis weight is 72.0 kg and the target weight is 70.0 kg. The unit counts 400 mL for rinse-back and drinks. The session is 4 hours. What is the hourly ultrafiltration rate?
2,000 mL plus 400 mL is 2,400 mL, and 2,400 divided by 4 hours is 600 mL per hour. Module 4.
Question 81Two patients each have 3,200 mL to remove in 4 hours. One weighs 50 kg and the other 80 kg. Which statement is correct?
800 divided by 50 is 16 mL per kg per hour, against 10 for the 80 kg patient. Module 4.
Question 82The order says blood flow 300 mL per minute, but the chart shows the pump ran at 250 all session. Who decides whether the prescription changes?
The technician records what was delivered and reports it; the prescription belongs to the nephrologist. Module 4.
Question 83A patient loses 15 minutes at each of 13 sessions in a month. How much treatment time is lost?
15 times 13 is 195 minutes, which is 3 hours 15 minutes. Module 4.
Question 84Why is the blood pump slowed briefly before the post-dialysis BUN sample is drawn?
A sample containing recirculated blood has a falsely low BUN and makes the session look better than it was. Module 4.
Question 85Pre-dialysis BUN is 80 mg/dL and post-dialysis BUN is 30 mg/dL. What is the URR, and how does it compare with the commonly cited minimum?
(80 minus 30) divided by 80 is 62.5 percent. Module 4.
Question 86A late patient’s session is cut from 4 hours to 3, and the 3,000 mL goal is unchanged. What should the technician do?
The hourly rate has risen without a decision; the nurse must confirm the goal. Module 4.
Question 87To whom do Standard Precautions apply in the dialysis unit?
Use Standard Precautions for every patient. Select protective measures according to the anticipated exposure during the task. Module 5.
Question 88What should you do after removing used gloves?
Gloves can have small defects and hands can become contaminated during removal. Hand hygiene is needed after removal. Module 5.
Question 89A task may splash blood toward your face. Which PPE addresses that specific risk?
Face and eye protection help prevent a splash from reaching mucous membranes. Use gloves and protective clothing when the task also exposes hands or clothing. Module 5.
Question 90An observer records 20 hand hygiene opportunities but sees the action performed 15 times. What can this observation support?
A process audit shows which safety steps were performed during observed opportunities. Use the findings for feedback and improvement. Module 5.
Question 91You handled used tubing with gloves and are now asked to get clean gauze from a common supply cart. What do you do?
Hands and gloves can carry contamination to clean stock. Remove the gloves and perform hand hygiene before entering the clean workflow. Module 5.
Question 92A patient is scheduled under the unit’s hepatitis B isolation protocol, but the station assignment includes equipment also used for other patients. What should you do?
Dialysis-specific precautions for hepatitis B can include assigned equipment and other controls. The charge nurse verifies the correct arrangement under the facility protocol. Module 5.
Question 93Which source sets the required wet contact time for an approved surface disinfectant?
The product instructions specify how long the surface must remain wet. Follow the facility-approved process for that product. Module 5.
Question 94A cleaned chair looks dry, but the surface dried before the required disinfectant contact time was reached. How should you interpret its status?
A dry appearance alone does not establish that the product had the required wet contact time. Complete the approved procedure before release. Module 5.
Question 95The prior patient is still at the station after treatment and requires assistance before leaving. What does this mean for routine station disinfection?
Routine disinfection begins after the station is vacated. Patient safety takes priority when the person cannot yet move safely. Module 5.
Question 96A used blood pressure cuff is about to be moved to a clean station, and the receiving chair is still within the required wet contact time. What should you do?
The cuff and receiving chair each need their own completed process. Release each for reuse only after the facility’s applicable cleaning and disinfection requirements are met. Module 5.
Question 97A used cuff was briefly placed on a clean supply cart before you noticed. What is the best response?
Treat the contact as a clean-workflow breach. Prevent further use of the affected items and follow the facility’s escalation and remediation process. Module 5.
Question 98Where should a used needle be discarded?
Point-of-use disposal in a designated sharps container reduces handling and protects clinical and waste personnel. Module 5.
Question 99The sharps container has reached its marked fill line. What does this indicate?
The fill line identifies when safe capacity has been reached. Close and replace the container through the approved process to limit protrusions and needlestick injuries. Module 5.
Question 100A used sharp punctures your skin during turnover. What should you do promptly?
Prompt first aid and reporting support timely assessment for bloodborne infections and any indicated follow-up. Module 5.
Question 101While a trainee removes used supplies, blood splashes into their eye. What is the appropriate immediate response?
Irrigation is the appropriate first aid for an eye exposure. Prompt reporting and clinical evaluation are needed even without symptoms. Module 5.
Question 102When removing protective equipment after turnover, which item comes off first?
Gloves are the most contaminated item and are removed first; hand hygiene is always the final step. Module 5.
Question 103You silence an alarm by touching the machine screen but do not touch the patient. Is hand hygiene needed before you go to another station?
Hands or gloves that touched one station can carry germs to the next. Module 5.
Question 104A surface is wiped and then dried with a towel 20 seconds later. The label contact time is 3 minutes. What is the status of the surface?
Disinfectant works only while the surface is wet for the time on the label. Module 5.
Question 105Unit policy calls for a 0.5 percent solution. The bottle is 5 percent. How much concentrate makes 2,000 mL?
(0.5 divided by 5) times 2,000 equals 200 mL, with 1,800 mL of water. Module 5.
Question 106The label ratio 1:100 is read as 1 part in 100 parts total. How much concentrate is needed for 1,000 mL?
1,000 divided by 100 is 10 mL, plus 990 mL of water. Module 5.
Question 107According to CDC guidance, which patient requires a dedicated machine and a separate room or area?
Hepatitis B survives on surfaces and spreads from tiny amounts of blood, so it alone requires isolation in that guidance. Module 5.
Question 108After a needlestick you have washed the wound. Why must you report it during the same shift?
Speed of reporting is the part the exposed person controls. Module 5.
Question 109Which component propels blood through the extracorporeal circuit?
The blood pump moves blood through the extracorporeal bloodlines and dialyzer. Module 6.
Question 110Where do blood and dialysate exchange small solutes across a membrane?
The dialyzer houses the membrane between the separate blood and dialysate pathways. Module 6.
Question 111A diagram labels one line ‘blood to patient.’ Which pathway is this on a typical two-line circuit?
In standard circuit nomenclature, the arterial line withdraws blood and the venous line returns blood to the patient. Module 6.
Question 112How does the conventional circuit expose blood to dialysate for exchange while keeping the fluids separated?
The dialyzer membrane allows selective transport between separate fluid pathways. Module 6.
Question 113A detector monitors spent dialysate for evidence that blood crossed the membrane. Which safeguard is this?
The dialyzer blood leak detector monitors spent dialysate for blood. External leakage at a connection requires separate observation and response. Module 6.
Question 114On a disconnected trainer, you are asked to identify the area where the machine monitors for air before blood would return to the patient. What do you trace?
Trace the return path and identify the air detection and clamp functions on the actual model using its instructions. Module 6.
Question 115What written reference determines how your station’s specific machine and compatible disposables are prepared?
Models and disposables differ. Use the applicable instructions and the facility’s validated pre-use process. Module 6.
Question 116What is a purpose of priming the extracorporeal circuit before use?
Priming prepares the prescribed circuit for use and clears air as specified. Independent readiness checks are still required. Module 6.
Question 117The machine self-test passed. One assigned independent readiness check is not yet documented as complete. What does this tell you?
A passed self-test covers its defined test functions. Confirm that assigned independent checks were completed before releasing the station. Module 6.
Question 118The available bloodline set resembles the usual one, but compatibility with this machine is uncertain. What should you do?
Appearance is insufficient to verify compatibility. Confirm the exact disposable and configuration before opening or using it. Module 6.
Question 119After a supervised setup, you notice visible air remains in the mock return line. What is your next step?
Visible air requires correction and verification under the model instructions before the circuit is considered ready. Module 6.
Question 120What does the absence of a machine alarm establish about the access site and external connections?
Alarms monitor defined conditions within their ranges and have detection limits. Keep the access and external connections visible and follow the authorized monitoring process. Module 6.
Question 121Before the patient arrives, a required self-test fails and the failure remains unresolved after authorized checks. What should you do?
A failed readiness check leaves equipment unverified. Prevent use and follow the local reporting and release procedure. Module 6.
Question 122Which equipment log entry best supports follow-up after a failed pre-use self-test?
The entry identifies the equipment, exact observed failure, time, use status and notifications, so authorized staff can investigate and track follow-up. Module 6.
Question 123During treatment you notice blood at an external connection, but the blood leak alarm has not activated. What should you do?
External blood loss can occur without triggering the dialyzer blood leak detector. Respond to the observed hazard promptly and within scope. Module 6.
Question 124What is the main reason a dialyzer is primed from the bottom upward at a slow pump speed?
Air trapped in fibers blocks them and lowers clearance. Module 6.
Question 125A primed circuit shows pale streaks along the dialyzer. What do they most likely mean?
Streaks are air-locked fibers; the dialyzer needs further priming or replacement by the unit’s procedure. Module 6.
Question 126Which statement about the machine’s rinse program is correct?
A rinse is never counted as disinfection. Module 6.
Question 127After a chemical disinfection program, what must be shown before the machine is used?
Only the residual test proves the chemical has been rinsed out. Module 6.
Question 128When should the dialysate lines be connected to the dialyzer?
Dialysate that is out of range must not reach the dialyzer. Module 6.
Question 129Which treatment component is designed to remove chlorine and chloramine before reverse osmosis?
Activated carbon adsorbs or reduces disinfectant residues. The facility uses prescribed monitoring to detect breakthrough. Module 7.
Question 130What is the stream of treated water that passes through the reverse osmosis (RO) membrane called?
Permeate is the product stream that passes through the RO membrane. The reject stream carries concentrated contaminants away. Module 7.
Question 131Why can a clinic need dialysis-specific water treatment even when its source water is safe to drink?
Dialysis uses substantial water volumes and the membrane provides an exchange surface. Dialysis-specific chemical and microbiological controls apply to the water used to prepare dialysate. Module 7.
Question 132What is the main purpose of the dialysis-water distribution loop?
The distribution system delivers treated product water to points of use. Its design and maintenance help control contamination. Module 7.
Question 133On the facility’s approved diagram, you are asked to point to the route feeding product water to a station. Which path should you trace?
Product water travels from the RO product side through the facility’s distribution system to the station. Keep treated-water feeds distinct from drains and concentrate connections. Module 7.
Question 134Which two tests measure different aspects of microbiological water quality?
Culture looks for organisms recoverable under the specified culture conditions. Endotoxin testing measures a bacterial product and answers a different question. Module 7.
Question 135What does pH describe in a prepared dialysate sample?
pH measures acidity or alkalinity. Conductivity measures a property related to the dissolved ions that carry electrical current. Module 7.
Question 136The dialysate conductivity is within the expected range. What can you conclude from that result alone?
Conductivity describes the combined electrical behavior of dissolved ions. It cannot identify each electrolyte or establish microbiological quality. Module 7.
Question 137A total chlorine result and an endotoxin result both appear in a log. Why does the facility retain both?
Total chlorine helps assess disinfectant breakthrough. Endotoxin monitoring evaluates a different contaminant and remains necessary under the applicable plan. Module 7.
Question 138A concentrate container fits the connector but its label differs from the verified formulation. What should you do before station use?
Check the actual concentrate identity, compatibility and prescribed formulation. A compatible-looking connector or conductivity reading does not authorize a substitution. Module 7.
Question 139You are assigned to collect a mock RO-water sample, but the collection point on the form is unclear. What is the appropriate next step?
A result is interpretable only in relation to the correct sampling point, collection method and timing. Resolve missing or conflicting instructions before collecting. Module 7.
Question 140The final total chlorine entry shows ‘above action limit’ at the required sampling point before the first session. Which statement best describes the water status?
The out-of-range result identifies an unresolved water-quality concern. Use the facility’s procedure to contain the affected supply and obtain authorized clearance. Module 7.
Question 141A total chlorine check is above the facility’s action limit and no patients have started. What is your first priority?
Prevent exposure and promptly escalate. The authorized team determines repeat testing, corrective action, and clearance under site procedure. Module 7.
Question 142Which log entry best supports follow-up for that out-of-range result?
Capture the observation, time, exact result and units, affected use, notifications and follow-up status. Preserve the original result and link any subsequent checks. Module 7.
Question 143An authorized staff member obtains a normal repeat result after the hold. What should you do before telling staff the loop is ready?
A normal repeat is part of the investigation. Return to use follows the facility’s clearance and documentation requirements. Module 7.
Question 144Before a total chlorine sample is taken at the start of the day, the water system should have been running for at least how long?
Flowing water shows how the carbon performs under working conditions; water that sat overnight reads falsely low. Module 7.
Question 145Which widely used limit applies to total chlorine in water used for dialysis?
The widely used standard limit is 0.1 mg/L; drinking-water levels are far too high for dialysis. Module 7.
Question 146Why is the hardness test commonly done at the end of the treatment day?
Testing at the point of greatest exhaustion gives the earliest warning. Module 7.
Question 147A bicarbonate container is one third full of solution mixed earlier. A coworker suggests adding freshly mixed solution to fill it. What is correct?
Topping up carries bacteria into the new batch and makes the mixing time on the label false. Module 7.
Question 148Within ten minutes, three patients on the same shift report chest pain and shortness of breath, and the blood in their lines looks unusually clear and red. What should the technician suspect and do first?
The same complaint in several patients at once points to what they share: the water and dialysate. Module 7.
Question 149Total chlorine after the first carbon tank is 0.3 mg/L and after the second tank is below 0.1 mg/L. Who decides whether treatments may start?
The technician holds, reports exact results, and records the decision; release belongs to the authorized person. Module 7.
Question 150The acid concentrate label shows a different potassium value from the prescription, but machine conductivity is in range. What does this mean?
Small differences in potassium or calcium do not move conductivity out of range. Module 7.
Question 151What is an arteriovenous (AV) fistula?
An AV fistula connects the patient’s artery and vein directly to create access for hemodialysis. Module 8.
Question 152What distinguishes an AV graft from a typical AV fistula?
A graft uses an implanted conduit, typically synthetic, to bridge the artery and vein. A fistula joins the vessels directly. Module 8.
Question 153Where does a hemodialysis central venous catheter (CVC) provide access?
A CVC is a tube placed into a large central vein to withdraw and return blood for hemodialysis. Module 8.
Question 154Why is access type relevant when discussing bloodstream infection risk?
Bloodstream infection risk varies by access type. Central venous catheters carry the highest relative risk, and infection precautions apply to every type. Module 8.
Question 155On a teaching model you see a skin exit site with external catheter lumens. Which access does this best represent?
An external exit site and catheter lumens are consistent with a CVC model. Verify a patient’s actual access using the chart and the responsible clinician. Module 8.
Question 156What is the term for the vibration that can be felt over a functioning fistula or graft?
A thrill is a palpable vibration from blood flow. A bruit is the sound heard during authorized assessment. Module 8.
Question 157A patient asks why staff should avoid routine blood pressure measurements on the access arm. What is the best explanation?
Avoid routine compression of the access arm and confirm the appropriate alternate measurement site with the clinical team. Module 8.
Question 158During treatment a patient wants to cover the access to stay warm. How can you help?
Keeping the access and connections visible supports early recognition of bleeding or dislodgement. Offer warmth without blocking observation. Module 8.
Question 159You notice a CVC dressing is wet and partly loose before treatment. What should you do?
A wet or loose catheter dressing needs prompt review. The trainee should avoid unassigned dressing or line manipulation. Module 8.
Question 160A patient says their usual fistula vibration was present yesterday and is absent this morning. What does this finding suggest?
An absent or newly changed thrill can reflect impaired flow and is a warning sign. Clinical assessment determines the cause and readiness for use. Module 8.
Question 161Which combination warrants concern for a possible access infection?
New local inflammation or drainage may indicate infection. Report objective findings, including any fever or chills reported by the patient. Module 8.
Question 162The patient reports a newly absent thrill just before treatment. What is your first action?
A change in thrill needs assessment before use. The supervising clinician decides the next step. Module 8.
Question 163During treatment, new swelling and pain develop near an AV access needle. What should you do?
Swelling and pain can indicate infiltration or another access problem. The nurse must assess promptly, and trainees should avoid unassigned manipulation. Module 8.
Question 164You see heavy bleeding from an access site. What is the best response?
Heavy access bleeding can become life threatening. Summon help immediately and support the authorized response. Module 8.
Question 165Which handoff most accurately reports the opening-case concern?
A useful handoff distinguishes the patient report from a clinical diagnosis and states timing, the hold, and who needs to assess next. Module 8.
Question 166During the pre-treatment check you hear a high-pitched, whistling bruit over a fistula that usually has a low whoosh. What should you do?
A change to a high-pitched or whistling sound can mean stenosis and is reported before treatment. Module 8.
Question 167Why is tape never wrapped in a full circle around the access arm?
Anything that squeezes the arm slows flow, and slow flow can clot the access. Module 8.
Question 168A catheter patient arrives with one cap missing from a lumen. What is the correct response?
An uncapped lumen risks air entry, bleeding, and infection, and catheter handling is a nursing task. Module 8.
Question 169When holding pressure after needle removal, how firm should the pressure be?
Pressure that stops all flow can clot the access; peeking or pressing on a needle still in the vessel causes harm. Module 8.
Question 170During treatment a patient reports burning and you see a firm swelling at the venous needle. What is this most likely, and what do you do?
Swelling and pain at a needle suggest blood entering the tissue, which the nurse must assess at once. Module 8.
Question 171In your report of an absent thrill, why do you include when the patient last felt the usual buzz?
The last known normal time helps the team judge how quickly it must act. Module 8.
Question 172Which is an appropriate pair for verifying identity before dialysis when approved by the facility?
Use two person-specific identifiers and match them to the order and care record. The station and schedule identify a location and time. Module 9.
Question 173Which source defines the treatment parameters to be verified before the session?
The current order is the source of the prescribed treatment. Verify any discrepancy through the authorized clinician. Module 9.
Question 174Today’s repeated pre-treatment weight differs markedly from the patient’s recent trend. What does a consistent repeat measurement establish?
Confirm scale, units, patient identity and measurement conditions as assigned. The verified current weight is a data point for clinical review, not a new prescription. Module 9.
Question 175You confirm a verified pre-treatment weight remains unexpectedly high. What should you do before applying treatment settings?
The nurse or authorized clinician reviews the unexpected result and confirms the treatment plan. A technician reports the measurement and avoids independent changes. Module 9.
Question 176A medication listed on a previous record is absent from today’s order. Someone asks you to prepare it as usual. What should you do?
Medication preparation, administration and changes depend on a verified order, legal scope and facility authorization. Module 9.
Question 177What is the term for observations made while the dialysis treatment is running?
Intradialytic observations are made during treatment, such as assigned vital signs, symptoms and equipment or access observations. Module 9.
Question 178Why record the actual time of an assigned vital sign and the treatment phase?
Timing gives the value clinical context and supports comparison across the treatment record. Module 9.
Question 179The machine has not alarmed. The patient reports dizziness and appears pale. Which interpretation is most appropriate?
Machine alarms respond to monitored device conditions. They cannot replace patient observation or timely reporting of symptoms. Module 9.
Question 180A patient becomes lightheaded and an assigned blood pressure reading is markedly lower than the initial value. What should you do?
Symptoms with a substantial blood pressure change warrant prompt nursing assessment. Relay facts and follow authorized instructions. Module 9.
Question 181You are authorized to assist with specimen checks. A specimen label has the correct name but a different record number. What should you do?
A mismatched identifier makes the specimen identity uncertain. Reconcile it through policy before sending or relying on it. Module 9.
Question 182Which measurements should be distinguished when documenting a session?
The pre- and post-treatment weights are measurements. The target is a clinical goal and should remain identifiable as such. Module 9.
Question 183A session ended early. Why should the record show the actual treatment duration and the reason reported to the nurse?
The difference between planned and delivered treatment is relevant to clinical review, continuity and documentation. Module 9.
Question 184The patient has left and visible soil was removed. The routine disinfectant wet contact time is still underway. Is the station ready?
The station is released only after the approved cleaning and disinfection requirements are completed. Module 9.
Question 185After disconnecting, the patient reports dizziness while seated. The next patient has arrived. What should you do?
A new symptom requires nursing assessment. Patient safety takes priority and routine turnover waits until the station is vacated safely. Module 9.
Question 186Which end-of-treatment handoff is most useful?
State what happened, when, who responded and what remains outstanding. The receiving clinician can confirm the pending action. Module 9.
Question 187An alarm sounds. What do you look at first?
Patient first, access and lines second, screen third. Module 9.
Question 188The venous pressure reading drops suddenly during treatment. What must you do at once?
A fall in venous pressure can mean the line has separated or the needle has come out. Module 9.
Question 189The air detector alarms and the venous clamp closes. Which action is correct?
The clamp is what protects the patient from air. Module 9.
Question 190Three machines alarm for low water supply at the same time. What does that suggest?
One machine or several is the question that separates a machine fault from a supply fault. Module 9.
Question 191A venous pressure alarm keeps repeating. A colleague suggests widening the limits. Why is that unsafe?
Limits are a protection; the cause of the alarm has to be found. Module 9.
Question 192What does intradialytic hypotension describe?
Intradialytic hypotension is low blood pressure during dialysis. A symptomatic fall needs prompt clinical review under the facility’s procedure. Module 10.
Question 193Which observation is a vascular access emergency?
Heavy access bleeding can be life-threatening. Initiate the unit’s emergency escalation immediately. Module 10.
Question 194The machine has no active alarm, but a patient reports new chest pain and difficulty breathing. What does the quiet alarm panel tell you?
A machine monitors defined equipment conditions. New chest pain and breathlessness require immediate clinical attention whether or not the machine signals an alarm. Module 10.
Question 195A patient becomes lightheaded and develops cramps. An assigned measurement shows a major blood pressure drop from baseline. Which concern should you report?
Report the symptoms, pressure, timing and trend. The supervising clinician assesses the cause and directs treatment. Module 10.
Question 196A patient with a dialysis catheter has new chills and drainage near the exit site. Which concern should you raise?
Chills and new exit-site drainage can accompany an access infection. Report both promptly without assigning a diagnosis. Module 10.
Question 197You find that a venous needle has moved and the patient is bleeding. No alarm is sounding. What should you do?
Dislodgement can cause rapid blood loss. Call for help based on the observed hazard and assist under the emergency procedure within your training. Module 10.
Question 198What is the purpose of a TeamSTEPPS call-out?
A call-out shares critical information quickly with the team at the point of care. Include the location and immediate concern. Module 10.
Question 199What is a check-back?
The receiver repeats the instruction, and the sender confirms or corrects it. This closes the communication loop. Module 10.
Question 200Which initial call-out is most useful in the opening case?
A concise call-out states the urgency, location and observed problem so the team can act quickly. Module 10.
Question 201The nurse assigns you to call the emergency team while another colleague stays at the station. What should you do?
Acknowledge and complete the assigned task promptly. Closing the loop lets the team know it has been completed. Module 10.
Question 202Once immediate care is underway, which documentation entry best supports handoff and review?
Record the chronology, symptoms, escalation, assigned actions and follow-up objectively after urgent care needs are addressed. Module 10.
Question 203The emergency drill exposed confusion over who calls for help. What should your team contribute during debrief?
Debrief uses observed events to identify what worked and what the team can practice or improve before the next drill. Module 10.
Question 204Several stations on one water loop show an unexpected interruption. Why does the nurse or lead need to know which stations and patients are affected?
The scope of a disruption informs both clinical priorities and technical containment. Report affected stations and whether treatments are active. Module 10.
Question 205A power interruption occurs while multiple patients are receiving treatment. What should a trainee do?
Notify clinical leadership quickly. Conditions across machines may differ, so the trainee reports status and carries out assigned contingency actions. Module 10.
Question 206Water service returns after a disruption. Before preparing a station for treatment, what should you verify?
Utility restoration is an input to recovery. Required water and equipment checks and authorized release must occur before use. Module 10.
Question 207A patient has a seizure in the dialysis chair. Which first action is within the technician’s scope?
The technician calls, protects from injury, and times the event; nothing goes in the mouth and the circuit is the nurse’s. Module 10.
Question 208After a suspected dialyzer reaction, what should happen to the used dialyzer and its package?
The team may need the dialyzer type, lot, and circuit to find the cause. Module 10.
Question 209During the daily cart check the oxygen gauge reads below the minimum your unit sets. What do you do?
A cart item that fails its check is corrected immediately, not later. Module 10.
Question 210You are assigned as recorder during a code. Which practice is correct?
A code record is a time log made as events happen, on a single clock. Module 10.
Question 211In an emergency clamp-and-disconnect, what happens to fistula needles?
Removing needles takes time and needs pressure on each site; in an evacuation they stay in until the patient is safe. Module 10.
Question 212The power fails and machines switch to battery. Which technician action is correct in the first five minutes?
The technician observes, reports, and brings equipment; the nurse decides what happens to each treatment. Module 10.
Question 213Which entry in your written account after an event is a fact, not an opinion?
Accounts record what was seen and when; causes are worked out in the review. Module 10.
Question 214What does the label ‘single use only’ mean for the dialyzer?
Product labeling and instructions determine whether the device is intended for single use. A single-use dialyzer is discarded under the facility process after use. Module 11.
Question 215When reuse is authorized, for whom may the previously used dialyzer be prepared?
An authorized dialyzer reuse program is patient-specific. A similar prescription or shared station does not establish patient identity. Module 11.
Question 216The label permits multiple use, but the facility runs a single-use program. What does the model’s label establish?
A multiple-use designation is one prerequisite. Local policy, applicable requirements, and a validated program govern whether reuse occurs. Module 11.
Question 217What does an acceptable documented residual disinfectant check indicate?
The result addresses a specific chemical safety check. Identity, integrity and all other required criteria need separate verification. Module 11.
Question 218Stock is low and a coworker suggests reprocessing a dialyzer already used once even though the label specifies single use. What should you do?
A stock shortage does not change product labeling. The supervisor can arrange an authorized replacement or alternate plan. Module 11.
Question 219What makes a reprocessed dialyzer traceable?
Traceability connects the specific device and patient with processing history and outcome. The unit defines the required identifiers and checks. Module 11.
Question 220A dialyzer has a recorded acceptable integrity result, but its patient identifier is unreadable. Which requirement remains unmet?
A device test and a verified patient match address different risks. Acceptable test results cannot resolve an unreadable identifier. Module 11.
Question 221An authorized reviewer has put a dialyzer on hold because its record is incomplete. What does hold mean?
A hold prevents use while the concern is reviewed. The applicable policy defines further evaluation, rejection or authorized disposition. Module 11.
Question 222At preparation, a reprocessed dialyzer label and the chart disagree. Recorded technical checks are acceptable. What should you do?
Prevent use and preserve the record so the authorized team can investigate. A technician should never infer or recreate an uncertain identity. Module 11.
Question 223Which entry best documents the mismatch for follow-up in the authorized log?
The note identifies the item, problem, hold status, notification and pending disposition. Record patient identifiers only through the approved record process. Module 11.
Question 224Which document identifies the approved way to operate a specific model of dialysis machine?
Operation and maintenance must follow the applicable model instructions and authorized facility procedures. Module 11.
Question 225Why does a preventive maintenance record matter separately from a successful pre-use check?
Preventive maintenance and pre-use verification address different tasks and timing. Each needs its own documentation under the facility program. Module 11.
Question 226A machine shows the same safety fault twice during required checks. What should you do within your role?
Prevent patient exposure to equipment with an unresolved failure. Capture the asset and fault details so qualified staff can investigate. Module 11.
Question 227You receive verbal word that a held machine is probably repaired. What should you verify before making it available?
The return-to-service process verifies completion of required technical and clinical readiness steps. A tentative verbal update does not establish release. Module 11.
Question 228A second shift needs to know why a machine remains unavailable. Which message best supports the handoff?
Communicate the equipment identity, observed problem, action taken and outstanding responsibility. This keeps the next team from releasing equipment prematurely. Module 11.
Question 229A dialyzer’s original total cell volume was 120 mL. Today it measures 93 mL. What is the correct decision?
93 divided by 120 is about 78 percent, which is below the 80 percent limit. Module 11.
Question 230In automated reprocessing, which task still depends entirely on a person?
No machine verifies identity; it will reprocess the wrong dialyzer perfectly. Module 11.
Question 231Why is water pushed backward across the membrane during rinsing?
Reverse rinsing dislodges clots; too much pressure can tear fibers. Module 11.
Question 232Before rinsing a stored dialyzer at the chair, the germicide presence test is negative. What does that mean?
No germicide in storage means no assurance of disinfection. Module 11.
Question 233On one morning, every dialyzer fails the automated volume test. What is the most sensible first action?
A fault shared by every dialyzer points to the equipment. Module 11.
Question 234Which patient’s dialyzer is not reprocessed?
Dialyzers from patients with hepatitis B are not reprocessed. Module 11.
Question 235In semi-automated reprocessing, which step is most often missed?
Hand steps that sit between automatic steps are easy to forget. Module 11.
Question 236Germicide splashes into a technician’s eye. What comes first?
Flushing within seconds limits the injury; reporting follows. Module 11.
Question 237Which information should you compare before matching a patient to a dialysis order?
Two person-specific identifiers tie the patient to the correct record and treatment order. Location and schedule do not establish identity. Module 12.
Question 238What does a dialysis water log record?
A usable log links results to the sampling location, time and required response. It lets staff verify whether the affected system is cleared. Module 12.
Question 239The station is clean and the equipment checks passed, but a required water result is marked pending. How should this affect readiness?
Cleanliness, equipment readiness and water quality are separate checks. Pending water verification prevents authorized release of affected use. Module 12.
Question 240At shift start, the water check remains unresolved and a patient identifier does not match the order. What is the safest response?
Both discrepancies need resolution through the appropriate pathways. Neither can be cleared using information from the other. Module 12.
Question 241A machine passes its self-test, but the station disinfectant has not reached the required wet contact time. What should the trainee do?
Machine and infection-control checks are independent. Complete the required disinfection process before introducing the patient or clean supplies. Module 12.
Question 242Which communication technique has the receiver repeat an instruction and the sender confirm or correct it?
A check-back uses confirmation between team members to verify that the instruction was understood. Module 12.
Question 243Which report gives the receiving nurse an actionable situation and request?
A concise handoff names the affected station, identifies each observed exception, states the hold and requests action. Module 12.
Question 244A preceptor says to hold a station until authorized release. The trainee repeats the instruction, but the preceptor has not confirmed. What remains incomplete?
A repeat is followed by the sender’s confirmation or correction. Continue following the hold while clarifying the instruction. Module 12.
Question 245During a mock treatment, the patient reports sudden chest pain and breathlessness. No alarm is active. What should you do first?
Urgent symptoms require immediate escalation. State the location and observations, then assist within your assigned emergency role. Module 12.
Question 246The nurse asks you to notify the water lead while the nurse verifies the patient identifiers. What action best closes the handoff?
A handoff is useful when responsibility is explicit and follow-through is reported. Each unresolved hold remains active until appropriately cleared. Module 12.
Question 247What is a near miss?
A near miss reveals a hazard or process weakness even when intervention prevented patient exposure. Module 12.
Question 248Which proposed outcome would best show whether a new pre-use water-readiness checklist is being followed?
This measures the intended behavior with a defined denominator and can be compared across audits. Module 12.
Question 249A learner passed the written quiz but missed a required critical step in the simulated emergency station. What does that mean for skills sign-off?
Written knowledge and observed performance are distinct. Capstone sign-off requires evidence that the learner can complete required critical steps. Module 12.
Question 250A trainee has observed a procedure but has not been signed off for it. A colleague asks them to perform it without the assigned supervisor. What should the trainee do?
Observation records exposure. Authorization for supervised or independent performance requires the applicable competency and facility process. Module 12.
Question 251The identifier mismatch is caught before treatment. Which entry and follow-up best support improvement?
Objective chronology supports review of how the issue reached the station, how it was caught and which process could be tested for improvement. Module 12.
Question 252In the opening walk-through, why are the water room and chlorine test done before stations are set up?
Checks with the widest effect come first. Module 12.
Question 253The total chlorine test reads 0.3 mg/L against a limit of 0.1 mg/L. A repeat test reads lower. What do you record?
Every result is recorded, and a better second test does not cancel a failed first one. Module 12.
Question 254A patient’s target weight is 58.0 kg and the confirmed pre-weight is 63.4 kg. What is the gain above target weight?
63.4 minus 58.0 equals 5.4 kg, about 5,400 mL. Module 12.
Question 255A low venous pressure alarm sounds and the patient is dozing under a blanket. What is your first move?
A fall in venous pressure can mean a dislodged needle bleeding out of sight. Module 12.
Question 256At a practical station you perform two of three criteria satisfactorily and miss the third. What is the station result?
A station is satisfactory only when every criterion is, and stations are not averaged. Module 12.
Question 257On the self-rating grid, which description matches “performs independently”?
Independence means no prompts and an understanding of the purpose of each step. Module 12.
Question 258You were signed off on one machine model and the unit installs a different model. What should you do?
Competence is specific to the task and the machine model. Module 12.
