Before you begin

How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Vascular Surgeon series. Each sitting draws 40 questions at random from a bank of 100, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark32 of 40 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.
What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

How to get your certificate of completion

  1. Pass all 20 module quizzes. Each module quiz is passed at 80%, or at the pass mark the module states. The Certificate checklist above the examination shows which modules are done; each line links to that module's quiz.
  2. Use the same device and browser for the modules and this examination. Progress is saved in your browser, not in an account. On iPhone and iPad, the Home Screen app and Safari keep separate progress.
  3. Pass this timed examination (80%; where these instructions say so, every safety-critical question must also be correct). You have one minute per question from the moment you tap Start the examination; only your first check of a set counts. If you pass before finishing the modules, your pass is saved: finish the module quizzes, then come back here.
  4. Type your name the way you want it printed and tap Make my certificate. You can tick Add a preceptor sign-off line if your unit will sign the printed copy.
  5. Save or print your completion packet: two pages, the certificate and a record of your module results. Use Print or save as PDF to keep both pages together.
  6. Be online when you make it, so the certificate gets its QR code. Anyone can scan the code, or enter it at renalcarematters.com/verify, to confirm it.

Not finished yet? Use Print a progress record in the checklist to show a preceptor how far you have gone; it is clearly marked "not a certificate". The certificate is a self-study certificate of completion, not CPD units: competence is demonstrated on your own unit and signed off by a preceptor. To make the QR code work, your name, track, date and score are saved with the certificate code; to have them removed, use Report a correction on any guide.

Examination

End-of-course examination

Question 1A surgeon receives an access referral with no urgency information. What should happen first?
Triage information decides whether routine scheduling is safe. An acknowledgment alone resolves nothing clinically. Module 1.
Question 2Who authorizes a surgeon to perform independent access surgery after completing this track?
Learning completion cannot grant operative authority. Privileges come from the hospital where the surgeon operates. Module 1.
Question 3Under the Philippine Society of Nephrology (PSN) 2024 hemodialysis guidelines, what does Section 1.2 b3 assign to the attending nephrologist?
The clause makes access a coordinated responsibility. It does not credential a surgeon or transfer operative decisions. Module 1.
Question 4A patient’s fistula clotted this morning and the session was abandoned. Who decides whether he needs dialysis tonight?
Thrombosis is a dialysis-safety problem first. The nephrologist owns the dialysis decision while the surgeon owns salvage. Module 1.
Question 5Which statement is an actionable surgical recommendation to a dialysis unit?
An actionable recommendation names what is restricted, what to watch for, and who to call and where to go. Module 1.
Question 6What belongs in an individualized access plan?
The Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (KDOQI) Life-Plan links the current decision to the patient’s whole treatment course. Module 2.
Question 7A patient worries about repeated travel to the hospital. What is the best response?
A workable shared decision weighs burdens without letting one concern replace the full assessment. Module 2.
Question 8A meta-analysis of cohort studies found higher mortality with catheters than with fistulas. What is the most accurate interpretation?
The data are observational; confounding by illness and unplanned starts is likely. Module 2.
Question 9What share of new fistulas failed primarily in the Al-Jaishi meta-analysis?
Primary failure was 23% (95% confidence interval 18% to 28%). Module 2.
Question 10UK Kidney Association (UKKA) 2025 statement 1.2 recommends treating access choice as what?
Statement 1.2 (grade 1C) frames access choice as the patient’s decision, with team support and time to consider. Module 2.
Question 11Which history is especially relevant before choosing an arm for access?
Prior central instrumentation and device leads can obstruct outflow and change the plan. Module 3.
Question 12A patient has unilateral arm swelling and a large superficial vein on the same side. What should happen?
Superficial appearance does not resolve an outflow warning sign; image the central veins or choose the other side. Module 3.
Question 13In the El Ters case-control study, a previous peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) was associated with what?
The adjusted odds ratio was 2.8; the design shows association, not proof of causation in each case. Module 3.
Question 14Why record a baseline hand examination before access creation?
Without a baseline, a later sensory or motor deficit cannot be judged as new or old. Module 3.
Question 15UKKA 2025 suggests central vein imaging before access creation in which patients?
Statement 2.5 (grade 2C) targets imaging to those with signs or risk factors. Module 3.
Question 16What makes a mapping report useful for access selection?
The report must answer the clinical question, including whether the access can be needled. Module 4.
Question 17Two mapping reports disagree about a vein’s diameter. What is the best next action?
Method (tourniquet, temperature) changes diameter; resolve consequential uncertainty first. Module 4.
Question 18In the Ferring randomized trial, routine preoperative ultrasound most clearly reduced what?
Immediate failure fell; assisted primary survival at one year also improved, while primary survival did not differ significantly. Module 4.
Question 19UKKA 2025 statement 3.3 recommends what when a vessel is under 2.0 mm?
It is a counseling threshold (grade 1C), not an absolute contraindication. Module 4.
Question 20Why does UKKA recommend favoring distal sites first?
Statement 3.2 (grade 1B) preserves future options, accepting a higher primary failure rate distally. Module 4.
Question 21A patient who missed dialysis arrives for elective access surgery with a high potassium and electrocardiographic changes. What is the best action?
An elective benefit does not remove an immediate physiologic threat. Module 5.
Question 22Which perioperative medication plan is appropriate?
Medication and bleeding decisions depend on the patient and the procedure. Module 5.
Question 23In the Aitken randomized trial, what did brachial plexus block improve compared with local anesthesia?
The surrogate outcome of three-month primary patency improved; longer-term outcomes were not tested. Module 5.
Question 24Why does regional anesthesia plausibly help a new fistula?
Vasodilation raises early flow, which may reduce early thrombosis. Module 5.
Question 25UKKA 2025 statement 3.8 suggests avoiding what during the perioperative and maturation period?
Low pressure lowers fistula flow; the statement is graded 2C. Module 5.
Question 26A complete operative handoff should include what?
The dialysis pathway needs actionable information after the operation. Module 6.
Question 27Unexpected anatomy requires an alternative beyond the consent and the learner’s competence. What is the best response?
Consent, competence and privileges constrain a non-emergency change of plan. Module 6.
Question 28In the Dialysis Access Consortium (DAC) clopidogrel trial, what happened to suitability of fistulas for dialysis?
Thrombosis fell from 19.5% to 12.2%, but failure to attain suitability was 61.8% versus 59.5%. Module 6.
Question 29What did the Hemodialysis Fistula Maturation (HFM) study report as the median time from creation to maturation?
Median 115 days, with wide variation by whether the patient was already on dialysis. Module 6.
Question 30Which mechanism narrows a new fistula from inside?
Neointimal hyperplasia, often at the swing segment, competes with outward remodeling. Module 6.
Question 31Why might a graft be considered for a patient?
Selection rests on the patient’s feasible pathway and the trade-offs. Module 7.
Question 32When may a graft first be cannulated?
Device design and tissue healing determine first use. Module 7.
Question 33Where do most graft stenoses form?
Compliance mismatch and turbulence drive neointimal hyperplasia at the venous end. Module 7.
Question 34In the Dialysis Access Consortium (DAC) graft trial, dipyridamole plus aspirin changed one-year primary unassisted patency by how much?
A modest effect (hazard ratio 0.82); most grafts still lost primary patency within a year. Module 7.
Question 35According to UKKA 2025 statement 3.4, when may a graft be considered over a fistula in adults?
Statement 3.4 is graded 1C. Module 7.
Question 36New breathlessness and hypotension follow poor function of a recently exchanged catheter. What is the best response?
Clinical deterioration changes the task from troubleshooting to possible insertion-complication management. Module 8.
Question 37What should accompany every catheter indication?
Catheters belong inside the patient’s ongoing access strategy. Module 8.
Question 38UKKA 2025 recommends which vein as the routine first choice for a tunneled catheter?
Statement 5.1 (grade 1C); the subclavian route is avoided where alternatives exist (5.2). Module 8.
Question 39What does UKKA recommend as initial treatment for catheter dysfunction?
Statement 6.2 (grade 1C); exchange follows if thrombolytics fail (6.3). Module 8.
Question 40Why do subclavian catheters particularly threaten future arm access?
Wall injury where the vein bends and is compressed leads to stenosis that later obstructs arm access outflow. Module 8.
Question 41A fistula is six weeks old and has a thrill. Is that enough to authorize first use?
Calendar age and patency do not alone establish safe cannulation. Module 9.
Question 42Which routine cannulation strategy does KDOQI favor for most fistulas?
KDOQI favors rope-ladder and reserves buttonhole for selected patients, given infection concerns. Module 9.
Question 43In Robbin’s study, what share of fistulas meeting both diameter of at least 4 mm and flow of at least 500 mL/min were adequate for dialysis?
95% when both criteria were met, versus 33% when neither was. Module 9.
Question 44What did the Dialysis Outcomes and Practice Patterns Study (DOPPS) associate with a higher risk of later fistula failure?
Relative risk 2.1 for cannulation within 14 days; observational evidence. Module 9.
Question 45UKKA recommends investigation when non-maturity persists beyond how many weeks?
Statement 3.9 (grade 1C): assess between 2 and 6 weeks and investigate non-maturity beyond 6. Module 9.
Question 46A stenosis is seen incidentally in an access with stable clinical function. What is the best interpretation?
An anatomic finding alone does not establish an indication. Module 10.
Question 47Which is the most useful dysfunction referral?
A defined clinical question helps select and prioritize investigation. Module 10.
Question 48An access is pulsatile, does not collapse when the arm is raised, and needle sites bleed for a long time. Where is the stenosis most likely?
Outflow obstruction traps arterial pressure upstream. Module 10.
Question 49In the meta-analysis of 14 trials, what did preemptive correction of stenosis do?
Access loss risk ratio 0.81 (confidence interval crossed 1); thrombosis risk ratio 0.79. Module 10.
Question 50UKKA 2025 statement 4.2 recommends intervention for which lesions?
Grade 1B; both anatomic and clinical significance are required. Module 10.
Question 51Before placing a device across a venous junction, what should be reviewed?
A technically successful intervention can consume later options. Module 11.
Question 52What is the most meaningful question after treatment?
Functional benefit and patient experience complement technical outcome. Module 11.
Question 53In the Haskal trial for graft venous anastomotic stenosis, what was six-month treatment-area patency?
Circuit patency was 38% versus 20%; most circuits still needed more procedures. Module 11.
Question 54Which is a limitation of the drug-coated balloon trial in fistulas?
Target-lesion patency at six months is a surrogate; the sponsor was the manufacturer. Module 11.
Question 55Why do stenoses recur after balloon angioplasty?
Injury from dilation triggers the same healing response that caused the lesion. Module 11.
Question 56A patient with a thrombosed graft is breathless after a missed session. What is the priority?
The immediate threat and access rescue need parallel coordination. Module 12.
Question 57A salvage plan should also address what?
Restoring flow without fixing the cause or planning dialysis leaves the problem unresolved. Module 12.
Question 58What is the commonest cause of graft thrombosis?
Hyperplasia at the venous end gradually lowers flow until the graft clots. Module 12.
Question 59The meta-analysis of randomized trials comparing surgical and endovascular thrombectomy found what?
Six trials, 573 grafts; fistula evidence was absent. Module 12.
Question 60Where should a bridge catheter go for a patient trying to save a left-arm graft?
Choose a site that protects the access arm’s central outflow. Module 12.
Question 61New severe hand pain and weakness follow access creation, but a thrill is present. What is the best action?
Access flow does not show adequate hand perfusion or exclude nerve injury. Module 13.
Question 62Which finding most clearly raises the urgency of assessment?
New functional loss can mean a time-critical limb or nerve problem. Module 13.
Question 63What best explains access-related hand ischemia?
Steal is common and often harmless; low distal pressure causes symptoms. Module 13.
Question 64Ischemia within 24 hours of routine access surgery is most strongly associated with what?
In the systematic review, 88% of acute cases followed non-autogenous access. Module 13.
Question 65How does UKKA 2025 suggest mild steal be managed?
Statement 4.9, grade 2C. Module 13.
Question 66An aneurysmal area has ulcerated skin and recent bleeding. What is the best response?
Skin threat and recent bleeding mean impending rupture. Module 14.
Question 67Major access bleeding begins in a simulation. What is the first response?
Hemorrhage control and emergency response come before anything else. Module 14.
Question 68Where did most fatal access hemorrhages occur in the forensic series of 100 deaths?
81% of the hemorrhages occurred at home, so home teaching is essential. Module 14.
Question 69Which technique best controls bleeding from an access needle site?
Focused pressure concentrates force on the defect. Module 14.
Question 70Rapid aneurysm growth with rising venous pressures suggests what?
Downstream narrowing raises intra-access pressure; treat it with the aneurysm. Module 14.
Question 71A patient with a draining graft is hypotensive. What is the best response?
Systemic instability needs urgent coordinated care. Module 15.
Question 72What should happen when microbiology results become available?
Stewardship includes reassessment, dialysis dosing and effective transitions. Module 15.
Question 73Why is an infected synthetic graft often removed rather than treated with antibiotics alone?
Synthetic material cannot clear infection as living tissue can. Module 15.
Question 74In LOCK IT-100, what were the catheter-related bloodstream infection (CRBSI) rates with taurolidine-heparin versus heparin?
Hazard ratio 0.29; local availability must be checked. Module 15.
Question 75UKKA 2025 suggests favoring catheter replacement for bacteremia with which features?
Statement 6.6, grade 2C. Module 15.
Question 76Recurrent breathlessness in a patient with an upper-arm access should lead to what?
A plausible relationship needs evaluation before an irreversible decision. Module 16.
Question 77How should an asymptomatic central venous stenosis on imaging be interpreted?
UKKA 7.3 (grade 2C) suggests conservative management when asymptomatic. Module 16.
Question 78In Basile’s study, access flow of what level predicted high-output heart failure most accurately?
Sensitivity 89%, specificity 100% in a small single-center cohort; a prompt, not a diagnosis. Module 16.
Question 79Why does an access increase cardiac work?
The shunt bypasses arteriolar resistance; output rises to maintain pressure. Module 16.
Question 80Which test helps predict whether flow reduction will help a patient with suspected high-output failure?
A change on compression supports an access contribution. Module 16.
Question 81Repeated access failures should trigger what?
Prior procedures and failures change the next choice. Module 17.
Question 82When local expertise is insufficient for a complex reconstruction, what is appropriate?
Capability and rescue capacity belong in the access strategy. Module 17.
Question 83What did the Canadian cohort find about second fistulas?
Second fistulas did worse on catheter-free use; outcomes were hard to predict. Module 17.
Question 84Which option spares the veins entirely for a patient with exhausted upper-limb access?
Peritoneal dialysis avoids vascular access if the abdomen and home support allow. Module 17.
Question 85UKKA 2025 statement 4.1 suggests managing access complications how?
Grade 2C. Module 17.
Question 86A fistula-review patient reports new rest pain and a toe wound. What is the best response?
Appointment labels cannot replace assessment of a serious vascular problem. Module 18.
Question 87What does completing this track establish about transplant surgery?
Distinct procedures need their own training and hospital privileges. Module 18.
Question 88Why may the ankle–brachial index mislead in dialysis patients?
Medial calcification is common in kidney failure; toe pressures or imaging help. Module 18.
Question 89A dialysis patient still passes 600 mL of urine daily and needs angiography. What is the best approach?
Residual function is worth protecting; minimize nephrotoxic exposure. Module 18.
Question 90What does the Philippine Association of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery (PATACSI) directory of accredited programs establish?
It is a verification tool, not a complete eligibility standard. Module 18.
Question 91An official classification table includes an access procedure. What does that establish?
Classification, payment and accreditation are separate questions. Module 19.
Question 92Which outcome report is more transparent?
Clear definitions make results interpretable. Module 19.
Question 93Under the Society for Vascular Surgery (SVS) reporting standards, what distinguishes secondary from primary patency?
Primary patency ends at any intervention or thrombosis; secondary ends at abandonment. Module 19.
Question 94Why should a small service show confidence intervals with its infection rate?
Uncertainty is large with few events; show counts too. Module 19.
Question 95A rate per 1,000 catheter-days and a National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN) rate per 100 patient-months are what?
Different denominators and definitions. Module 19.
Question 96What closes a referral handoff?
The pathway requires ownership and follow-through. Module 20.
Question 97A learner scores highly but misses an emergency safety action. What happens?
Knowledge scores cannot compensate for unaddressed critical errors. Module 20.
Question 98What authority does an access coordinator hold?
The coordinator owns tracking, not surgical or dialysis decisions. Module 20.
Question 99How does the Academy relate to Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) competency frameworks?
Principles are used; equivalence is not claimed. Module 20.
Question 100Which is the best measure for a service change meant to speed access creation?
A measure tied to the gap, reported transparently, shows whether the change worked. Module 20.
