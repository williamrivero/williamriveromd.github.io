Course for the people who own and govern hemodialysis services

This module is study material for owners and governing boards of adult maintenance hemodialysis services. The owner supplies authority, resources, and assurance; clinical decisions belong to the attending nephrologist and the clinical head, nursing assignments to the nursing lead, and technical release to the designated technician. Regulations and payer rules change: confirm each one against the current issuance. Every case, person, and peso figure is fictional.

Learning objectives

After this module you should be able to

  • Qualify a substitute product through registration, technical, clinical, training, and traceability gates.
  • Manage related-party suppliers through declaration, recusal, and equal criteria.
  • Calculate stock runway and reorder points, and act early when a shortage becomes a clinical decision.
  • Trace a recalled lot to every affected patient within an hour.

The big picture, in plain words

In plain wordsBuying for a dialysis unit is like buying parts for an airplane, not for a car. A cheaper part that fits is not good enough; it must be certified for that aircraft, the mechanics must know it, and you must be able to find every plane it went into if it fails.

The picture has a limit. An airline can ground a plane. A unit cannot ground a patient, who needs the next session in two days. That is why supply planning in dialysis looks ahead in weeks, and why a shortage becomes a clinical decision early.

Figure 1 · Key terms for this module

QualificationThe checks a new product passes before use on patients.
Related partyA supplier connected to an owner, leader, or their family.
RunwayDays of stock left before the next verified delivery.
Reorder pointThe stock level that triggers an order: daily use × lead time + safety stock.
Lot traceabilityRecording which lot of a product was used for which patient.

Your work is to buy honestly, plan ahead, and be able to find every patient who received a faulty product.

Opening case · fictional

Case: The cheaper dialyzer

Collections at a unit in Tarlac City are six weeks behind. The stockroom says the current dialyzer runs out in three days, and the usual supplier can deliver in five. Mr. Jerome Uy, a supplier and the owner’s nephew, offers a different brand that is 120 pesos cheaper per unit and can arrive tomorrow. The technical lead has never seen it.

Before reading on, decide:

  • What is the gap, in days and treatments?
  • What must be true before the new dialyzer is used?
  • How is the family tie handled?

Keep your answers in mind. The lesson gives you what you need, and the case review near the end shows one sound response.

Lesson · part 1

A cheaper substitute must pass five gates

A dialyzer, bloodline, or concentrate touches a patient’s blood. Before any new product is used, it passes five gates: registration with the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) of the Philippines for this use; technical compatibility with your machines, confirmed by the technical lead; clinical review of its specifications against your patients’ prescriptions by the clinical head; staff training; and lot traceability in your records.

Price is a reason to start qualifying a product, not a reason to skip a gate. A product that arrives tomorrow cannot be qualified by tomorrow without cutting corners.

Stop and reportNo unqualified product is used on a patient, however short the stock or cheap the offer.
RememberFive gates, every time, for every new product that touches blood.
Lesson · part 2

When the supplier is family

Incentives around supplies and drugs can shape practice. In United States data, for-profit facilities gave more of a highly reimbursed drug than nonprofit facilities, an association that shows how purchasing and payment can drive clinical patterns.1 A related-party supplier adds a personal incentive to a financial one.

Handle it as Module 5 described: the related person declares the tie in writing, steps out of the decision, and the product and price are evaluated by others on the same criteria as any supplier. The board minutes record how the decision was made.

RememberFamily suppliers are allowed. Hidden family suppliers are not.
Lesson · part 3

Runway and reorder points

Know how many days each critical item will last. Reorder point = average daily use × lead time + safety stock. When a supplier warns of a delay, recalculate with the longer lead time the same day.

Worked example (fictional). The unit uses 20 dialyzers a day. Stock lasts three days; the verified delivery arrives on day five. The gap is two days, about 40 treatments. Paying more for verified stock from the usual supplier, or borrowing from a partner unit, is almost always cheaper than transferring 40 treatments, and it carries no clinical risk from an unqualified product.

If a gap cannot be closed, it becomes a clinical decision: the attending nephrologists decide which patients can safely have a shorter or delayed session and which cannot. The owner’s job is to see it coming early enough that this rarely happens.

RememberRecalculate the reorder point the day a supplier warns of delay.
Lesson · part 4

One-hour recall

When a product is recalled, the unit must find every patient who received the affected lot. That is only possible if the lot number was recorded at each session. Test it: once a quarter, pick a lot at random and time how long it takes to list the patients. The aim is under an hour.

RememberIf you cannot find the patients, you cannot protect them. Test your recall trace every quarter.
Case review

Back to the case: The cheaper dialyzer

The gap. Two days, about 40 treatments at 20 a day.

Before use. The new dialyzer would need FDA registration for this use, technical compatibility, clinical review, staff training, and traceability. That cannot be done safely by tomorrow. Close the gap with verified stock: pay for partial early delivery, borrow from a partner unit, and ask the nephrologists to prioritize only if a gap remains.

The family tie. The owner declares it, steps out, and the product is evaluated later on equal criteria. It may become a good second supplier, after qualification.

Skills lab · with your preceptor

Skills checklist: Supplier scorecard, conflict declaration, and recall trace

Build a supplier scorecard, complete a conflict declaration, and run a timed recall trace. Your preceptor rates each step as satisfactory, needs practice, or not observed. Steps marked Critical must all be satisfactory before sign-off. This checklist supports supervised learning; your unit’s own procedure and competency form are the authority. A printable version of every checklist is on the trainer page.

  • Lists the five qualification gates and who owns each.
  • CriticalPrevents use of any unqualified product that touches blood.
  • CriticalRequires written declaration and recusal for related-party suppliers.
  • Calculates runway and the reorder point at the usual and delayed lead time.
  • Compares the full cost of options, including transfers and clinical risk.
  • Completes a recall trace for one lot and records the time taken.
Placement activity. Pick one lot number from last month and time how long the unit takes to list every patient who received it.
