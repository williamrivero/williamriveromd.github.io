Examination

End-of-course examination

Question 1An intern is asked by a dialysis nurse to sign the physician’s section of treatment sheets because the Physician on duty (POD) is late. What does RA 2382 Section 11(a) mean for this request?
Section 11(a) exempts a graduate under training only when serving without professional fee and under the direct supervision and control of a registered physician. With no physician present, signing as the physician falls outside the exemption. A nurse’s countersignature does not supply a physician’s supervision. Module 1.
Question 2A second-year internal medicine resident is offered a paid POD shift at a freestanding unit. Her program allows moonlighting. What should she confirm before accepting?
The unit’s license depends on a qualified Physician on duty (POD) under the DOH order as the unit applies it, with the PSN guidelines setting society expectations such as ACLS and a POD course. A program’s permission is about training rules, not the unit’s regulatory staffing. Completing this course confers no eligibility. Module 1.
Question 3A first-year nephrology fellow is introduced to a family as “Fellow of the PSN, the kidney specialist.” What should the fellow do?
In the PSN, Fellow is a membership category for qualified nephrologists, not a training status. Families decide based on who they think is advising them. The fellow can still help; he simply states his status accurately. Module 1.
Question 4At 2 a.m. a resident covering the renal ward calls the attending nephrologist about a patient with a potassium of 7.2 mmol/L and electrocardiogram (ECG) changes. There is no answer after two attempts over five minutes. What is the best next step?
A silent chain is a reason to move down the list, not to wait. Emergency care within privileges and protocol proceeds while escalation continues. A chat message without acknowledgment does not transfer responsibility, and an intern cannot carry the decision. Module 1.
Question 5A fellow has worked 26 hours in the hospital and is offered a 12-hour POD shift starting in one hour. What does the evidence on extended shifts suggest?
Landrigan and colleagues found 136.0 versus 100.1 serious errors per 1,000 patient-days and serious diagnostic errors of 18.6 versus 3.3 per 1,000 patient-days. The study measured errors, not deaths, and was in interns, but nothing suggests seniority removes the effect of sleep loss. Module 1.
Question 6A thin 80-year-old woman has a serum creatinine of 1.0 mg/dL, within the laboratory range. Why may her kidney function still be markedly reduced?
Serum creatinine reflects production, filtration and secretion. With little muscle, production is low and a reduced GFR can hide inside the normal range. Older adults still filter and secrete creatinine. Module 2.
Question 7A patient with stable CKD has a creatinine rise of 0.3 mg/dL one week after starting trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, with normal urine output and no other change. What is the most likely explanation?
Trimethoprim blocks tubular creatinine secretion, so creatinine rises without a fall in filtration. Acute tubular necrosis remains possible and should be considered if urine output falls or the rise continues, but the isolated small rise with a known secretion blocker fits best. Module 2.
Question 8A fellow must decide whether a frail amputee with an eGFRcr of 48 mL/min/1.73 m² can take a drug restricted below an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 45. What does Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) 2024 recommend in this situation?
Low muscle mass makes eGFRcr unreliable, and the decision hinges on the number. Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) 2024 recommends eGFRcr-cys here (1C), with measured GFR where accuracy will change treatment, and advises against race in eGFR. Module 2.
Question 9A patient in septic shock has creatinines of 1.0 mg/dL yesterday and 1.9 mg/dL today, with urine output of 10 mL per hour. The CKD-EPI eGFR today reads 35. What is the best interpretation?
Standard equations assume steady state. While creatinine is climbing toward a new plateau, the current value understates the loss, so the eGFR is too high. A kinetic estimate and the urine output give a truer sense of current filtration. Module 2.
Question 10An older woman on a thiazide, eating mainly tea and bread, has a sodium of 118 mmol/L and no edema. Which statement best explains it?
Hyponatremia is mainly a water problem. Thiazides act in the distal convoluted tubule, where urine is diluted, and a low-solute diet leaves too little solute to carry water out. Edema would point to excess total sodium instead. Module 2.
Question 11An intern presents a patient referred for a raised creatinine. Which element of the presentation most helps the team decide whether this is acute kidney injury?
KDIGO 2012 defines AKI by change from baseline creatinine or by low urine output over time. Without a baseline and output, the team cannot tell AKI from CKD. Other history matters, but not for this decision. Module 3.
Question 12A resident writes: “Female, 70, crea 2.8, for evaluation.” Which rewrite is the best problem representation?
A problem representation names tempo, key features, syndrome and context in one sentence. The second option does so and points to a hemodynamic cause; the others are vague, a list of values, or administrative. Module 3.
Question 13A fellow finishes a consult on a patient with potassium of 6.3 mmol/L at 11 p.m. What best ensures the plan is carried out?
Goldman’s principles include specific recommendations, contingency plans and direct communication. A note alone may not be read in time for a dangerous potassium, and a chat message without acknowledgment does not confirm receipt. Module 3.
Question 14A 45-year-old man has creatinine 4.1 mg/dL at his first visit, with no previous results. Ultrasound shows kidneys of normal size. What is the best statement in the consult note?
KDIGO 2024 advises not to assume chronicity from a single abnormal value. With no baseline and normal-sized kidneys, the tempo is unknown, so trend the value and look for acute causes before labeling. Module 3.
Question 15While reviewing an AKI patient, a resident fixes on contrast nephropathy after a Computed tomography (CT) two days ago. Which step best protects against premature closure?
Graber found premature closure the most common cognitive cause of diagnostic error. Deliberately naming alternatives and the finding that would disprove the leading diagnosis is the practical defense; blanket testing adds cost without focus. Module 3.
Question 16An intern reports a dipstick with blood 3+ and a microscopy count of 0 to 2 red cells per high-power field in a man with severe muscle pain. What should the intern report as the most likely meaning?
The blood pad detects heme peroxidase activity, so myoglobin or hemoglobin turns it positive without red cells. Muscle pain makes rhabdomyolysis likely, and pigment can cause AKI, so it needs same-day review. Module 4.
Question 17A resident sees a patient with a rising creatinine whose sediment shows red cell casts and many acanthocytes. Which syndrome does this point to?
Red cell casts form inside the nephron and acanthocytes are highly specific for glomerular bleeding (specificity 98% at ≥5%). Muddy brown casts would point to ATN and white cell casts to interstitial nephritis. Module 4.
Question 18A fellow is asked whether a patient with an eGFR of 25 mL/min/1.73 m², not on dialysis, can have an intravenous contrast CT for a suspected abscess. Which response best reflects the Albumin-to-creatinine ratio (urine) (ACR)–National Kidney Foundation (NKF) consensus statement?
The consensus judged the risk overstated and indicated saline prophylaxis for AKI or eGFR below 30 in patients not on dialysis. Withholding a diagnostic scan that will change treatment can do more harm; it is a consensus statement, not a graded recommendation. Module 4.
Question 19A 55-year-old woman with diabetes has a negative urine dipstick for protein at a rural clinic. Why is this not enough to exclude albuminuria?
Sumida and colleagues found dipstick sensitivity of 62% for ACR above 30 mg/g. A quantitative ACR, or PCR converted with caution, is needed in people at high risk. Module 4.
Question 20A 70-year-old man has a creatinine of 5.2 mg/dL with no prior results. Ultrasound shows both kidneys small with thin, echogenic cortex and no hydronephrosis. What does this suggest?
Small kidneys with a thin bright cortex point to chronic disease and supply evidence of chronicity, but an acute insult can still sit on top. No hydronephrosis makes obstruction unlikely. Module 4.
Question 21An intern wants the most reliable bedside trend of total body fluid in a ward patient. What should she rely on most?
A same-scale daily weight shows that fluid has accumulated, and a cumulative balance shows the trend that single-shift totals hide. Edema reflects interstitial fluid in dependent tissue and is changed by albumin, venous disease and drugs. Module 5.
Question 22A ventilated patient has an inferior vena cava of 2.4 cm with no respiratory variation. What is the most accurate interpretation?
A large, fixed IVC means high right-sided pressure, not necessarily too much fluid. Positive-pressure ventilation alone can enlarge the IVC. Treat it as one piece of a combined assessment. Module 5.
Question 23In the PUSH-AHF trial of natriuresis-guided diuretic therapy, what did guidance achieve?
Natriuresis is a surrogate. Guidance moved the surrogate but did not change the clinical composite. ADVOR showed more decongestion with acetazolamide, again without a difference in death or readmission. Module 5.
Question 24In specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound (SMART), major adverse kidney events at 30 days occurred in 14.3% with balanced crystalloids and 15.4% with saline. What is the best summary for a teaching round?
Give absolute numbers: 1.1 points, about one composite event per 90 patients. BaSICS and PLUS, both double-blind, found no difference in 90-day death. Mortality in specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound (SMART) did not differ significantly. Module 5.
Question 25A patient with dengue is on day six, afebrile since day four, with a falling hematocrit, rising platelets, crackles, effusions and a weight 4 kg above admission. Urine output is low. The nurse asks for a fluid bolus. What is the best next step?
This is the recovery phase, when leaked plasma is reabsorbed; World Health Organization (WHO) warns that continued intravenous fluid then causes overload. Low urine output in a congested, well-perfused patient is not a fluid trigger. Module 5.
Question 26A 74-year-old man with a sodium of 118 mmol/L has been vomiting repeatedly and is now hard to rouse. What should the intern do first?
Vomiting and deep somnolence are severe symptoms in the European guideline. Severe symptoms decide urgency, not the number. Fluid restriction is a treatment for SIAD without severe symptoms. Module 6.
Question 27A euvolemic woman not on diuretics has a sodium of 124 mmol/L, serum osmolality 258 mOsm/kg, urine osmolality 480 mOsm/kg and urine sodium 62 mmol/L, with normal thyroid and adrenal tests. What is the best classification and first-line treatment per the European guideline?
Low tonicity, urine osmolality above 100 mOsm/kg, urine sodium above 30 mmol/L, euvolemia and no diuretic, adrenal or thyroid cause fit SIAD. Saline can worsen SIAD because the kidney excretes the sodium and keeps the water. Module 6.
Question 28A patient with chronic hyponatremia started at 110 mmol/L. Twelve hours later the sodium is 119 mmol/L and the urine output is 300 mL an hour, dilute. What does the house standard require?
Nine of the 10 mmol/L allowed in the first 24 hours are used in 12 hours, and a water diuresis has begun. The European guideline recommends prompt intervention to re-lower if a limit is exceeded (1D); acting now may avoid it. Module 6.
Question 29A man with poorly controlled diabetes has a measured sodium of 126 mmol/L and a glucose of 900 mg/dL. Using the Hillier correction, what is his glucose-corrected sodium, and what does it mean?
Corrected sodium = 126 + 2.4 × (900 − 100) ÷ 100 = about 145 mmol/L. His effective osmolality is high; the sodium will rise as insulin lowers the glucose, revealing the water deficit. Module 6.
Question 30A 2025 meta-analysis of 16 cohort studies associated rapid correction of severe hyponatremia with lower in-hospital mortality. How should a fellow explain this on rounds?
Patients whose sodium rises slowly are often sicker. Observational associations cannot establish that speed itself saves lives. The analysis found no statistically significant increase in osmotic demyelination, which is not the same as proving safety. Module 6.
Question 31An intern receives a potassium of 7.1 mmol/L for a dialysis patient. Which ECG finding most demands an immediate call and treatment?
Widening QRS and bradycardia signal impaired conduction from sodium channel inactivation and can progress to a sine wave and arrest. A normal electrocardiogram (ECG) would not make 7.1 mmol/L safe either; Montague found the ECG insensitive. Module 7.
Question 32A resident has given intravenous calcium to a patient with hyperkalemia and ECG changes. The QRS has narrowed. What is the most important next step?
Calcium protects the heart but does not lower potassium. Shift agents buy hours; removal by kidney, gut or dialysis is required, and potassium rebounds when shift agents wear off. Module 7.
Question 33What did the STOP ACEi (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor) trial show in patients with advanced, progressive CKD (eGFR below 30)?
Stopping did not help the surrogate (eGFR) or the hard outcome. The KDIGO 2021 practice point favors lowering potassium over stopping RASi where possible. Module 7.
Question 34A woman on furosemide and a proton pump inhibitor has a potassium of 2.7 mmol/L that has not risen despite two days of replacement. What should be checked and corrected?
Magnesium deficiency releases its block on distal ROMK channels and increases potassium secretion, especially with a diuretic raising distal sodium delivery. Both furosemide and proton pump inhibitors cause hypomagnesemia. Module 7.
Question 35A 70-year-old man with essential thrombocythemia (platelets 1,200,000 per microliter) and normal kidney function has a serum potassium of 6.6 mmol/L. His ECG is normal and he takes no potassium-raising drugs. What is the best next step?
Very high platelet counts release potassium when blood clots in the tube, producing pseudohyperkalemia. With no reason for true hyperkalemia and a normal ECG, repeat it correctly at once. Module 7.
Question 36A blood gas shows pH 7.24, PaCO₂ 28 mm Hg and bicarbonate 12 mmol/L. What is the primary disorder?
The pH is low (acidemia) and the bicarbonate is low, so the primary process is metabolic acidosis. The low PaCO₂ is compensation, moving in the same direction. Module 8.
Question 37A patient has sodium 135, chloride 100, bicarbonate 20 mmol/L and albumin 1.8 g/dL. Using the Figge correction and a normal albumin of 4.0 g/dL, what is the corrected anion gap?
Measured gap = 135 − (100 + 20) = 15 mmol/L. Correction = 2.5 × (4.0 − 1.8) = 5.5. Corrected gap = about 20.5 mmol/L. Reading the measured 15 as normal would miss an unmeasured acid; subtracting the correction instead of adding it gives the wrong answer of 9.5. Module 8.
Question 38A fellow is teaching about the delta ratio in high anion gap acidosis. Which statement is most accurate?
Kraut and Madias showed wide variation in the rise of the gap relative to the fall in bicarbonate. Use the ratio with the history, other electrolytes and compensation. Module 8.
Question 39A man who drank homemade liquor yesterday has blurred vision, pH 7.12, anion gap 30 mmol/L and an osmolal gap of 8 mOsm/kg. A colleague says the normal osmolal gap excludes methanol. What is the best response?
The osmolal gap reflects the parent alcohol; the anion gap reflects its toxic acids. Vision deficits, pH 7.15 or less and an anion gap above 24 mmol/L are EXTRIP indications for extracorporeal treatment. Module 8.
Question 40A 52-year-old woman with type 2 diabetes on an sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor is admitted two days after surgery with vomiting. Her glucose is 190 mg/dL, pH 7.21, bicarbonate 13 mmol/L and beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.5 mmol/L. What is the diagnosis by the 2024 consensus criteria?
The 2024 criteria accept a known history of diabetes in place of glucose of 200 mg/dL or more, which captures euglycemic DKA, a recognized risk with sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors after surgery or fasting. Module 8.
Question 41A 70-kg man has passed 450 mL of urine over the last 24 hours. His creatinine rose from 1.0 to 1.4 mg/dL over the same day. By KDIGO 2012, what is his stage?
450 mL ÷ 70 kg ÷ 24 h is about 0.27 mL/kg/h, below 0.3 for 24 hours: stage 3 by urine output. The creatinine rise of 0.4 mg/dL alone is stage 1, which is why the first option is tempting, but KDIGO stages by the worse criterion. Do the arithmetic before you stage. Module 9.
Question 42A farmer with fever, calf pain and jaundice after floodwater exposure has a creatinine of 3.8 mg/dL, good urine output and a potassium of 3.1 mmol/L. Which mechanism best explains this pattern?
Leptospirosis targets the proximal tubule and interstitium; urine output can be preserved early and potassium is often low. Dehydration alone would not explain jaundice, calf pain and hypokalemia with a rising creatinine. Module 9.
Question 43What is the status of the KDIGO 2026 AKI and AKD guideline as of November 2026, and how should a fellow use it?
KDIGO released the 2026 guideline as a public-review draft. Its framework, including structural biomarkers in the definitions, is useful for thinking, but the 2012 guideline remains the final guideline until KDIGO publishes otherwise. Module 9.
Question 44A 66-year-old woman with an eGFR of 52 mL/min/1.73 m² and suspected abdominal abscess needs a contrast CT. The team wants to cancel the scan because of kidney risk. What is the best response?
The ACR-NKF consensus judges the risk of intravenous contrast to have been overstated and advises intravenous saline prophylaxis for AKI or eGFR below 30. Missing an abscess is the larger danger. KDIGO advises against oral fluids alone and against high-osmolar contrast. Module 9.
Question 45A man is discharged after dialysis-requiring AKI from sepsis; his creatinine is 1.6 mg/dL, down from a peak of 6.2 and a baseline of 1.0. What does the evidence on long-term outcomes support?
KDIGO 2012 advises evaluation 3 months after AKI (not graded). A meta-analysis of 13 cohorts found an adjusted hazard ratio of 8.8 for CKD after AKI, highest after severe AKI. The data are observational, but they justify a planned check rather than an assumption of recovery. Module 9.
Question 46On a night shift, a ward patient with AKI has a potassium of 6.9 mmol/L with peaked T waves and a urine output of 5 mL per hour. What should the intern do first?
Hyperkalemia with ECG changes is an urgent indication for treatment and possibly KRT. The protocol’s first measures bridge the time to dialysis. A diuretic in near-anuria will not fix this quickly, and waiting until morning is dangerous. Module 10.
Question 47Before a first KRT session in the ICU, which preparation step does KDIGO 2012 recommend with grade 1A?
KDIGO recommends ultrasound guidance (1A). It suggests an uncuffed nontunneled catheter to start (2D) and lists the right internal jugular first and the subclavian last among sites (not graded). It suggests not using diuretics to speed recovery (2B). Module 10.
Question 48A fellow is asked to explain the Standard versus Accelerated Initiation of Renal Replacement Therapy in Acute Kidney Injury trial (STARRT-AKI) result to the ICU team. Which summary is most accurate?
Mortality was 43.9% vs 43.7%. Among 90-day survivors, 10.4% vs 6.0% remained on dialysis, and adverse events were 23.0% vs 16.5%. Initiating Dialysis Early and Late trial (IDEAL)-ICU, not Standard versus Accelerated Initiation of Renal Replacement Therapy in Acute Kidney Injury trial (STARRT-AKI), was stopped for futility. Module 10.
Question 49A septic patient on norepinephrine needs KRT in a provincial hospital with one standard hemodialysis machine and no continuous kidney replacement therapy (CKRT). Which choice is best supported?
SLED gives outcomes similar to CKRT in randomized trials and uses equipment the hospital has; International Society for Peritoneal Dialysis (ISPD) 2020 calls acute peritoneal dialysis suitable for AKI in all settings (1B). A fast standard session risks hypotension, and waiting a day for a machine or a creatinine number is not justified when indications exist. Module 10.
Question 50After five days of SLED, a patient’s urine output has risen to 1,100 mL per day without diuretics, potassium and pH are stable, and he is off vasopressors. What is the best next step?
Spontaneous urine output is the strongest predictor of successful discontinuation; diuretics weaken that signal and KDIGO suggests not using them to enhance recovery (2B). Waiting for a normal creatinine prolongs catheter and hypotension risk. Module 10.
Question 51A 60-year-old woman has an eGFR of 52 mL/min/1.73 m² and an ACR of 45 mg/g, both confirmed twice over 6 months. How is her CKD classified?
eGFR 45–59 is G3a and ACR 30–300 mg/g is A2. G3b would need an eGFR of 30–44; A3 would need an ACR above 300 mg/g. Module 11.
Question 52KDIGO 2024 recommends which approach to kidney failure risk in CKD G3 to G5?
Recommendation 2.2.1 is graded 1A. The heat map is a population summary and does not include age; eGFR alone ignores albuminuria. Module 11.
Question 53In a CKD clinic, a patient’s 2-year Kidney failure risk equation (KFRE) risk is 45%. According to KDIGO 2024 practice points, what should this prompt?
A 2-year risk above 40% can guide modality education, preparation for KRT and transplant referral. It is not an indication to start dialysis, which depends on symptoms and complications. Module 11.
Question 54A 45-year-old man with type 2 diabetes diagnosed 2 years ago has nephrotic-range proteinuria, no retinopathy and dysmorphic red cells in the urine. What is the best next step?
Short duration, no retinopathy and an active sediment break the diabetic chain. Intensifying glucose control alone would miss a treatable glomerular disease. Module 11.
Question 55An 80-year-old woman has a stable eGFR of 33 for 5 years, ACR 15 mg/g and a 5-year KFRE risk of 1.5%. Her family asks for a fistula. What fits the evidence best?
Her risk is far below the 3% to 5% referral threshold and below the 40% 2-year preparation threshold; cardiovascular events are more likely than kidney failure. An access now would expose her to a procedure she probably will never need. Module 11.
Question 56An intern is about to take a blood pressure in clinic. Which practice best matches standardized office measurement?
This is the protocol KDIGO 2021 recommends (1B). Measuring on arrival or over clothing adds error, and a single reading is less reliable. Module 12.
Question 57A 55-year-old woman without diabetes has CKD G3a with an ACR of 650 mg/g and high blood pressure. Which statement matches KDIGO 2021?
Severely increased albuminuria (A3) without diabetes earns a 1B recommendation. The 2C suggestion applies to A2. Dual blockade should be avoided (1B). Module 12.
Question 58A resident asks why KDIGO 2021 says systolic below 120 mm Hg while the 2025 American Heart Association (AHA)/ACC guideline says below 130/80. Which explanation is most accurate?
SPRINT-style readings run lower than routine clinic readings, SPRINT excluded diabetes, and the guidelines serve different populations. Neither guideline found harm at the other’s target. Module 12.
Question 59Two weeks after starting an ARB, a patient’s creatinine rises from 1.8 to 2.1 mg/dL and potassium is 5.1 mmol/L. He feels well. What is the best response?
A rise of about 17% is within the KDIGO practice point of 30% within 4 weeks, and reflects lower glomerular pressure. Stopping would remove kidney protection; dual RASi is to be avoided. Module 12.
Question 60A 61-year-old man with CKD G3a is on amlodipine, losartan and chlorthalidone at full doses, takes them daily by pill count, and has standardized office and home readings above target. Potassium is 4.2 mmol/L. What next?
The 2025 AHA/ACC guideline recommends screening adults with resistant hypertension for primary aldosteronism regardless of potassium, continuing most drugs. Renal denervation is a specialist option with limited CKD evidence. Module 12.
Question 61Which set correctly lists the four pillars of kidney protection in type 2 diabetes with albuminuric CKD?
Each of the four has kidney outcome evidence or KDIGO-graded support. Dual ACE inhibitor plus ARB is to be avoided, and the other drugs lack kidney outcome trials of this kind. Module 13.
Question 62Three weeks after starting an SGLT2 inhibitor, a patient’s eGFR falls from 48 to 44 mL/min/1.73 m². She is well, eating normally and euvolemic. What is the best action?
The early dip is expected hemodynamics after restoring tubuloglomerular feedback. Stopping it would throw away a 1A-recommended benefit; a large fall, or a fall with volume depletion, needs review. Module 13.
Question 63A fellow must explain DAPA-CKD in absolute terms. Which statement matches the trial report?
DAPA-CKD reported 9.2% vs 14.5% (heart rate (HR) 0.61) with an NNT of 19, similar with and without diabetes. A relative reduction is not the share of patients who benefit; 13.1% vs 16.9% are EMPA-KIDNEY figures. Module 13.
Question 64A 66-year-old man with type 2 diabetes has an eGFR of 34 mL/min/1.73 m². He is not yet on metformin. According to the Food and Drug Administration (Philippines) (FDA) labeling, what applies?
The 2016 labeling: contraindicated below 30, do not start at 30 to 45, reassess if already taking and eGFR falls below 45. An SGLT2 inhibitor is recommended at this eGFR (KDIGO 2024, 1A). Module 13.
Question 65A patient on a maximally tolerated ARB and an SGLT2 inhibitor still has an ACR of 410 mg/g, eGFR 52 and potassium 4.4 mmol/L. He can now afford one more medicine. Which addition has the most direct guideline support?
Persistent albuminuria of 30 mg/g or more with normal potassium on a maximal RASi is the KDIGO 2A indication for an nsMRA, the diabetic kidney disease (DKD) population of the FIDELIO-DKD trial. Dual RASi is to be avoided. Module 13.
Question 66An intern is asked to send iron studies for a patient with CKD who is about to receive a first transfusion tonight. What should the intern do?
Transfused red cells change the indices, reticulocytes and ferritin, so samples drawn afterward mislead. Sending only ferritin fails because ferritin is an acute-phase protein and KDIGO thresholds always pair it with TSAT. Module 14.
Question 67A man with CKD G3b not on dialysis has Hemoglobin (Hb) 10.2 g/dL, ferritin 180 ng/mL and TSAT 19%. By KDIGO 2026, what is the iron decision?
For people not on dialysis or on peritoneal dialysis, KDIGO 2026 suggests iron if ferritin is below 100 ng/mL with TSAT below 40%, or ferritin 100 to 300 ng/mL with TSAT below 25% (2D). The first option applies only the ferritin cut point and ignores the TSAT arm. Module 14.
Question 68A fellow is asked why KDIGO 2026 recommends keeping Hb below 11.5 g/dL in adults on an ESA. Which answer is most accurate?
No trial tested 11.5 g/dL itself. CHOIR showed more composite events and TREAT nearly doubled stroke at higher targets, with little or no quality-of-life gain. The second option is wrong because quality of life did not meaningfully improve. Module 14.
Question 69A 45-year-old woman on maintenance hemodialysis in Cebu has Hb 9.4 g/dL, ferritin 420 ng/mL and TSAT 24% with no infection. What does KDIGO 2026 suggest?
In hemodialysis, KDIGO 2026 suggests starting iron if ferritin is ≤500 ng/mL and TSAT ≤30% (2D), intravenously rather than orally (2D). The 300 ng/mL cut point applies to patients not on dialysis or on peritoneal dialysis. Module 14.
Question 70A man with CKD G4 on an ESA has had a falling Hb for three months despite rising doses. What should the resident do first?
KDIGO 2026 asks you to identify and treat the causes of hyporesponsiveness first (practice point). Escalating the dose is the trap: in the trials, patients needing the most ESA did worst. A Hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF)-PHI trial is considered only after causes are addressed. Module 14.
Question 71A patient’s measured calcium is 8.0 mg/dL and albumin is 2.0 g/dL. What should the intern report?
Corrected calcium = 8.0 + 0.8 × (4.0 − 2.0) = 9.6 mg/dL. Reporting 8.0 as low ignores the albumin effect and could lead to unneeded calcium. Subtracting instead of adding gives the wrong 6.4. Module 15.
Question 72A man with CKD G4 has normal phosphate and calcium, but PTH has risen on three consecutive results. What does KDIGO 2017 suggest first?
For CKD G3a–G5 not on dialysis, KDIGO suggests evaluating modifiable factors when PTH is rising or persistently high (2C), and suggests not using calcitriol or analogs routinely (2C). Module 15.
Question 73A fellow is asked why Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (KDOQI) 2020 and KDIGO 2024 give different protein targets for CKD not on dialysis. Which answer is best?
Both avoid high intake. KDOQI goes lower but ties its target to supervision and metabolic stability; KDIGO sets a target near the general recommended intake. The first option misstates KDIGO, which warns against high intake. Module 15.
Question 74A hemodialysis patient in Batangas has phosphate 7.4 mg/dL, calcium 10.1 mg/dL and PTH within the KDIGO range. He takes calcium carbonate with meals. What is the most appropriate step?
Adding calcium or calcitriol would worsen hypercalcemia. Stopping binders would leave phosphate higher. Lowering the phosphate load without adding calcium is the guideline direction. Module 15.
Question 75A 62-year-old woman on dialysis has new, very painful purplish skin lesions on her thighs. What should the resident do?
Painful violaceous lesions or ulcers in a dialysis patient raise concern for calciphylaxis, which carries high mortality and needs urgent attending input. Treating it as simple cellulitis risks missing it. Module 15.
Question 76An intern reconciles medicines for a 70-year-old admitted with AKI. Which finding most needs to be flagged?
The NSAID, RASi and diuretic triple combination was associated with AKI (rate ratio 1.31, 1.82 in the first 30 days). The other items carry far less kidney risk. Module 16.
Question 77A frail 82-year-old woman weighing 40 kg has a stable creatinine of 1.0 mg/dL. Her drug’s label gives kidney bands in creatinine clearance. What is the best approach?
The label was tested in creatinine clearance, and indexed eGFR overstates the actual GFR of a small patient. A normal creatinine in low muscle mass can hide reduced function. Module 16.
Question 78A fellow is asked to dose a dialyzable antibiotic for a patient on CKRT. Which statement is most accurate?
Clearance on CKRT depends on the effluent rate and the drug; using old anuric doses risks treatment failure. Module 16.
Question 79A patient with sepsis and AKI has a creatinine rising from 1.1 to 2.6 mg/dL over 24 hours. The emergency team wants to halve the first antibiotic dose because of the creatinine. What is the best response?
The loading dose fills the volume of distribution; kidney function affects maintenance. The rising creatinine also means the eGFR is not at steady state. Module 16.
Question 80A man with CKD G3b on metformin, an ARB and an SGLT2 inhibitor asks what to do if he gets gastroenteritis. Which advice best matches KDIGO 2024 practice points?
KDIGO suggests planned temporary discontinuation of such drugs in certain circumstances and a clear restart plan. Stopping permanently removes protection; NSAIDs add injury. Module 16.
Question 81An intern sees cola-colored urine in a 24-year-old two days after a sore throat. Which finding best confirms that the blood is coming from the glomerulus?
Casts form in the tubule and dysmorphic cells are deformed passing through it, so both prove a glomerular source. A dipstick reads blood from anywhere in the urinary tract, and also reads myoglobin. Module 17.
Question 82A resident sees a patient with a nephritic sediment whose creatinine has risen from 1.0 to 2.4 mg/dL in five days. What is the best next step?
A creatinine more than doubling in days with a nephritic sediment is rapidly progressive until proven otherwise; ANCA and anti-GBM diseases need treatment within days. Anti-PLA2R is a nephrotic-syndrome test and does not answer the urgent question. Module 17.
Question 83According to KDIGO 2025, what is the proteinuria treatment goal in IgA nephropathy?
KDIGO 2025 lowered the target from the older 1 g/day threshold, because registries show progression even at 0.5 to 1 g/day. A 25% reduction is a lupus-style response marker, not the IgA nephropathy (IgAN) goal. Module 17.
Question 84A 52-year-old with nephrotic syndrome and normal eGFR has a strongly positive anti-PLA2R antibody. Which statement is most accurate?
KDIGO 2021 allows a diagnosis of membranous nephropathy on a positive anti-PLA2R in a typical case without biopsy. Secondary causes (hepatitis B, malignancy, drugs) must still be sought, because a positive test supports but does not exclude them in every case. Module 17.
Question 85In the TESTING trial of oral methylprednisolone for high-risk IgA nephropathy, what happened?
TESTING showed a hard kidney composite benefit (HR 0.53) over 4.2 years, but early serious infections forced a lower dose and pneumocystis prophylaxis. The population was largely Asian. Proteinuria-only benefit describes surrogate trials such as the PROTECT slope analysis. Module 17.
Question 86An intern sees a 62-year-old with hemoptysis, red cell casts and a creatinine of 3.1 mg/dL that was 1.2 mg/dL two weeks ago. What should the intern do first?
Hemoptysis with a nephritic urine and a creatinine more than doubling in two weeks is a pulmonary-renal syndrome until proven otherwise; hours matter in anti-GBM and ANCA disease. An ultrasound helps later but does not answer the urgent question. Module 18.
Question 87A resident suspects Rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis (RPGN). Which set of tests should be sent today?
These tests sort RPGN into anti-GBM, immune-complex and pauci-immune mechanisms, each with time-critical treatment. Anti-PLA2R is for membranous nephropathy, a nephrotic disease. Module 18.
Question 88What does KDIGO 2024 recommend as initial treatment of new-onset ANCA-associated vasculitis?
Recommendation 9.3.1.1 is graded 1B. Plasma exchange is a considered option in the sickest patients after PEXIVAS showed no significant benefit on death or kidney failure; avacopan may replace glucocorticoids. Module 18.
Question 89A 21-year-old with class IV lupus nephritis asks why she must keep taking hydroxychloroquine. Which answer reflects KDIGO 2024?
KDIGO 2024 recommends hydroxychloroquine for everyone with SLE unless contraindicated (1C). It does not replace class III/IV immunosuppression. Module 18.
Question 90A 45-year-old from Tondo is about to start high-dose glucocorticoids and rituximab for ANCA vasculitis. His brother has active tuberculosis. What is the most important step before the first dose?
The Philippines has one of the highest tuberculosis burdens worldwide, and rituximab can reactivate hepatitis B. If treatment cannot wait, the screen still starts the same day with a documented plan. Plasma exchange does not address infection risk. Module 18.
Question 91An intern notes that a patient’s creatinine began rising five days after starting a new antibiotic. Urine shows sterile pyuria and white cell casts. What should the intern do first?
Drug-induced AIN is the most common form, and the first treatment is stopping the drug, which needs the timeline. Eosinophiluria has limited diagnostic value, so waiting for it delays the key decision. Module 19.
Question 92A resident sees a normal anion gap acidosis with potassium 2.9 mmol/L, urine pH 6.5 and a positive urine anion gap. Which diagnosis fits best?
Distal RTA shows low potassium, inability to acidify the urine below 5.5, and low ammonium (positive urine anion gap). Diarrhea gives a negative urine anion gap; type 4 RTA gives high potassium; ketoacidosis has a high anion gap. Module 19.
Question 93According to KDIGO 2025, which adult with autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) should be offered tolvaptan?
The 1B recommendation covers adults with eGFR of 25 or more at risk of rapid progression (Mayo class 1C to 1E, or eGFR decline of 3 or more per year). Class 1A is slowly progressive, and an eGFR of 15 is below the threshold. Module 19.
Question 94A 33-year-old whose father has ADPKD has two cysts in the right kidney and one in the left on ultrasound. Family genotype is unknown. What does this mean by the Pei criteria?
At ages 15 to 39, three or more cysts in total establish the diagnosis in families of unknown genotype. Four in each kidney is the threshold for age 60 and older. Module 19.
Question 95In TEMPO 3:4, what did tolvaptan do compared with placebo over 3 years?
TEMPO 3:4 showed slower growth in total kidney volume, a surrogate, and fewer worsening-kidney-function events (2 versus 5 per 100 person-years). Discontinuation was 23% versus 14%. Mortality was not a trial outcome. Module 19.
Question 96An intern sees a 70-year-old man with no urine output for 10 hours, lower abdominal discomfort and a dull, enlarged bladder on percussion. What should the intern do first?
A full bladder in an anuric man suggests outlet obstruction, most often from the prostate; the treatment is a catheter. A diuretic cannot push urine past an obstruction. Module 20.
Question 97A resident sees fever, hypotension and a 6-mm obstructing ureteral stone with hydronephrosis. What is the most important next step besides antibiotics and fluids?
EAU 2026 strongly recommends urgent decompression in sepsis with obstructing stones and delaying definitive stone treatment until sepsis resolves. Removing the stone during sepsis is the step it advises against. Module 20.
Question 98A fellow is advising a 45-year-old with a third calcium oxalate stone. Which statement best reflects the evidence?
Borghi 1996 showed an absolute 15-point reduction with water. NOSTONE (416 patients) showed similar recurrence with hydrochlorothiazide and placebo in patients not selected for hypercalciuria; the American Urological Association (AUA) 2026 guideline reserves thiazides for recurrent formers with high urinary calcium. Module 20.
Question 99A 30-year-old woman has a first episode of pyelonephritis without sepsis and is improving on effective therapy. What duration does IDSA 2025 suggest?
IDSA 2025 suggests these shorter courses (conditional recommendations, moderate and very low certainty) rather than 10 to 14 days, counted from the first day of effective therapy. Repeat cultures to prove sterility are not required in a patient who is improving. Module 20.
Question 100After a urethral catheter relieves 1,800 mL of retained urine in a man with obstructive AKI, his urine output over the next hours is 350 to 400 mL per hour. What is the best approach?
Most post-obstructive diuresis is appropriate excretion of retained solute and water, but some patients become volume depleted or develop electrolyte disorders. Replacing all output automatically can perpetuate the diuresis; clamping risks re-obstruction. Module 20.
Question 101An intern sees a woman at 30 weeks of gestation with known CKD. Her blood pressure is 162/112 mm Hg and she has a headache. What should the intern do?
Blood pressure of 160/110 mm Hg or higher is severe range, and headache is a severe feature of preeclampsia. Restarting losartan is wrong because ACE inhibitors and ARBs carry fetal risk. Module 21.
Question 102A resident is asked to interpret a creatinine of 0.9 mg/dL in a woman at 24 weeks of gestation whose prepregnancy creatinine was 0.6 mg/dL. What is the best interpretation?
Pregnancy raises GFR and lowers creatinine, so a creatinine above the prepregnancy value is abnormal even if it sits within the nonpregnant range. Calling it normal applies the wrong reference point. Module 21.
Question 103An 84-year-old woman with CKD G5, frailty and dementia is referred for dialysis. Her children disagree about it. What should the fellow do?
KDIGO 2024 asks clinicians to inform people about both kidney replacement and conservative management and to offer conservative management as a valid choice. Age alone is not a reason to refuse, and the decision belongs to the patient and family with the team. Module 21.
Question 104A 9-year-old boy with AKI is admitted to an adult medical ward in a provincial hospital. Which plan is safest?
Children need weight-based fluids, pediatric reference ranges and pediatric equations, and AKI in a child is an indication to call the pediatric nephrologist. Adult fluids at a fixed rate risk overload and hyponatremia. Module 21.
Question 105A 19-year-old transplant recipient from a pediatric program misses his first two adult clinic visits after moving to Manila for college. What should the adult team do?
The period around transfer carries high risk of missed doses and rejection. The ISN–IPNA consensus frames transition as a shared, planned process; acting quickly on missed visits is part of it. Module 21.
Question 106An intern has explained peritoneal dialysis to a patient. What is the best way to check that the explanation worked?
Teach-back tests the explanation by asking the patient to restate it. “Do you understand?” usually gets a polite yes that hides confusion. Module 22.
Question 107A man with CKD G4 has a 2-year KFRE risk of 45% and an eGFR of 18 mL/min/1.73 m². He has no symptoms. What does KDIGO 2024 advise?
A 2-year risk above 40% is the KDIGO 2024 practice-point threshold for modality education and preparation. Starting dialysis now ignores the absence of symptoms; waiting until eGFR is below 10 leaves no time to prepare. Module 22.
Question 108A fellow is asked what the IDEAL trial showed. Which answer is most accurate?
IDEAL found no survival or adverse-event difference (HR 1.04), and 75.9% of the late-start group began above the target because of symptoms. It supports starting for symptoms and close monitoring, not delay at all costs. Module 22.
Question 109A 72-year-old retired teacher in Leyte with CKD G5 and severe heart failure says she does not want dialysis. Her son insists. What should the fellow do?
KDIGO 2024 asks clinicians to offer conservative management as a valid choice for those who decline KRT, and conservative management actively treats symptoms. Discharging her or overriding her choice are both wrong. Module 22.
Question 110A woman with CKD G5 choosing hemodialysis needs blood tests on the ward. Her non-dominant left arm has good forearm veins. What should the intern do?
The arm most likely to carry a future fistula must be protected; repeated punctures and central lines damage the veins needed for access. Avoiding all tests is unsafe. Module 22.
Question 111An intern is asked which part of a hemodialysis order sets how long the interdialytic gap is. Which is it?
Frequency and the days chosen set the gap, which drives weight gain and pre-dialysis potassium. Blood flow and dialyzer size change clearance per minute, not the gap. Module 23.
Question 112A patient on thrice-weekly dialysis has a spKt/V of 1.15. The post-dialysis sample was drawn 40 minutes after the session ended. What is the best interpretation?
Urea rebounds from cells into blood within 30 to 60 minutes, so a late sample overestimates post-dialysis urea and underestimates Kt/V. The KDOQI minimum delivered spKt/V for thrice-weekly dialysis is 1.2 (1B), not 1.0. Module 23.
Question 113A fellow considers starting a new patient on twice-weekly incremental hemodialysis. Which finding most supports this plan?
Incremental dialysis depends on residual function. In the Obi cohort, twice-weekly schedules were not associated with higher mortality when residual urea clearance exceeded 3.0, but were when it was 3.0 or less (hazard ratio 1.61). Preference alone does not make a schedule incremental. Module 23.
Question 114A 70-kg patient arrives 4.2 kg above dry weight for a 4-hour session. What is the ultrafiltration rate, and what does observational evidence suggest?
4,200 mL ÷ 4 h ÷ 70 kg is 15 mL/kg/h. Hemodialysis (HEMO) Study (HEMO) secondary analysis and a cohort of 118,394 patients associated rates above 13 mL/kg/h with higher mortality. No randomized trial has shown that lowering the rate saves lives, so the last option overstates the evidence. Module 23.
Question 115A resident presents Comparison of high-dose hemodiafiltration (HDF) with high-flux Hemodialysis (HD) trial (CONVINCE) at journal club. Which statement is accurate?
Comparison of high-dose HDF with high-flux hemodialysis (HD) trial (CONVINCE) enrolled 1,360 prevalent patients judged able to reach a convection volume of at least 23 L; hazard ratio 0.77. It was open-label, so the result applies to similar patients in units that can make ultrapure fluid. Module 23.
Question 116An intern is asked to draw blood from a patient with CKD stage 4 whose chart says “save left arm.” What is the best action?
KDOQI considers it reasonable to protect arm veins and avoid peripherally inserted central catheters (PICCs) and unnecessary venipunctures in CKD (expert opinion). A PICC injures arm and central veins and can ruin future access. Module 24.
Question 117A resident examines a fistula that is strongly pulsatile and stays distended when the arm is raised above the heart. Venous pressure alarms have increased. What is most likely?
A narrowing downstream raises pressure upstream, so the vein becomes pulsatile and does not collapse on elevation. Inflow stenosis gives a flat, weak fistula with poor augmentation. Module 24.
Question 118A fellow plans access for a 45-year-old man with progressive CKD, eGFR 18 mL/min/1.73 m², good forearm veins and no living donor. Which plan best follows KDOQI 2019?
KDOQI suggests referral for access when eGFR is 15–20 (expert opinion) and an individualized, documented Life-Plan. A forearm fistula first preserves the upper arm; waiting for a catheter start is what the plan is meant to avoid. Module 24.
Question 119A patient with a tunneled catheter develops rigors and a temperature of 39°C 20 minutes into dialysis. The exit site looks clean. What should the resident do first?
Fever or rigors soon after connection suggests catheter-related bloodstream infection even with a clean exit site. Cultures come before antibiotics; removal and antibiotics are decided with the attending per IDSA guidance and the unit protocol. Module 24.
Question 120A resident presents the Ravani systematic review (62 cohort studies). Which statement is accurate?
The review pooled observational cohorts; sicker patients more often have catheters, so the association does not show the effect of switching a given patient. Grafts were also associated with higher mortality than fistulas (1.18). Module 24.
Question 121An intern on a dialysis rotation is called because a patient at hour two says her vision is dimming and she is yawning. Her pressure is 84/50 mm Hg. What should the intern do first?
Yawning and dimming vision are early signs of a falling pressure. The first steps are quick and low risk, and the physician needs the pressure, pulse, time and actions. Fluid comes per protocol after the first steps fail, in measured portions. Module 25.
Question 122Ten minutes into a session, a man becomes flushed and itchy, then wheezes, and his pressure falls. What happens to the blood in the circuit?
A reaction in the first minutes with itching and wheeze suggests a severe dialyzer or drug reaction. The blood that has been in contact with the trigger is not returned. Returning it is right only for a cause in the patient with a sound circuit. Module 25.
Question 123A fellow reviews a patient with frequent intradialytic hypotension. What did the MyTEMP trial show about cooler dialysate?
MyTEMP tested a policy across 84 centers and found no benefit; the mean intradialytic fall in systolic pressure was also similar. It did not test cooling for an individual patient with frequent episodes, so the temperature stays a prescription decision. Module 25.
Question 124During a night shift, two patients on adjacent machines develop back pain, and the blood in one venous line looks translucent and cherry red. What is the best interpretation and action?
Translucent cherry-red blood with pain suggests hemolysis, and two patients at once point to a shared supply. In the 1998 multistate outbreak, faulty tubing from one lot caused hemolysis in 30 patients, and two died. Module 25.
Question 125A patient collapses in the dialysis chair and has no pulse. What is the correct order of priorities?
Compressions come first. In registry data, staff-initiated CPR was associated with about threefold higher odds of discharge alive; the circuit is managed without delaying compressions, and hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, air and hemorrhage are considered next. Module 25.
Question 126An intern finishes disconnecting a patient’s catheter and is asked to help at the next chair. What is the correct sequence?
Gloves carry blood and bacteria between stations. Hand hygiene after removing gloves and before the next patient is the main barrier to bloodborne virus and bacterial transmission; Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) lists monthly hand hygiene audits among its core interventions. Module 26.
Question 127A catheter patient develops rigors 30 minutes into dialysis; his pressure is stable. Two other patients on fistulas are well. What should the resident do?
A single febrile catheter patient most likely has a catheter-related bloodstream infection. Cultures before antibiotics guide treatment; catheter management is the attending’s decision per the IDSA approach. A pyrogenic reaction becomes likely when several patients react together. Module 26.
Question 128According to KDIGO 2022, how should a unit manage patients with hepatitis C infection?
KDIGO 2022 recommends standard procedures (1A) and audits (1C), recommends against dedicated machines (1D), suggests against isolation (2C), allows dialyzer reuse with standard precautions (2D) and recommends screening every 6 months (1B). Hepatitis B, unlike C, still calls for separation. Module 26.
Question 129A unit using tanker water after a pipe repair finds a total chlorine of 0.2 mg/L after the first carbon tank. The second tank reads 0.05 mg/L. What is the most appropriate response?
The first tank is no longer removing chlorine, so only one barrier remains. The US rule requires testing the second tank whenever the first reads at or above 0.1 mg/L total chlorine. In the 2004 chloramine outbreak, anemia resolved after the charcoal column was replaced. Module 26.
Question 130Within one shift, three patients, two with fistulas and one with a catheter, develop chills and fever within the first hour of dialysis. What is the most likely explanation and first priority?
Early fever in several patients, including those without catheters, points to a shared source such as endotoxin in the water or dialysate. The next shift is already exposed, so the response starts with stopping exposure and testing. Module 26.
Question 131An intern records exchanges for a continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) patient. Which record is most useful to the team?
Net ultrafiltration is drain minus fill for each exchange, and urine output is part of total fluid removal. “Drained well” hides a long dwell that drains less than it was filled. Module 27.
Question 132A resident sees that a patient’s overnight 10-hour dwell drains 300 mL less than it was filled, while day dwells drain more. Which explanation fits the three-pore model best?
Glucose crosses the small pores into the blood; once the gradient is gone, lymphatic and tissue reabsorption make net ultrafiltration negative. This is fastest in high transporters. Sieving lowers dialysate sodium early in a dwell; it does not make a long dwell negative. Module 27.
Question 133A fellow reviews a stable CAPD patient who feels well, is euvolemic, passes 900 mL of urine a day and has a total weekly Kt/V of 1.8. What does ISPD 2020 suggest?
ISPD 2020 practice points state there is no survival advantage in aiming routinely above 1.7 and no change is needed only to reach a clearance target. ADEMEX and the Hong Kong trial found no survival benefit from higher peritoneal clearance. Module 27.
Question 134A new urgent-start PD patient’s drain volumes fall on day 5. The effluent is clear, he has not passed stool for four days, and drainage improves when he lies on his left side. What is the most likely cause?
Positional drainage and constipation point to a mechanical cause. A membrane problem does not change with position and would not appear this early; the drain-volume sequence checks bowel, tip and leak first. Module 27.
Question 135A resident presents the Cochrane review of urgent-start PD. Which statement is accurate?
The review included one randomized trial and 15 non-randomized studies. Only the leak signal reached low certainty; peritonitis, technique survival and death were very low certainty. Module 27.
Question 136An intern sees a PD patient in the emergency room whose drained bag is cloudy. The patient has mild abdominal discomfort. What should the intern do first?
ISPD 2022 recommends presuming peritonitis with cloudy effluent (1C). The drained bag is the best specimen; discarding it or waiting delays diagnosis and treatment. Module 28.
Question 137A resident evaluates a CAPD patient with abdominal pain. The effluent after a 4-hour dwell has 650 white cells/µL with 80% neutrophils; the culture is pending. Which statement is correct?
ISPD 2022 requires at least two of three criteria (1C). Pain plus the cell criterion is enough; waiting for culture delays treatment. Module 28.
Question 138A fellow follows a patient on day 5 of appropriate antibiotics. The effluent count was 2,400, 2,100, 1,900, 1,950 and 1,800/µL on days 1 to 5, and the bag is still cloudy. What does ISPD 2022 recommend?
Refractory peritonitis is failure to clear after 5 days of appropriate antibiotics, and removal is recommended (1D). Longer observation is suggested only when the count is falling toward normal (2C), which this nearly flat curve is not. Module 28.
Question 139A CAPD patient’s effluent culture grows Candida. What is the recommended action?
ISPD 2022 recommends immediate removal in fungal peritonitis (1C) and antifungal treatment for at least 2 weeks after removal (2C). Fungi persist on the catheter; keeping it risks death and loss of the membrane. Module 28.
Question 140A patient on PD for one month develops right-sided breathlessness. A chest radiograph shows a large right pleural effusion, and the fluid glucose is very high. What is the most likely problem and first step?
A right effusion with high glucose in a PD patient suggests dialysate crossing a diaphragmatic defect. Continuing PD, or raising the glucose and fill, worsens it; PD is paused while the team decides on repair. Module 28.
Question 141An intern on a medicine ward hears a patient’s sister say she wants to give him a kidney. The patient has CKD with an eGFR of 15 mL/min per 1.73 m². What should the intern do?
The intern’s job is to record and report. Ordering tests or predicting a match is the transplant team’s work, and waiting for a year of dialysis contradicts KDIGO 2020, which favors pre-emptive transplantation. Module 29.
Question 142According to KDIGO 2020, when should a transplant-eligible patient with progressive CKD be referred for transplant evaluation?
KDIGO 2020 advises referral at least 6 to 12 months before anticipated dialysis to allow donor evaluation and pre-emptive transplantation (Not Graded). A living donor is not a prerequisite, and waiting until after dialysis closes the pre-emptive option. Module 29.
Question 143A 30-year-old donor candidate has a measured GFR of 58 mL/min per 1.73 m² and an albumin excretion of 20 mg/day. What does KDIGO 2017 state?
KDIGO 2017 states that donor candidates with GFR below 60 mL/min per 1.73 m² should not donate. Individualized decisions apply to GFR 60 to 89; normal albuminuria and follow-up do not override a GFR below 60. Module 29.
Question 144A patient’s co-worker offers to donate a kidney if the patient’s family pays off her loan. What is the correct response under Philippine rules?
Colleagues can be directed living non-related donors only if donation is altruistic, without any compensation or gratuity package. A loan payoff is a gratuity; arranging it may also meet the definition of trafficking for the sale of organs. Referral abroad is transplant tourism. Module 29.
Question 145Wolfe and colleagues (1999) found an annual death rate of 6.3 per 100 patient-years among wait-listed patients and 16.1 among all dialysis patients. What is the best interpretation of that particular comparison?
The waiting list selects healthier patients. The fairer comparison, recipients versus those still waiting, still showed 48% to 82% lower long-term mortality, but the study was observational. Module 29.
Question 146A transplant recipient is admitted to your ward for pneumonia. Which item is most important for the intern to record and report at once?
A complete medicine list, including brand switches and new antimicrobials, is what lets the team foresee tacrolimus toxicity or rejection. Clinic dates and donor details can wait. Module 30.
Question 147A recipient 3 months after transplant has a creatinine rise from 1.1 to 1.6 mg/dL. The resident is unsure whether it is rejection or toxicity. What does KDIGO 2009 recommend?
KDIGO 2009 R7.1 recommends biopsy for declining function of unclear cause (1C), because rejection, toxicity and BK nephropathy need opposite treatments. Empiric changes in tacrolimus in either direction can harm the graft. Module 30.
Question 148Which BK polyomavirus screening schedule does the 2024 international consensus recommend for adult kidney recipients?
The 2024 consensus recommends monthly plasma BK DNA to month 9, then every 3 months to 2 years (3 years in children). The older KDIGO 2009 schedule ended at 1 year; screening only on a creatinine rise misses the window for early intervention. Module 30.
Question 149A recipient 8 months after transplant is started on rifampin-based treatment for tuberculosis at a health center. Two weeks later her creatinine rises. What is the most likely mechanism?
Rifampin is a strong enzyme inducer that lowers calcineurin inhibitor levels; KDIGO 2009 recommends monitoring levels with rifampin (1C). The fall in levels can allow acute rejection within weeks. Module 30.
Question 150A recipient with stable function switched to a cheaper tacrolimus brand last month. What is the best advice?
A brand change is a change in medication that may alter levels, which KDIGO 2009 says should trigger level monitoring (2C). Doubling the dose or switching silently risks toxicity or rejection. Module 30.
Question 151Two hours after a kidney biopsy, the patient’s pulse rises from 84 to 112 per minute and she feels dizzy. What should the intern do first?
A rising pulse and dizziness after biopsy suggest bleeding; the intern should keep her flat, recheck and call immediately. Waiting for the hemoglobin delays care, and walking may worsen bleeding or cause a fall. Module 31.
Question 152A resident is asked to obtain consent for a native kidney biopsy that a fellow will perform. Which statement belongs in the consent conversation?
Consent should give real numbers (pooled transfusion rate 1.6%) and state the trainee’s role. A capable patient consents for herself, and misstating who performs the procedure invalidates consent. Module 31.
Question 153According to KDIGO 2012, which is the preferred vein for a temporary dialysis catheter in AKI, and how should it be inserted?
KDIGO 2012 prefers the right internal jugular first, femoral second, left jugular third, subclavian last (Not Graded), and recommends ultrasound guidance (1A). The subclavian vein risks stenosis that ruins future arm access. Module 31.
Question 154A thin patient (body mass index 21 kg/m²) needs urgent dialysis; the right internal jugular vein is thrombosed. Which choice is best supported?
KDIGO 2012 ranks femoral second, and Cathedia found femoral colonization lower than jugular in patients with body mass index (BMI) below 24.2. 3SITES studied general central lines and the subclavian site threatens future access. Module 31.
Question 155In the LUST trial, a lung ultrasound-guided strategy in patients on hemodialysis relieved congestion more often (78% vs 56%). What happened to the primary outcome?
Congestion on ultrasound is a surrogate; improving it did not change the composite of death, myocardial infarction or decompensated heart failure. A post hoc analysis suggested fewer recurrent heart failure episodes. Module 31.
Question 156During rounds, an 80-year-old man on hemodialysis tells the intern quietly, “Pagod na ako, ayoko na.” What should the intern do?
Noticing and exploring suffering, then reporting it, is the intern’s role. Stopping dialysis is ethically permitted for a patient with capacity, so telling him it is not allowed is wrong, and dismissing it misses a goals-of-care need. Module 32.
Question 157In the REMAP framework for goals-of-care conversations, what does the “Map” step involve?
Map out patient goals: open questions about values guide the recommendation. Reframe gives the headline; Align reflects values back; Propose offers a plan. Module 32.
Question 158What did Verberne and colleagues (2016) find in patients aged 80 or older choosing conservative management versus dialysis?
The survival advantage of dialysis seen in the whole cohort was no longer significant at 80 or older. The study was observational and not randomized, so it informs counseling but does not prove equivalence. Module 32.
Question 159A family asks how long their father will live after he stops hemodialysis. Which answer best reflects the evidence?
In 1,947 hospice patients who stopped dialysis, mean survival was 7.4 days (range 0 to 40). Giving a range helps families prepare; refusing to estimate leaves them unprepared. Module 32.
Question 160A frail 86-year-old woman’s son wants dialysis started, while she says she wants to stay home. She has capacity and says, “Bahala na ang anak ko.” What is the best next step?
A capable patient may choose to share decisions, but it must be her choice; exploring her words protects her real wishes. Age alone does not decide, and the son is not the decision-maker while she has capacity. Module 32.
Question 161A medical intern on a medicine rotation is asked by a nurse to adjust an intravenous fluid order because “the resident is busy.” What is the intern’s correct position?
RA 2382 Section 11(a) frames trainee service as service under the direct supervision and control of a registered physician. Enrollment in a program (the tempting distractor) does not make the intern an independent prescriber. Module 33.
Question 162A second-year internal medicine resident from a private hospital, with 4 months of dialysis experience and no PSN POD course, is offered POD shifts at a freestanding unit. Which statement fits the DOH order and the PSN 2024 guideline as written?
The DOH residency route is limited to training hospitals; the general-practitioner route and the PSN private-resident route need 6 months of center work, and PSN adds a POD course. ACLS alone (the best distractor) is necessary but not sufficient. Module 33.
Question 163A fellow on the quality committee is told that a patient’s fall in the dialysis unit should be left out of the logbook “to avoid problems with the inspection.” What is the best response?
The DOH order requires a complications and adverse events logbook and PSN asks for monthly CQI with falls among the risk metrics. Keeping it in nurses’ notes only (the best distractor) hides it from the review that could prevent the next one. Module 33.
Question 164At 10 p.m. the POD is asked by the unit owner to cover 19 stations alone because a colleague is sick. What should the POD do?
The DOH order caps POD coverage at 1:15. The PSN 1:20 allowance (the tempting distractor) is a professional standard for scarcity, not a waiver of the licensing order, and the decision belongs with the attending and management, not the POD alone. Module 33.
Question 165A resident wants advice on a patient’s potassium result and is about to post the patient’s full laboratory sheet, with name and photograph, to a 300-member alumni Viber group. What is the safest choice?
Health information is sensitive personal information under the Data Privacy Act and must be processed for a legitimate purpose and in proportion to it. Removing only the photograph (the best distractor) still discloses the name and results to hundreds of people outside the care team. Module 33.
Question 166An intern is writing the progress note of a patient newly started on maintenance hemodialysis. Which entry best supports both the next clinician and the PhilHealth registration?
PhilHealth Dialysis Database (PDD) registration is for chronic kidney disease stage 5, and the next clinician needs the evidence that the disease is chronic. A bare label (the tempting distractor) tells neither of them why. Module 34.
Question 167A resident explains the HD benefit to a newly registered patient. Which statement is accurate as of PhilHealth’s 2025 release?
PhilHealth states up to 156 sessions a year at PHP 6,350 per session for PDD-registered patients with chronic kidney disease stage 5, and its guide says unused sessions are not added to the next year. The carry-over option is the most tempting error. Module 34.
Question 168A fellow reviews a request to reduce a patient’s HD from three to two sessions a week “so the benefit lasts.” What principle should guide the decision?
PhilHealth itself states that services follow clinical assessment, not demand, and 156 sessions equal three a week for 52 weeks. Following the request without assessment (a tempting distractor) ignores the patient’s safety. Module 34.
Question 169A family whose father is on hemodialysis declines a transplant evaluation because they fear losing the dialysis benefit and being billed beyond PhilHealth. Under PhilHealth Circular 2026-0019, what can you accurately tell them?
The circular keeps HD and PD eligibility after graft failure and bars copayment in basic or ward accommodation and balance billing for covered services. Losing the dialysis benefit (the tempting distractor) is a common fear the circular contradicts; confirm the effective date with the liaison. Module 34.
Question 170A patient on HD moves from Iloilo City to a unit in Roxas City. Which handoff is most likely to prevent a missed session and an unsafe first treatment?
Discharge information is often missing at the first visit, and structured handoffs are associated with fewer errors. Sending the whole chart to a general address (a tempting distractor) is neither structured nor a safe privacy practice. Module 34.
Question 171An intern summarizes a trial: “The drug reduced albuminuria by 30%.” Which description of this outcome is correct?
Albuminuria is a laboratory surrogate. Treating it as equivalent to preventing dialysis (the tempting distractor) is the error CONFIDENCE illustrates: it measured albuminuria at 180 days, not kidney failure. Module 35.
Question 172In FIDELIO-DKD, a trial in diabetic kidney disease (DKD), the primary outcome occurred in 17.8% with finerenone and 21.1% with placebo over a median 2.6 years. What are the absolute risk reduction and crude number needed to treat?
absolute risk reduction (ARR) = 21.1% minus 17.8% = 3.3 percentage points; NNT = 1 divided by 0.033, about 30 over the trial period. The 18% figure (the tempting distractor) is the relative reduction implied by the hazard ratio, not the absolute one. Module 35.
Question 173A fellow wants to compare two reminder methods for potassium checks by randomly assigning clinic patients to each, and to publish the results. What is required?
Random allocation and intent to publish make this research; the Declaration of Helsinki requires research ethics committee review before the study begins, and PHREB’s 2022 guidelines govern Philippine practice. Low risk (the tempting distractor) affects the type of review, not whether review is needed. Module 35.
Question 174A resident’s slide says a drug “worked especially well in women” based on one of 24 subgroups in the forest plot, not prespecified, with no interaction test reported. How should the fellow respond?
Subgroup credibility criteria ask about prespecification with a stated direction, the number of subgroups and the interaction test. A confidence interval within one subgroup (the tempting distractor) does not show that the effect differs between subgroups. Module 35.
Question 175A dialysis unit tested a new catheter-care checklist and reports only that infections were 1.1 per 100 patient-months the year before and 0.9 the year after. What is the best next step to judge whether the change helped?
Run charts show timing and persistence that a before-and-after average hides. Concluding success from two averages (the tempting distractor) cannot separate improvement from ordinary variation or other changes. Module 35.
Question 176An intern sees a man on hemodialysis who missed two sessions during a typhoon. His ECG shows peaked T waves and a wide QRS. What should the intern say first when calling the resident?
Structured handoffs start with illness severity and the immediate dangers. A complete history (the tempting distractor) delays the action that the ECG demands. Module 36.
Question 177A resident manages a patient with potassium 7.4 mmol/L and hyperkalemic ECG changes who is on maintenance hemodialysis. What is the correct order of priorities?
Calcium stabilizes the membrane, insulin with glucose and beta-agonists shift potassium temporarily, and dialysis removes it. Diuretics (a tempting distractor) are unlikely to work in a patient with little urine, and binders act too slowly in this setting. Module 36.
Question 178A fellow is asked to defend a disaster plan for her dialysis unit. Which finding from Hurricane Katrina most directly supports teaching patients the unit’s emergency plan?
In the Katrina survey, unawareness of the facility plan was among the factors linked to missing three or more sessions (adjusted odds ratio for hospitalization 2.16). It is observational, but it points to a modifiable risk. Module 36.
Question 179At a provincial hospital the generator fails one hour after a patient with severe hyperkalemia received calcium and shift measures, and dialysis cannot start. What is the most appropriate step?
Shift measures are temporary and potassium rebounds, so a patient without dialysis needs close monitoring, repeated bridge measures and an early transfer decision by the attending. An oral binder (a tempting distractor) acts too slowly to replace dialysis. Module 36.
Question 180Before typhoon season, which item belongs in a patient’s emergency kit, according to CDC advice for dialysis patients?
The CDC advises a kit with contacts, a medicine list and at least a 3-day supply, and an emergency diet if dialysis is missed. Bananas and coconut water (the tempting distractor) are high in potassium. Module 36.
