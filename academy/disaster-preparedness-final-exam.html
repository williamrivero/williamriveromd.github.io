Before you begin

How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Disaster Preparedness and Response series. Each sitting draws 36 questions at random from a bank of 120, 3 from each module, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark29 of 36 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.
What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

How to get your certificate of completion

  1. Pass all 12 module quizzes. Each module quiz is passed at 80%, or at the pass mark the module states. The Certificate checklist above the examination shows which modules are done; each line links to that module's quiz.
  2. Use the same device and browser for the modules and this examination. Progress is saved in your browser, not in an account. On iPhone and iPad, the Home Screen app and Safari keep separate progress.
  3. Pass this timed examination (80%; where these instructions say so, every safety-critical question must also be correct). You have one minute per question from the moment you tap Start the examination; only your first check of a set counts. If you pass before finishing the modules, your pass is saved: finish the module quizzes, then come back here.
  4. Type your name the way you want it printed and tap Make my certificate. You can tick Add a preceptor sign-off line if your unit will sign the printed copy.
  5. Save or print your completion packet: two pages, the certificate and a record of your module results. Use Print or save as PDF to keep both pages together.
  6. Be online when you make it, so the certificate gets its QR code. Anyone can scan the code, or enter it at renalcarematters.com/verify, to confirm it.

Not finished yet? Use Print a progress record in the checklist to show a preceptor how far you have gone; it is clearly marked "not a certificate". The certificate is a self-study certificate of completion, not CPD units: competence is demonstrated on your own unit and signed off by a preceptor. To make the QR code work, your name, track, date and score are saved with the certificate code; to have them removed, use Report a correction on any guide.

Examination

End-of-course examination

Question 1Why does a disaster harm a dialysis patient twice?
Best: the second harm, missed sessions while water, salt and potassium keep building up, is usually the larger one. Why not the others: cost, heat and records matter, but they are not the core two-step harm. Module 1.
Question 2Among New Orleans patients after Hurricane Katrina, what was missing three or more sessions associated with?
Best: missing three or more was associated with hospitalization (adjusted odds ratio 2.16). Why not the others: none matches the study. Module 1.
Question 3Which group of patients was among the more likely to miss three or more sessions after Katrina?
Best: not knowing the plan was linked to missing three or more. That is why Module 4 makes every patient carry the plan. Why not the others: the study did not report these as the finding. Module 1.
Question 4How should the Hurricane Sandy early-dialysis finding be described?
Best: it was observational, and healthier patients may have been the ones who came early. Why not the others: “prevents” and “proven” overstate it; “no effect” contradicts it. Module 1.
Question 5What did the six-country study of patient self-disconnection find?
Best: 20.0% had been trained and 40.3% identified the first stage. The Academy therefore teaches patients to stay connected and wait for staff. Why not the others: none is supported. Module 1.
Question 6Which link of the causal chain does a wrong phone number on the call list break?
Best: the patient never hears the plan or the change in schedule. Why not the others: the number matters before any event, physiology or restart. Module 1.
Question 7A unit reopens after a flood before its water treatment has been tested and released. Which link is this?
Best: reopening too early is the restart link, guarded by the five signed gates in Module 11. Why not the others: they come earlier in the chain. Module 1.
Question 8Which agency raises the tropical cyclone wind signals in the Philippines?
Best: PAGASA raises the wind signals. Why not the others: PHIVOLCS covers earthquakes and volcanoes; the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) covers fire; the Department of Health (DOH) sets health policy. Module 1.
Question 9How much warning time should a unit plan for in an earthquake?
Best: earthquakes give no warning, so the response must already be drilled. Why not the others: those are typhoon-style lead times. Module 1.
Question 10Why is relief food a risk in link 4 (physiology)?
Best: salt drives thirst and fluid gain, and potassium builds up between missed sessions. Why not the others: they are not the main concern. Module 1.
Question 11What two questions does a hazard and vulnerability assessment ask about each hazard?
Best: likelihood and impact place each hazard on the matrix. Why not the others: they matter later, but they are not the rating. Module 2.
Question 12Who should rate your unit’s hazards?
Best: the rating is a local judgment. Why not the others: no national body rates an individual unit’s hazards. Module 2.
Question 13In the Pampanga case, why did the water fail when the generator worked?
Best: power and water failed together because the pump was not on the generator. Why not the others: the generator ran; the machines worked. Module 2.
Question 14What is a single point of failure?
Best: one pump, one switch or one trained person can stop everything. Why not the others: they describe different things. Module 2.
Question 15Which pair of dependencies typically fails together in a flood?
Best: the same flooded road blocks both staff and patients. Why not the others: they can fail, but not usually from the same cause. Module 2.
Question 16What should a generator test include to catch the Pampanga problem?
Best: testing the whole chain shows what the generator really carries. Why not the others: they test the generator alone. Module 2.
Question 17Under which law are patient and staff lists on the dependency map protected?
Best: names, addresses and phone numbers are personal data. Why not the others: those laws cover fire and disaster management. Module 2.
Question 18Who owns the dependency map and its fixes?
Best: the manager owns the plan, its fixes and the budget; the safety nurse runs the review. Why not the others: they are not accountable for the unit’s plan. Module 2.
Question 19When should the dependency map be reviewed?
Best: these are the moments the map is most likely to be wrong. Why not the others: waiting lets the map go out of date. Module 2.
Question 20A technician finds that the only person who can operate the transfer switch is on leave. What is this?
Best: a person can be a single point of failure. Why not the others: it is a staffing dependency. Module 2.
Question 21Who directs the response during an event in a dialysis unit?
Best: the people with clinical responsibility for the patients on the shift command. Why not the others: the safety nurse plans and drills but does not command; the owner and a new technician do not direct care. Module 3.
Question 22What is the safety nurse’s role during a real event?
Best: the safety nurse plans, drills and helps, but command stays with the charge nurse and physician. Why not the others: early dialysis is the nephrologist’s decision. Module 3.
Question 23What is the lead time for PAGASA Tropical Cyclone Wind Signal No. 2?
Best: Signal No. 2 means gale-force winds within 24 hours. Why not the others: those are Signals No. 1, 4 and 3. Module 3.
Question 24Why does each unit set its own actions for each signal level?
Best: the trigger is national; the action depends on the unit’s own risks. Why not the others: none is true. Module 3.
Question 25Which communication channel often still works when voice calls fail?
Best: text messages often get through on overloaded networks; build several channels. Why not the others: they are less reliable or not always available. Module 3.
Question 26In what order does a typical call-down run?
Best: knowing who can come in decides what the unit can offer. Why not the others: alphabetical or owner-first ignores risk and capacity. Module 3.
Question 27What must a receiving-unit agreement name?
Best: an agreement works only if someone answers and knows what to expect. Why not the others: they leave out what makes the transfer safe. Module 3.
Question 28A freestanding unit coordinates evacuation routes and warnings with which office?
Best: Republic Act No. 10121 set up local disaster offices for this. Why not the others: they do not coordinate local disaster response. Module 3.
Question 29Where should the one-page plan be kept?
Best: anyone on the shift must be able to open it, even without power. Why not the others: they make it hard to reach in an event. Module 3.
Question 30The incident lead cannot be reached when Signal No. 2 is raised. What happens?
Best: every role has a deputy so the plan does not wait. Why not the others: waiting loses the lead time. Module 3.
Question 31What does a patient on the machine do when a disaster strikes?
Best: staff take patients off with the blood rule and the right clamps. Why not the others: self-disconnection risks bleeding, air and a lost access. Module 4.
Question 32Why does the Academy not teach patients to self-disconnect?
Best: the six-country study found 20.0% trained and 40.3% correct on the first stage. Why not the others: none is the reason given. Module 4.
Question 33Which item belongs on the front of the emergency card?
Best: a receiving unit must know how to reach the circulation. Why not the others: they are not needed to dialyze safely. Module 4.
Question 34Who writes the patient’s daily fluid allowance on the three-day diet sheet?
Best: the team fills in the “My information” page with the patient; if no number is written, the sheet shows the national plan’s 2 cups (about 480 mL) a day. Why not the others: the allowance is a clinical decision. Module 4.
Question 35When does a patient start the three-day emergency kidney diet?
Best: starting early does no harm and slows the build-up. Why not the others: waiting lets potassium and fluid rise, often without symptoms. Module 4.
Question 36A relief pack has buko juice for “hydration.” What do you tell a patient on hemodialysis?
Best: the sheet lists buko juice first on the hold-for-now list. Why not the others: any amount adds potassium and fluid. Module 4.
Question 37How many phone numbers should the unit hold for each patient?
Best: phones are lost and batteries die; a second number and a family contact keep the patient reachable. Why not the others: one number fails too often. Module 4.
Question 38What do patients on peritoneal dialysis (PD) especially need in their preparation?
Best: PD continues at home only with supplies and contact. Why not the others: hand cranks and needles belong to hemodialysis. Module 4.
Question 39A patient says she is “on the emergency diet” so she can skip Friday’s session. What do you say?
Best: no diet replaces dialysis. Why not the others: they encourage missed sessions. Module 4.
Question 40During shaking, how does a patient in a recliner protect himself?
Best: moving during shaking risks falls and dislodged needles. Why not the others: they expose the patient to falls and falling objects. Module 4.
Question 41Who decides which patients dialyze early before a typhoon?
Best: any change to schedule, session length or fluid goal is the nephrologist’s decision. Why not the others: they carry it out or request it. Module 5.
Question 42What did the Hurricane Sandy study find about early dialysis?
Best: an observational association, supporting the practice without proving it. Why not the others: they misstate the finding. Module 5.
Question 43What does PAGASA Signal No. 3 mean?
Best: No. 3 is storm-force winds with 18 hours’ lead time. Why not the others: those describe Signals No. 1 and No. 4, or nothing. Module 5.
Question 44Which patient is a likely first choice for early dialysis?
Best: large weight gains and heart failure make a long gap dangerous. Why not the others: they face less risk from the delay or are not on hemodialysis. Module 5.
Question 45What is the “last safe session” time?
Best: it protects people from travelling as conditions worsen. Why not the others: they are not the planning concept. Module 5.
Question 46A machine is standing in flood water. What do you do?
Best: wet electrical equipment can electrocute. Why not the others: each brings a person into contact with live equipment. Module 5.
Question 47A nurse waded through flood water to reach the unit and now has fever and muscle pain a week later. What is the concern?
Best: fever and muscle pain after wading suggest leptospirosis, which can damage the kidneys. Why not the others: they miss the exposure. Module 5.
Question 48Who is called first in a typhoon call-down?
Best: the unit cannot promise sessions it cannot staff. Why not the others: they come after staffing is known. Module 5.
Question 49What must happen before a flooded unit reopens?
Best: reopening is a signed decision (Module 11). Why not the others: they skip the safety checks. Module 5.
Question 50What do you tell patients who dialyze early before a typhoon?
Best: early dialysis lengthens the gap after it, so diet and readiness matter more. Why not the others: they increase risk. Module 5.
Question 51What do connected patients do during shaking?
Best: moving or disconnecting during shaking risks falls, bleeding and air. Why not the others: each exposes the patient to greater harm. Module 6.
Question 52What do staff do during shaking?
Best: staff stay low and stay with patients. Why not the others: moving and disconnecting during shaking cause injuries. Module 6.
Question 53After the shaking, what is checked first?
Best: patients first, then machines, then the room. Why not the others: they come after the patients. Module 6.
Question 54What is the main question for staying or taking patients off?
Best: without an order or damage, sessions continue or end per protocol. Why not the others: they matter, but they are not the deciding question. Module 6.
Question 55During an emergency take-off, the venous line shows foam. What happens to the circuit blood?
Best: blood that may contain air is never returned (the blood rule shared with the Resuscitation Lab). Why not the others: each pushes air toward the patient. Module 6.
Question 56What is done before separating the lines from a central venous catheter?
Best: an open catheter hub can draw air into a central vein. Why not the others: they do not prevent air entry. Module 6.
Question 57Who checks the building structure before the unit treats patients again after a strong earthquake?
Best: structural safety is an engineering decision. Why not the others: they are not qualified to judge it. Module 6.
Question 58How often does the Nationwide Simultaneous Earthquake Drill run?
Best: the Office of Civil Defense holds it each quarter. Why not the others: they are wrong. Module 6.
Question 59How do patients take part in an earthquake drill?
Best: drills never put connected patients at risk, and a watcher stays with them. Why not the others: they create real danger. Module 6.
Question 60Which step secures the unit before the next earthquake?
Best: falling and sliding objects cause most indoor injuries. Why not the others: they increase the hazard. Module 6.
Question 61What does RACE stand for?
Best: rescue, alarm, contain, extinguish or evacuate (RACE) is a teaching aid for the first actions in a fire. Why not the others: they are not the aid. Module 7.
Question 62When may a staff member use an extinguisher?
Best: fight only what is small and leave a way out. Why not the others: large fires and no exit kill rescuers. Module 7.
Question 63A nurse with a connected patient wants to fetch the extinguisher. What is the rule?
Best: a connected patient left alone can bleed or panic. Why not the others: each puts the patient at risk. Module 7.
Question 64Why is oxygen a fire hazard?
Best: oxygen feeds fire. Why not the others: oxygen itself does not burn or explode on its own. Module 7.
Question 65In a fire, which blood decision is usually right during take-off?
Best: there is rarely time to return blood in a fire. Why not the others: they waste time or break the blood rule. Module 7.
Question 66When are mobility categories set?
Best: helpers must be assigned before smoke appears. Why not the others: they are too late. Module 7.
Question 67What does the census nurse do at the assembly point?
Best: knowing who is out is the most common failure in evacuations. Why not the others: searching inside is for firefighters. Module 7.
Question 68A patient is missing at the assembly point. What happens?
Best: firefighters search; staff stay out. Why not the others: they risk more lives or lose the person. Module 7.
Question 69How often does the Fire Code’s implementing rules require fire exit drills where drills are required?
Best: the rules do not give the unit a number; the Academy suggests yearly. Why not the others: they state requirements that are not in the rules. Module 7.
Question 70Who may take part in an evacuation drill?
Best: no evacuation drill with patients on machines. Why not the others: they risk real patients or leave out the team. Module 7.
Question 71What is the first step when the power fails mid-session?
Best: patients first, machines second. Why not the others: they come after the patients are safe. Module 8.
Question 72Why does one staff member watch each patient during manual blood return?
Best: the operator’s eyes replace the air detector. Why not the others: they miss the safety reason. Module 8.
Question 73When is circuit blood not returned during a power failure?
Best: the blood rule, shared across the Academy. Why not the others: they misstate it. Module 8.
Question 74A generator has 120 liters of usable diesel and uses 8 liters per hour. About how long will it run?
Best: 120 ÷ 8 = 15 hours. The number does not guarantee the generator keeps running. Why not the others: arithmetic errors. Module 8.
Question 75What should a generator test include?
Best: test what the generator must actually carry. Why not the others: they miss load-related failures. Module 8.
Question 76What is the first step in a water failure?
Best: no new patient should start on water that may fail. Why not the others: they expose more patients. Module 8.
Question 77Two patients in the same room report chest and back pain, and one venous line looks wine-colored. What do you suspect?
Best: two patients with the same symptoms on one loop is a water problem until proven otherwise. Why not the others: they miss the pattern. Module 8.
Question 78Who decides that dialysis water is fit again?
Best: resume only after a signed release. Why not the others: they lack the authority and the test results. Module 8.
Question 79Where should the supply buffer be stored?
Best: a buffer must survive the event and not expire. Why not the others: they risk loss or blocked exits. Module 8.
Question 80The technician reports the generator will run out of fuel within the hour. What should happen?
Best: a known end time allows a normal take-off with working alarms. Why not the others: they turn a planned end into an emergency. Module 8.
Question 81On a high heat-index day, a patient asks if she can drink freely because it is hot. What do you say?
Best: heat does not loosen the fluid limit on its own. Why not the others: they risk fluid overload, dehydration or high potassium. Module 9.
Question 82Where is the main heat risk for many dialysis patients?
Best: travel and waiting expose patients to heat. Why not the others: they are not the main exposure. Module 9.
Question 83Which agency publishes daily heat index forecasts?
Best: PAGASA publishes heat index forecasts. Why not the others: they do not. Module 9.
Question 84During ashfall, what happens to the waiting-area windows?
Best: keep ash out of the building. Why not the others: they let ash in or spread it. Module 9.
Question 85What does the water treatment technician do during ashfall?
Best: ash can clog filters and enter the water. Why not the others: they ignore the risk or are not standard steps. Module 9.
Question 86How should ash be removed from live electrical equipment?
Best: wet ash conducts electricity. Why not the others: they risk shock or damage. Module 9.
Question 87Which agency raises the volcano alert level?
Best: PHIVOLCS monitors volcanoes. Why not the others: the LDRRMO orders local evacuations based on those warnings. Module 9.
Question 88What is cohorting during an outbreak?
Best: cohorting limits spread while dialysis continues. Why not the others: they are not cohorting. Module 9.
Question 89Why is an outbreak treated as a disaster in this course?
Best: the unit loses dependencies, as in any disaster. Why not the others: they are not the reason. Module 9.
Question 90A patient with lung disease arrives coughing after ash exposure. What happens before connection?
Best: a clinical decision comes first. Why not the others: they skip assessment or escalation. Module 9.
Question 91Who sets the priority roster when there are fewer sessions than patients?
Best: priority is a medical decision. Why not the others: they supply facts or follow the order. Module 10.
Question 92Which four inputs go into the priority roster?
Best: these reflect medical need. Why not the others: they are not the basis of medical priority. Module 10.
Question 93Which is one of the six conditions for shortened sessions?
Best: shortened sessions need a real shortage, a written order and an end date, among others. Why not the others: none meets the conditions. Module 10.
Question 94Which patients are excluded from shortened sessions?
Best: high-risk patients keep full sessions or move first to a receiving unit. Why not the others: they are not the defined exclusion. Module 10.
Question 95How should the Fukushima shortened-session finding be described?
Best: one small retrospective study. Why not the others: they overstate or misstate it. Module 10.
Question 96What is the target for finding every patient after a disruption in this course?
Best: 24 hours is the unit’s own measure; no external standard is claimed. Why not the others: they are wrong or claim a rule that does not exist. Module 10.
Question 97What must the handoff to a receiving unit include?
Best: the receiving unit needs what it takes to dialyze safely and protect other patients. Why not the others: they are insufficient. Module 10.
Question 98Before a patient travels to a receiving unit, what is confirmed?
Best: a named person who expects the patient prevents a wasted trip. Why not the others: they leave the placement uncertain. Module 10.
Question 99A patient has not been reached after 24 hours. What happens?
Best: a missing patient may be in danger. Why not the others: they lose the patient. Module 10.
Question 100What does the disaster triage list (CALC-11) do?
Best: tools support the decision. Why not the others: the decision stays with the nephrologist. Module 10.
Question 101What are the five restart gates?
Best: these five signed checks come from Head Nurse, Module 12. Why not the others: they are not the gates. Module 11.
Question 102The water technician says by phone that readings “look fine,” but test results are not back. Can the unit open?
Best: a verbal opinion is not a release. Why not the others: none replaces the signed gate. Module 11.
Question 103Who decides that water is fit after a flood?
Best: they hold the test results and the authority. Why not the others: they do not. Module 11.
Question 104What does the staff gate require?
Best: the unit reopens only with a safe, qualified team. Why not the others: they fall short. Module 11.
Question 105What goes on the reconciliation board?
Best: it is the record of every patient’s path and next need. Why not the others: they miss patients or the purpose. Module 11.
Question 106Who decides whether a patient needs an extra session after reopening?
Best: it is a prescription decision. Why not the others: they do not prescribe. Module 11.
Question 107About what share of New Orleans hemodialysis patients reported posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) symptoms about a year after Katrina?
Best: 23.8% of 391 patients. Why not the others: they misstate the finding. Module 11.
Question 108In the Katrina cohort, which condition was associated with higher risk of hospitalization or death?
Best: depression, not PTSD, was associated with those outcomes. Why not the others: they misstate the study. Module 11.
Question 109Which step helps staff after a disaster?
Best: rest and support keep staff able to respond. Why not the others: they increase harm. Module 11.
Question 110A patient tells you after the flood that she feels hopeless. What do you do?
Best: hopelessness after a disaster needs prompt attention. Why not the others: they delay help. Module 11.
Question 111Which exercise type tests decisions and roles without moving patients or equipment?
Best: a tabletop talks through a scenario as injects arrive. Why not the others: they involve real actions. Module 12.
Question 112What is an inject?
Best: injects drive a tabletop. Why not the others: they are not injects. Module 12.
Question 113What is the watcher’s only job during a drill?
Best: the watcher never joins the drill and can stop it. Why not the others: those are other roles. Module 12.
Question 114When may an evacuation drill be run with patients on machines?
Best: no evacuation drill with patients on machines. Why not the others: each puts patients at risk. Module 12.
Question 115Which is a good tabletop objective?
Best: specific and checkable. Why not the others: they cannot be checked. Module 12.
Question 116What does the after-action report contain?
Best: the one-page format turns findings into owned actions. Why not the others: blame and attendance do not change practice. Module 12.
Question 117Does the facilitator play a role in the exercise?
Best: the facilitator keeps the scenario moving and watches. Why not the others: playing a role blinds the facilitator. Module 12.
Question 118What does the evidence show about hospital disaster drills?
Best: drill effectiveness is supported by low-quality evidence, and tabletops were not tested in the review. Why not the others: they overstate or ignore the evidence. Module 12.
Question 119Who keeps the unit’s disaster drill records?
Best: records have an owner and a reviewer. Why not the others: they do not own unit records. Module 12.
Question 120What makes an action in the after-action report real?
Best: an action with no owner is not an action. Why not the others: they do not ensure it happens. Module 12.
