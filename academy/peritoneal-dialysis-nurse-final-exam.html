How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Peritoneal Dialysis Nurse series. Each sitting draws 40 questions at random from a bank of 180, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark32 of 40 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.
What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

End-of-course examination

Question 1Which process moves urea from blood into dialysis solution during a dwell?
Urea moves from the high level in the blood to the low level in fresh solution. Aquaporins move water, not urea. Lymphatics return fluid to the body. Tubular secretion happens in the kidney, not the peritoneum. Module 1.
Question 2Why does net ultrafiltration slow, and sometimes reverse, late in a long glucose dwell?
The osmotic pull is spent as glucose crosses into the blood, while lymphatic and tissue reabsorption keep going. Obstruction causes a poor drain, not a reversal. Blood urea and urine output do not drive water removal in the dwell. Module 1.
Question 3Why is icodextrin often used for the long dwell?
Its large molecules stay in the abdomen and keep drawing water through the small pores. It does not prevent infection or replace the kidneys. Whether it is used is the prescriber’s decision. Module 1.
Question 4Aling Rosario’s exchanges show positive net UF, but her weight is rising and her urine has fallen. What is the best interpretation?
Urine and UF are two routes for the same fluid. When urine falls and UF does not rise, the difference stays in the body. Check the scale, but do not assume it. Skipping an exchange is unsafe. Module 1.
Question 5Which statement about CAPD and APD is correct?
APD keeps the human risks and adds program mismatch, alarms and power dependence. Many APD prescriptions include a day dwell. The cycler belongs to APD. Neither form suits everyone. Module 1.
Question 6What does sodium sieving early in a strong glucose dwell reflect?
Water moving through aquaporins leaves sodium behind, so the solution’s sodium falls briefly. The other options describe unrelated processes. Module 1.
Question 7Why do PD patients lose albumin into the effluent every day?
A small number of large pores lets protein out. Losses rise during peritonitis but happen every day. Glucose binding and tubular damage are not the mechanism. Module 1.
Question 8Mr. Arnel feels well, but his night drains are always short and his ankles swell. Which membrane pattern fits best?
Fast transporters absorb glucose quickly, lose their osmotic pull early and drain short after long dwells. Slow transporters do the opposite. The prescriber confirms the pattern with a peritoneal equilibration test (PET). Module 1.
Question 9Which action is within the PD nurse’s role without a new order?
Verification and reporting are core nursing work. Solution, icodextrin and antibiotics are prescription decisions. Antibiotics may be given under a written standing protocol, but never on the nurse’s own decision. Module 1.
Question 10Aling Rosario’s urine fell from about 700 mL to about 250 mL a day, and her exchange UF did not change. About how much extra fluid stays in her body each day?
700 minus 250 is about 450 mL a day that no longer leaves by the kidneys. With unchanged UF and intake, it accumulates, which matches her weight gain. Module 1.
Question 11Which finding in this module calls for the emergency pathway rather than routine review?
Breathlessness at rest may mean acute fluid overload or a heart problem. Edema and one small drain need assessment and trend review. A diet question is routine teaching. Module 1.
Question 12How should you report a worsening fluid pattern?
A complete report with numbers lets the prescriber decide without calling you back. A vague word, a single number or a delay does not. Module 1.
Question 13Mang Tonyo’s night dwell drains 150 mL less than was filled, but his day total is +550 mL. What is the best response?
A positive total can hide a long dwell that gives fluid back. Confirm the numbers and report the pattern. Changing dwell length or solution is a prescription decision. Module 1.
Question 14In observational studies, preserved residual kidney function in PD is:
The evidence is observational. It shows an association, not proof that protecting urine output causes longer survival. The other options overstate or contradict the data. Module 1.
Question 15A patient asks why he cannot leave the night dwell in longer “for more cleaning.” The best answer explains that:
Small-solute transport plateaus and a long glucose dwell can turn net negative. Blockage and infection are not the reason. Module 1.
Question 16What is the best opening for a modality conversation?
Goal-directed care starts from the person’s priorities. The other openings are directive, unsupported as stated, or start from the equipment. Module 2.
Question 17How should observational survival comparisons between PD and hemodialysis be described to a patient?
The data are observational and shaped by who chooses each modality. Claims of proven superiority overstate them. Module 2.
Question 18According to the 2026 ISPD position statement, about what proportion of people who start PD are no longer on it at two years?
Nearly half, mostly through transfer to hemodialysis, death or transplantation. Transplantation is a good outcome; much of the rest is the target of better readiness. Module 2.
Question 19Which barrier is usually remediable rather than a suitability question for the nephrologist?
Vision aids can solve it, and you can verify that by watching. The others are clinical suitability questions. Module 2.
Question 20What establishes readiness after a home barrier is addressed?
Readiness is demonstrated, not declared. Statements, forms and calendars do not show performance. Module 2.
Question 21A caregiver will perform the cycler setup. Who must demonstrate it?
The person who does the task shows it. A backup is needed for illness, work or travel. Module 2.
Question 22The 2020 ISPD goal-directed prescribing recommendations are best described as:
They are labeled practice points. Never assign a grade the source did not give. Module 2.
Question 23Why does a readiness plan need a reassessment date?
Readiness is a judgment at one point in time. A patient slowed by uremia may learn much better after starting dialysis. Module 2.
Question 24During a home visit, the family cat sleeps in the planned exchange room. What is the best action?
The 2022 International Society for Peritoneal Dialysis (ISPD) recommendations address pets as a modifiable risk. Unsuitability or giving the pet away is out of proportion. Module 2.
Question 25Mrs. Teresita says, “If I choose PD, I can never have a transplant.” What is correct?
PD does not prevent transplantation, and evaluation can run alongside. Module 2.
Question 26A patient has a tremor and struggles with clamps. What should you consider first?
Adapt, then verify by watching. Skipping a step is unsafe, and declaring PD impossible is premature. Module 2.
Question 27What is the nurse’s role when a patient has had several major abdominal operations?
Suitability belongs to the nephrologist. Your part is an accurate history routed to the right person. Module 2.
Question 28What does assisted PD mean in the 2024 ISPD position paper?
Assistance is defined broadly and can be short term, such as after a hospital stay, or long term. Module 2.
Question 29Mr. Rolando’s ground floor floods each rainy season. What is the best readiness action?
Name the barrier, the fix and how you will verify it. A flood-prone ground floor is a storage and location problem, not a reason to deny PD. Module 2.
Question 30Which documentation best reflects a remediable barrier?
It names who does what and how success will be checked. The others give the next nurse nothing to act on. Module 2.
Question 31Which finding defines exit-site infection in the 2023 ISPD recommendations?
Pus is the defining feature. Redness alone may be mechanical or part of healing. Crust and discomfort are nonspecific. Module 3.
Question 32What defines tunnel infection?
Tunnel infection is inflammation along the course under the skin. Cloudy effluent points to peritonitis; fever alone is nonspecific. Module 3.
Question 33Mr. Dante, three weeks after insertion, climbs scaffolding with his catheter tucked loosely in his waistband. Why does this matter?
The new lining of the tract is fragile for weeks. Repeated pulling injures it and invites infection. Module 3.
Question 34Which organism is classically linked to periluminal infection and catheter loss?
S. aureus, together with P. aeruginosa, are the classic periluminal organisms. Module 3.
Question 35What is the 2023 ISPD target for the overall exit-site infection rate?
The 2023 update set a target of no more than 0.40 episodes per year at risk. Module 3.
Question 36Which prevention measure carries the only 1A recommendation in the 2022 peritonitis guideline?
Insertion prophylaxis is the 1A recommendation. Routine topical antibiotics were downgraded in 2023. Module 3.
Question 37How should the PDOPPS association between topical exit-site antibiotics and peritonitis be described?
The rate ratio was 0.79 with a confidence interval that crossed 1, in an observational design. That is a hint, not proof. Module 3.
Question 38Ms. Cora has 5 mm of redness, no drainage and no tunnel signs. She carried heavy boxes last weekend. Best action?
Redness alone after trauma needs measurement and follow-up, not automatic treatment. Module 3.
Question 39Purulent exit-site discharge plus new cloudy effluent should prompt:
Cloudy effluent means possible peritonitis, which may be catheter-related. Both problems need attention today. Module 3.
Question 40When should an exit-site swab be taken relative to ordered antibiotics?
Cultures taken before antibiotics are more likely to identify the organism, which guides the prescriber’s choice. Module 3.
Question 41A program had 11 exit-site infections over 34.5 patient-years at risk. What is the rate?
11 divided by 34.5 is about 0.32, within the 2023 target of no more than 0.40. Module 3.
Question 42A cuff visible at the exit site suggests:
An extruding cuff is a same-day escalation. Module 3.
Question 43Which catheter interventions short of full replacement did the 2023 update describe for persistent infection?
These are procedures done by the access team. Module 3.
Question 44Why should exit-site findings be documented the same way at every visit?
Consistent descriptions, with measurements, show whether the site is improving or worsening. Module 3.
Question 45Which route of infection is mainly addressed by exchange technique rather than exit-site care?
Technique protects the inside of the system. Exit-site care protects the outside. Module 3.
Question 46Why does flushing fresh solution to the drain bag before filling reduce peritonitis risk?
That is the mechanism of flush before fill. It does not warm, remove glucose or measure anything. Module 4.
Question 47In the Cochrane review of 12 trials, Y-set or double-bag systems compared with standard systems:
Randomized evidence showed a large reduction, which is why twin-bag systems are the standard. Module 4.
Question 48Fill 2,000 mL, drain 1,850 mL. What is net UF?
1,850 minus 2,000 is −150 mL: fluid was absorbed. Module 4.
Question 49Fill 2,000 mL, drain 2,350 mL. What is net UF?
2,350 minus 2,000 is +350 mL: fluid was removed. Module 4.
Question 50A bag has a small leak at the seal. Best action?
A compromised bag may be contaminated. Never use it, and report the defect so the supplier can check the batch. Module 4.
Question 51The patient touches the tip of the new bag connector; the clamp is closed and no fluid has entered. This is:
The system stayed closed and nothing was infused after the break. Module 4.
Question 52During a brownout, the patient touches the connection with the clamp open and the fill continues. This is best classified as:
The system was open at the touch and fluid entered afterward. Module 4.
Question 53What information matters most in a contamination call?
These details tell the team which response fits. Module 4.
Question 54Which warming method is acceptable?
Other methods risk overheating or contaminating the bag. Module 4.
Question 55Mr. Gary’s UF fell sharply in one week, just after his son began weighing bags on a different scale. What should you consider first?
Rule out a measurement change before reading a falling UF as physiology. Module 4.
Question 56A patient wipes the touched connector with alcohol and continues. The best teaching response is:
Wiping does not restore sterility, and blame discourages reporting. Module 4.
Question 57How can a patient check effluent clarity simply?
If print cannot be read through it, the effluent may be cloudy. Module 4.
Question 58Mr. Efren’s wife has been buying supplies from an eight-month-old prescription card. What is the best response?
Verification against the current order is nursing work. The mismatch may explain clinical changes, so the nephrologist needs to know. Module 4.
Question 59Who decides whether preventive antibiotics are given after wet contamination?
Antibiotic decisions belong to the prescriber or a written standing protocol. Module 4.
Question 60Why is a single touch contamination significant in PD?
A few bacteria can grow during the dwell, which is why the response is time-sensitive. Module 4.
Question 61The programmed last-fill solution differs from the written order. What should happen?
Never start on a mismatch, and never guess which side is right. Ask the prescriber and document who resolved it. Module 5.
Question 62Why do short overnight dwells help small-solute clearance?
Frequent fresh fills restore the gradient before it flattens. Module 5.
Question 63A long glucose day dwell may:
The glucose is absorbed and reabsorption continues, which is why icodextrin is often used for the day dwell. Module 5.
Question 64Mr. Ramon has sharp pelvic pain at the end of every drain. What does tidal mode do for him?
Partial drains keep some fluid in, which reduces end-of-drain pain and alarms. The prescriber decides on tidal mode. Module 5.
Question 65In PDOPPS, higher facility APD use was:
The rate ratio was 0.95 per 10 percentage points, confidence interval 0.91 to 1.00, in an observational design. Module 5.
Question 66A drain alarm occurs and the patient reports severe abdominal pain. First priority?
Person first. Severe pain with an alarm is a clinical problem until proven otherwise. Module 5.
Question 67Which check is usually permitted for a drain alarm?
External checks per the device instructions are permitted. The others are unsafe. Module 5.
Question 68Drain volumes fall over several nights and the patient has had no bowel movement for four days. This most suggests:
A loaded bowel can displace the catheter tip. Constipation is a common cause of catheter dysfunction. Module 5.
Question 69What must accompany an escalation for recurring low drain?
Complete data let the team decide without calling back. Module 5.
Question 70When may backup CAPD be used during a power failure?
Backup must be planned and validated before the storm, not improvised during it. Module 5.
Question 71Swelling of the genitals during APD fill suggests:
Leaks can track into the abdominal wall and genitals. Module 5.
Question 72A patient’s drains and UF per night are unchanged, but three nights last week stopped early and weight rose 1.4 kg. What should you explore first?
Read completion before volumes. Undelivered therapy explains the weight gain; the reason may be social, technical or fear of alarms. Module 5.
Question 73Breathlessness soon after the last fill should prompt:
A pleural leak or overfill can make breathing difficult and needs urgent assessment. Module 5.
Question 74Who sets the APD prescription?
The nurse verifies the program against the order and monitors the result. Module 5.
Question 75Why is the cycler record described as a monitoring tool?
The record carries clinical signals about the catheter, the bowel, the abdomen and the household. Module 5.
Question 76In ADEMEX, increasing peritoneal creatinine clearance:
Clearance rose and stayed higher, but survival was the same. Module 6.
Question 77Kt/V urea is best described as:
It is a laboratory surrogate, not an outcome patients feel. Module 6.
Question 78The 2020 ISPD goal-directed prescribing recommendations are:
They are labeled practice points because the evidence base is limited. Do not assign a grade. Module 6.
Question 79When should the first PET be done according to the 2021 ISPD recommendations?
The timing carries GRADE 1A. Later PETs follow clinical indications. Module 6.
Question 80A faster peritoneal solute transfer rate is associated with:
This is a GRADE 1A association in the 2021 recommendations. Module 6.
Question 81Which 4-hour PET result suggests low UF capacity with 3.86% glucose?
The ISPD 2021 threshold for 3.86% glucose is less than 400 mL (GRADE 1B). Module 6.
Question 82And with 2.27% glucose?
The threshold for 2.27% glucose is less than 100 mL. Module 6.
Question 83What does the sodium dip at 1 hour of a 3.86% dwell help assess?
A reduced dip suggests impaired free-water transport. Module 6.
Question 84Mrs. Gemma’s PET was filled 25 minutes late but sampled by the clock, and the final drain was estimated. What should you report?
Report deviations with the result. A spoiled test can mislabel a membrane and change a prescription unnecessarily. Module 6.
Question 85A 24-hour effluent collection missed one bag. Best action?
Transparency preserves the result’s meaning. An estimate looks like evidence but is not. Module 6.
Question 86Which modifiable peritonitis risk factor named in the 2022 ISPD recommendations also appears in metabolic monitoring?
Low potassium is common in PD and is a named modifiable risk factor. Module 6.
Question 87In the Hong Kong trial, patients assigned to the lowest Kt/V target (1.5 to 1.7):
Survival did not differ significantly, but more were withdrawn for clinical problems. Module 6.
Question 88Lola Pacita and Mr. Noel have the same Kt/V. He has lost 3 kg and stopped working; she is meeting her goals. What should your reviews show?
Clearance is one input among many. Your report decides whether the nephrologist sees the difference. Module 6.
Question 89Icodextrin compared with glucose for the long dwell:
This is from the ISPD 2021 recommendations. Whether to use it is the prescriber’s decision. Module 6.
Question 90When reporting a trend, which statement is most useful?
Baseline, interval, current values and context. Module 6.
Question 91A patient feels well but reports cloudy effluent. What is the response?
Cloudy effluent without pain still counts, especially in APD and in older or diabetic patients. Module 7.
Question 92How many of the three ISPD 2022 criteria are needed to diagnose peritonitis?
At least two of three: clinical features, the cell count criterion and a positive culture. Module 7.
Question 93Which effluent result meets the cell-count criterion after a dwell of at least 2 hours?
That is the 2022 criterion. Red cells and lymphocytes do not meet it. Module 7.
Question 94Why record dwell duration with the sample?
The same count means different things after a 90-minute and an 8-hour dwell. Module 7.
Question 95What is the ISPD 2022 target for culture-negative peritonitis?
Less than 15% (GRADE 1C). Higher rates usually point to sampling problems. Module 7.
Question 96Sampling before antibiotics is preferred. What if the patient is septic and sampling is delayed?
Treatment must not wait for an unstable patient. Module 7.
Question 97Mang Isko had five days without a bowel movement and grows mixed gram-negative organisms. Which route fits?
Constipation and mixed bowel organisms suggest enteric peritonitis. Module 7.
Question 98S. aureus peritonitis 19 days after finishing treatment for S. aureus peritonitis is:
Same organism, within 4 weeks of completing therapy. Module 7.
Question 99E. coli peritonitis 19 days after finishing treatment for S. aureus peritonitis is:
Different organism, within 4 weeks. Module 7.
Question 100What did the 2022 update change about refractory peritonitis?
A falling count beyond day 5 allows watchful management rather than mandatory removal. Module 7.
Question 101Which is a modifiable peritonitis risk factor named in the 2022 ISPD update?
Along with pets in the exchange area and histamine-2 receptor antagonist use. Module 7.
Question 102A unit finds 5 of 18 episodes culture-negative; three samples sat over a weekend. What is the best interpretation?
28% is above the target. Transport delays, late sampling and wrong containers are fixable process problems. Module 7.
Question 103Why does UF often fall during peritonitis?
An inflamed membrane absorbs glucose faster. Watch weight and UF during the episode. Module 7.
Question 104Ms. Andrea’s bag is pink and hazy on day 2 of her period, with no pain or fever. What should happen?
Treat it as possible peritonitis until the team decides. Module 7.
Question 105Which element makes a peritonitis handoff closed-loop?
Closing the loop means someone has accepted the plan, and someone will follow the result. Module 7.
Question 106Which finding changes low-drain troubleshooting into an emergency response?
Breathlessness at rest may mean acute fluid overload or a pleural leak. The others are routine to same-day concerns. Module 8.
Question 107What is a common reversible cause of outflow failure in many programs?
A loaded bowel can push the catheter tip out of the pelvis. Bowel care often restores drainage. Module 8.
Question 108Which action is unsafe without a specific order and training?
Force can push a plug deeper, tear tissue or introduce bacteria. The other checks are permitted when the patient is stable. Module 8.
Question 109Ms. Weng has new right-sided heaviness and breathlessness after every fill, with falling UF. This suggests:
Dialysate may be crossing into the chest. Stop fills and call. Module 8.
Question 110Mang Pedring has a painless groin bulge that grows with fills and goes back when he lies down. This is most likely:
It becomes urgent if painful, firm, red or accompanied by vomiting. Module 8.
Question 111A painful, firm, red hernia with vomiting requires:
Bowel trapped in a hernia can lose its blood supply. Module 8.
Question 112Mrs. Linda’s gauze soaks with clear fluid after each fill, nine days after an urgent-start catheter. This suggests:
The tunnel has not sealed. The team decides on smaller fills, position or a pause. Module 8.
Question 113Uneven, doughy abdominal wall swelling with falling UF suggests:
Fluid may be dissecting into the tissue planes of the abdominal wall. Module 8.
Question 114Which three details matter most after a power outage during APD?
These tell the team what was lost and whether the backup plan is safe to use. Module 8.
Question 115Pain in the last minutes of drain is usually due to:
Report it; the team may consider tidal APD. Module 8.
Question 116When may backup CAPD be used after an outage?
Backup is planned and validated in advance. Module 8.
Question 117Blood-tinged effluent with dizziness and falling blood pressure requires:
Bleeding with signs of low blood volume is an emergency. Module 8.
Question 118Why does a full abdomen raise the risk of leaks and hernias?
That is why lifting heavy loads, such as rice sacks, matters. Module 8.
Question 119A long-term former PD patient has recurrent bowel obstruction symptoms and weight loss. What should be considered and reported?
This rare late complication can appear even after PD has stopped. Module 8.
Question 120Which item belongs in a typhoon go-bag for a PD patient?
With a go-bag, PD can continue in an evacuation center. Module 8.
Question 121What evidence best supports independent competence?
Readiness is shown, not stated. Attendance and signatures do not show performance. Module 9.
Question 122Which question is a true teach-back?
Teach-back asks the learner to explain in their own words. Yes-or-no questions invite a polite yes. Module 9.
Question 123How does the 2025 ISPD teaching position paper characterize its evidence?

Question 124In PDOPPS, facility training of 6 or more days was:

Question 125A caregiver completes a setup correctly only after reminders about the connector. Best action?

Question 126Mrs. Corazon’s son will replace his sister as her caregiver. What is needed?
A change of caregiver is a retraining trigger. Watching is not the same as doing. Module 9.
Question 127Which is a retraining trigger?
Peritonitis, exit-site infection, contamination, drift, a device or caregiver change and hospitalization all trigger reassessment. Module 9.
Question 128Which training method best fits adult learners?
Adults learn best in short sessions tied to what they want, with practice and coaching. Module 9.
Question 129Why should training cover problems, not only routine steps?
The routine keeps the patient well on good days. Problem responses keep them safe on bad days. Module 9.
Question 130Mang Fred nods at every explanation but cannot find the strength on the bag. A suitable adaptation is:
Adapt the method to the learner, then confirm by observation. Module 9.
Question 131Why make mistakes in training feel safe?

Question 132Who should be trained for a task?

Question 133Which element belongs in readiness documentation?
The next nurse needs to know what was seen, what helped and when to check again. Module 9.
Question 134Mr. Arturo has had a year without peritonitis but now rests the cap on the table during fills. What is appropriate?
Drift is normal after months of success. Catching and correcting it is part of the job. Module 9.
Question 135What does health literacy practice require?
Meet the learner where they are, and check by watching. Module 9.
Question 136Why can some glucose meters give falsely high readings in patients on icodextrin?

Question 137What is the main danger of that interference?

Question 138Why is low potassium more common in PD than in hemodialysis?
PD removes potassium every hour, and many patients eat little or still follow hemodialysis-era restrictions. Module 10.
Question 139Old and new prescriptions conflict at discharge. What should the nurse do?
Never send a patient home with two versions of the order. Module 10.
Question 140What makes culture follow-up closed-loop?
A result nobody owns is a result nobody acts on. Module 10.
Question 141The 2026 ISRNM and ISPD nutrition recommendations are notable because they:
They mix graded recommendations with expert-consensus practice points. Module 10.
Question 142Which item must be in a ward handoff for a PD patient?
The receiving team may never have handled PD. Tell them who does the next exchange. Module 10.
Question 143Why is protein-energy wasting a concern in PD?
Several mechanisms push in the same direction. Module 10.
Question 144Mrs. Nenita avoids all fruit because of hemodialysis-era advice and has low potassium. Best action?
Potassium decisions belong to the team and dietitian. Do not leave the old rule standing, and do not replace it with your own. Module 10.
Question 145What does the 2026 ISPD position statement recommend about “technique failure”?
Specific causes point to specific fixes. A vague label hides them. Module 10.
Question 146After a long admission for peritonitis, what should discharge planning include?
Peritonitis and long admissions are both retraining triggers. Module 10.
Question 147Mr. Lando and Mr. Toto both leave PD for hemodialysis. Which made Mr. Lando’s exit planned?
Raising the predictive trend early allowed access planning and an outpatient start. Module 10.
Question 148Why ask about constipation at routine visits?

Question 149Glucose absorbed from PD solution:
The same calories mean different things for Lola Ising and for Mr. Ben. Module 10.
