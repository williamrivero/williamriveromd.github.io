How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Attending Pediatric Nephrologist series. Each sitting draws 40 questions at random from a bank of 145, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark32 of 40 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.
What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

  1. Pass all 29 module quizzes. Each module quiz is passed at 80%, or at the pass mark the module states. The Certificate checklist above the examination shows which modules are done; each line links to that module's quiz.
  2. Use the same device and browser for the modules and this examination. Progress is saved in your browser, not in an account. On iPhone and iPad, the Home Screen app and Safari keep separate progress.
  3. Pass this timed examination (80%; where these instructions say so, every safety-critical question must also be correct). You have one minute per question from the moment you tap Start the examination; only your first check of a set counts. If you pass before finishing the modules, your pass is saved: finish the module quizzes, then come back here.
  4. Type your name the way you want it printed and tap Make my certificate. You can tick Add a preceptor sign-off line if your unit will sign the printed copy.

  6. Be online when you make it, so the certificate gets its QR code. Anyone can scan the code, or enter it at renalcarematters.com/verify, to confirm it.

To make the QR code work, your name, track, date and score are saved with the certificate code; to have them removed, use Report a correction on any guide.

Examination

End-of-course examination

Question 1A hospital tells you that your PNSP certification means you may perform any pediatric kidney procedure there. What actually grants that permission?
Certification shows training; the facility grants privileges for specific procedures, modalities and patient sizes. Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) accreditation and Academy completion are neither. Module 1.
Question 2A PhilHealth pediatric PD annex lists a solution bag volume. How should it be used in a child’s PD order?
Coverage is not a prescription. Bag volumes are supply units; the fill volume is set from the child’s size, tolerance and the unit’s pediatric protocol. Module 1.
Question 3What is the status of the pediatric automated PD pilot after July 31, 2026, as of this module?
Advisory 2026-0013 extended the pilot to July 31, 2026. No later issuance was found, so the status is shown as unresolved. Module 1.
Question 4A referral note says only “pedia nephro to follow.” What is the attending’s first task?
Ownership must be explicit. Until a written co-management note exists, the person facing the decision owns it. Module 1.
Question 5A 13-year-old disagrees with a treatment her parents have accepted. What does the AAP describe as the right approach?
Parental permission and the child’s assent are both part of an active process, with the adolescent’s voice carrying increasing weight. Module 1.
Question 6A 12-year-old athlete taking creatine has a creatinine above the pediatric range. What is the best next step?
Muscle mass, supplements and recent exercise raise creatinine without changing glomerular filtration rate (GFR). A cystatin C estimate is less dependent on muscle. Module 2.
Question 7In which child is a creatinine-based estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) most likely to be falsely high?
Low muscle mass means little creatinine is produced, so the estimate overstates GFR. Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) 2024 suggests a cystatin C-based equation in such children. Module 2.
Question 8By AAP 2017, how is a blood pressure of 132/84 mm Hg classified in a 14-year-old?
From age 13, AAP 2017 uses adult-style cut points: 130/80 to 139/89 mm Hg is stage 1 hypertension. Module 2.
Question 9Which sample best quantifies proteinuria in a school-age child while excluding orthostatic protein?
A first-morning UPCR avoids the protein that appears when a child is upright and active. IPNA defines nephrotic range as UPCR ≥200 mg/mmol (2 mg/mg). Module 2.
Question 10What did the Chronic Kidney Disease in Children study (CKiD) U25 equations change compared with the bedside Schwartz formula?
Chronic Kidney Disease in Children study (CKiD) U25 uses K × height / creatinine with sex-specific or age- and sex-dependent K, and parallel cystatin C equations, developed in 928 participants aged 1 to 25. Module 2.
Question 11Why do hypotonic maintenance fluids cause hyponatremia in hospitalized children?
Non-osmotic ADH release from vomiting, pain, volume depletion and lung or brain disease means infused free water is kept. Module 3.
Question 12Which statement matches the AAP 2018 maintenance fluid guideline?
The guideline excludes neonates under 28 days, renal dysfunction and other groups, where fluids must be individualized. Module 3.
Question 13What did the PIMS trial show?
PIMS, a double-blind randomized controlled trial (RCT), found 12 of 319 versus 35 of 322 children developed hyponatremia; no cerebral edema occurred in either group. Module 3.
Question 14A child is seizing with acute hyponatremia. What is the correct immediate principle?
Hyponatremic encephalopathy is an emergency; hypertonic saline reverses brain swelling. Restriction alone does not. Module 3.
Question 15In which dengue phase is fluid overload most likely to appear?
World Health Organization (WHO) describes reabsorption of leaked fluid in recovery; children given generous fluid during the leak can become overloaded. Module 3.
Question 16What does intravenous calcium do in hyperkalemia?
Calcium protects the heart while shifting and removal take effect; it does not change the potassium concentration. Module 4.
Question 17A well 5-day-old has a potassium of 7.2 mmol/L on a squeezed heel-prick sample and a normal electrocardiogram (ECG). What is the best next step?
Pseudohyperkalemia from hemolysis and tissue squeezing is common in small children. Treat the electrocardiogram (ECG) and confirm the number. Module 4.
Question 18Which set fulfills laboratory tumor lysis syndrome by Cairo–Bishop in a child?
Two or more of potassium ≥6.0, phosphate ≥2.1 (children), uric acid ≥476 µmol/L or calcium ≤1.75 mmol/L, within 3 days before to 7 days after therapy. Module 4.
Question 19What did the pediatric randomized trial of rasburicase versus allopurinol show?
In 52 high-risk children, uric acid fell 86% versus 12% four hours after the first dose. The trial was not powered for dialysis or death. Module 4.
Question 20A child on amphotericin B has hypokalemia that does not correct despite repeated potassium. What should you check?
Hypomagnesemia causes refractory hypokalemia and hypocalcemia; amphotericin B and aminoglycosides are common culprits. Module 4.
Question 21A 15-year-old has repeated office readings of 134/82 mm Hg, confirmed by auscultation. How does AAP 2017 classify this?
From age 13, AAP 2017 uses adult-style cut points: 130/80 to 139/89 mm Hg is stage 1. Module 5.
Question 22How fast should acute severe hypertension with encephalopathy be lowered, according to AAP 2017?
Expert opinion (grade D, weak): a shifted autoregulatory range makes fast falls dangerous. Module 5.
Question 23What did ESCAPE show?
heart rate (HR) 0.65; about 12 fewer children per 100 reached a 50% GFR fall or kidney failure over 5 years. Withdrawals did not differ. Module 5.
Question 24Which child most needs an extensive search for a secondary cause of hypertension?
Renal and renovascular causes are more common in younger children; stage 2 pressure with abnormal kidneys needs a full workup. Module 5.
Question 25KDIGO 2021 suggests which blood pressure target for children with CKD?
Recommendation 5.1 (2C), based largely on ESCAPE. When ABPM is unavailable, office targets are ungraded practice points. Module 5.
Question 26A postnatal ultrasound at 12 hours of life shows mild pelvic fullness in a boy with antenatal bilateral hydronephrosis. Why is this reassuring finding unreliable?
Early relative oliguria can underestimate dilation. A boy with a distended bladder still needs action now. Module 6.
Question 27In the UTD system, how is a postnatal APRPD of 16 mm with no other findings classified?
P2 is APRPD ≥15 mm or peripheral calyceal dilation. P3 requires parenchymal, ureteral or bladder abnormalities. Module 6.
Question 28What is the strongest predictor of long-term kidney function in boys with posterior urethral valves?
Multiple cohorts and a meta-analysis of 1,731 boys support nadir creatinine; day 1 creatinine reflects the mother’s. Module 6.
Question 29What did a 2016 review conclude about fetal intervention for bladder outlet obstruction?
The benefit is thought to come from relieving oligohydramnios during lung development; kidney outcomes have not changed. Module 6.
Question 30A 6-month-old with valves and a low nadir creatinine is polyuric and falling across weight percentiles. What is the most likely reason?
Dysplastic tubules concentrate poorly and waste sodium; a good nadir does not mean the tubules are normal. Module 6.
Question 31A 10-month-old with fever has a bag urine culture growing E. coli and Enterococcus. What is the best next step?
A bag culture, especially with mixed growth, does not establish UTI. The AAP definition requires pyuria and at least 50,000 colonies per mL of a single uropathogen in an appropriately collected specimen. Module 7.
Question 32What did RIVUR show about renal scarring?
Prophylaxis halved recurrences (39/302 vs 72/305) but did not change scarring, and resistance to transmembrane pressure (TMP)-SMX rose (63% vs 19%). Module 7.
Question 33In PREDICT, what was the number needed to treat for 2 years to prevent one first UTI in infants with grade III to V reflux?
First UTI 21.2% vs 35.6%; number needed to treat (NNT) 7 (95% confidence interval (CI) 4 to 29). New scars and eGFR did not differ substantially, and resistant and non-E. coli organisms were more common with prophylaxis. Module 7.
Question 34After a first febrile UTI in an 8-month-old, which imaging does the AAP 2011 guideline advise routinely?
VCUG is not recommended routinely after the first febrile UTI. Ultrasound findings, an atypical course or recurrence trigger it. Module 7.
Question 35A 6-year-old girl has a fourth cystitis, a normal ultrasound, voids twice a day and has hard stools. What comes first?
Bladder and bowel dysfunction drives recurrence. In the RIVUR/CUTIE cohort, BBD carried an adjusted hazard ratio of 2.07 for recurrence. Module 7.
Question 36A 3-year-old has hypokalemic alkalosis, normal magnesium, high urine chloride and high urine calcium. Which segment is most likely affected?
This is the loop-diuretic picture of Bartter syndrome. Gitelman (distal convoluted tubule) typically brings low magnesium and low urine calcium. Module 8.
Question 37A child with hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis has a very low urine chloride. What does that point to?
When chloride is lost outside the kidney, the kidney conserves it. Salt-wasting tubulopathies and active diuretics raise urine chloride. Module 8.
Question 38What did ILLUMINATE-A demonstrate for lumasiran in primary hyperoxaluria type 1?
Urinary oxalate fell by 53.5 percentage points more than placebo. The trial was small (39 patients) and short, and it did not test kidney failure or stones. Module 8.
Question 39What is the strength of evidence for early Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibition in Alport syndrome?
The direction is consistent but the precision is weak: observational registry data plus an underpowered randomized trial. Module 8.
Question 40A genetic panel reports one pathogenic variant and one variant of uncertain significance in a recessive tubulopathy gene. What is the best response?
Under ACMG/AMP classification only pathogenic and likely pathogenic variants support a diagnosis. A VUS can be reclassified either way. Module 8.
Question 41A nephrotic 4-year-old has tachycardia, capillary refill of 4 seconds, abdominal pain and a Fractional excretion of sodium (FENa) of 0.1%. Furosemide is ordered. What do you advise?
These are signs of hypovolemia. The IPNA recommends withholding diuretics in hypovolemia (grade X, strong) and using albumin; Fractional excretion of sodium (FENa) below 0.2% suggested volume contraction in a small prospective study. Module 9.
Question 42By the IPNA 2022 definitions, which course is frequently relapsing?
FRNS is ≥2 relapses in the first 6 months after remission, or ≥3 in any 12 months. Two consecutive relapses on therapy or within 14 days of stopping define steroid dependence. Module 9.
Question 43What did PREDNOS show?
FRNS (50% vs 53%), SDNS (44% vs 42%) and need for other immunosuppression did not differ. KDIGO 2025 recommends 8 or 12 weeks (1B). Module 9.
Question 44Which feature in a child with a first episode of nephrotic syndrome calls for kidney biopsy according to the IPNA?
Atypical features (macroscopic hematuria, low C3, acute kidney injury (AKI) not from hypovolemia, sustained hypertension, arthritis or rash) call for biopsy (grade A, strong). Typical disease over age 1 does not. Module 9.
Question 45A nephrotic child on high-dose glucocorticoids is due for school vaccinations. What is correct?
The IPNA reports a low risk of vaccine-induced relapse, recommends avoiding live vaccines during high-dose immunosuppression (grade X), and recommends vaccinating the household (grade A). Module 9.
Question 46A child relapses twice in a row, once during the taper and once 9 days after stopping glucocorticoids, and always remits. How is the course classified?
Two consecutive relapses during therapy or within 14 days of stopping define steroid dependence (IPNA 2022). Remission each time excludes resistance. Module 10.
Question 47What did the randomized levamisole trial show in frequently relapsing children?
Levamisole lengthened remissions; most children still relapsed within the year. Neutropenia occurred in 4 of 50 and reversed. Module 10.
Question 48What proportion of children with SRNS have a monogenic cause, according to the IPNA 2020 recommendations?
With exome sequencing, 10% to 30% are diagnosed with a monogenic disease, more often with early onset, family history or extrarenal features. Module 10.
Question 49A child with confirmed SRNS has two pathogenic NPHS2 variants. What does the IPNA recommend?
Monogenic SRNS rarely responds to immunosuppression (grade B, moderate recommendation to withhold CNI and stop glucocorticoids). Module 10.
Question 50In the Indian trial of tacrolimus versus intravenous cyclophosphamide for SRNS, what was the complete remission rate?
Tacrolimus was superior; three children needed tacrolimus for one additional remission, and cyclophosphamide had more withdrawals for infection. Module 10.
Question 51Which finding most reliably places hematuria in the glomerulus?
Red cell casts form only in the tubule from red cells that crossed the glomerulus. Clots and bright red urine suggest a lower-tract source. Module 11.
Question 52An oliguric 8-year-old with PSGN has edema, crackles and a blood pressure of 150/100. What is the right fluid order?
Nephritic oliguria reflects a low GFR with primary salt retention; the child is volume expanded. Diuretics are the most effective treatment for PSGN hypertension and edema. Module 11.
Question 53Three months after presumed PSGN, a child’s C3 remains low and proteinuria is rising. What is the next step?
C3 returns to normal within 6 to 8 weeks in 80% of postinfectious GN. Persistence with rising proteinuria does not fit PSGN. Module 11.
Question 54According to the IPNA 2025 recommendations, which child with IgA vasculitis should have a kidney biopsy?
Nephrotic-range proteinuria (grade X, strong) and reduced eGFR (grade X, moderate) are indications; moderate proteinuria for 2 to 4 weeks is a weaker indication (grade D). Module 11.
Question 55A child develops gross hematuria on the second day of a febrile sore throat; C3 is normal. What is most likely?
Synpharyngitic hematuria with normal complement points to IgA nephropathy; PSGN follows infection after a latent period with low C3. Module 11.
Question 56What does KDIGO 2024 recommend for all patients with Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), including those with lupus nephritis?
Recommendation 10.2.1.1 is graded 1C. It applies whatever the biopsy class. Module 12.
Question 57How does KDIGO 2024 define complete response of lupus nephritis in children younger than 18?
The guideline uses a body-surface-area-indexed proteinuria threshold for children. Module 12.
Question 58Which statement about BLISS-LN and AURORA 1 is correct?
BLISS-LN: renal response 43% vs 32% at 104 weeks; AURORA 1: complete renal response 41% vs 23% at 52 weeks; both in adults. Module 12.
Question 59A 14-year-old with cola urine, hemoptysis and rapidly rising creatinine has serology pending for three days. What is the right approach?
KDIGO 2024 advises that biopsy should not delay treatment in a compatible presentation, particularly with rapid deterioration; pulmonary-renal syndrome is an emergency. Module 12.
Question 60What did PEXIVAS show in adults with severe Antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (ANCA) vasculitis?
Hazard ratio 0.86 for plasma exchange; reduced-dose glucocorticoids 27.9% vs 25.5%, with an infection incidence rate ratio of 0.69. Module 12.
Question 61A 3-year-old has had bloody diarrhea for two days and is still passing urine. A sibling had the same illness. Which advice best fits the evidence?
Volume restoration is associated with less dialysis in observational data, while antibiotics are associated with HUS in cohort and meta-analysis data. Antimotility drugs slow toxin clearance. Module 13.
Question 62In the Ardissino 2016 study of early volume expansion in STEC-HUS, what does the design allow you to conclude?
It was a single-center comparison with historical controls. The association is strong, but other changes over time may contribute, and it says nothing in favor of fluid for an already overloaded, anuric child. Module 13.
Question 63A child with pneumococcal empyema develops Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) with a positive direct antiglobulin test. Which product should be avoided?
Neuraminidase exposes the T antigen; plasma carries antibodies that bind it. The review cited in this module advises avoiding fresh frozen plasma. Module 13.
Question 64Before plasma therapy or complement blockade in suspected atypical HUS, what should be done?
Plasma and C5 blockade change complement results. Samples take minutes to draw and can be stored and sent later; treatment is not delayed waiting for results. Module 13.
Question 65What did the pediatric single-arm trial of eculizumab in atypical HUS report by week 26?
Greenbaum 2016 had no control group, so it shows response rather than a comparison with plasma therapy. No meningococcal infections occurred in that trial. Module 13.
Question 66A 28-week baby’s creatinine was 1.1 mg/dL on day 1, 0.8 mg/dL on day 4 and 1.3 mg/dL on day 8. What is the reference value for staging?
The neonatal modified KDIGO criteria use the lowest previous creatinine as the reference, because the birth value reflects the mother. Module 14.
Question 67What absolute creatinine defines stage 3 in the neonatal modified KDIGO criteria?
A neonatal creatinine of 2.5 mg/dL already reflects a very low GFR, so the adult 4.0 mg/dL threshold was lowered. Module 14.
Question 68What did AWAKEN show?
AWAKEN was observational: AKI was common and independently associated with death (adjusted OR 4.6), but it does not prove that prevention saves lives. Module 14.
Question 69Which trigger did Baby NINJA use to flag high-risk nephrotoxin exposure?
Flagged babies had daily creatinine; nephrotoxin-associated AKI fell from 30.9% to 11.0% in this single-center project. Module 14.
Question 70Why do automated PD machines not suit most neonates, according to the International Society for Peritoneal Dialysis (ISPD) 2020 pediatric acute PD guideline?
The guideline rates automated peritoneal dialysis (PD) as suitable for pediatric AKI except in neonates, for whom fill volumes are too small (1D). Module 14.
Question 71A 10-year-old on day 6 of dengue has a falling hematocrit, stable blood pressure, a full pulse, pleural effusions and low urine output. What is the best next step?
Falling hematocrit with stable hemodynamics and effusions signals reabsorption. More fluid worsens congestion; bleeding must be excluded. Module 15.
Question 72In Assessment of Worldwide Acute Kidney Injury, Renal Angina, and Epidemiology study (AWARE), what proportion of patients with low urine output would have been missed by creatinine alone?
Creatinine alone failed to identify AKI in 67.2% of patients who met the urine output criterion. Measure urine output. Module 15.
Question 73How is percentage fluid overload calculated in the pediatric CKRT registry?
This is the formula used by Sutherland and colleagues; daily weights are an alternative. Module 15.
Question 74What does the fluid overload meta-analysis by Alobaidi and colleagues show?
The pooled data are observational; sicker children may receive more fluid, so causation is not proven. Module 15.
Question 75Which statement about KDIGO AKI guidance is correct as of November 2026?
Quote the 2012 criteria and grades; use the 2026 acute kidney disease concept as draft guidance only. Module 15.
Question 76In the ppCRRT registry, what was mortality in children who started CKRT with 20% or more fluid overload, compared with less than 10%?
Mortality was 65.6% with ≥20% and 29.4% with <10%. The association is observational and does not prove that earlier initiation saves lives. Module 16.
Question 77An 8 kg infant has a blood volume of about 640 mL. The available CKRT set holds about 150 mL. What follows?
Pediatric literature advises keeping the extracorporeal volume to about 10–15% of blood volume in infants; above that, units prime with blood or blood–albumin, buffered and corrected. Module 16.
Question 78What did the ppCRRT anticoagulation analysis show?
Mean circuit life was 42.1 hours with heparin and 44.7 with citrate, against 27.2 with none. The data are observational. Module 16.
Question 79Which sign suggests citrate accumulation during CKRT?
When the liver cannot metabolize citrate, citrate-bound calcium accumulates, ionized calcium falls and acidosis worsens. Module 16.
Question 80What did the Kwiatkowski 2017 trial of PD versus furosemide in infants after cardiac surgery show?
The primary outcome (negative balance on day 1) was neutral; secondary outcomes favored PD. PD stopped early in 9 of 41 for pleural–peritoneal communication. Module 16.
Question 81What did ESCAPE show?
Hazard ratio 0.65; the end point was a 50% GFR decline or kidney failure, a partly surrogate composite. Module 17.
Question 82According to the KDIGO 2024 pediatric summary, what is the advice on protein intake in children with CKD?
Protein restriction risks growth impairment in children. Module 17.
Question 83Which combination best estimates a child’s time to kidney failure in the CKiD and ESCAPE staging?
Median time to event ranged from over 10 years to 0.8 years across six eGFR and proteinuria stages. Module 17.
Question 84A short child with CKD stage 4 has acidosis, a protein-restricted diet and salt wasting. What comes first?
Growth hormone is considered for persistent growth failure after correctable causes are addressed. Module 17.
Question 85What did the CKiD neurocognitive study find?
It was cross-sectional; it supports screening school performance, not a causal claim. Module 17.
Question 86A child on PD has hemoglobin 8.0 g/dL, ferritin 60 ng/mL and Transferrin saturation (TSAT) 14%. What does the KDIGO 2026 pediatric algorithm indicate first?
Ferritin below 100 ng/mL with transferrin saturation (TSAT) below 40% meets the algorithm’s indication for iron. Module 18.
Question 87Why should transfusion be avoided where possible in a child who may need a kidney transplant?
Sensitization narrows the pool of compatible donors. Module 18.
Question 88What did KDIGO 2017 recommend about growth monitoring in children with CKD G2–G5D?
This is recommendation 3.2.5 of the 2017 update, graded 1B. Module 18.
Question 89What did the Goodman 2000 study of young people on dialysis find?
Exposure accumulated in childhood shows in early adulthood; the study was small and cross-sectional. Module 18.
Question 90What does the CKiD analysis of bicarbonate and growth show?
The authors call for trials; persistent alkali use was associated with better height z scores. Module 18.
Question 91Which kidney replacement therapy do European pediatric experts recommend as preferred for infants?
PD is preferred in infants (1B): it preserves residual function and veins and runs at home. Most centers postpone transplantation until 8–10 kg. Module 19.
Question 92In the ERA Registry, which first transplant was associated with lower mortality and graft failure in childhood-onset kidney replacement therapy (KRT)?
Preemptive (adjusted hazard ratios 0.66 for death, 0.89 for graft failure) and living-donor transplants were associated with better outcomes; the data are observational. Module 19.
Question 93When should withholding KRT in an infant be considered, according to the infant recommendations?
The recommendations grade consensus decision-making for starting or withholding KRT 1B and advise deciding early. Module 19.
Question 94What did the International Pediatric Peritoneal Dialysis Network (IPPN) registry show about mortality on pediatric PD?
Probability of death at 3 years was 5% (2% to 9% by region); income explained 50.1% of between-region variance. Module 19.
Question 95A family asks how many HD sessions PhilHealth gives. What is the best approach?
Coverage is not a prescription; confirm the benefit on the date of service. Module 19.
Question 96Why are pediatric PD fill volumes scaled to body surface area rather than body weight?
Fills prescribed per kilogram are small relative to the membrane in young children, giving fast equilibration and early loss of ultrafiltration. Scaling to BSA removes most of the apparent age difference. Module 20.
Question 97A 10-month-old on PD started the day after catheter insertion has glucose-positive fluid at the exit site. What is the best first step?
This is a pericatheter leak. Lowering intraperitoneal pressure and resting the catheter give it the best chance to seal. Module 20.
Question 98A 3-year-old on rapid nightly cycling has a high Kt/V, a high phosphate and falling height centiles. What is the most likely explanation?
Phosphate needs three to four times longer than urea to equilibrate. A long dwell and a dietitian review address the problem the Kt/V hides. Module 20.
Question 99Which statement about evidence for pediatric PD catheters is correct?
Pediatric catheter guidance is weak (2D) and largely registry-based; omentectomy is recommended for infants without proven preventive value. Module 20.
Question 100A family asks whether the PhilHealth PD package decides how many exchanges their child will do. What is the correct answer?
Coverage is not a prescription. The pediatric automated peritoneal dialysis (APD) pilot status after July 31, 2026 is unresolved and must be confirmed with the PhilHealth liaison. Module 20.
Question 101What does the evidence on caregiver training for pediatric PD show?
The evidence is observational and conflicting; the 2024 pediatric guideline makes weak (2D) suggestions to tailor training and to include a home visit. Module 21.
Question 102A child on APD has slow outflow, good inflow and no stool for four days. What is the first step?
Constipation is the commonest reversible cause of outflow failure; it displaces and compresses the catheter. Module 21.
Question 103Which pattern should raise concern for encapsulating peritoneal sclerosis?
In the European pediatric survey, EPS children had longer PD vintage and most developed ultrafiltration failure a median 2.8 months before obstruction. Module 21.
Question 104A child on PD becomes breathless with a right pleural effusion and falling drains. What is the most likely diagnosis?
Stop PD, image the chest, compare pleural fluid glucose with blood and involve the surgeon. Module 21.
Question 105A family asks whether PhilHealth pediatric APD coverage continues after July 31, 2026. What is the correct answer?
Advisory 2026-0013 extended the pilot to July 31, 2026. Never say it ended or continues without a later issuance. Module 21.
Question 106Which combination meets the 2024 pediatric criteria for PD peritonitis?
Two of three criteria are needed: clinical features, the effluent cell count and differential after an adequate dwell, and a positive culture. Module 22.
Question 107A child on continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) has cloudy effluent at night. When should empirical antibiotics start?
The pediatric guideline states antibiotics should begin as soon as possible after appropriate microbiological specimens are obtained. Module 22.
Question 108Fungal elements are seen in the effluent of a child on PD. What does the 2024 pediatric guideline suggest?
Fungal peritonitis threatens the membrane; removal is suggested at once (2D). Module 22.
Question 109A program has 6 peritonitis episodes in 14 patient-years. Which statement is correct?
Episodes divided by patient-years at risk: 6 / 14 = 0.43. Module 22.
Question 110In the SCOPE collaborative, which factor was associated with a lower peritonitis rate?
Bundle compliance had a rate ratio of 0.49; upward exit sites and touch contamination were associated with more peritonitis. The data are observational. Module 22.
Question 111What does the European Pediatric Dialysis Working Group guideline advise about extracorporeal volume?
A larger circuit takes more of a small child’s blood volume and returns more fluid at the end. Blood or albumin priming is used in babies and small infants. Module 23.
Question 112A very uremic 6-year-old starts HD. Why is a standard adult session at high blood flow unsafe?
Urea falls faster in blood than in brain; water shifts into brain cells. First sessions are short with low blood flow. Module 23.
Question 113A 12-year-old on HD has cramps late in most sessions; she has grown 4 cm since her target weight was set. What is the most likely problem?
In infants and adolescents dry weight should be reassessed almost monthly; a fixed target in a growing child removes tissue, not fluid. Module 23.
Question 114What did the 3H study show?
The findings are associations with surrogates and growth; the authors call for randomized trials. Module 23.
Question 115PhilHealth covers up to 156 HD sessions a year. How should this shape a child’s prescription?
Coverage never becomes clinical frequency. Longer or extra sessions need a documented clinical reason and a cost discussion. Module 23.
Question 116A 13-year-old dialyzing through a tunneled catheter develops rigors 15 minutes after connection. What is the most appropriate first step?
Rigors soon after connection suggest catheter-related bloodstream infection. Cultures first, then empirical therapy covering gram-positive and gram-negative organisms (IDSA A-II), chosen from the unit’s pediatric protocol. Module 24.
Question 117Which culture result makes IDSA 2009 recommend removing an infected hemodialysis catheter?
IDSA recommends removal for S. aureus, Pseudomonas and Candida (A-II). Other organisms can be treated with the catheter in place if the child responds within 2 to 3 days. Module 24.
Question 118What did the IPHN registry show about catheters and fistulas in children?
Catheter infections ran at 1.3 per 1,000 catheter-days; dysfunction 2.5 vs 1.2 per 1,000 days. The study was observational; catheter use was associated with age and with an expected early transplant. Module 24.
Question 119A catheter’s arterial pressure alarm sounds at the start of each session and improves when the child turns the head. What is the most likely cause?
Positional, early-session inflow problems point to tip position. Growth can pull a tip back into the superior vena cava; a chest radiograph and access review follow if it recurs. Module 24.
Question 120Which ESPN 2019 statement applies to a child with a central venous stenosis on the left?
A fistula drains through the central veins on its side. The ESPN group advises avoiding an arteriovenous fistula (AVF) on the side of a central venous stenosis (ungraded) and imaging central veins when stenosis is suspected (2D). Module 24.
Question 121A newly started 12-year-old develops headache, vomiting and restlessness in the first hour of his third session; blood pressure is high and pulse slow. What is the most likely diagnosis?
Early sessions, a very high starting urea and neurologic symptoms with a high pressure and slow pulse point to cerebral edema from the urea gradient. Prevention with limited first sessions matters most. Module 25.
Question 122Foam appears in the venous line and the child suddenly becomes breathless. What should the nurse do with the blood in the circuit?
Suspected air embolism means stopping the pump and clamping; blood is not returned. Lay the child on the left side, head down, and give oxygen. Module 25.
Question 123Under Department of Health (DOH) administrative order (AO) 2013-0003, who is responsible for the quality of water in a hemodialysis clinic?
The order makes the medical director responsible and requires documentation of that involvement, for example by initialing test results. Module 25.
Question 124What did Hothi and colleagues find in children on HD?
In 74 retrospective records, early slope and heart rate change mattered; thresholds at hours 1 to 3 discriminated, but no hour-4 value did. Module 25.
Question 125PhilHealth covers a set number of HD sessions under PC 2024-0023. How should that number be used?
Coverage is not a prescription. Confirm benefit rules on the date of service and write clinical frequency from the child’s needs. Module 25.
Question 126A 14-year-old with posterior urethral valves has a high-pressure bladder and recurrent febrile urinary tract infections (UTIs). A living donor is ready. What is the best next step?
A high-pressure, poorly emptying bladder transmits pressure and infection to the graft. The bladder plan comes before the date. Module 26.
Question 127What does DOH AO 2010-0018 say about non-directed living non-related donation?
The order, aligned with the anti-trafficking law, the Declaration of Istanbul and WHO principles, does not permit non-directed living non-related donation. Module 26.
Question 128Why does genetic testing matter before transplant in a child with steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome?
IPNA reports a monogenic cause in 10–30% of SRNS. The donor kidney does not carry the child’s podocyte defect, but a circulating factor can attack it. Module 26.
Question 129How did the KDIGO 2017 living-donor guideline change donor evaluation?
The guideline favors comprehensive, quantitative risk assessment and shared decision-making; many recommendations are ungraded expert opinion. Module 26.
Question 130A family says the transplant date depends on when PhilHealth approves the Z benefit. What is the correct framing?
Coverage is not a clinical decision. Confirm benefit rules with the Z benefit coordinator on the date of service. Module 26.
Question 131A 16-year-old’s tacrolimus troughs alternate between high and low, and her creatinine has risen 30%. She admits missing evening doses. What is the best next step?
Intermittent dosing causes swinging levels; a higher dose adds toxic peaks without covering missed doses. Work up rejection, BK, obstruction and toxicity, and solve the adherence problem. Module 27.
Question 132What did the USRDS analysis by Foster and colleagues show?
Crude rates peaked at 19 (6.6 per 100 person-years); hazard ratio 1.20 at 17–24 vs 25–29. The design was observational. Module 27.
Question 133What did the TAKE-IT trial demonstrate?
Odds ratio 1.66 for taking doses and 1.74 for timing. The trial was underpowered for rejection and graft loss. Module 27.
Question 134According to the 2024 BK polyomavirus consensus, how long should children be screened with plasma BK viral load?
The schedule extends to 2 years in adults and 3 years in children. Urine cytology can be used where plasma testing is limited. Module 27.
Question 135A transplanted 7-year-old who was EBV-seronegative before transplant has a rising EBV viral load. What does the American Society of Transplantation (AST) guideline name as the best validated intervention?
Reduction of immunosuppression is the best validated intervention; preemptive strategies rest on low-quality evidence, and tissue biopsy diagnoses PTLD. Module 27.
Question 136A family has been giving the same number of milliliters of a liquid medicine after a pharmacy changed its concentration. Which safeguard best prevents this error?
Same volume at a different concentration delivers a different dose. Milligrams, the weight used, and an independent pharmacist check close the gap. Module 28.
Question 137Under the 1993 DOJ rules implementing RA 7610, what must the attending physician do when examining a child who appears to have been abused?
The head of the institution and the attending physician and nurse must report within 48 hours; good-faith reporting is protected from civil or administrative liability. Module 28.
Question 138In the Kaushal study of pediatric inpatients, where did most potential adverse drug events begin?
79% began at ordering, and 34% involved incorrect dosing; reviewers judged order entry systems and ward pharmacists could prevent most. Module 28.
Question 139A child’s PD logbook shows exchanges were not done on several days. The monthly PhilHealth essential-services checklist is due. What should the attending do?
A signature is a statement of fact. A false checklist misleads the payer and hides the child’s clinical problem. Module 28.
Question 140Why is a run chart preferred to a yearly average for a unit’s peritonitis rate?
Perla and colleagues describe run charts, read with probability-based rules, as more useful than summaries that ignore time order. Module 28.
Question 141In the capstone, a candidate scores 9 of 10 but delays treatment of a fever on HD through a catheter until the next session. What is the result?
A pass requires 8 of 10 and every critical action met. A high total cannot offset a missed critical action. Module 29.
Question 142A newborn with suspected posterior urethral valves has a distended bladder and rising creatinine in a hospital without pediatric urology. What is the first priority?
Obstruction is relieved first, and the baby moves to a capable center the same day (Modules 1 and 6). Module 29.
Question 143A family asks whether PhilHealth will cover automated PD for their child after July 31, 2026. What is the correct answer in this track?
PhilHealth Advisory 2026-0013 extended the pilot to July 31, 2026. The track does not say it ended or continues; confirm on the date of service. Module 29.
Question 144At 13, the transplant program asks for a readiness summary for a child with valves whose last bladder study was two years ago. What should come before a date is set?
A high-pressure bladder threatens the graft. The bladder plan is a critical action (Module 26). Module 29.
Question 145When is the transition from pediatric to adult care complete?
The loop closes at the confirmed first adult visit, not at the letter (Modules 27 and 28). Module 29.
