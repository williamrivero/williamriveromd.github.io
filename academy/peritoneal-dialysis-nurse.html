Start here

The nurse in the home program

Most Filipinos with kidney failure start dialysis in a hemodialysis center, often after an urgent admission. Peritoneal dialysis (PD) lets many of them dialyze at home instead, with fewer trips, no needles, and more control of the day. It also asks a great deal: a catheter that must stay clean for years, a technique repeated more than a thousand times a year, and a person at home who must recognize trouble early. Internationally, nearly half of people who start PD are no longer on it at two years.1 Peritonitis, technique drift and unplanned transfers explain much of that, and the PD nurse is the program’s main defense against all three.

This track continues Nephrology Nursing Module 10: Peritoneal Dialysis and Home Dialysis Nursing. It takes a registered nurse from the physiology of a single dwell to the quality numbers of a whole program, in twelve modules over six weeks.

Figure 1 · Exit competencies

A · ReadinessExplain PD physiology, support an informed modality choice, and assess patient and home readiness.
B · Technique and accessVerify treatment, demonstrate aseptic continuous ambulatory PD (CAPD) and automated PD (APD), and assess the catheter and exit site using International Society for Peritoneal Dialysis (ISPD) 2023 definitions.
C · TriageTriage infection, contamination, mechanical and volume problems, and close the loop.
D · TeachingTeach patients and caregivers, judge readiness by return demonstration and teach-back, and plan retraining.
E · Monitoring and transitionsMonitor goal-directed treatment, collect valid samples, and hand over safely between home and hospital.
F · QualityCalculate PD infection and loss-from-PD indicators against ISPD targets and improve one thing at a time.

Prerequisites: a current Philippine nursing license, Nephrology Nursing Module 10 or equivalent, and basic infection prevention and medication safety. Rare emergencies are practiced in simulation, and every simulated event is recorded as simulation.

⚠️

What this series is, and is not

It is a training track written independently from published guidelines, research and Philippine issuances. It is not a license, Philippine Society of Nephrology (PSN) or National Kidney and Transplant Institute (NKTI) training, a Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) staffing credential, continuing professional development (CPD) credit, or clinical privileges. It does not replace your unit’s protocols, the device instructions for use, or the nephrologist’s written orders.

The role

What a peritoneal dialysis nurse does

A PD nurse is part clinician, part teacher, part coordinator. Most of the work happens away from the patient: on the telephone at 6 AM, in a training room with a nervous caregiver, at a kitchen table during a home visit, or in a ward explaining to a colleague how a cycler works. The patient does the treatment; you make sure they can, and you notice early when something is going wrong.

The proposed Philippine job description below was written for this track from the blueprint’s review of Philippine requirements and international employer postings. A facility should adapt it to its own policies and the current PhilHealth contract.

Where your role stops

Under the Philippine Nursing Act (Republic Act 9173), nursing practice includes assessment, health teaching, collaboration and carrying out written orders.12 In PD that becomes a clear division of labor:

You doYou route to the nephrologist or authorized prescriber
Verify the patient, modality, solution, strength and volumes against the written orderAny change in solution, dwell, cycler program or icodextrin use
Compare the treatment actually delivered with the treatment orderedAny medicine started, stopped or changed, including antibiotics
Assess fluid status, symptoms, exit site, tunnel and effluentInterpreting adequacy and membrane tests for a prescription change
Teach, observe return demonstration and judge readiness against program criteriaModality change, catheter procedures and admission decisions

RememberThe line is not about importance. Your observations are often the team’s first warning. Report the pattern with numbers and times; leave the prescription to the prescriber.

Staffing, experience and how other countries set the bar

The PhilHealth PD First self-assessment tool lists at least one full-time PD nurse, a nurse-to-patient ratio stated as 1:20, a current license from the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), and recognized PD training through the listed pathways with validation by the unit head.14 Do not import the hemodialysis 1:4 ratio into PD; the workload is different. Confirm with the PhilHealth Regional Office how caseload and coverage are counted for your contract.13

Quick reference

Red flags the whole track comes back to

Most PD problems are slow and quiet. A few are not. These situations override routine troubleshooting in every module; learn them before anything else. Exact steps follow your unit’s protocol and the nephrologist’s orders.

Modules

Twelve modules in six weeks

Study the modules in order. Each starts from a plain picture of what is happening, moves to the physiology and the evidence, and ends with what you do at the bedside, on the phone, and in the record.

Week 1 · Foundations

Module 1 How PD Works, and What the PD Nurse Does How diffusion, osmosis and fluid reabsorption work across the peritoneum, CAPD and APD, residual kidney function, and where the nurse's role stops. Module 2 Modality Counseling, Shared Decisions and Home Readiness Goal-directed modality conversations, honest evidence on PD versus hemodialysis, six-domain home readiness, and assisted PD.

Week 2 · Access and CAPD

Module 3 The Catheter, the Tunnel and the Exit Site Catheter anatomy, how bacteria travel the tunnel, ISPD 2023 exit-site and tunnel infection definitions, and same-day triage. Module 4 The Manual Exchange: Measurement and Contamination Flush before fill and its trial evidence, supply checks, honest ultrafiltration measurement, and dry versus wet contamination.

Week 3 · APD and monitoring

Module 5 The Cycler: Setup, Treatment Record and Alarms What each APD prescription element does, cross-checking the cycler, reading the treatment record, and alarms as clinical signals. Module 6 Monitoring, Membrane Testing and Goal-Directed PD Why more clearance did not prolong life, goal-directed review, the peritoneal equilibration test and low-ultrafiltration thresholds, and valid samples.

Week 4 · Infection and urgent problems

Module 7 Peritonitis: Recognition, Sampling and Prevention Routes of infection, the ISPD 2022 diagnostic criteria, the first hour after a cloudy bag, outcome definitions, and prevention. Module 8 Mechanical, Volume and Urgent Problems Outflow failure, leaks, hernias, pleural leak, emergencies that override troubleshooting, and typhoon and power-failure plans.

Week 5 · Teaching and transitions

Module 9 Teach, Validate and Retrain Adult learning, teach-back, the ISPD 2025 teaching position paper, readiness by return demonstration, and retraining triggers. Module 10 Longitudinal Care, Nutrition and Safe Transitions Routine follow-up, nutrition and potassium, the icodextrin glucose-meter hazard, ward handoffs, and planned exits from PD.

Week 6 · Special populations and quality

Module 11 Pediatric, Acute and Assisted PD Orientation to pediatric PD, acute PD for acute kidney injury with ISPD grades, and building an assisted PD plan. Module 12 Philippine Practice, Records and Quality Who governs a Philippine PD program, PhilHealth documentation, infection-rate arithmetic against ISPD targets, and improvement.

Assessment

Self-assessment is not sign-off

Each module ends with a self-assessment of 5 questions drawn at random from a bank of 15; the mastery mark is 4 of 5 on two different sets. The self-assessments are for learning. They tell you what to reread; they do not show that you can do the work.

Remediation. When a domain is weak, the preceptor arranges coached practice and reassesses on an equivalent case. When consent or opportunities limit observation, the gap is recorded and the affected task is deferred or supervised longer. Reassessment is triggered by a relevant incident, an extended absence, a change of device or protocol, and the facility’s scheduled interval.

Completing milestone 1 or 3 never substitutes for milestone 5. Academy completion is an educational learning record only.

End-of-course examination. When you have finished every module, take the end-of-course examination. It draws questions at random from all 12 modules, so no two sittings are the same.

Philippine practice

Where the PD nurse fits in a PhilHealth PD program

PhilHealth pays for peritoneal dialysis through Z-benefit contracting with accredited PD providers, under PhilHealth Circular 2024-0036 and its current annexes.13 The nurse does not decide eligibility or payment, but the records the nurse keeps are what make a claim possible. Confirm every step below with your benefits lead and the current annexes.

Figure 5 · A typical documentation path

  1. EligibilityCheck that the patient has chronic kidney disease stage 5 and is registered in the PhilHealth Dialysis Database, as instructed by benefits staff.
  2. PreauthorizationRoute the request through the contracting team using the current forms.
  3. InitiationRecord training dates, readiness sign-off and the first home treatment.
  4. Follow-upKeep treatment records, visits, laboratory results and supply deliveries current.
  5. Infection episodesRecord each peritonitis and exit-site infection with dates, organism and outcome.
  6. Exit or transferRecord the date and cause using standardized definitions of loss from PD.

The pediatric automated PD pilot was extended through July 31, 2026; its status after that date had not been confirmed when this track was written.15 Benefit amounts and inclusions change, so confirm them with the PhilHealth Regional Office before you counsel a patient.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need hemodialysis experience first?

No, but you need the foundations: kidney assessment, chronic kidney disease, infection prevention, medication safety, and Nephrology Nursing Module 10 or its equivalent. Many employers prefer a year of PD experience for independent roles; this track does not replace that.

Does completing the track make me a recognized PD nurse for PhilHealth?

No. PhilHealth lists specific training pathways and unit-head validation.14 The Academy records your learning; recognized training, privileges and credentialing are separate.

How long does it take?

The suggested pilot is six weeks and 60 planned hours: 24 of self-study and cases, 12 of simulation or coached review, and 24 of supervised practice. Extend supervised practice until you have the required opportunities.

Can I study only the modules I need?

Yes, for review. For sign-off, work through all twelve in order, because later modules assume the physiology and triage habits of earlier ones.

Does the track cover pediatric and acute PD?

At orientation level only, in Module 11. Children’s prescriptions and acute PD in unstable patients belong to specialized teams, and independent practice needs further training.

Is any patient information stored?

No. Quiz answers are scored in your browser, and the optional saved result stays on your device. Keep patient records and staff credentials in your facility’s approved systems.

For trainers and preceptors

Using the series in a training program

A PD nurse educator or unit head can use these modules as the guided-study part of a new PD nurse’s orientation. The trainer and preceptor page gathers the proposed hours, completion criteria, and every skills checklist in one printable place. These are proposals for a provider to adapt and validate on its own equipment; they are not requirements of any regulator.
