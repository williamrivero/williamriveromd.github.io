Before you begin

How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Water Treatment System Technician series. Each sitting draws 30 questions at random from a bank of 104, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark24 of 30 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.
What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

Examination

End-of-course examination

Question 1Why must dialysis water be cleaner than drinking water?
The gut protects a person who drinks water. In dialysis, hundreds of liters a week are separated from blood only by a membrane. Module 1.
Question 2Which contaminant destroys red blood cells and is removed mainly by carbon tanks?
Chloramine and chlorine cause hemolysis. Activated carbon is the barrier against them. Module 1.
Question 3What is endotoxin?
Endotoxin comes from the cell wall of certain bacteria and can cause reactions even when no live bacteria are found. Module 1.
Question 4In the international standard, what is the maximum total chlorine in dialysis water?
The limit is 0.1 mg/L, far below the level in tap water. Module 1.
Question 5Which fluid has the strictest limits for bacteria and endotoxin?
Ultrapure fluid has limits of less than 0.1 colony-forming unit (CFU)/mL and less than 0.03 endotoxin unit (EU)/mL. Module 1.
Question 6What is an action level?
Acting at the action level keeps results from ever reaching the limit. Module 1.
Question 7The utility announces a higher disinfectant dose. The reverse osmosis display is normal. What should you do first?
The reverse osmosis display does not measure chlorine. Test where chlorine is removed and tell the nurse before patients are connected. Module 1.
Question 8Who carries overall responsibility for dialysis water quality in a facility?
The medical director is responsible. The technician’s duty is to test, record, stop, and report. Module 1.
Question 9A test kit reports hardness as 2 grains per gallon. About how many mg/L as calcium carbonate is that?
One grain per gallon is about 17 mg/L, so two is about 34 mg/L. Module 1.
Question 10Several patients’ hemoglobin levels fall over two months with no other explanation. Which water problem should a technician think of first?
Chloramine damages red blood cells and can show first as a pattern of worsening anemia. Module 1.
Question 11A unit switches from city water to a deep well. What is true of the well water?
Well water is typically undisinfected and mineral-rich, so the softener works harder and microbial control needs attention. Module 2.
Question 12Which valve must be closed and locked or tagged during treatment?
An open carbon bypass sends chlorine and chloramine directly downstream. Module 2.
Question 13Iron in the feed water mainly threatens which components?
Iron fouls resin and membranes and leaves reddish deposits. Module 2.
Question 14Why does adding stations threaten the carbon tanks even if the reverse osmosis unit keeps up?
Contact time is bed volume divided by flow; more flow means less time. Module 2.
Question 15What is validation?
Validation is the evidence that a new or changed system is fit for use. Module 2.
Question 16A tanker delivers water during a shortage. How is it handled?
Each delivery can differ, so it is tested before use. Module 2.
Question 17After a service visit, what is checked before release?
Valves left in service positions are a common and dangerous error. Module 2.
Question 18On a feed water report, which line most directly tells you how hard the softener must work?
Hardness is the calcium and magnesium the softener must remove. Module 2.
Question 19A filter reads 58 psi before and 46 psi after. What is the pressure drop?
58 minus 46 is 12 psi. Compare it with the unit’s limit and with earlier readings. Module 3.
Question 20What does a softener remove?
The resin exchanges calcium and magnesium for sodium, protecting the membrane from scale. Module 3.
Question 21Why are softeners set to regenerate outside treatment hours?
A valve or timer fault during regeneration can send salt water downstream. Module 3.
Question 22After a power interruption, which softener check comes first?
A wrong clock moves regeneration into treatment hours. Module 3.
Question 23During a backwash, in which direction does water flow through the bed?
Upward flow lifts the media and carries trapped dirt to drain. Module 3.
Question 24Sand appears in the drain during backwash. What does this suggest?
Media should stay in the tank; losing it means a fault. Module 3.
Question 25A clock-based softener runs out early during an unusually busy week. Why?
More water used means the resin is exhausted before the scheduled regeneration. Module 3.
Question 26Pressure is normal at the feed gauge and after the sediment filter, but low after the softener. Where is the problem?
The fault lies between the last good gauge and the first bad one. Module 3.
Question 27A carbon bed of 240 liters receives 40 liters per minute. What is the empty-bed contact time?
240 divided by 40 is 6 minutes. Module 4.
Question 28Chlorine is detected after the first carbon tank but not after the second. What does this mean?
Breakthrough after the worker is a warning to increase testing and arrange replacement as the procedure states. Module 4.
Question 29Does backwashing restore exhausted carbon?
Backwashing prevents channeling but does not renew the carbon’s capacity. Module 4.
Question 30A new 5-micrometer cartridge clogs in three days. What is the best interpretation?
Rapid clogging points to a problem upstream that needs to be found. Module 4.
Question 31Which test detects chloramine?
Total chlorine includes free chlorine and chloramine. A free chlorine test misses chloramine. Module 4.
Question 32Where is the first chlorine sample of the day taken?
Testing after the worker shows whether it is still doing its job. Module 4.
Question 33Chlorine is above the limit after the second carbon tank. What happens next?
Breakthrough after the polisher means chlorine can reach patients. Module 4.
Question 34Why must the color be read at exactly the stated time?
Test chemistry is timed; follow the kit instructions. Module 4.
Question 35A unit uses a free chlorine test. The utility switches to chloramine. What will the test show?
A free chlorine test does not detect chloramine. Module 4.
Question 36Why test the feed water with the same kit once a week?
A kit that cannot read positive on chlorinated feed water cannot be trusted. Module 4.
Question 37After carbon replacement, what must be done before release?
New carbon sheds fines and must be proven to remove chlorine. Module 4.
Question 38Which carbon is not used for dialysis water treatment?
Regenerated carbon may carry contaminants from its previous use. Module 4.
Question 39What passes through a reverse osmosis membrane most easily?
Pressure forces water through; most dissolved and suspended material is held back. Module 5.
Question 40Feed conductivity is 500 and product conductivity is 25. What is the percent rejection?
(500 − 25) ÷ 500 × 100 = 95%. Module 5.
Question 41Feed flow is 20 liters per minute and product flow is 12. What is the percent recovery?
12 ÷ 20 × 100 = 60%. Module 5.
Question 42Rejection has fallen steadily for three weeks but no alarm has sounded. What should you do?
A trend is information. Reporting it early prevents a failure. Module 5.
Question 43Which of these does conductivity NOT detect?
Chlorine and chloramine at harmful levels barely change conductivity. Only the chlorine test detects them. Module 5.
Question 44Product flow drops on an unusually cold morning with no other change. What is the most likely reason?
Membrane output falls as feed water temperature falls. Module 5.
Question 45Product flow is rising and rejection is falling. What is the most likely cause?
A damaged membrane lets more water and more salt through. Module 5.
Question 46The low feed pressure alarm stops the pump. What is checked first?
Low feed pressure originates upstream of the pump. Module 5.
Question 47Why does the loop return water to its starting point?
Stagnant water allows biofilm to form. Module 6.
Question 48What is a dead leg?
Dead legs let bacteria grow and seed the rest of the loop. Module 6.
Question 49Cultures are under 5 CFU/mL at the loop start and 38 CFU/mL at the loop return. What does this suggest?
A clean start and a dirty return point to a source within the loop, such as a dead leg. Module 6.
Question 50What is the main weakness of a tank-fed system?
Stored water gives bacteria time and surfaces to grow on. Module 6.
Question 51Why is an ultrafilter placed after an ultraviolet lamp?
The lamp kills bacteria but does not remove them or their fragments. Module 6.
Question 52An ultraviolet lamp is glowing. Does that prove it is working?
Lamp hours, intensity readings, and sleeve cleaning show whether it is effective. Module 6.
Question 53Which material must not contact product water?
Very pure water dissolves copper, brass, zinc, and aluminum into the water. Module 6.
Question 54Loop cultures are clean, but dialysis fluid from two machines is above the action level. Where do you look?
The contamination is entering after the loop. Module 6.
Question 55Why are acid and bicarbonate supplied as two separate concentrates?
Keeping them apart until dilution prevents precipitation. Module 7.
Question 56Two acid concentrates differ only in potassium. Will the machine’s conductivity alarm tell them apart?
Conductivity measures total salts; the label is the safeguard. Module 7.
Question 57Which water is used to mix bicarbonate concentrate?
Only treated dialysis water is used. Module 7.
Question 58Why should bicarbonate not be mixed vigorously for a long time?
Over-mixing drives off carbon dioxide and makes the solution more alkaline. Module 7.
Question 59A bicarbonate container is half full of yesterday’s batch. What is correct?
Old solution seeds bacteria into the new batch and is past its use time. Module 7.
Question 60Low conductivity alarms occur on six machines at once shortly after a new bicarbonate batch goes on line. What is the likely cause?
Simultaneous alarms point to the shared supply. Module 7.
Question 61By convention, which color marks acid concentrate connectors?
Acid is red and bicarbonate is blue. Module 7.
Question 62A mixed batch fails verification. What do you do?
An out-of-range batch is discarded, not corrected. Module 7.
Question 63Which log entry is acceptable?
A usable record gives the number, unit, place, time, and person. Module 8.
Question 64You forgot the midday chlorine test. What do you write?
Records must be true. Report the gap and test immediately. Module 8.
Question 65Pressure drop across the sediment filter has risen a little every week for a month. No limit has been reached. What should you do?
Trends allow planned maintenance instead of emergency repair. Module 8.
Question 66Product conductivity is excellent today. What does this tell you about chlorine?
Each check answers one question only. Module 8.
Question 67What is a baseline?
Baselines come from your own system in good condition. Module 8.
Question 68A log shows the same pressure reading to the decimal for three weeks. What does this most likely indicate?
Real systems vary slightly day to day. Module 8.
Question 69Who should carry out the weekly log review?
Fresh eyes catch drift the daily recorder has stopped seeing. Module 8.
Question 70What is the last step of the start-of-day round?
Release is a stated, recorded handover. Module 8.
Question 71In the international standard, what is the bacteria limit for dialysis water?
The limit is less than 100 CFU/mL, with an action level typically at 50. Module 9.
Question 72A culture result lies between the action level and the limit. What does this mean?
Action levels exist so that the limit is never reached. Module 9.
Question 73When are routine culture samples best taken?
Sampling before disinfection shows the highest level patients were exposed to. Module 9.
Question 74Why is the sample port flushed before collecting?
Stagnant water in the tap does not represent the system. Module 9.
Question 75How often is chemical analysis of product water done, at a minimum, in most monitoring plans?
Chemical contaminants change slowly, but any change of source or membrane calls for a new analysis. Module 9.
Question 76A laboratory cultures dialysis water at body temperature for 48 hours. What is the likely effect?
Water bacteria grow slowly and need low-nutrient media at room temperature for about a week. Module 9.
Question 77Endotoxin is high but the culture is low. How should this be treated?
Endotoxin comes from dead as well as live bacteria. Module 9.
Question 78How should culture samples be stored before transport?
Refrigeration holds the count steady; warmth raises it and freezing lowers it. Module 9.
Question 79What is biofilm?
Biofilm sheds bacteria and endotoxin and resists disinfectant. Module 10.
Question 80After a chemical disinfection, what must be shown before any patient is connected?
Residual disinfectant reaching a patient causes harm. Module 10.
Question 81Why must bleach never be mixed with acid products?
Chlorine gas is toxic. Use and store these products separately. Module 10.
Question 82Several patients on one shift develop chills at about the same time. What is the technician’s first step?
Report first. The clinical team decides about patients, and samples should be taken before disinfection. Module 10.
Question 83A procedure calls for 100 liters at 2 percent. The stock is 10 percent. How much stock is needed?
(2 × 100) ÷ 10 = 20 liters, made up to 100 liters. Module 10.
Question 84Why are two different test strips used during chemical disinfection?
High-range and residual strips answer different questions. Module 10.
Question 85Counts return to their old level two weeks after disinfection. What does this suggest?
Rapid regrowth means something survived or keeps reseeding. Module 10.
Question 86Why might the reverse osmosis membrane and the loop have different disinfection procedures?
Agent compatibility differs between components. Module 10.
Question 87What triggers carbon replacement before its scheduled date?
Breakthrough shows the carbon is exhausted. Module 11.
Question 88After carbon is replaced, which check is essential before release?
New carbon must be proven to remove chlorine, and the bypass must be closed. Module 11.
Question 89A new reverse osmosis membrane has been fitted. What should be recorded?
Future readings are judged against the new baseline. Module 11.
Question 90A section of loop pipe was opened for repair. What is required?
Opening the loop can introduce bacteria. Module 11.
Question 91An engineer leaves a report saying only “Serviced. OK.” What is correct?
A report must give evidence that can be checked later. Module 11.
Question 92Why write the installation date on each replaceable part?
Visible dates prevent overdue parts. Module 11.
Question 93A pressure gauge has read exactly the same value for a month. What should you suspect?
Gauges fail too; compare with a reference. Module 11.
Question 94What is the first step before any maintenance that takes a stage out of service?
Maintenance is planned around patient treatment. Module 11.
Question 95What are the first three moves for any out-of-range water result?
Stop new starts, place a hold, and report. Investigation follows. Module 12.
Question 96The first chlorine test is above the limit; a repeat is below it. What is correct?
Conflicting results mean the true value is unknown. Module 12.
Question 97Who decides what happens to patients already on dialysis when the water is in doubt?
The technician reports quickly; clinical decisions belong to the clinical team. Module 12.
Question 98Several patients have fever and chills and contamination is suspected. Why sample before disinfecting?
Samples taken afterward cannot show what was in the water during the event. Module 12.
Question 99Power returns after a long outage. What should be checked before treatment resumes?
A restart is not a release. Timers drift and stagnant water may have grown bacteria. Module 12.
Question 100Delivered tanker water arrives during a supply cut. How should it be regarded?
A different source can bring different contaminants. Module 12.
Question 101Which is a root cause rather than a description?
A root cause is something that can be changed to prevent a repeat. Module 12.
Question 102Before handling a disinfectant for the first time, what do you read?
The safety data sheet gives hazards, protective equipment, and first aid. Module 12.
Question 103In an urgent water report, which fact tells the nurse how many patients are at risk right now?
The clinical team’s first decision concerns patients already on dialysis. Module 12.
Question 104You cannot reach the charge nurse within a minute during a breakthrough. What do you do?
A hold must be communicated; use the next contact. Module 12.
