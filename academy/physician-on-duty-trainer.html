Program design

Using the series to orient a new physician on duty

The series supports unit orientation; it does not replace it. A practical plan is self-study of the First-Shift Essentials before the first day, a unit tour with the head nurse, two shifts observing an experienced physician on duty, and three shifts with backup on call, with the checklists signed along the way.

Figure 1 · Suggested orientation sequence

  1. Self-studyFirst-Shift Essentials, each self-assessment at 7 of 8.
  2. Day 1Unit tour, emergency cart, machine walk-through, unit protocols, contact list.
  3. Shifts 1 and 2Observe an experienced physician on duty. Complete the access and assessment checklists.
  4. Shifts 3 to 5Take duty with backup on call. Complete the emergency response checklists.
  5. Within one monthMock cardiac arrest and emergency take-off drill.
  6. Within two monthsCore modules complete; cleared for independent duty by the medical director.

Record the physician’s Advanced Cardiovascular Life Support (ACLS) certificate and physician-on-duty course certificate, which the Philippine Society of Nephrology (PSN) standards expect.1

Completion

Proposed completion criteria

  • Self-assessments: 7 of 8 on two different sets for each module, with every missed question reviewed.
  • Every Critical step on every checklist demonstrated satisfactorily to a preceptor.
  • A mock cardiac arrest led as team leader, and one emergency take-off drill.
  • Current ACLS certificate and the physician-on-duty course certificate on file.
  • Clearance for independent duty by the medical director.

These are proposals for each unit to adapt.

Written examination. The end-of-course examination draws 30 questions at random from the question banks of all modules. A proposed pass mark is 80 percent, with missed questions reviewed against their modules before a second sitting. For a supervised sitting, the learner takes it on a unit device with the preceptor present and the preceptor records the score.

Printable

Skills checklists for every module

Print this page for a full set. Each checklist starts on a new sheet. Steps marked Critical must all be satisfactory before sign-off; a missed critical step is repeated with the preceptor. Replace any step that does not match your equipment or approved procedure.

Module 15: Heart Emergencies in the Chair: Chest Pain, Arrhythmia, and Cardiac Arrest

Observable stepSatisfactoryNeeds practiceNot observed
Part A. Explains the test to the patient, exposes the chest with privacy, and keeps the access arm still and visible.
CriticalPlaces the six chest leads and four limb leads correctly, moving a limb lead up the arm when the access is in the way.
CriticalRecords a tracing free of artifact and labels it with the patient’s name, the date, the time, and the symptoms.
CriticalReads aloud in order: rate, regularity, QRS width, ST segments, T waves, and QT interval.
CriticalCompares the tracing with the most recent ECG on file and states what has changed.

CriticalPart B. Confirms unresponsiveness and abnormal breathing, calls the code, and sends for the cart and defibrillator.

CriticalAssigns roles by name, including one nurse to the dialysis machine and one person to guard the access.
CriticalLooks at the circuit and states whether the blood is to be returned or withheld, and why, without pausing compressions.
CriticalBefore a shock, confirms the patient is disconnected or the machine is defibrillation-proof, does not wait for blood return, and checks that everyone is clear.
Directs that the dialysis access stays in place and is used for drugs per unit protocol.
