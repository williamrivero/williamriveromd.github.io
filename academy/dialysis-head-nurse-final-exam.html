Before you begin

How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Dialysis Head Nurse series. Each sitting draws 39 questions at random from a bank of 195, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark32 of 39 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.
What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

How to get your certificate of completion

  1. Pass all 13 module quizzes. Each module quiz is passed at 80%, or at the pass mark the module states. The Certificate checklist above the examination shows which modules are done; each line links to that module's quiz.
  2. Use the same device and browser for the modules and this examination. Progress is saved in your browser, not in an account. On iPhone and iPad, the Home Screen app and Safari keep separate progress.
  3. Pass this timed examination (80%; where these instructions say so, every safety-critical question must also be correct). You have one minute per question from the moment you tap Start the examination; only your first check of a set counts. If you pass before finishing the modules, your pass is saved: finish the module quizzes, then come back here.
  4. Type your name the way you want it printed and tap Make my certificate. You can tick Add a preceptor sign-off line if your unit will sign the printed copy.
  5. Save or print your completion packet: two pages, the certificate and a record of your module results. Use Print or save as PDF to keep both pages together.
  6. Be online when you make it, so the certificate gets its QR code. Anyone can scan the code, or enter it at renalcarematters.com/verify, to confirm it.

Not finished yet? Use Print a progress record in the checklist to show a preceptor how far you have gone; it is clearly marked "not a certificate". The certificate is a self-study certificate of completion, not CPD units: competence is demonstrated on your own unit and signed off by a preceptor. To make the QR code work, your name, track, date and score are saved with the certificate code; to have them removed, use Report a correction on any guide.

Examination

End-of-course examination

Question 1Why can a small process failure harm a dialysis patient faster than a ward patient?
The circuit, the fluid, and the missing kidney function remove the body’s own safety margin. Module 1.
Question 2Which source sets out the three-month structured renal dialysis training for a head nurse?
The PSN standards describe the head nurse’s preparation. Department of Health (DOH) and the Nursing Act set other requirements. Module 1.
Question 3A nurse holds a renal dialysis training certificate but has not been observed connecting a patient in your unit. How is she counted today?
A certificate and a site sign-off are different evidence. Only signed-off nurses count toward the rule. Module 1.
Question 4Twelve stations are planned. How many nurses with sign-off does the DOH provision require?
Twelve divided by four is three. Module 1.
Question 5Two signed-off nurses are present for twelve planned stations. How many stations may open on nursing grounds alone?
Each nurse covers four stations under the provision. Module 1.
Question 6The only ACLS-certified nurse on the roster has an expired certificate. What is the status of that gate?
The licensing order expects a nurse with an updated certificate on each shift. Module 1.
Question 7Machine 7 was repaired but its release is unsigned. Who may put it back into use?
Technical release belongs to the technical staff. You confirm it exists. Module 1.
Question 8Which decision belongs to the attending nephrologist or physician on duty, not the head nurse?
Whether a patient can wait is a medical judgment about that patient. Module 1.
Question 9Units where every nurse had two years of dialysis experience met more targets. What kind of evidence is this?
Better-resourced units may have both experienced nurses and healthier patients. Module 1.
Question 10What did the DOPPS analysis find about highly trained staff hours?
Skipping was itself associated with 30% higher mortality in the same cohort. Module 1.
Question 11The administrator asks you to start now and fix the paperwork later. What is the best response?
A hold with an owner protects patients without abandoning them. Module 1.
Question 12What is the main purpose of an evidence register?
It turns “show me” into a two-minute answer. Module 1.
Question 13How far ahead should the register be reviewed for expiring certificates?
Ninety days leaves time to book renewal courses. Module 1.
Question 14Older PhilHealth circulars listed head nurse credentials. What should you do about them?
Their current status could not be confirmed from public documents. Module 1.
Question 15Where a limit is not written in your unit’s protocol, what does this series ask you to do?
Inventing a limit at the chairside is how errors start. Module 1.
Question 16For a Monday-Wednesday-Friday patient, on which day is mortality highest?
In the Foley cohort, deaths were higher on the day after the long interval. Module 2.
Question 17Why is breathlessness when lying flat important even when the weight gain looks usual?
A patient who has lost muscle can be overloaded at an unchanged dry weight. Module 2.
Question 18A 60-kg patient needs 3,400 mL removed in 4 hours. What is the UF rate?
3,400 ÷ 4 ÷ 60 = 14.2. Module 2.
Question 19The same patient wants to leave after 3 hours with the same goal. What happens to the rate?
Less time for the same fluid means faster removal. Module 2.
Question 20What did Flythe and colleagues find for UF rates above 13 mL/kg/h?
The study was observational. No trial has shown that a cap saves lives. Module 2.
Question 21In DOPPS, the four-month risk of missing a session ranged from under 1% to 24% between countries. What does this suggest?
A fiftyfold difference between countries points to systems. Module 2.
Question 22Which nadir pressure definition was most strongly associated with death?
Definitions built on symptoms or interventions were not associated with death. Module 2.
Question 23Why does water removal faster than refilling lower the pressure?
The machine removes water only from the blood. Module 2.
Question 24What did the lung ultrasound outcome trial show?
The benefit was seen on images, not on outcomes patients feel. Module 2.
Question 25A patient missed Friday, has palpitations, and has a critical potassium with no documented call. Which lane?
Each finding alone is urgent. Together they are a Now finding. Module 2.
Question 26What closes a critical result loop?
Until someone owns the next step, the loop is open. Module 2.
Question 27Hemoglobin has drifted from 10.8 to 9.9 g/dL over three months. Which lane?
It is a trend for the monthly review with the nephrologist. Module 2.
Question 28Which question has outcome data behind it as a routine check?
Longer recovery was associated with hospitalization and death in DOPPS. Module 2.
Question 29A family member asks for an extra hour after a missed session. Who decides?
Time, rate, and dialysis fluid are part of the prescription. Module 2.
Question 30Why should a potassium from a hemolyzed sample still be reported?
Report the value with its context. Do not decide alone that it is false. Module 2.
Question 31Which process clears urea and potassium mainly?
Small molecules move down their concentration gradient across the membrane. Module 3.
Question 32Why does dialysis fluid flow opposite to the blood?
Countercurrent flow keeps fresh fluid meeting blood that still carries solute. Module 3.
Question 33The signed order says four hours, the machine is set for three, and the handover sheet says three. What governs?
The current order governs when it is clear. Remove the outdated sheet. Module 3.
Question 34When should the nurse call the prescriber about a mismatch?
A clear current order is simply followed. An unclear one is clarified. Module 3.
Question 35Pre-dialysis urea is 80 and post-dialysis urea is 28 mg/dL. What is the URR?
(80 − 28) ÷ 80 = 0.65. Module 3.
Question 36A patient’s Kt/V jumps from 1.25 to 1.9 with no change in prescription. What do you check first?
A sample taken at full flow or after rinse-back gives a falsely good result. Module 3.
Question 37What did the HEMO Study show?
The relative risk of death with the higher dose was 0.96. Module 3.
Question 38What is the KDOQI 2015 minimum delivered single-pool Kt/V for thrice-weekly dialysis?
The target is 1.4, with a minimum delivered dose of 1.2. Module 3.
Question 39Why is the TiME trial weak evidence against longer sessions?
A trial cannot show the effect of a difference it did not achieve. Module 3.
Question 40What did MyTEMP show about cooler dialysis fluid as a center-wide policy?
It may still help an individual patient with frequent low pressure. Module 3.
Question 41In CONVINCE, death was 17.3% with HDF and 21.9% with hemodialysis. What is the absolute difference?
23% is the relative reduction, and 0.77 is the hazard ratio. Module 3.
Question 42Which patients were eligible for CONVINCE?
High-volume hemodiafiltration (HDF) needs good access flow. Module 3.
Question 43One hour is lost from a four-hour session with the same fluid goal. What happens to the UF rate?
Four hours of work done in three is a third faster. Module 3.
Question 44Unit delivered time is 97%. Why is that not enough?
Look at the sessions, not only the average. Module 3.
Question 45What should happen to a laminated card at a station that lists dialysis settings?
Any paper at the chair that can disagree with the order eventually will. Module 3.
Question 46What does a healthy fistula feel and sound like?
Flow continues through the whole heartbeat. Module 4.
Question 47A fistula is pulsatile, the bruit is high-pitched and systolic only, and bleeding lasts longer after needle removal. What is likely?
Downstream narrowing raises pressure in the access. Module 4.
Question 48The usual thrill is absent before treatment. What do you do?
No thrill suggests clotting. Module 4.
Question 49You see shiny, scabbed skin over a fistula bulge. What is correct?
Scabbed skin over an aneurysm signals a risk of rupture. Module 4.
Question 50Why can the venous pressure alarm stay silent when the needle comes out?
The reading may stay inside its alarm window. Module 4.
Question 51In the bench simulation, how often did the machine alarm sound after dislodgement?
It sounded in 42% of simulations. Module 4.
Question 52The pump runs at 300 mL/min and a dislodgement goes unnoticed for 5 minutes. What is the potential loss?
300 × 5 = 1,500 mL. Module 4.
Question 53What is the first action when a needle site bleeds heavily?
Stopping the pump stops the loss from the circuit. Clamp, press, and call follow at once. Module 4.
Question 54Why is a blanket over the access arm unsafe?
Only a visible access protects against silent bleeding. Module 4.
Question 55For catheters used for months, where do most bloodstream infections start?
That is why hub handling matters most. Module 4.
Question 56What did the Philippine catheter cohort report?
Use of the catheter for transfusion or medicines was a risk factor. Module 4.
Question 57What should the gram-negative pattern make you audit?
This is an inference from the organisms, not a study finding. Module 4.
Question 58What did the systematic review find about buttonhole cannulation?
Use it only for selected patients under a strict protocol. Module 4.
Question 59What did the KDOQI 2019 guideline change about access planning?
It replaced fistula first with an individualized life plan. Module 4.
Question 60Nine of 95 patients have had a catheter for more than 90 days. What is the catheter dependence?
9 ÷ 95 = 9.5%. Module 4.
Question 61Why does hepatitis B need a dedicated room, machine, and staff?
Durability on surfaces drives spread through shared equipment. Module 5.
Question 62Why is hepatitis C not isolated?
Outbreaks have followed lapses such as shared vials. Module 5.
Question 63For catheters used for months, where should prevention effort focus most?
Most long-term catheter infections start inside the lumen. Module 5.
Question 64Eighteen of 24 hand-hygiene opportunities were met. What should the audit report?
18 ÷ 24. The interval is wide because the sample is small. Module 5.
Question 65What goes in the denominator of a hand-hygiene audit?
Every observed opportunity counts. Module 5.
Question 66Why are 10 observations too few?
Eight of ten has an interval from about 49% to 94%. Module 5.
Question 67Three bloodstream infections occur among 95 patients in one month. What is the rate?
3 ÷ 95 × 100. Module 5.
Question 68What did the United States collaborative report?
No control group, and volunteer facilities, so the true effect may be smaller. Module 5.
Question 69Two patients develop chills twenty minutes after connection. Which cause is most likely?
Early onset points to the fluid or a product. Module 5.
Question 70What is the first priority when symptoms cluster?
Patients first, with the investigation running alongside. Module 5.
Question 71Where should medicines be drawn up?
Medicine preparation stays inside the clean boundary. Module 5.
Question 72The gap between shifts is too short to clean stations properly. What is the fix?
When the schedule makes the process impossible, the schedule is the hazard. Module 5.
Question 73What should you preserve when a new lot may be the source?
Evidence must still exist when the investigation asks for it. Module 5.
Question 74Why should infection results be posted for staff?
Surveillance with feedback to staff is part of the bundle. Module 5.
Question 75Two catheter infections occur among 30 catheter patients over 30 days. What is the rate per 1,000 catheter-days?
2 ÷ 900 × 1,000 = 2.2. Module 5.
Question 76Why is drinking water not safe for dialysis?
Small contaminants pass the membrane freely. Module 6.
Question 77Which stage is the only reliable barrier to chloramine?
Reverse osmosis does not reliably remove chloramine. Module 6.
Question 78Why is total chlorine tested every shift when cultures are monthly?
The test is the last line of defense. Module 6.
Question 79Three connected patients develop chest pain and dark, cherry-red blood in the venous lines. What is the rule about the blood?
Suspected hemolysis means the circuit is the cause. Module 6.
Question 80What is the ISO limit for bacteria in dialysis water?
Endotoxin must be under 0.25 endotoxin unit (EU)/mL. Module 6.
Question 81Which fluid must be ultrapure?
High-volume HDF infuses fluid directly into the blood. Module 6.
Question 82What is an action level?
It lets the unit act before the limit is reached. Module 6.
Question 83Can yesterday’s normal result clear today’s abnormal one?
Each result stands for its own time. Module 6.
Question 84How should a missing water result be treated?
If you cannot see it, the gate is closed. Module 6.
Question 85What three documents are required before restart?
A verbal assurance is none of them. Module 6.
Question 86Feed conductivity is 400 and product is 12 µS/cm. What is the RO rejection?
(1 − 12 ÷ 400) × 100 = 97%. Module 6.
Question 87Why must carbon contact time be recalculated when stations are added?
Contact time is volume divided by flow. Module 6.
Question 88What enables a fast, targeted recall of a dialyzer lot?
Lot numbers link the product to the patients. Module 6.
Question 89Two patients have chills and fever within the first hour. Which contaminant group comes first to mind?
Endotoxin causes early pyrogenic reactions. Module 6.
Question 90Who releases a water system after correction?
You confirm that the signed release exists. Module 6.
Question 91What is the first question in any dialysis emergency?
The answer decides whether blood may be returned. Module 7.
Question 92In which events is blood not returned?
In these, the circuit is the cause. Module 7.

Question 94What was staff-started CPR associated with?
The study was observational, but no stronger evidence is likely. Module 7.
Question 95Air is seen in the venous line and the patient coughs. What position is taught?

Question 96Why does “somebody call the doctor” fail?
