Before you begin

How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Attending Nephrologist series. Each sitting draws 40 questions at random from a bank of 120, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark32 of 40 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.
What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

How to get your certificate of completion

  1. Pass all 24 module quizzes. Each module quiz is passed at 80%, or at the pass mark the module states. The Certificate checklist above the examination shows which modules are done; each line links to that module's quiz.
  2. Use the same device and browser for the modules and this examination. Progress is saved in your browser, not in an account. On iPhone and iPad, the Home Screen app and Safari keep separate progress.
  3. Pass this timed examination (80%; where these instructions say so, every safety-critical question must also be correct). You have one minute per question from the moment you tap Start the examination; only your first check of a set counts. If you pass before finishing the modules, your pass is saved: finish the module quizzes, then come back here.
  4. Type your name the way you want it printed and tap Make my certificate. You can tick Add a preceptor sign-off line if your unit will sign the printed copy.
  5. Save or print your completion packet: two pages, the certificate and a record of your module results. Use Print or save as PDF to keep both pages together.
  6. Be online when you make it, so the certificate gets its QR code. Anyone can scan the code, or enter it at renalcarematters.com/verify, to confirm it.

Not finished yet? Use Print a progress record in the checklist to show a preceptor how far you have gone; it is clearly marked "not a certificate". The certificate is a self-study certificate of completion, not CPD units: competence is demonstrated on your own unit and signed off by a preceptor. To make the QR code work, your name, track, date and score are saved with the certificate code; to have them removed, use Report a correction on any guide.

Examination

End-of-course examination

Question 1What should be settled before the first shift?
An order set cannot assign responsibility when a clinician is unavailable or a patient needs transfer. Module 1.
Question 2Which document defines whether the facility may operate an HD service?
A facility license, physician credentials and payer accreditation are separate authorities. Module 1.
Question 3Under the PSN 2024 HD guidelines, how often should the AN review a maintenance HD patient?
Section 1.2 sets a minimum monthly consultation. A documented review supports continuity and the claim you sign. Module 1.
Question 4A clinic’s night POD cannot reach you during an emergency. What design element was missing?
Escalation must not depend on one person answering. A time trigger turns an unanswered call into an action. Module 1.
Question 5One physician serves as both the AN and the unit’s Medical Director. What should the record show?
Patient care and facility oversight carry different duties; naming the acting role keeps accountability clear. Module 1.
Question 6Which finding most strengthens a glomerular explanation?
Sediment morphology places the injury at the glomerular capillary wall in a way creatinine cannot. Module 2.
Question 7Which data clarify whether a creatinine change is acute?
Change over time classifies acuity and gauges progression. Ask for the results the patient keeps at home. Module 2.
Question 8Why does a single creatinine underestimate injury early in AKI?
Production continues while filtration falls, so the serum level rises gradually toward the new equilibrium. Module 2.
Question 9In which patient is cystatin C most useful for estimating GFR?
Cystatin C depends less on muscle mass, so it corrects the overestimate of GFR from creatinine when muscle is low. Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) 2024 recommends the combined estimate when the creatinine estimate is less accurate and GFR affects a decision (1C). Module 2.
Question 10Muddy-brown granular casts most strongly suggest:
Sloughed tubular epithelial cells form granular casts; prerenal states typically show a bland sediment. Module 2.
Question 11Which detail closes the communication loop?
Read-back by the person who will act confirms who acts and on what instruction. A “seen” mark only shows the message was opened. Module 3.
Question 12What should a POD communicate during an HD emergency?
The attending needs trend and action data to advise what happens next. Module 3.
Question 13An attending at home says ‘monitor closely.’ What is missing?
A vague instruction passes on worry, not responsibility. Module 3.
Question 14What does the Data Privacy Act require when you share data for a transfer?
Share what the purpose needs, through channels the institution controls. Module 3.
Question 15A patient with presyncope insists on leaving. What must happen before discharge against advice?
Capacity is decision-specific; if present, an informed refusal is documented with a safety plan. Module 3.
Question 16Which medication instruction supports safe follow-up?
KDIGO 2024 asks for a clear restart plan whenever medicines are stopped during an acute illness (practice point). Module 4.
Question 17How should the physician check consent comprehension?
Teach-back reveals misunderstandings while there is time to address them. Module 4.
Question 18Two weeks after starting an SGLT2 inhibitor, creatinine rises 18% with stable potassium. Best action?
Lower intraglomerular pressure produces an early, reversible estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) dip. KDIGO 2024 states it is generally not a reason to stop (practice point). Module 4.
Question 19Which drug property most predicts substantial removal by high-flux hemodialysis (HD)?
Unbound drug in plasma water crosses the membrane; drug held in tissue is not reached. Module 4.
Question 20Under the KDIGO 2022 diabetes guideline, metformin should be stopped when eGFR falls below:
The guideline recommends metformin at eGFR 30 or more (1B), halves the dose at 30 to 44, and stops it below 30. Module 4.
Question 21What should determine the next step after initial stabilization?
The trend in physiology and complications sets the urgency while the cause is worked up. Module 5.
Question 22What belongs in the post-AKI discharge plan?
The next clinician needs a defined action, a time frame and a name. KDIGO advises review at about 3 months. Module 5.
Question 23Which electrolyte pattern is characteristic of early leptospirosis-associated AKI?
Leptospiral injury to the proximal tubule and thick ascending limb wastes sodium and potassium. Module 5.
Question 24What did BICARICU-2 show in severe metabolic acidemia with moderate to severe AKI?
The primary outcome was neutral (62.1% vs 61.7%). The KRT finding was one of 18 secondary outcomes. Module 5.
Question 25Why does venous congestion worsen AKI?
Perfusion depends on arterial minus venous pressure; a tense, encapsulated kidney compounds the drop. Module 5.
Question 26Which disposition is appropriate for potassium 7.1 mmol/L with a broad QRS in an HD patient?
electrocardiogram (ECG) toxicity signals immediate arrhythmia risk. The UK Kidney Association (UKKA) guideline recommends urgent dialysis for HD patients with potassium ≥6.5 mmol/L. Module 6.
Question 27What must be anticipated when correcting chronic severe hyponatremia?
Duration, risk factors and an evolving water diuresis determine safety. Module 6.
Question 28Why is a 10% glucose infusion added after insulin-glucose when baseline glucose is below 7 mmol/L?
Insulin’s effect outlasts the 25 g glucose, especially in chronic kidney disease (CKD) with reduced insulin clearance. UKKA suggests 10% glucose at 50 mL/h for 5 hours (2B). Module 6.
Question 29Which treatment actually removes potassium from the body?
Calcium protects the membrane, insulin and salbutamol shift potassium; only the gut, the kidney or dialysis remove it. Module 6.
Question 30HCO₃⁻ 12 mmol/L with pCO₂ 38 mm Hg indicates:
Winter’s formula predicts pCO₂ of 26 ± 2; a value of 38 means ventilation is inadequate. Module 6.
Question 31What most strengthens the acute KRT indication?
Indications depend on the complication and its response to treatment. Module 7.
Question 32What belongs in the acute KRT order review?
The prescription must address the indication and change with illness and recovery. Module 7.
Question 33What did STARRT-AKI show?
Mortality was 43.9% versus 43.7%; dialysis dependence among survivors was 10.4% versus 6.0%. Module 7.
Question 34Which patient most clearly favors CRRT over intermittent HD?
Gradual solute removal avoids rapid osmotic shifts that can worsen cerebral edema; KDIGO 2012 suggests CRRT here (2B). Module 7.
Question 35Which sign during KRT most suggests renal recovery?
Returning native filtration shows up as urine and as creatinine that no longer climbs between sessions. Module 7.
Question 36Which measure best reveals a delivery gap?
Interruptions can greatly reduce delivered treatment despite a correct order; KDIGO recommends frequent assessment of the delivered dose (1B). Module 8.
Question 37Which team should review drug exposure after an unexpected CRRT interruption?
Delivered therapy affects drug clearance and can change exposure. Module 8.
Question 38A 70-kg patient receives 2,100 mL/h effluent for 12 of 24 hours. What is the delivered dose?
2,100 × 12 / 24 / 70 = 15 mL/kg/h, below the 20–25 mL/kg/h target. Module 8.
Question 39During regional citrate anticoagulation, a total-to-ionized calcium ratio of 2.8 with worsening acidosis suggests:
Unmetabolized citrate binds ionized calcium while total calcium rises, widening the ratio above 2.5. Module 8.
Question 40What did the RENAL and ATN trials show about CRRT intensity?
RENAL: 44.7% mortality in both arms. ATN: 53.6% versus 51.5%. Both support a delivered 20–25 mL/kg/h without escalation. Module 8.
Question 41Which measure adds important prognostic information to eGFR?
Albuminuria carries prognostic information independent of eGFR and changes which treatments apply. Module 9.
Question 42Which finding helps establish CKD chronicity?
Serial results more than 3 months apart separate chronic disease from an acute change. Old results the patient brings often settle it. Module 9.
Question 43How do SGLT2 inhibitors lower intraglomerular pressure?
Feedback-mediated afferent constriction complements the efferent dilation from Renin-angiotensin system (RAS) blockade. Module 9.
Question 44What did CONFIDENCE demonstrate?
The trial measured albuminuria change at 180 days. It does not show fewer dialysis starts or deaths. Module 9.
Question 45In KDIGO 2024, a KFRE 2-year risk above 40% can be used to:
Practice Point 2.2.3 uses a 2-year risk above 40% to time modality education. It is a practice point, not a graded recommendation. Module 9.
Question 46Why review transferrin saturation with ferritin?
Ferritin rises as an acute-phase protein; transferrin saturation (TSAT) shows how much iron is actually available to the marrow. Module 10.
Question 47How should CKD-MBD labs guide reassessment?
Trends in related measures support one coordinated plan. Module 10.
Question 48Per KDIGO 2026, what is first-line therapy for renal anemia once correctable causes are addressed?
The guideline suggests an ESA rather than a HIF prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor (HIF-PHI) because long-term cardiovascular and thrombotic safety of HIF-PHIs is uncertain. Module 10.
Question 49In which patient should a HIF-PHI be avoided?
KDIGO 2026 advises against HIF-PHI use with active malignancy or a recent cardiovascular or thrombotic event. Module 10.
Question 50What did TREAT show when darbepoetin targeted a hemoglobin of about 13 g/dL?
Stroke occurred in 5.0% versus 2.6%, with no benefit on the primary outcomes; this is why ESAs are not used to normalize hemoglobin. Module 10.
Question 51Which action fits suspected pulmonary-renal syndrome?
A pulmonary-renal presentation can involve life-threatening organ injury and needs urgent evaluation at a center that can treat it. Module 11.
Question 52What makes kidney biopsy most useful?
Clinical impact and safety guide biopsy selection. Module 11.
Question 53What did PEXIVAS show?
Death or kidney failure was 28.4% versus 31.0% (hazard ratio 0.86, not significant); reduced-dose glucocorticoids met noninferiority with fewer serious infections. Module 11.
Question 54Which screening is essential before rituximab?
Rituximab can reactivate occult hepatitis B, sometimes fatally; core-antibody-positive patients need monitoring or prophylaxis. Module 11.
Question 55Why are nephrotic patients prone to thrombosis?
The coagulation balance tips toward clotting as anticoagulant proteins leak and procoagulants rise. Module 11.
Question 56Who evaluates suitability of a potentially affected sibling as donor?
Independent assessment addresses donor safety, inherited risk and voluntariness. Module 12.
Question 57What should prompt urgent assessment for thrombotic microangiopathy?
The combination raises concern for organ-threatening vascular injury. Module 12.
Question 58Why is ADAMTS13 sent before giving plasma in suspected TMA?
A pre-treatment level distinguishes TTP from complement-mediated and other TMAs; the ISTH guideline asks for the sample before plasma exchange or any blood product. Module 12.
Question 59What is the mechanism of tolvaptan in ADPKD?
Lower cyclic AMP slows cell proliferation and fluid secretion into cysts. Module 12.
Question 60Which feature suggests drug-induced interstitial nephritis?
Interstitial inflammation produces a leukocyte-predominant sediment with low-grade proteinuria. Module 12.
Question 61What justifies starting dialysis in an otherwise uncertain case?
KDIGO 2024 practice points base the start on a composite of symptoms, signs, preferences, GFR and laboratory results. Module 13.
Question 62Which action supports planned kidney failure care?
Preparation gives patients time to choose and services time to coordinate. Module 13.
Question 63What did the IDEAL trial show?
Deaths were 37.6% with early start and 36.6% with late start; most late starters began early because of symptoms. Module 13.
Question 64Why protect residual kidney function after dialysis starts?
Native clearance works 24 hours a day and eases fluid limits; the survival link is observational. Module 13.
Question 65Which timeline is most appropriate for creating an arteriovenous fistula?
Maturation commonly takes weeks to months, and some fistulas need intervention before use. Module 13.
Question 66What is a sound first step in falling adequacy with frequent alarms?
Confirm why delivery fell before changing the prescription. Module 14.
Question 67What should a target-weight revision consider?
Volume goals balance congestion against the harms of fluid removed too fast. Module 14.
Question 68What is the KDOQI 2015 minimum delivered spKt/V for thrice-weekly HD?
Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (KDOQI) 2015 recommends a target of 1.4 and a minimum delivered value of 1.2 (1B). Module 14.
Question 69Why is equilibrated Kt/V lower than single-pool Kt/V?
Delayed transfer from the cells raises post-dialysis urea. Module 14.
Question 70Which statement about hemodiafiltration evidence is accurate?
One large trial, in patients able to reach at least 23 liters of convection, showed 17.3% vs 21.9% mortality. Module 14.
Question 71What is the priority when several patients deteriorate concurrently?
Clustering changes the immediate risk to other patients and requires parallel unit action. Module 15.
Question 72Why is circuit blood not returned in suspected hemolysis?
Lysed red cells release potassium; reinfusion adds to the cardiac risk. Module 15.
Question 73Immediate positioning for suspected venous air embolism:
This position aims to trap air in the right atrium and away from the pulmonary outflow. Module 15.
Question 74First steps for intradialytic hypotension:
Removing the cause and restoring blood volume usually resolves it; recurrent episodes need target-weight review. Module 15.
Question 75Which exposure classically causes cluster hemolysis in HD units?
Chloramine oxidizes hemoglobin; carbon tank exhaustion is a classic outbreak cause. Module 15.
Question 76Which action matters most in an unstable patient with suspected access sepsis?
Instability requires immediate intervention and source assessment. Module 16.
Question 77What makes a vascular access life-plan individualized?
Selection should anticipate current and future access needs. Module 16.
Question 78What did LOCK IT-100 show?
The primary endpoint was time to adjudicated catheter-related bloodstream infection. Module 16.
Question 79Which organism usually requires catheter removal in catheter-related bloodstream infection?
S. aureus, Pseudomonas and Candida infections are poorly cleared from biofilm. Module 16.
Question 80Why compare infection rates per 1,000 catheter-days rather than raw counts?
Exposure-based rates allow fair comparison over time. Module 16.
Question 81Which information is essential in a PD review?
Goal-directed assessment brings together what is delivered, residual clearance, and how the patient is doing. Module 17.
Question 82How should the team respond to recurring missed exchanges?
Schedule, training, and supply barriers are common and fixable; a prescription the patient cannot deliver does not work. Module 17.
Question 83A fast transporter on a long glucose dwell is most likely to experience:
The glucose gradient dissipates quickly, so fluid is reabsorbed late in the dwell; icodextrin helps. Module 17.
Question 84What did ADEMEX show about peritoneal small-solute clearance?
The relative risk of death was 1.00. This supports goal-directed rather than number-driven prescribing. Module 17.
Question 85Why does an early hypertonic dwell cause sodium sieving?
Water crosses through aquaporins faster than sodium diffuses, lowering dialysate sodium for a while. Module 17.
Question 86Which workflow is most appropriate for cloudy effluent with pain and fever?
Early treatment and reassessment limit membrane injury and help identify refractory or complicated infection. Module 18.
Question 87What must happen after starting treatment for suspected PD peritonitis?
Follow-up identifies response, the need to narrow treatment, and the catheter decision by day 5. Module 18.
Question 88Which effluent finding meets the ISPD 2022 laboratory criterion?
The criterion is >100/µL with >50% neutrophils after a dwell of at least 2 hours. Module 18.
Question 89What is the ISPD 2022 target for overall peritonitis rate?
International Society for Peritoneal Dialysis (ISPD) recommends this target (1C); units should calculate it at least yearly. Module 18.
Question 90When does ISPD 2022 recommend catheter removal?
Removal is recommended for refractory (1D) and fungal (1C) peritonitis. Watching longer than 5 days is reasonable if the effluent count is falling (2C). Module 18.
Question 91What should guide immunosuppression changes in a febrile recipient with rising creatinine?
Several high-risk causes can coexist, and changes made without the transplant team may worsen outcomes. Module 19.
Question 92Which information improves a transplant referral?
The center can prioritize and coordinate a complete evaluation. Module 19.
Question 93Clarithromycin raises tacrolimus levels by:
CYP3A4 inhibition reduces clearance, raising levels and the risk of nephrotoxicity. Module 19.
Question 94When does KDIGO 2020 advise referral for transplant evaluation?
Early referral makes pre-emptive transplantation possible. Module 19.
Question 95Why is a living donor evaluated by an independent team?
Separating donor advocacy from recipient care avoids conflicts of interest. Module 19.
Question 96Which element makes a dialysis trial time limited?
Explicit measures and timing support an honest review of benefit and burden. Module 20.
Question 97What is part of comprehensive conservative care?
Conservative management remains an active clinical pathway; KDIGO 2024 supports it as an option (practice point). Module 20.
Question 98The family asks the physician to decide privately before speaking to the patient. Best response?
A patient with capacity holds the decision; family support is welcome but not a substitute. Module 20.
Question 99What does observational evidence suggest for very elderly patients with high comorbidity?
These are nonrandomized data with selection bias; they inform the conversation, they do not decide it. Module 20.
Question 100Capacity assessment is best described as:
Capacity is assessed for the particular decision at the particular time. Module 20.
Question 101What should the attending do first when records conflict?
A truthful attestation depends on a traceable reconciliation of delivered services. Module 21.
Question 102Which records support verification of an actual dialysis session?
Claims attest to services delivered on verifiable dates. Module 21.
Question 103How many HD sessions per year does PhilHealth cover under Circular 2023-0009?
The circular expanded the benefit from 90 to 156 sessions, three a week for 52 weeks. Confirm the current rule on the date of service. Module 21.
Question 104What is the correct way to fix a duplicated claim entry?
A traceable correction preserves integrity and protects staff. Module 21.
Question 105Which principle governs sharing claims data?
Proportional, secure processing protects patients and the facility. Module 21.
Question 106What determines disposition during an outage?
Safe continuity depends on risk-based triage and a verified handoff. Module 22.
Question 107When can the unit close an outage tracker entry?
Confirmed disposition closes the continuity gap. Module 22.
Question 108Which patient is at highest risk after missed sessions?
No urine output and a history of high potassium predict rapid, dangerous accumulation. Module 22.
Question 109What does the Hurricane Katrina study of 386 New Orleans patients show?
The adjusted odds ratio was 2.16. The design was observational, so it shows association, not cause. Module 22.
Question 110Why include serologies on the emergency card?
Hepatitis B segregation is an infection-prevention requirement in every unit. Module 22.
Question 111Which comparison supports assessment of the apparent improvement?
The exposure denominator accounts for a changing population at risk. Module 23.
Question 112What is an example of a balancing measure in a catheter-reduction project?
Balancing measures detect new harms introduced by an improvement. Module 23.
Question 113A trial shows event rates of 20% vs 15%. What is the NNT?
absolute risk reduction (ARR) = 5 percentage points; number needed to treat (NNT) = 1 / 0.05 = 20. Module 23.
Question 114Why does the same relative risk reduction yield more benefit in high-risk patients?
A constant relative effect applied to a larger baseline risk removes more events. Module 23.
Question 115Which feedback is most useful to a learner?
Specific, behavior-focused feedback gives a repeatable action. Module 23.
Question 116Which follow-through is required after the acute transfer?
Continuity includes confirmed acceptance, documentation integrity and a named next clinician. Module 24.
Question 117What outcome would make the capstone QI claim credible?
A baseline, a denominator and a planned review make improvement observable. Module 24.
Question 118After stabilization from catheter sepsis, what should happen to his home-therapy preference?
An urgent start does not cancel an informed modality choice. Module 24.
Question 119Which is a critical action in the capstone claims task?
Integrity takes precedence over deadlines. Module 24.
Question 120A missed critical action at a station results in:
Critical actions are non-compensable. Module 24.
