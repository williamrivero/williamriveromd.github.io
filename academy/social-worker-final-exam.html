Before you begin

How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Renal Social Worker series. Each sitting draws 40 questions at random from a bank of 200, two from each module, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark32 of 40 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.
What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

End-of-course examination

Question 1Who should sign a professional social work assessment such as a social case study?
Best: a signature states that the signer did the assessment, and professional reports belong to the responsible registered social worker. Why not the others: course completion confers no authority; chart access is not professional authority; a later countersignature would make the report false. Module 1.
Question 2Before inviting a relative into an adult patient’s interview, what should you do first?
Best: an adult who can decide chooses who joins. Why not the others: paying for care is not permission; sharing the chart breaches privacy; waiting for the whole family delays help and still skips the patient’s choice. Module 1.
Question 3A pharmacy offers you a “token” for each patient you refer. What is the best response?
Best: a referral benefit creates a conflict of interest that must be declined and managed through supervision. Why not the others: a good price does not remove the conflict; secrecy compounds it; the patient cannot waive your professional duty. Module 1.
Question 4Which of these decisions belongs to the attending nephrologist, not the social worker?
Best: treatment decisions are medical. Why not the others: transport, formulation, and assistance coordination are social work tasks. Module 1.
Question 5What should you say before asking a new patient any personal question?
Best: people share more safely when they know why you ask and who will see it. Why not the others: silence or a name alone leaves them uninformed; promising total secrecy is false because confidentiality has limits, such as immediate risk. Module 1.
Question 6A navigator is asked to sign a social case study because the social worker is on leave. What should the navigator do?
Best: the navigator can prepare everything and route the professional task to the covering social worker. Why not the others: signing is outside the navigator’s authority; sending the family away abandons them; a nurse cannot sign a social work assessment either. Module 1.
Question 7Which statement best describes the purpose of the renal social worker?
Best: the role starts from the person’s goal and covers assessment, support, and access. Why not the others: assistance paperwork is one tool, not the profession; persuasion on the team’s behalf is not the role; transplant eligibility is the transplant team’s decision. Module 1.
Question 8A patient’s family asks you not to tell him his kidneys will not recover. What is the best first step?
Best: respect the family’s concern while protecting the adult patient’s right to information, with the clinician who gives the news. Why not the others: agreeing sets aside the patient’s rights; rushing ahead ignores the family and the nephrologist’s role; ending the meeting solves nothing. Module 1.
Question 9Why should a unit have a written role map with absence cover?
Best: written roles stop work from flowing to whoever is nearest. Why not the others: the reviewed DOH order sets no social worker ratio; the map is about tasks, not importance; it keeps professional tasks with professionals. Module 1.
Question 10During a routine benefit request, the charge nurse reports that the patient said she “cannot keep doing this” and cried. What comes first?
Best: a possible safety or stop-treatment statement comes before forms and goes to the clinician and social worker the same day. Why not the others: delay risks missing danger; assuming the meaning skips the question; the daughter cannot answer for her. Module 1.
Question 11Why is the two-day weekend gap the riskiest part of a three-times-a-week dialysis schedule?
Best: without kidney function, water and potassium accumulate between sessions, most over the longest gap; a large cohort found more deaths on the day after it. Why not the others: the machines do not change; weekend eating usually adds, not reduces, load; staffing is not the mechanism. Module 2.
Question 12A patient wants to skip Friday dialysis for a family trip. What is your role?
Best: the schedule is a medical decision; your work is the social part (temporary dialysis elsewhere, papers, cost). Why not the others: approval is not yours to give; one missed session is not harmless; forbidding travel is neither your decision nor the goal. Module 2.
Question 13Severe breathlessness appears during a social work interview. What should happen?
Best: breathlessness can mean fluid in the lungs and needs clinical assessment now. Why not the others: finishing first delays care; skipping would worsen fluid overload; a routine booking ignores the urgency. Module 2.
Question 14A patient asks which dialysis type is medically best for her. What should you do?
Best: medical suitability is the clinician’s; you bring her life and preferences to the decision. Why not the others: cost alone is not the decision; promises outrun clinical assessment; choosing for her removes her voice. Module 2.
Question 15Which statement about conservative kidney management is accurate?
Best: conservative management is planned, active care. Why not the others: the team continues to care; it is not a cure; it is a clinical pathway, not a billing label. Module 2.
Question 16Which kidney job explains why many people on dialysis also need injections for anemia?
Best: failing kidneys make too little of this hormone, which dialysis cannot replace. Why not the others: water, potassium, and urea removal are partly done by dialysis and do not cause anemia this way. Module 2.
Question 17In the DOPPS cohort, how was skipping one or more sessions a month related to mortality?
Best: Dialysis Outcomes and Practice Patterns Study (DOPPS) is observational, so the right verb is “associated with.” Why not the others: an observational cohort cannot prove causation; the association was present; there was no protective effect. Module 2.
Question 18A patient stopped her phosphate binders because they cost more than her rice. What is the best response?
Best: the clinical decision goes to the clinician; the cost problem stays with you. Why not the others: judging the medicine’s importance or giving instructions is medical advice; doing nothing leaves both problems unsolved. Module 2.
Question 19Why might a patient’s tiredness and poor concentration be misread as low motivation?
Best: kidney failure itself causes fatigue and poor focus, so describe what you see and let clinicians sort the cause. Why not the others: it is a stereotype; there is no such hormone; tiredness has many causes. Module 2.
Question 20For most people who can have one, how does a transplant compare with dialysis, and how strong is the evidence?
Best: consistent cohort evidence, but observational. Why not the others: there are no such randomized trials; cohorts consistently favor transplant; it is not right or possible for everyone. Module 2.
Question 21Does a United States rule on dialysis social worker qualifications apply in the Philippines?
Best: jurisdiction matters; foreign rules are comparisons, not local requirements. Why not the others: citation or preference does not create legal authority. Module 3.
Question 22A licensing checklist could not be retrieved. How should you record it?
Best: an inaccessible document is an open task. Why not the others: you cannot verify what you did not read, and absence of access is not absence of a rule. Module 3.
Question 23Which of these is an Academy design choice, not a legal requirement?
Best: the hours are an educational plan. Why not the others: the law, the circular, and the Data Privacy Act are issued rules. Module 3.
Question 24What is the most defensible basis for proposing social work staffing for a dialysis unit?
Best: workload in hours reflects the actual work and local rules. Why not the others: a borrowed ratio has no local authority; chairs ignore complexity; salaries are irrelevant to demand. Module 3.
Question 25A clinical head says, “DOH did not require a social worker.” Which reply is most accurate?
Best: a bounded finding states exactly what was reviewed. Why not the others: no such ratio was identified; nothing prohibits social work; “no rule anywhere” overstates a limited review. Module 3.
Question 26When should you check a rule’s effective date?
Best: rules can be published before they take effect and may have transition provisions. Why not the others: circulars change often; waiting for complaints is too late; publication is not effectivity. Module 3.
Question 27Which belongs on a one-page social work service charter?
Best: a charter explains the service and its limits. Why not the others: salary and patient details do not belong; an unkeepable guarantee becomes a broken promise. Module 3.
Question 28A unit generates 15 hours of social work a week and 12 hours are assigned. What is the balance?
Best: balance = available − demand = 12 − 15 = −3. Why not the others: the sign or arithmetic is wrong. Module 3.
Question 29You inherit 40 unseen referrals. How should you start?
Best: triage by risk and make the shortfall visible. Why not the others: order of arrival or loudness ignores risk; closing without contact abandons people. Module 3.
Question 30Which body issues the rules on who may practice social work in the Philippines?
Best: Republic Act No. 4373 as amended, administered with the PRC. Why not the others: the DOH licenses facilities; PhilHealth sets benefits; employers set job descriptions. Module 3.
Question 31A patient stays silent while a relative answers every question. What does the silence prove?
Best: silence has many meanings, including hiya or fear. Why not the others: each conclusion assumes what you have not assessed. Module 4.
Question 32For a sensitive interview in the patient’s regional language, whom should you prefer as interpreter?
Best: a neutral interpreter protects accuracy and privacy. Why not the others: children and interested relatives may filter answers; bystanders breach privacy; avoiding interpretation leaves her unheard. Module 4.
Question 33Which is the safest opening question about family involvement?
Best: it gives the choice to the patient. Why not the others: the others impose, presume payment equals authority, or accuse. Module 4.
Question 34What should a culturally safe assessment do?
Best: culture is learned from the person, not assumed. Why not the others: inference stereotypes; avoidance misses strengths; consensus cannot be assumed. Module 4.
Question 35Which question uses teach-back?
Best: it checks understanding and puts the burden on the explanation. Why not the others: yes-no questions invite polite agreement; blame and pressure do not check understanding. Module 4.
Question 36An older patient answers “Opo” to every explanation. What is the best interpretation?
Best: politeness and hiya can make it hard to admit confusion. Why not the others: each draws a conclusion you have not checked. Module 4.
Question 37In the systematic review of health literacy in chronic kidney disease, about how common was limited health literacy among people on dialysis?
Best: pooled prevalence was 27% on dialysis (25% overall), with caveats about study quality and setting. Why not the others: the figures are wrong or the claim is false. Module 4.
Question 38How should you use the concept of utang na loob in an assessment?
Best: cultural concepts are questions to explore. Why not the others: they become stereotypes or excuses for pressure; ignoring them may miss real dynamics. Module 4.
Question 39How do you see a patient alone without offending a relative who insists on staying?
Best: a universal rule protects privacy without accusing. Why not the others: accusation or force damages trust; skipping privacy loses safety and money questions. Module 4.
Question 40What should you record after asking about family involvement?
Best: a dated record lets the team follow the patient’s choices. Why not the others: they miss the patient’s choices or the record entirely. Module 4.
Question 41Which statement is a formulation?
Best: a formulation explains why. Why not the others: a label, a fact without explanation, and a task are not formulations. Module 5.
Question 42Family and patient accounts conflict. What should you do?
Best: sourced accounts let the team weigh them. Why not the others: hiding, favoring payers, or penalizing the person are unfair and unsafe. Module 5.
Question 43An assessment names a caregiver. What should you establish next?
Best: a named caregiver may have little capacity. Why not the others: relationship alone says nothing about capacity; social media is irrelevant; caregivers do not replace nurses. Module 5.
Question 44What is a distress-screen result?
Best: a screen points where to look. Why not the others: it does not diagnose, replace consent, or decide transplant. Module 5.
Question 45Which written note uses descriptive language?
Best: it states what happened and the stated reason. Why not the others: labels explain nothing and blame the person. Module 5.
Question 46In the four Ps, which factor keeps a problem going?
Best: perpetuating factors maintain the problem. Why not the others: predisposing makes vulnerable, precipitating triggers, protective helps. Module 5.
Question 47Midway through an assessment the patient says, “Sometimes I think it would be better if I were gone.” What do you do?
Best: a possible suicide signal comes before every domain. Why not the others: delay, avoidance, or asking someone else risks missing danger. Module 5.
Question 48Why record a strength beside each barrier?
Best: strengths carry the plan. Why not the others: length and agency rules are not the reason; barriers still matter. Module 5.
Question 49What does writing an “Unknown” line at the end of an assessment do?
Best: listing unknowns directs follow-up. Why not the others: unknowns are normal; assumptions must not fill them; a complete form can still hide unknowns. Module 5.
Question 50Why should you check a formulation with the person?
Best: the person knows their life best. Why not the others: signatures, legal claims, and persuasion are not the purpose. Module 5.
Question 51What does a sent referral mean?
Best: sent is the first of five stations. Why not the others: each assumes an outcome that has not been confirmed. Module 6.
Question 52Which care plan action is strongest?
Best: it has an owner, a specific action, and a date. Why not the others: they are vague, untimed, or blaming. Module 6.
Question 53An assistance agency declines a referral. What is the best next step?
Best: a decline with a reason closes the loop and guides the next step. Why not the others: silence, falsification, and oversharing harm the person. Module 6.
Question 54Demand exceeds safe service capacity. What should you do?
Best: a visible, prioritized shortfall can be managed. Why not the others: hiding or abandoning work is unsafe; an invented ratio is false. Module 6.
Question 55Which goal is written in the person’s own terms?
Best: it is specific, timed, and meaningful to the person. Why not the others: vague, blaming, or an action rather than a goal. Module 6.
Question 56A referral was sent March 3, due March 13, and today is March 20. What does the aging tool show?
Best: open = 20 − 3 = 17 days; overdue = 20 − 13 = 7 days, which is the overdue tier (escalate starts beyond 7). Why not the others: the arithmetic or tier is wrong. Module 6.
Question 57At which station does a referral loop close?
Best: help is what reached the household. Why not the others: each earlier station is necessary but not sufficient. Module 6.
Question 58What did the navigation trial in 23 hemodialysis units show?
Best: the trial measured steps completed. Why not the others: it did not show more transplants or lower mortality, and it did show an effect on steps. Module 6.
Question 59Which event should bring a care plan review forward?
Best: admissions and missed sessions signal change. Why not the others: routine or unrelated events are not triggers. Module 6.
Question 60When you step a case down, what must the person know?
Best: a clear way back is part of closure. Why not the others: closure is not abandonment and should be explained. Module 6.
Question 61What is a published annual hemodialysis benefit ceiling?
Best: benefits are paid under conditions, usually to providers. Why not the others: they misdescribe what a benefit is. Module 7.
Question 62Who determines the final payer outcome of a claim?
Best: PhilHealth decides claims. Why not the others: none of these has that authority. Module 7.
Question 63A new transplant circular has just been published. For an existing case, what should you do first?
Best: publication is not effectivity, and older cases may follow older rules. Why not the others: each skips the checks or makes a promise. Module 7.
Question 64A family disputes a dialysis charge. What should it trigger?
Best: check the charge against the rule with the claims officer. Why not the others: falsification, unsafe advice, and promises are wrong. Module 7.
Question 65Under PC 2024-0023 as officially explained, how many hemodialysis sessions are covered a year?
Best: up to 156 sessions a year, per the circular and Tamang Sagot. Why not the others: the numbers are wrong; the benefit is not unlimited. Module 7.
Question 66A social media post says dialysis is “100% free, all year, no limits.” What is accurate?
Best: the official explanation covers no copayment for package services in public and private facilities, within a session limit. Why not the others: they overstate, understate, or misstate the rule. Module 7.
Question 67A patient has used 120 sessions by September 30 on three sessions a week. About when will the 156 run out?
Best: 156 − 120 = 36 sessions; 36 ÷ 3 = 12 weeks. Why not the others: the arithmetic is wrong. Module 7.
Question 68The peritoneal dialysis circular could not be fully retrieved. How should you discuss package amounts?
Best: unverified amounts must not be quoted. Why not the others: estimates and rumors become false promises. Module 7.
Question 69Which line best documents a benefit explanation?
Best: naming the version and date makes the advice checkable and correctable. Why not the others: they do not say which rule was used. Module 7.
Question 70A daughter asks how much PhilHealth will pay for her father’s transplant. What is the best answer?
Best: point to the official source and the provider, without promising. Why not the others: promises and refusals both fail her. Module 7.
Question 71How should a guarantee letter be treated?
Best: a guarantee letter covers a stated amount for a stated provider. Why not the others: it is not total coverage, cash, or a privacy waiver. Module 8.
Question 72What should an assistance directory contain?
Best: dated, specific information sends families to the right place. Why not the others: names alone, promises, or credentials are useless or harmful. Module 8.
Question 73Which assistance summary is strongest?
Best: honest, specific reports are credible. Why not the others: invention and copying are false documentation. Module 8.
Question 74A request is denied. What should you do?
Best: keep treatment safe and look for another route. Why not the others: promises, falsification, and unassessed loans harm the person. Module 8.
Question 75An office asks for a letter saying the patient “is dying” when that is not true. What do you do?
Best: false documentation harms the person and every future applicant. Why not the others: it remains false whoever writes it; sending them away abandons them. Module 8.
Question 76Total cost this month is PHP 12,000, confirmed support PHP 6,000, and a local request is pending. What is the gap?
Best: subtract only confirmed support. Why not the others: they count pending support, ignore confirmed support, or add instead of subtract. Module 8.
Question 77Which source is usually not available in a private freestanding dialysis unit?
Best: Malasakit Centers are in DOH hospitals and designated hospitals. Why not the others: those programs are reached through their own offices. Module 8.
Question 78A politician’s staff offer to pay a balance if the family appears in a photo. What do you do?
Best: the choice is theirs, made freely. Why not the others: deciding for them removes their choice. Module 8.
Question 79Why track applications to their decisions instead of counting submissions?
Best: help is what arrives. Why not the others: they misstate the purpose or the facts. Module 8.
Question 80What should you list before choosing assistance sources?
Best: start from the costs, then match sources. Why not the others: they are unfocused, excessive, or private. Module 8.
Question 81When is a transport plan incomplete?
Best: the return after dialysis, when people are weak, is often the hardest part. Why not the others: those are parts of a good plan. Module 9.
Question 82How should an employer letter be handled?
Best: consent and minimum disclosure. Why not the others: they overshare, overpromise, or exceed your role. Module 9.
Question 83A patient shortens sessions to catch a cheap ride. What is the best response?
Best: shortened sessions are clinical; the ride is your problem to solve. Why not the others: praise or advice is unsafe; a label solves nothing. Module 9.
Question 84What does an access cost worksheet establish?
Best: it is planning arithmetic. Why not the others: it creates no bill, entitlement, or eligibility. Module 9.
Question 85Six visits at PHP 120 out, 120 back, 0 escort, and 30 other, plus PHP 300 one-time, with PHP 1,000 confirmed support. What is the gap?
Best: 6 × 270 + 300 = 1,920; 1,920 − 1,000 = 920. Why not the others: they ignore support or miscalculate. Module 9.
Question 86Household health spending is PHP 4,000 of PHP 25,000 total spending. What is the share, and what does it mean?
Best: 4,000 ÷ 25,000 = 16%; the marks describe hardship, not program eligibility. Why not the others: wrong arithmetic or wrong meaning. Module 9.
Question 87What did the DOPPS travel time study find?
Best: an association from observational data with a single unvalidated question. Why not the others: they contradict or overstate the finding. Module 9.
Question 88A patient says the family eats rice and instant noodles “to save for the fare.” What should you do?
Best: food insecurity is a clinical risk (salt, weight loss) with a social cause. Why not the others: advice is unsafe; waiting lets harm happen. Module 9.
Question 89What should you ask every patient on dialysis about the PWD ID?
Best: people on dialysis may qualify as persons with disability (PWD), and the ID carries a legal discount. Why not the others: they miss or misdescribe the benefit. Module 9.
Question 90A patient lost his driving job after starting dialysis. Which step could recover income?
Best: benefits from past contributions may apply. Why not the others: unsafe, defeatist, or impossible. Module 9.
Question 91What does a screening score establish by itself?
Best: a screen such as the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) is the start of assessment. Why not the others: they overstate what a score can do. Module 10.
Question 92Sudden confusion appears during a session. What should happen?
Best: acute confusion can signal a medical emergency. Why not the others: they delay or misattribute it. Module 10.
Question 93You lack training in a specialized psychotherapy. What should you do?
Best: supportive work is yours; specialized therapy needs training. Why not the others: overreach, falsification, or withdrawal. Module 10.
Question 94The PHQ-9 total is low, but the patient discloses immediate intent to harm himself. What is the priority?
Best: a disclosure overrides any total. Why not the others: they let the number hide the danger. Module 10.
Question 95Items scored: interest 2, mood 2, sleep 3, energy 3, appetite 2, failure 2, concentration 1, slowness 1, item 9 = 0. What is the total and its meaning?
Best: the sum is 16, above the dialysis cutoff of 10; item 9 is 0. Why not the others: a score is not a diagnosis; 14 is wrong; the safety pathway follows item 9, not the total. Module 10.
Question 96By clinical interview, about how common was depression among people on dialysis in the Palmer meta-analysis?
Best: 22.8% by interview, 39.3% by scales, with scales likely overestimating. Why not the others: wrong figures or method. Module 10.
Question 97Which PHQ-9 items overlap most with symptoms of kidney failure?
Best: uremia, anemia, and itch affect sleep, energy, and appetite. Why not the others: those are core mood items the kidneys do not explain. Module 10.
Question 98What did the Cochrane review find about cognitive behavioral therapy for depression in dialysis?
Best: moderate-certainty improvement in symptom scores. Why not the others: survival was not shown; benefit was found; no harm was reported as the finding. Module 10.
Question 99What did ASCEND compare and find?
Best: an open-label comparison of two active treatments. Why not the others: they misdescribe the trial. Module 10.
Question 100A patient declines a mental health referral. What should you do?
Best: patients decide for their own reasons; your role is to offer and stay available. Why not the others: they override or abandon him. Module 10.
Question 101A person reports that they intend to harm themselves today. What is the first priority?

Question 102A suspected abuser controls the patient’s phone. How should you plan contact?
Best: contact must not alert the abuser. Why not the others: each risks exposure to the person causing harm. Module 11.
Question 103Which response to immediate danger is inadequate?
Best: a routine email may not be read in time. Why not the others: those are parts of an adequate response. Module 11.
Question 104What should a safeguarding note distinguish?
Best: a clear note lets others act and stands up to review. Why not the others: each leaves out what the next person needs. Module 11.
Question 105Does asking someone directly about suicidal thoughts increase their risk?
Best: the review found no evidence that asking induces ideation. Why not the others: they repeat a myth or use uncertainty as a reason to stay silent. Module 11.
Question 106A patient says, “My family would be better off without me.” What is the best next question?

Question 107In the meta-analysis of elder abuse in community settings, about what share of adults aged 60 or over experienced abuse in the past year?

Question 108An older patient’s nephew holds her ATM card and there is often no food at home. She asks you not to make trouble. What is the best approach?
Best: respect her autonomy while reducing harm and following the safeguarding procedure. Why not the others: confrontation can endanger her; inaction abandons her; capacity is not judged from a request for privacy. Module 11.
Question 109A woman discloses partner violence, and her partner brings her to dialysis. What should the safety plan address first?
Best: in kidney care, safety plans must keep treatment going. Why not the others: forcing action, disclosing, or stopping treatment endanger her. Module 11.
Question 110A patient asks to stop dialysis and also says he has thought of ending his life. What should happen?
Best: the two can overlap; safety comes first, and both go to the nephrologist. Why not the others: they miss the risk or dismiss the request. Module 11.
Question 111Which is a motivational opening?

Question 112A patient disagrees with his treatment. What does that alone prove?
Best: disagreement is information. Why not the others: each draws an unsupported conclusion. Module 12.
Question 113What makes a plan ethical in motivational work?
Best: partnership and respect for autonomy. Why not the others: coercion, guarantees, or bypassing consent are not ethical. Module 12.
Question 114Before exploring motivation after a missed session, what should you check?
Best: a missed session can be a clinical emergency. Why not the others: they are irrelevant or judgmental. Module 12.
Question 115What are the four core skills of motivational interviewing (OARS)?
Best: OARS are the conversational skills. Why not the others: they are not the motivational interviewing skills. Module 12.
Question 116A patient says, “I lose customers if I am late.” Which reply is motivational?
Best: a reflection that honors his reasons without arguing. Why not the others: arguing or dismissing triggers resistance. Module 12.
Question 117A patient leaves at hour two because of severe leg cramps. Whose problem is the cramp?
Best: a physical symptom during dialysis needs clinical review. Why not the others: counseling or willpower will not treat a cramp. Module 12.
Question 118What is the righting reflex?
Best: resisting it is central to motivational work. Why not the others: they misdefine it. Module 12.
