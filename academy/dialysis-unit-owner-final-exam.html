Before you begin

How this examination works

This examination covers the whole Dialysis Unit Owner series. Each sitting draws 24 questions at random from a bank of 144, one from each module, in random order, with the answer choices shuffled. Choose the single best answer for each question, then select Check answers.

When to take itAfter you have finished every module and reviewed the questions you missed.
How to take itIn one sitting, without notes. Allow about one minute per question.
Proposed pass mark20 of 24 best answers (80 percent). Your trainer may set a different mark.
If you fall shortEach explanation names its module. Reread those modules, then select New set of questions.

Abbreviations in this examination. Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth): Philippine Health Insurance Corporation. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC): Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. debt service coverage ratio (DSCR): debt service coverage ratio. full-time equivalent (FTE): full-time equivalent. Hemodialysis (HEMO) Study (HEMO): the Hemodialysis Study. in-center hemodialysis (ICH) Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems (CAHPS): In-Center Hemodialysis Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems. Department of Health (DOH): Department of Health. Philippine Society of Nephrology (PSN): Philippine Society of Nephrology. PhilHealth Dialysis Database (PDD): PhilHealth Dialysis Database. health maintenance organization (HMO): health maintenance organization. Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI): Herfindahl-Hirschman Index. Food and Drug Administration (Philippines) (FDA): Food and Drug Administration.

What a pass meansA pass shows that you know the material. It does not show that you can do the work. Competence is demonstrated on your unit’s own equipment and signed off by a preceptor.

Examination

End-of-course examination

Question 1A biomedical technician has held a machine after a failed alarm test. Who may return it to service?
Technical release belongs to the qualified technical lead under the unit’s procedure. Ownership and commercial pressure confer no technical competence. Module 1.
Question 2Which delegation will still work on a holiday?
Without a limit, a backup, and a trigger for escalation, the delegation fails exactly when the delegate is absent or the decision is too big. Module 1.
Question 3Which is the strongest evidence that a corrective action worked?
Payment, optimism, and silence are low on the assurance ladder. A re-audit against the closure criterion shows the control works. Module 1.
Question 4Which of these is a protected decision?
Fitness to dialyze is a clinical judgment. The others are reserved owner decisions. Module 1.
Question 5A shareholder orders a held water loop back into use. What should the board record and do?
The hold stands, the owner’s role is to fund the recovery, and the attempt is recorded so the charter can be corrected. Module 1.
Question 6In the national survey of hospital board chairs by Jha and Epstein, what did the data show?
The survey described attitudes and practices, including that fewer than one in ten chairs examined stratified quality data. It did not measure patient outcomes. Module 1.
Question 7Why does the weekend gap matter more than the midweek gaps?
With three sessions a week, one gap is about two and a half days. The longer the gap, the higher the fluid and potassium peak. Module 2.
Question 8In the study by Foley and colleagues, when were deaths highest?
Deaths, especially cardiac deaths, were higher on the day after the long gap. The study was observational. Module 2.
Question 9A schedule has no free chairs. What is the main safety problem?
Make-up slots are a safety control. Without them, a missed session turns into a much longer gap. Module 2.
Question 10The randomized HEMO Study tested a higher dose within the usual three-times-weekly schedule. What did it find?
The higher dose did not improve survival. Faster or more intense sessions are not a free substitute for time and frequency. Module 2.
Question 11A patient on your census receives a kidney transplant. How should the board record this?
Referral for transplant is good care. Reporting it as a quality measure keeps incentives pointed at patients. Module 2.
Question 12What did Garg and colleagues report about for-profit ownership in their United States cohort?
Adjusted hazard ratios were 1.20 for death and 0.74 for waitlisting. The study was observational and from another health system. Module 2.
Question 13Which document gives a facility the legal authority to provide hemodialysis?
The DOH license is the legal authority. Accreditation is a payer relationship; attestation is professional review. Module 3.
Question 14The PSN guideline and the DOH order give different nurse limits. What should the owner plan to?
Society guidance does not relax a binding licensing condition. Module 3.
Question 15Four stations are installed but not yet on the license. Patients are waiting. What is correct?
Treating on unlicensed stations exceeds the approved scope, however urgent the waiting list. Module 3.
Question 16What must every line in a compliance register have?
Without an owner and an alert, a requirement quietly expires. Module 3.
Question 17Which change should always trigger a review of the register before it takes effect?
Station count, address, named appointees, and new regulations all touch licensing and accreditation. Module 3.
Question 18What does a quarterly self-tracer do?
A tracer follows one patient and one machine through the evidence. Gaps found early are cheaper to fix. Module 3.
Question 19A letter names a nephrologist clinical head of “the company’s dialysis units” without listing sites. What is the main problem?
A role with no listed sites can silently expand beyond what one person can oversee. Module 4.
Question 20Which clause protects patients from commercial pressure most directly?
These clauses keep clinical decisions on clinical grounds. Module 4.
Question 21Which is an owner decision rather than a protected one?
Funding is an owner decision. The other three need clinical or technical judgment. Module 4.
Question 22What does the PSN guideline say about a separate unit manager?
The guideline separates the role above 15 stations. It is a society provision, not law. Module 4.
Question 23A bonus tied to filled chairs for the clinical head mainly risks:
Pay tied to volume pressures clinical decisions such as who is fit to dialyze or referred. Module 4.
Question 24Which evidence best supports a good annual review of a clinical head?
Patient-relevant outcomes and closed actions show performance. Volume and silence can hide risk. Module 4.
Question 25A bonus rewards the manager when every chair is filled. Which risk does it create?
Rewarding full chairs removes the slack that protects patients who miss a session. Module 5.
Question 26What did Thamer and colleagues find in 2004 United States data?
Ownership was associated with dosing. The study was observational. Module 5.
Question 27A board member’s cousin bids for the dialyzer contract. What should happen?
Declared conflicts are managed by recusal and equal criteria. Module 5.
Question 28A family complains about waiting times. What must never happen?
Fear of losing a slot silences patients. Care continues during a complaint. Module 5.
Question 29Before an involuntary discharge, which is required?
Discharge can leave a patient without dialysis; it is a last resort with safeguards. Module 5.
Question 30After acquisitions of independent United States facilities by large chains, what did Eliason and colleagues observe?
Acquired facilities converged on the chains’ strategies. The study was observational, using Medicare data. Module 5.
Question 31What should a feasibility study count first?
Need and travel time decide whether the unit adds access. Module 6.
Question 32Fixed costs are 630,000 pesos a month and contribution is 2,100 pesos a session. What is break-even?
630,000 ÷ 2,100 = 300 sessions a month. Module 6.
Question 33What does a sensitivity analysis tell you?
It ranks assumptions by their effect, so you test the dangerous ones. Module 6.
Question 34Which gate do families most often skip, with the most harm?
A building without nurses or water to standard cannot safely treat patients. Module 6.
Question 35What did Erickson and colleagues find about United States dialysis markets?
Markets stayed concentrated; new facilities offset some effects of consolidation on choice. Module 6.
Question 36Claims take 75 days to pay instead of 30. What changes most?
Collection time changes cash needs, not accounting break-even. Module 6.
Question 37Which line is most often missing from a new unit’s uses column?
Without working capital, the unit cannot pay staff while claims are pending. Module 7.
Question 38Annual cash available is 3.2 million pesos; annual debt service is 2.32 million. What is the DSCR?
3.2 ÷ 2.32 is about 1.38. Module 7.
Question 39What does a DSCR below 1 mean?
Below 1, the unit must find cash elsewhere to pay the loan. Module 7.
Question 40Why test coverage with slow collections?
Collection delay is one of the commonest reasons a sound plan runs short of cash. Module 7.
Question 41Which condition should block an owner distribution?
Payroll, remittances, and funded safety actions come before distributions. Module 7.
Question 42Why set aside a replacement reserve every year?
Planned replacement avoids a crisis that stops every session. Module 7.
Question 43Eleven usable stations, three shifts, six days. What is physical capacity?
11 × 3 × 6 = 198 station-sessions a week. Module 8.
Question 44Cleared staff can cover nine stations per shift. Safely staffed capacity is:
9 × 3 × 6 = 162 station-sessions a week. Module 8.
Question 45How many patients at three sessions a week can 162 station-sessions support with no slack?
162 ÷ 3 = 54. Module 8.
Question 46Why keep make-up slots free?
Rescheduling missed sessions is associated with fewer hospitalizations. Module 8.
Question 47Staffing is the narrowest gate. What should happen before more referrals are accepted?
Widen the narrowest gate first. Module 8.
Question 48Which outside service needs a written agreement before patients who need it are accepted?
Emergencies and admissions depend on a hospital agreement and a contact. Module 8.
Question 49Who should join design meetings besides the architect?
Each sees safety risks the architect may not. Module 9.
Question 50Why must clean and soiled flows be separated?
Crossing flows carry contamination to clean supplies and patients. Module 9.
Question 51When does commissioning end?
Construction and commissioning are different finishes. Module 9.
Question 52Who releases a new water system for patient use?
Water release is a protected technical decision. Module 9.
Question 53The contractor finishes early, but water validation is incomplete. What is the right decision?
No patient is treated on unreleased water. Module 9.
Question 54Which guideline should be the design brief for a Philippine dialysis clinic?
The DOH publishes planning and design guidelines for dialysis clinics. Module 9.
Question 55What did Thomas-Hawkins and colleagues associate with a higher patient load per registered nurse?
The study was observational, from United States units. Module 10.
Question 56Three posts, eighteen shifts, six hours, 48 contracted hours. What is the base FTE?
3 × 18 × 6 ÷ 48 = 6.75. Module 10.
Question 57Why add a relief factor?
Without relief, the roster fails whenever someone is absent. Module 10.
Question 58Can a patient care technician count toward the DOH nurse-to-station limit?
The limit is for nurses, and an Advanced Cardiovascular Life Support (ACLS)-trained nurse is required on every shift. Module 10.
Question 59The budget is short. Which response is safest?
Safe staffing is a protected constraint; volume and other costs can flex. Module 10.
Question 60What did the meta-analysis of nurse burnout find?
Burnout is a patient safety issue as well as a staffing one. Module 10.
Question 61Why must dialysis water be purer than drinking water?
Dialysis skips the gut’s protection. Module 11.
Question 62What did chlorine and chloramine in dialysis water cause in Brazilian outbreaks?
Residual chlorine and chloramine caused hemolysis outbreaks. Module 11.
Question 63The morning chlorine result is missing. What is correct?
A missing test is a failed test. Module 11.
Question 64Who releases a held water system?
Release is a protected technical decision. Module 11.
Question 65Why keep an asset register with replacement years?
Planned replacement prevents failures during sessions. Module 11.
Question 66What makes a generator a real backup?
Untested backups fail when needed. Module 11.
Question 67Which vascular access carries the highest bloodstream infection risk?
Catheters carry the highest risk. Module 12.
Question 68According to CDC data, how does the risk of S. aureus bloodstream infection in dialysis patients compare with other adults?
CDC surveillance put the risk at about 100 times higher. Module 12.
Question 69A supply required by the infection protocol was cut to save money. What should the owner do?
Savings on required infection supplies are paid by patients. Module 12.
Question 70How should a unit report bloodstream infections internally?
A rate with a denominator shows whether risk is changing. Module 12.
Question 71Who decides whether a patient with hepatitis B dialyzes in the isolation area?
Individual isolation decisions are clinical; the owner funds the area and machines. Module 12.
Question 72What did the CDC collaborative on the core interventions report?
The design had no separate control group, so it suggests rather than proves benefit. Module 12.
Question 73Stock lasts three days and the verified delivery arrives on day five. What is the gap?
Five minus three. Module 13.
Question 74Which is NOT one of the five qualification gates?
Price is a reason to start qualifying, not a gate. Module 13.
Question 75The owner’s nephew offers a cheaper product. What happens first?
Declare, recuse, and evaluate on equal criteria. Module 13.
Question 76What is the reorder point formula?
Recalculate it when lead time changes. Module 13.
Question 77Why record the lot number at every session?
Traceability is a safety system. Module 13.
Question 78A supply gap cannot be closed. Who decides which patients may have a delayed session?
Prioritizing treatment is a clinical decision. Module 13.
Question 79What is the PhilHealth hemodialysis package per session under Circular 2024-0023?
The package includes both the facility and professional fees. Module 14.
Question 80How many sessions a year does the package cover?
Up to 156 sessions per calendar year. Module 14.
Question 81A unit adds a flat 650-peso charge to every covered hemodialysis session. Under the current package, this is:
The package covers the minimum standard of care with no co-payment. A co-payment applies only to additional or premium services, explained in advance, with additional professional fees capped at 450 pesos. Module 14.
Question 82When should a patient receive a written estimate of charges?
Charges are disclosed before care begins. Module 14.
Question 83A patient will reach the 156-session limit in November. What should the unit do?
Care is not changed to fit the package. Module 14.
Question 84Which link is required before a hemodialysis claim is paid?
Registration in the PDD is one of the required links. Module 14.
Question 85Which five counts make up a session-to-cash reconciliation?
Each count should equal the one before it. Module 15.
Question 86Seven agreement forms were unsigned. A manager suggests backdated signatures. This is:
Backdating creates a false record. Module 15.
Question 87What does an exceptions log help the unit do?
Patterns point to system fixes. Module 15.
Question 88Why track the age of every open claim?
Age and ownership keep claims from expiring. Module 15.
Question 89Which is a lawful correction?
Correction means true documentation within the rules. Module 15.
Question 90What number links claims to working capital?
The longer collections take, the more cash the unit must finance. Module 15.
Question 91Fixed costs 920,000 pesos; contribution 4,250 pesos. Break-even is:
920,000 ÷ 4,250 = 216.5, so 217 sessions. Module 16.
Question 92Net revenue 3,000 pesos; variable cost 3,000 pesos. What is break-even?
With zero contribution, no volume covers fixed costs. Module 16.
Question 93Why does cost per treatment fall as volume rises?
Until a step adds fixed cost. Module 16.
Question 94Which cost should be held fixed in every scenario?
Safety costs are constraints. Module 16.
Question 95A competitor advertises free care but adds compulsory purchases. This is:
Patients need the true total. Module 16.
Question 96What is a step cost?
Steps change cost per treatment suddenly. Module 16.
Question 97Why can a profitable unit run short of cash?
The collection gap must be financed. Module 17.
Question 98Net revenue 2.54 million pesos a month; claims take 75 days. About how much is tied in receivables?
About 84,700 × 75. Module 17.
Question 99Cash on hand 3 million; weekly cash costs about 508,000. Runway is about:
3,000,000 ÷ 508,000 is about 5.9 weeks. Module 17.
Question 100Which control separates duties?
Segregation of duties prevents loss and fraud. Module 17.
Question 101Cash is short. What comes first?
People and legal obligations come first. Module 17.
Question 102Why build a disruption case in the forecast?
Disruptions are predictable in kind, if not in date. Module 17.
Question 103Which is a well-defined indicator?
A denominator makes rates comparable. Module 18.
Question 104On a run chart, which is a signal?
Runs and trends are signals; single points are usually noise. Module 18.
Question 105The Devereaux meta-analysis found for-profit United States centers were associated with:
Eight observational studies; association only. Module 18.
Question 106What did Salerno and colleagues find about star-rating improvement?
Independents improved less than large chains. Module 18.
Question 107Which column turns a report into governance?
Indicators should lead to decisions. Module 18.
Question 108A dashboard is all green, but patients are dying of fluid overload. The likeliest problem is:
Choose indicators that track real risks. Module 18.
Question 109In the ICH CAHPS study by Dad and colleagues, how did responders compare with nonresponders?
Surveys under-represent the sickest patients. Module 19.
Question 110A flyer offers a “free first session” covered by the package. The problem is:
Misleading claims attract by inducement. Module 19.
Question 111A 500-peso thank-you for every referral is:
Referral payments create inducements and legal risk. Module 19.
Question 112A unit has 14 free slots but can safely staff 8 more patients. How many should it accept?
The narrowest gate sets the pace. Module 19.
Question 113A unit reports zero complaints all year. This means:
Silence is not proof of quality. Module 19.
Question 114Who is best placed to collect bedside feedback?
Patients speak more freely to someone without power over their slot. Module 19.
Question 115Under the Data Privacy Act, health records are:
They need extra protection and lawful, proportionate processing. Module 20.
Question 116Among 374 ransomware attacks on US health care organizations, what share disrupted care?
44.4% disrupted care delivery. Module 20.
Question 117What makes a backup trustworthy?
Only a tested restore proves a backup works. Module 20.
Question 118What should the owner see from patient records?
Board reports need counts and rates. Module 20.
Question 119During downtime, how do nurses continue dialysis safely?
A printed kit keeps care safe. Module 20.
Question 120A vendor maintains the record system. What should the agreement include?
Vendors get need-to-know access under written terms. Module 20.
Question 121What did Lurie and colleagues find about dialysis before Hurricane Sandy?
Observational evidence supports dialyzing early before landfall. Module 21.
Question 122Which is a dependency that needs a tested backup?
Power stops the blood pump and the water system. Module 21.
Question 123Where should a partner unit for transfers ideally be?
A partner in the same hazard zone fails at the same time. Module 21.
Question 124After a typhoon, when can the unit reopen?
The restart gate mirrors opening day. Module 21.
Question 125Who decides the order in which patients are dialyzed after reopening?
Priority is a clinical triage decision based on urgency, not a simple missed-session order. Module 21.
Question 126When should the generator be tested?
Untested backups fail when needed. Module 21.
Question 127After a serious event, what comes first?
Care and safety come first. Module 22.
Question 128Why preserve the machine, lines, and logs?
Altered evidence hides system causes. Module 22.
Question 129What did the Massachusetts communication-and-resolution study find?
Observational evidence from another legal system. Module 22.
Question 130A nurse involved in an event is distressed. This is called:
Support, not blame, is the right response. Module 22.
Question 131What should the media hear?
One spokesperson, truthful and respectful of privacy. Module 22.
Question 132A risk with no matching insurance policy is:
Know your gaps before an event. Module 22.
Question 133What did Eliason and colleagues observe after chain acquisitions?
Observational evidence from US Medicare data. Module 23.
Question 134What did the systematic review of private equity in health care most consistently find?
Quality effects were mixed to harmful. Module 23.
Question 135A buyer promises to “standardize staffing models.” What should a seller ask?
Staffing changes drove outcomes in the research. Module 23.
Question 136When is a closure finished?
Responsible exit is measured by patients placed. Module 23.
Question 137Why write a succession plan before a sale?
Units without succession plans can stall in a crisis. Module 23.
Question 138Before opening a second site, the owner should:
Expand from strength, through the same gates. Module 23.
Question 139The water loop is held and payroll is due Friday. Which decision is the owner’s?
The owner funds and protects; the qualified people decide the rest. Module 24.
Question 140What belongs on the board pack’s cover sheet?
Board packs exist to produce decisions. Module 24.
Question 141Which is a capstone critical failure?
Crossing a protected line fails the capstone. Module 24.
Question 142What are the three phases of the first ninety days?
Secure the basics before building. Module 24.
Question 143A chain calls about buying the unit during a crisis week. What is a sensible response?
Strategy waits while safety and people come first. Module 24.
Question 144Who should sit on the capstone panel?
Applied judgment is tested by people who understand the protected lines. Module 24.
