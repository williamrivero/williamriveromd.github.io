His practice integrates evidence-based medicine with functional and integrative approaches — recognizing that kidney disease, diabetes, hypertension, and metabolic disorders are deeply interconnected and require a whole-person strategy. Pinagsasama ng kanyang pagsasanay ang medisina batay sa ebidensya at mga integratibong pamamaraan — kinikilala na ang sakit sa bato, diyabetes, hypertension, at mga metabolikong karamdaman ay malalim na magkakaugnay at nangangailangan ng buong-taong estratehiya. Ang iyang praktis naghiusa sa medisina nga nakabase sa ebidensya ug integratibong mga pamaagi — nga nakaila nga ang sakit sa kidney, diabetes, hypertension, ug mga metabolikong sakit hugot nga magkakaugnay ug nanginahanglan og tibuok-tawo nga estratehiya.

Dr. Rivero is committed to clear communication and patient education, ensuring that every patient and family understands their condition, their medicines, and their path forward. Common Questions

Frequently asked questions Mga Madalas na Tanong Mga Kanunay nga Gipangutana

Answers to the questions patients most commonly ask before their first visit. Mga sagot sa mga tanong na madalas itanong ng mga pasyente bago ang kanilang unang pagbisita. Mga tubag sa mga pangutana nga kanunay gipangutana sa mga pasyente sa wala pa ang ilang unang pagbisita.

What is a nephrologist, and when should I see one? Ano ang isang nefrolohista, at kailan ko siya dapat bisitahin? Unsa ang usa ka nephrologist, ug kanus-a ko siya angay bisitahan?
A nephrologist is a specialist in kidney diseases. You should see one if you have been told your kidneys are not functioning normally, if your creatinine or eGFR is abnormal, if you have persistent protein or blood in your urine, or if you have diabetes or hypertension and have not had a kidney function check in the past year. Your family doctor or internist can refer you, but you may also book directly.
Do you accept PhilHealth? What about HMOs? Tumatanggap ba kayo ng PhilHealth? Paano ang HMO? Modawat ba kamo og PhilHealth? Unsa ang HMO?
Yes — PhilHealth is accepted at all clinic locations. For HMO coverage, please contact your HMO provider directly and ask for an LOA (Letter of Authorization) before your appointment, as accepted HMOs vary by clinic location. When in doubt, call the clinic directly or message via SeriousMD.
What is the difference between an internist and a nephrologist? Ano ang pagkakaiba ng isang internista at isang nefrolohista? Unsa ang kalainan tali sa internist ug nephrologist?
An internist (specialist in Internal Medicine) manages a wide range of adult diseases — diabetes, hypertension, infections, heart disease, and more. A nephrologist is an internist who completed additional subspecialty training specifically in kidney diseases, dialysis, and electrolyte disorders. Dr. Rivero is both — he completed his Internal Medicine residency and Nephrology fellowship at St. Luke's Medical Center, and manages patients across both disciplines.
I have diabetes. Should I be seeing a nephrologist? Mayroon akong diyabetes. Dapat ba akong makita ng isang nefrolohista? Aduna akoy diabetes. Kinahanglan ba akong makita sa usa ka nephrologist?
If you have had diabetes for more than 5 years, or if your doctor has found protein in your urine or a rising creatinine, a nephrology consultation is strongly recommended. Diabetic kidney disease is the leading cause of kidney failure in the Philippines and is often silent until significantly advanced. Early detection and treatment can slow or stop progression entirely.
What should I bring to my first consultation? Ano ang dapat dalhin sa aking unang konsultasyon? Unsa ang akong dad-on sa akong unang konsultasyon?
Bring all recent laboratory results (blood tests, urine tests, imaging), a list of current medications with doses, your PhilHealth MDR card, any previous medical records or discharge summaries, and your HMO LOA if applicable. If you have had a kidney ultrasound or CT scan, bring the actual images (CD/film), not just the report.
Can I consult online? Maaari ba akong kumonsulta online? Makakakonsulta ba ako online?
Yes. Online consultations are available through SeriousMD — bookable 24/7. Online consultations are suitable for follow-up visits, medication adjustments, result interpretations, and second opinions. Initial consultations for new, complex, or undiagnosed conditions are best done in person so that a physical examination can be performed.
How often do I need to see a nephrologist once I'm diagnosed with CKD? Gaano kadalas ako dapat makita ng nefrolohista kapag na-diagnose na ako ng CKD? Unsa kadalas nga makita ko ang nephrologist kung na-diagnose na ako sa CKD?
Visit frequency depends on your CKD stage: Stage 1–2: every 6–12 months. Stage 3: every 3–6 months. Stage 4: every 1–3 months. Stage 5 / dialysis: monthly or more frequently. More frequent visits may be needed when starting new medications, adjusting doses, or managing acute changes. Regular monitoring is what protects your kidneys — do not skip follow-ups even when you feel well.
My creatinine is slightly elevated but I feel fine. Is that a problem? Medyo mataas ang aking creatinine ngunit okay naman ang pakiramdam ko. Problema ba iyon? Medyo taas ang akong creatinine apan maayo man ang akong gibati. Problema ba kana?
Yes — and this is exactly why CKD is called a "silent disease." Symptoms of kidney disease typically don't appear until 60–70% of kidney function is already lost. A mildly elevated creatinine may represent Stage 2–3 CKD with no symptoms at all. The earlier it is evaluated and managed, the better the long-term outcome. Please do not wait for symptoms before seeking evaluation.
