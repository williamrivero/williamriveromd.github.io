    CrCl ≥60 mL/min
    IV for metabolic acidosis (adults and children 2 years and older): 2 to 5 mEq/kg over 4 to 8 hours, by bicarbonate, total CO2, pH and clinical state (label). Treat the cause.
    CrCl 30–59 mL/min
    No kidney dose change in the label. Kidney disease and anuria increase sensitivity to the sodium load: watch for fluid overload, edema and blood pressure, and adjust other sodium sources (label 5.1). Monitor bicarbonate, sodium and potassium; avoid overshoot alkalosis. Oral tablets for CKD metabolic acidosis: consider treatment to keep serum bicarbonate from falling below 18 mmol/L, and do not overshoot the upper limit of normal; watch blood pressure, potassium and fluid status (KDIGO 2024 PP 3.10.1, 3.10.2). Each 650 mg tablet supplies about 7.7 mEq of sodium and bicarbonate (325 mg about 3.9 mEq); 8.4% solution is 1 mEq/mL (84 mg per mEq; label 3: 12.6 mg/mL = 0.150 mEq/mL). Oral doses are not in the label.

    Hemodialysis (intermittent)
    No dose change; anuria raises the risk of fluid overload from the sodium load (label 5.1). Dialysate bicarbonate also corrects acidosis; coordinate with the dialysis prescription (clinical judgment).
    Peritoneal dialysis
    No dose change; watch sodium load and fluid status (label 5.1).
    CRRT
    No data in label. CRRT fluids are usually bicarbonate-buffered; added IV bicarbonate is a specialist decision guided by blood gases.
    • Dialyzable? Bicarbonate crosses dialysis membranes in both directions according to the dialysate bicarbonate concentration (physiology; not stated in label).
    • Monitoring Blood gases or serum bicarbonate, sodium, potassium (hypokalemia as acidosis corrects), fluid status, blood pressure, edema. Extravasation site (hypertonic solutions).
