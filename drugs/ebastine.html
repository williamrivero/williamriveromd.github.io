Clinical evidence and limitations

Study regimens are descriptions of research, not prescribing recommendations.

StudyDesign and populationTreatment and durationMain findingsLimitations
Seasonal rhinitis RCT 2000 2Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled study; 565 participants; four weeks.Two oral ebastine regimens, loratadine and placebo.Both ebastine regimens improved rhinitis symptom scores; the higher regimen improved scores more than loratadine. Overall adverse-event frequency was similar across groups.Short symptom trial; efficacy and safety should not be generalized to every allergic condition or long-term exposure.
Renal pharmacokinetic study 2007 3Open-label parallel study; 24 renal-impaired participants (8 each mild/moderate/severe) and 12 healthy controls; five days.Ebastine with measured 24-hour creatinine clearance and ECG/laboratory monitoring.No clinically important exposure, half-life or short-term safety difference was found; between-subject variability was large.Small and short; the creatinine-clearance measure must not be replaced with eGFR. This study does not establish a dialysis regimen.
Ketoconazole interaction study 4Randomized healthy-male interaction study.Ebastine alone, then with ketoconazole; placebo/ketoconazole control.Ketoconazole increased parent-ebastine AUC 42.5-fold. QTc rose with combination treatment; the incremental rise over ketoconazole alone was not statistically significant.Healthy subjects, brief exposure; lack of statistical significance does not prove absence of arrhythmia risk in susceptible patients.

Mechanism of action

Blocks histamine H1-mediated allergic responses; carebastine is its active metabolite 2,3.

Class: Second-generation histamine H1 antagonist
