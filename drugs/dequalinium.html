Clinical evidence and limitations

Study regimens are descriptions of research, not prescribing recommendations.

StudyDesign and populationTreatment and durationMain findingsLimitations
Raba 2024 2Triple-blind double-dummy randomized noninferiority trial; 147 premenopausal adult women; one-month follow-up.Vaginal dequalinium tablets versus oral metronidazole for bacterial vaginosis.Early clinical cure was 92.8% versus 93.2%; the prespecified 15-percentage-point noninferiority criterion was met.Short follow-up, broad margin, manufacturer involvement. This is vaginal-formulation evidence; it cannot establish efficacy of antiseptic lozenges or long-term recurrence prevention.
Local throat-preparation study 2025 3Noninterventional observational study; 90 adults; before/after one exposure.Dequalinium plus cinchocaine lozenges versus different-agent spray or gargle.Only 10% of lozenge-group samples showed reduced postexposure bacterial-growth intensity; the gargle performed better on this microbiological measure.Combination product, unblinded, no randomized allocation or placebo; 20-minute bacterial-density surrogate does not establish sore-throat symptom benefit or efficacy of dequalinium alone.

Adverse reactions

Common

  • In the vaginal-tablet trial, reported treatment-related events included candidiasis, pruritus and headache. These findings are not an adverse-reaction inventory for throat lozenges 2.
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Mechanism of action

Antimicrobial activity has been studied in different local formulations; evidence from vaginal tablets and combination throat lozenges addresses different clinical questions 2,3.
