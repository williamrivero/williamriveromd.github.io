    Use: Klor-Con is indicated for the treatment and prophylaxis of hypokalemia with or without metabolic alkalosis, in patients for whom dietary management with potassium-rich foods or diuretic dose reduction is insufficient. US FDA label

    CrCl ≥60 mL/min
    Hypokalemia treatment: 40 to 100 mEq/day in divided doses, no more than 40 mEq per dose; prevention: about 20 mEq/day. Use IV potassium if serum potassium is below 2.5 mEq/L (label).
    CrCl 30–59 mL/min
    Reduced potassium excretion and a much higher risk of hyperkalemia: start at the low end of the dose range, especially with RAAS inhibitors or NSAIDs; monitor serum potassium frequently and kidney function periodically (label 8.7). IV concentrate: must be diluted; never IV push (see IV label).
    CrCl 15–29 mL/min
    Reduced potassium excretion and a much higher risk of hyperkalemia: start at the low end of the dose range, especially with RAAS inhibitors or NSAIDs; monitor serum potassium frequently and kidney function periodically (label 8.7). IV concentrate: must be diluted; never IV push (see IV label).
    CrCl <15 mL/min (not on dialysis)
    Reduced potassium excretion and a much higher risk of hyperkalemia: start at the low end of the dose range, especially with RAAS inhibitors or NSAIDs; monitor serum potassium frequently and kidney function periodically (label 8.7). Specialist input. IV concentrate: must be diluted; never IV push (see IV label).
    Hemodialysis (intermittent)
    No dialysis dose in label; potassium removal depends on the dialysate. Give only for documented hypokalemia, start low and check potassium frequently (label 8.7); specialist input. IV concentrate (2 mEq/mL) must be diluted before use; never give by IV push or undiluted (fatal arrhythmia and cardiac arrest reported). In kidney impairment potassium is retained: use the low end of the infusion rate in the IV product label, with ECG monitoring as needed, and recheck potassium after each replacement. Concentration and rate limits: see the IV product label.
    Peritoneal dialysis
    No PD dose in label. PD patients can become hypokalemic; replace by measured potassium, starting low (label 8.7); specialist input. Hypokalemia is a modifiable risk factor for PD peritonitis (ISPD 2022).
    CRRT
    No data in label; replacement guided by frequent potassium levels and CRRT fluid potassium. Specialist input.
    • Dialyzable? Potassium is removed by hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis (label lists dialysis among hyperkalemia treatments).
    • Monitoring Serum potassium frequently in kidney impairment and periodically on maintenance; kidney function; magnesium, acid-base status and ECG in cardiac or kidney disease (label 2.1, 8.7).

    From the Potassium chloride monograph (renalcarematters.com, reviewed).
