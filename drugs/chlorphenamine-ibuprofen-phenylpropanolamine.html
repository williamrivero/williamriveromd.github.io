    CrCl ≥60 mL/min
    Usual: pain 400 mg every 4 to 6 hours as needed; arthritis 1,200 to 3,200 mg/day in 3 or 4 doses; maximum 3,200 mg/day (label). Lowest effective dose, shortest duration.
    CrCl 30–59 mL/min
    No label dose change. Impaired kidney function is a risk factor for NSAID kidney injury: monitor creatinine, potassium and blood pressure; avoid volume depletion and the ACE inhibitor or ARB plus diuretic combination.
    CrCl 15–29 mL/min
    Not recommended in advanced renal disease (no controlled data); if it must be started, monitor kidney function closely (label, Warnings: Advanced Renal Disease).
    CrCl <15 mL/min (not on dialysis)
    Not recommended in advanced renal disease (no controlled data); if it must be started, monitor kidney function closely (label, Warnings: Advanced Renal Disease).
    Hemodialysis (intermittent)
    Not recommended (advanced renal disease; label). NSAIDs may hasten loss of residual kidney function.
    Peritoneal dialysis
    Not recommended (advanced renal disease; label). NSAIDs may hasten loss of residual kidney function.
    CRRT
    Avoid: NSAIDs can worsen acute kidney injury.
    • Dialyzable? Not stated in the label.
    • Monitoring Creatinine, potassium and blood pressure in CKD, heart failure, liver disease, older adults, or with diuretics, ACE inhibitors or ARBs. Gastrointestinal bleeding; hemoglobin with long-term use.

Kidney injury risk

Ibuprofen

Reduced kidney blood flow
  • Highest risk with volume depletion, heart failure, liver disease, older age, and use with diuretics, ACE inhibitors or ARBs.
Renal Effects Long-term administration of NSAIDs has resulted in renal papillary necrosis and other renal injury.US label: Ibuprofen (Amneal Pharmaceuticals LLC), Warnings and precautions
