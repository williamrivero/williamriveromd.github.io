Contraindications

  • CrCl below 30 mL/min
  • Heart failure (NYHA II–IV); established ischemic heart disease, peripheral arterial disease or cerebrovascular disease
  • Uncontrolled hypertension (persistently above 140/90 mmHg)
  • Active peptic ulcer or gastrointestinal bleeding; inflammatory bowel disease
  • Severe hepatic dysfunction (albumin <25 g/L or Child-Pugh 10 or more)
  • Aspirin- or NSAID-induced asthma, urticaria or allergic reactions
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding
  • Children under 16 years

Dosing: Adult

  • Osteoarthritis: 30 mg once daily; may increase to 60 mg once daily (maximum)
  • Rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis: 60 mg once daily; may increase to 90 mg once daily (maximum); step down to 60 mg when stable
  • Acute gouty arthritis: 120 mg once daily, for no more than 8 days
  • Pain after dental surgery: 90 mg once daily, for no more than 3 days
  • Hepatic impairment: Child-Pugh 5–6: maximum 60 mg once daily; Child-Pugh 7–9: maximum 30 mg once daily; Child-Pugh 10 or more: contraindicated

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Etoricoxib is a selective cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitor. Like other nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) it can reduce kidney blood flow, cause fluid retention, hypertension and hyperkalemia, and precipitate acute kidney injury. The European label needs no dose change at creatinine clearance (CrCl) 30 mL/min or above but contraindicates it below 30 mL/min. It has no US FDA label.

Kidney injury risk

Reduced kidney blood flow
  • Highest risk with volume depletion, heart failure, liver disease, older age, and use with diuretics, ACE inhibitors or ARBs.

Adverse reactions

Common

  • Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, nausea, diarrhea, heartburn
  • Edema or fluid retention
  • Hypertension
  • Dizziness, headache
  • Raised ALT or AST

Serious

  • Myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure
  • Gastrointestinal ulceration, perforation and bleeding
  • Kidney failure, hyperkalemia, proteinuria
  • Hypertensive crisis
  • Anaphylaxis, angioedema, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis
  • Hepatitis, hepatic failure
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Monitoring parameters

  • Kidney function and potassium in patients with reduced kidney perfusion, heart failure or cirrhosis, and after starting diuretics, ACE inhibitors or ARBs. Blood pressure within 2 weeks of starting and periodically. Edema and heart failure. Liver tests if symptoms (SmPC 4.4). Kidney
  • Blood pressure within 2 weeks of starting, then periodically
  • Kidney function and potassium in at-risk patients
  • Signs of edema, heart failure, gastrointestinal bleeding
  • Liver tests if symptomatic
