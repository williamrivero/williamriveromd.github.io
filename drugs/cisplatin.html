Dosing: Adult

Dosage and administration (US label) US FDA label
  • Administer pre-treatment hydration and pre- and post-treatment antiemetics. (2.1)
  • AXYCIS has been administered by slow continuous infusion for: ○ Advanced Testicular Cancer: 20 mg/m 2 daily for 5 days per cycle. (2.2)
  • ○ Advanced Ovarian Cancer: 75 mg/m 2 to 100 mg/m 2 per cycle once every 3 to 4 weeks. (2.3)
  • ○ Advanced Bladder Cancer: 50 mg/m 2 to 70 mg/m 2 intravenously per cycle once every 3 to 4 weeks. (2.4)
  • Avoid contact of cisplatin with aluminum parts. (2.5)
2.1 Important Instructions for Dosage and Administration

2.1 Important Instructions for Dosage and Administration Patients treated with AXYCIS must receive appropriate pre-treatment hydration. Maintain adequate hydration and urinary output for 24 hours after AXYCIS administration [see Warnings and Precautions (5.1)]. Administer pre-treatment and post-treatment antiemetics as appropriate [see Warnings and Precautions (5.3)].

To prevent inadvertent cisplatin overdosage, contact prescriber if dose ordered is greater than 100 mg/m 2 per cycle.

2.2 Advanced Testicular Cancer

2.2 Advanced Testicular Cancer AXYCIS has been administered at 20 mg/m 2 intravenously daily for 5 days per cycle. Other doses and combination regimens have been used.

2.3 Advanced Ovarian Cancer

2.3 Advanced Ovarian Cancer AXYCIS has been administered at 75 mg/m 2 to 100 mg/m 2 intravenously per cycle once every 3 to 4 weeks. Other doses and combination regimens have been used.

2.4 Advanced Bladder Cancer

2.4 Advanced Bladder Cancer AXYCIS has been administered at 50 mg/m 2 to 70 mg/m 2 intravenously per cycle once every 3 to 4 weeks. For heavily pretreated patients, an initial dose of 50 mg/m 2 per cycle repeated every 4 weeks is recommended. Other doses and combination regimens have been used.

2.5 Preparation, Handling, and Administration

2.5 Preparation, Handling, and Administration Preparation AXYCIS is a hazardous drug. Follow applicable special handling and disposal procedures. 1 Do not use needles or intravenous sets containing aluminum parts that can come in contact with cisplatin during preparation or administration. Aluminum reacts with cisplatin, causing precipitate formation and a loss of potency.

Parenteral drug products should be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration, whenever solution and container permit. Discard if the solution is discolored or contains particulate matter. AXYCIS is supplied as a multiple-dose vial. After first use, store the partially used vial in the original carton at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) to protect from light.

Discard after 28 days. Do not refrigerate. Select the appropriate number of vial(s) based on the prescribed dose. Withdraw the calculated volume of AXYCIS from the vial. Dilute the calculated volume of AXYCIS in an infusion bag with one of the following diluents: ○ 2 liters of 5% Dextrose in 0.45 % Sodium Chloride Injection, containing 37.5 g of mannitol. ○ 2 liters of 5% Dextrose in 0.33% Sodium Chloride Injection, containing 37.5 g of mannitol.

DO NOT dilute AXYCIS in solutions containing only 5% Dextrose Injection. After preparation, store the diluted solution: ○ at room temperature of 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [see USP Controlled Room Temperature] for no more than 12 hours under fluorescent light, or ○ at room temperature of 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) for no more than 24 hours when protected from light.

If not used immediately, discard diluted AXYCIS after 12 hours when stored under fluorescent light, and after 24 hours when protected from light. Administration Administer AXYCIS by slow intravenous infusion over a 6- to 8-hour period. AXYCIS SHOULD NOT BE GIVEN BY RAPID INTRAVENOUS INJECTION.

Dosing: Pediatric

Pediatric use (US label) US FDA label

8.4 Pediatric Use The safety and effectiveness of AXYCIS in pediatric patients have not been established. Ototoxic effects may be more severe and detrimental in pediatric patients receiving cisplatin, particularly in patients less than 5 years of age. Consider audiometric and vestibular function monitoring in all patients receiving AXYCIS.

The prevalence of hearing loss in pediatric patients is particularly high and is estimated to be 40% to 60%. Earlier detection of hearing loss can limit the potential impact of hearing impairment on a pediatric patient's cognitive and social development [see Warnings and Precautions (5.6)].

Dosing: Older adult

Geriatric use (US label) US FDA label

8.5 Geriatric Use For the treatment of metastatic testicular tumors or advanced bladder cancer, clinical studies of cisplatin did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine whether they respond differently from younger subjects. In four clinical trials of combination chemotherapy for advanced ovarian carcinoma, 1,484 patients received cisplatin either in combination with cyclophosphamide or with paclitaxel.

Of these, 426 (29%) were older than 65 years. In these trials, age was not found to be a prognostic factor for survival. However, in a later secondary analysis for one of these trials, geriatric patients were found to have shorter survival compared with younger patients. In all four trials, geriatric patients experienced more severe neutropenia than did younger patients.

Higher incidences of severe thrombocytopenia and leukopenia were also seen in geriatric patients compared with younger patients, although not in all cisplatin-containing treatment arms. In the two trials where nonhematologic toxicity was evaluated according to age, geriatric patients had a numerically higher incidence of peripheral neuropathy than did younger patients.

Other reported clinical experience suggests that geriatric patients may be more susceptible to nephrotoxicity, myelosuppression, and infectious complications than are younger patients [see Warnings and Precautions (5.1, 5.2, 5.4)].

5.1 Nephrotoxicity

5.1 Nephrotoxicity AXYCIS can cause dose-related nephrotoxicity, including acute renal failure that becomes more prolonged and severe with repeated courses of the drug. Renal toxicity typically begins during the second week after a dose of AXYCIS. Patients with renal impairment, geriatric patients, patients who are taking other nephrotoxic drugs, or patients who are not well hydrated may be more susceptible to nephrotoxicity [see Use in Specific Populations (8.5)].

Ensure adequate hydration before, during, and after AXYCIS administration [see Dosage and Administration (2.1)]. Measure serum creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine clearance, and serum electrolytes including magnesium prior to initiating therapy, and as clinically indicated. Consider magnesium supplementation as clinically needed.

Consider alternative treatments or reduce the dose of AXYCIS for patients with renal impairment or who develop significant reductions in creatinine clearance during treatment with AXYCIS according to clinical treatment guidelines.

5.2 Peripheral Neuropathy

5.2 Peripheral Neuropathy AXYCIS can cause dose-related peripheral neuropathy that becomes more severe with repeated courses of the drug. Neurologic symptoms can occur after a single dose. Neuropathy can also have a delayed onset from 3 to 8 weeks after the last dose of AXYCIS. Manifestations include paresthesias in a stocking-glove distribution, areflexia, and loss of proprioception and vibratory sensation.

The neuropathy may progress further even after stopping treatment. Peripheral neuropathy may be irreversible in some patients. Prolonged treatment, treatment with other platinum-containing therapies, or use in combination with other drugs that cause peripheral neuropathy may increase the incidence or severity of peripheral neuropathy.

Geriatric patients may be more susceptible to peripheral neuropathy [see Use in Specific Populations (8.5)]. Monitor for signs and symptoms of peripheral neuropathy. Withhold, reduce, or discontinue AXYCIS depending on the severity and persistence of peripheral neuropathy as clinically indicated.

5.3 Nausea and Vomiting

5.3 Nausea and Vomiting AXYCIS is a highly emetogenic antineoplastic agent. Administer pre-treatment and post-treatment antiemetics as clinically indicated [see Dosage and Administration (2.1)]. Without antiemetic therapy, marked nausea and vomiting occur in almost all patients treated with cisplatin and may be so severe that the drug must be discontinued.

Nausea and vomiting may begin within 1 to 4 hours after treatment and last up to 72 hours. Maximal intensity occurs 48 to 72 hours after administration. Various degrees of vomiting, nausea, and/or anorexia may persist for up to 1 week after treatment. Delayed nausea and vomiting (begins or persists 24 hours or more after chemotherapy) has occurred in patients attaining complete emetic control on the day of cisplatin therapy.

Consider the use of additional antiemetics following infusion. Monitor and manage patients with antiemetics or fluid replacement, as clinically indicated. Consider withholding or delaying AXYCIS if nausea or vomiting is severe or intolerable and is not responsive to antiemetics.

5.4 Myelosuppression

5.4 Myelosuppression AXYCIS can cause myelosuppression. Myelosuppression occurs in 25% to 30% of patients treated with cisplatin, including febrile neutropenia and infection, and can be fatal. Fatalities due to infection (secondary to myelosuppression) have been reported. Geriatric patients may be more susceptible to myelosuppression [see Use in Specific Populations (8.5)].

Patients with impaired kidney function are at increased risk of severe myelosuppression and may require dosage modifications. Monitor complete blood counts prior to each cycle and as clinically indicated. If myelosuppression occurs, modify AXYCIS dosage when required.

5.5 Hypersensitivity Reactions

5.5 Hypersensitivity Reactions AXYCIS can cause severe hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis and death. Hypersensitivity reactions included facial edema, wheezing, tachycardia, and hypotension. These adverse reactions may occur within minutes of administration and during any cycle. Monitor patients receiving AXYCIS for hypersensitivity reactions.

Ensure supportive equipment and medications are available to treat severe hypersensitivity reactions. Severe hypersensitivity reactions require immediate discontinuation of AXYCIS and aggressive therapy. Do not rechallenge patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity reactions with AXYCIS [see Contraindications (4)].

Cross-reactivity between platinum-based antineoplastic agents has been reported. Cases of severe hypersensitivity reactions have recurred after rechallenging patients with a different platinum agent.

5.6 Ototoxicity

5.6 Ototoxicity AXYCIS can cause ototoxicity, which is cumulative and may be severe. Monitor for signs and symptoms of ototoxicity and consider audiometric and vestibular function monitoring. Ototoxicity is manifested by tinnitus, hearing loss in the high frequency range (4,000 to 8,000 Hz) and/or decreased ability to hear normal conversational tones, and vestibular toxicity.

Ototoxicity can occur during or after treatment and can be unilateral or bilateral. Deafness after the initial dose of cisplatin has been reported. Ototoxic effects can be more severe and detrimental in pediatric patients, particularly in patients less than 5 years of age. The prevalence of hearing loss in pediatric patients is estimated to be 40-60%.

Additional risk factors for ototoxicity include simultaneous cranial irradiation, treatment with other ototoxic drugs and renal impairment. Consider audiometric and vestibular testing in all pediatric patients receiving cisplatin [see Use in Specific Populations (8.4)]. Genetic factors (e.g. variants in the thiopurine S-methyltransferase [TPMT] gene) may also contribute to the cisplatin-induced ototoxicity; this association has not been consistent across populations and study designs.

Consider permanent discontinuation of AXYCIS for patients who develop ototoxicity.

5.7 Ocular Toxicity

5.7 Ocular Toxicity AXYCIS can cause ocular toxicity including optic neuritis, papilledema, and cortical blindness. Blurred vision and altered color perception have been reported after the use of regimens with higher doses and dose frequencies of cisplatin. The altered color perception manifests as a loss of color discrimination, particularly in the blue-yellow axis and irregular retinal pigmentation of the macular area on fundoscopic exam.

Improvement and/or total recovery usually occurs after discontinuing AXYCIS but can be delayed. Monitor for signs and symptoms of ocular toxicity. Promptly refer patients to an eye care provider for any new or worsening ocular signs and symptoms. Interrupt, reduce the dose, or permanently discontinue AXYCIS based on severity.

5.8 Secondary Malignancies

5.8 Secondary Malignancies Secondary acute leukemia has occurred with use of cisplatin.

5.9 Embryo-Fetal Toxicity

5.9 Embryo-Fetal Toxicity Based on human data, AXYCIS can cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman. Advise pregnant women and females of reproductive potential of the potential risk to a fetus. Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for 14 months after the last dose of AXYCIS.

Advise male patients with female partners of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for 11 months after the last dose of AXYCIS [see Use in Specific Populations (8.1, 8.3)].

5.10 Injection Site Reactions

5.10 Injection Site Reactions Injection site reactions can occur during the administration of AXYCIS. Local soft tissue toxicity has been reported following extravasation of AXYCIS. Severity of the local tissue toxicity appears to be related to the concentration of the AXYCIS solution. Infusion of solutions with a cisplatin concentration greater than 0.5 mg/mL may result in tissue cellulitis, fibrosis, necrosis, pain, edema, and erythema.

Monitor patients for extravasation and advise patients to seek medical care for progression of symptoms.

Pregnancy considerations

Pregnancy (US label) US FDA label

8.1 Pregnancy Risk Summary Based on human data from published literature, AXYCIS can cause fetal harm when administered to pregnant women. Data demonstrates transplacental transfer of cisplatin. Exposure of pregnant women to cisplatin-containing chemotherapy has been associated with oligohydramnios, intrauterine growth restriction, and preterm birth.

Cases of neonatal acute respiratory distress syndrome, cytopenias, and hearing loss have been reported. Cisplatin administration to animals during and after organogenesis resulted in teratogenicity. A published study in mice showed placental transfer of cisplatin increased with placenta maturation. Advise pregnant women and females of reproductive potential of the potential risk to a fetus.

The background risk in the U.S. general population of major birth defects is 2-4% and of miscarriage is 15-20% of clinically recognized pregnancies.

Mechanism of action

12.1 Mechanism of Action Cisplatin is a platinum-based drug that binds to DNA and forms DNA cross-links. These crosslinks inhibit DNA replication and transcription and trigger cytotoxic processes that lead to cell death.

Pharmacokinetics

Half-life and elimination (verbatim, US label)

Elimination Cisplatin estimated elimination half-life is about 20 to 30 minutes.

Platinum terminal half-life in red blood cells is 36 to 47 days.

Cisplatin mean apparent clearance is 15 to 16 L/hr/m 2 and its mean renal clearance is 50 to 62 mL/min/m 2 .

Excretion After a single dose of 40 to 140 mg/m 2 , about 10% to 40% of the administered platinum is excreted in the urine in 24 hours and about 35% to 51% is excreted within 5 days.

Cisplatin accounts for 13% to 17% of the dose excreted within 1 hour after administration of 50 mg/m 2 .

Pharmacokinetics (US label) US FDA label

12.3 Pharmacokinetics Distribution Cisplatin steady-state apparent volume of distribution is 11 to 12 L/m 2. Platinum plasma protein binding is about 90%. Platinum binds to several plasma proteins, including albumin, transferrin, and gamma globulin. Platinum is present in tissues for as long as 180 days after the last administration.

Elimination Cisplatin estimated elimination half-life is about 20 to 30 minutes. Platinum terminal half-life in red blood cells is 36 to 47 days. Cisplatin mean apparent clearance is 15 to 16 L/hr/m 2 and its mean renal clearance is 50 to 62 mL/min/m 2. Metabolism The ratios of cisplatin to plasma ultrafiltrate range from 0.5 to 1.1 after a dose of 100 mg/m 2.

Excretion After a single dose of 40 to 140 mg/m 2, about 10% to 40% of the administered platinum is excreted in the urine in 24 hours and about 35% to 51% is excreted within 5 days. Cisplatin accounts for 13% to 17% of the dose excreted within 1 hour after administration of 50 mg/m 2.
