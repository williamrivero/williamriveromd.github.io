Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to gliclazide, other sulfonylureas or sulfonamides
  • Type 1 diabetes; diabetic precoma or coma; diabetic ketoacidosis
  • Severe renal or hepatic insufficiency (use insulin)
  • Concomitant miconazole
  • Breastfeeding
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Dosing: Adult

  • Type 2 diabetes, modified-release (MR): 30 mg once daily at breakfast to start; increase stepwise (60, 90, 120 mg) at intervals of about 1 month (or after 2 weeks if glucose has not fallen); maximum 120 mg/day
  • Type 2 diabetes, immediate-release 80 mg tablets: Start 40–80 mg/day before breakfast; usual maintenance 80–160 mg/day in 2 doses (before breakfast and dinner); maximum 320 mg/day; caution above 160 mg/day, particularly with kidney impairment (IR SmPC)
  • Switching: One 80 mg immediate-release tablet is comparable to 30 mg MR; monitor glucose when switching

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Gliclazide is cleared by the liver to inactive metabolites (under 1% leaves unchanged in urine), but hypoglycemia is more likely and can be prolonged in kidney impairment. Irish SmPCs keep the usual regimen with careful monitoring in mild to moderate impairment and contraindicate it in severe renal insufficiency, where insulin is recommended. It has no US FDA label.

Kidney function measure: The SmPCs grade renal impairment as mild, moderate or severe without numeric thresholds; our bands map 'severe' to creatinine clearance (CrCl) below 30 mL/min.

CrCl ≥60 mL/min
Usual dosing. Modified-release (MR): 30 to 120 mg once daily at breakfast. Immediate-release 80 mg tablets: 40 to 80 mg/day to start, maximum 320 mg/day; doses above 160 mg/day are split before breakfast and dinner (SmPC).
CrCl 30–59 mL/min
Mild to moderate impairment: same regimen as normal kidney function with careful glucose monitoring (MR SmPC). Immediate-release: use caution above 160 mg/day and consider limiting the maximum dose as serum creatinine rises (IR SmPC). Start at the lowest dose (MR 30 mg or IR 40–80 mg) and titrate slowly; reduce if hypoglycemia occurs (KDIGO 2022, Figure 6).
CrCl 15–29 mL/min
Severe renal insufficiency: contraindicated; insulin is recommended (SmPC). Our mapping of 'severe' to CrCl <30 is a judgment. Off-label use below 30 is a specialist decision: lowest dose, close glucose monitoring.
CrCl <15 mL/min (not on dialysis)
Contraindicated (severe renal insufficiency; SmPC).
Hemodialysis (intermittent)
Contraindicated (severe renal insufficiency; SmPC). Dialysis does not remove gliclazide because it is highly protein-bound (SmPC 4.9).
Peritoneal dialysis
Contraindicated (severe renal insufficiency; SmPC).
CRRT
Not appropriate; contraindicated in severe renal insufficiency (SmPC). Use insulin.
  • Dialyzable? No: dialysis is of no benefit in overdose because gliclazide is about 95% protein-bound (SmPC 4.9).
  • Monitoring Glucose (fasting plasma glucose, glycated hemoglobin [HbA1c], self-monitoring). Hypoglycemia can be prolonged in severe kidney impairment and needs longer observation (SmPC 4.4). Reassess kidney function periodically. G6PD deficiency: sulfonylureas can cause hemolytic anemia; use with caution and consider a non-sulfonylurea alternative (SmPC 4.4).

Adverse reactions

Common

  • Hypoglycemia (especially with irregular or skipped meals)
  • Gastrointestinal upset: abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, diarrhea, constipation (uncommon)

Serious

  • Severe or prolonged hypoglycemia needing hospital care
  • Blood disorders (anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, granulocytopenia; agranulocytosis and hemolytic anemia as class effects)
  • Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS)
  • Hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice
