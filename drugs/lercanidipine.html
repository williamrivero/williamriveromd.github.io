Dosing: Adult

  • Mild to moderate essential hypertension: 10 mg once daily at least 15 minutes before a meal; titrate gradually to 20 mg if needed (full effect may take about 2 weeks); maximum 20 mg/day

CKD · dialysis · CRRT

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Lercanidipine is a dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker cleared by CYP3A4 metabolism; levels are about 70% higher in severe kidney impairment and dialysis. Unlike amlodipine, the European label contraindicates it at glomerular filtration rate (GFR) below 30 mL/min, including dialysis, and asks for caution when increasing to 20 mg in mild to moderate impairment. In peritoneal dialysis it can cloud the effluent. It has no US FDA label.

Kidney function measure: European summary of product characteristics (SmPC) uses glomerular filtration rate (GFR, mL/min) for the contraindication; bands shown on creatinine clearance (CrCl).

Adverse reactions

Common

  • Peripheral edema
  • Headache, flushing
  • Tachycardia, palpitations

Serious

  • Angina or myocardial infarction (rare, isolated)
  • Hypotension, syncope
  • Angioedema
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Contraindications

  • Severe kidney impairment (GFR <30 mL/min), including dialysis
  • Severe hepatic impairment
  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, cyclosporine, grapefruit
  • Left ventricular outflow obstruction; untreated heart failure; unstable angina or myocardial infarction within 1 month
  • Pregnancy, breastfeeding, women of childbearing potential without contraception
  • Hypersensitivity
