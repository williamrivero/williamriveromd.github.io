Dosing: Adult

  • Renal bone disease, hyperparathyroidism, hypoparathyroidism, rickets and osteomalacia (adults): Start 1 microgram once daily (0.5 microgram in older adults); maintenance usually 0.25 to 2 micrograms/day, titrated to calcium, phosphate and PTH
  • Hemodialysis, intermittent: 0.5 to 2 micrograms after each dialysis session, adjusted in 0.25 to 0.5 microgram steps
renalcarematters.com, reviewed
CKD · dialysis · CRRT

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Alfacalcidol (1-alpha-hydroxyvitamin D3) is converted in the liver to calcitriol, bypassing the kidney's 1-alpha-hydroxylation step, so it is a kidney-indicated drug (uremic bone disease, secondary hyperparathyroidism). The Irish label titrates the dose to calcium, phosphate and parathyroid hormone (PTH) and gives an after-dialysis intermittent regimen. Hypercalcemia is the main risk. It has no US FDA label.

Kidney function measure: Not renally cleared; dose titrated to calcium, phosphate and PTH. Bands shown on creatinine clearance (CrCl).

CrCl ≥60 mL/min
No kidney dose adjustment: alfacalcidol is designed for impaired renal 1-alpha-hydroxylation. Start 1 microgram/day (0.5 microgram in older adults); usual maintenance 0.25 to 2 micrograms/day, titrated to calcium, phosphate, PTH and alkaline phosphatase (SmPC). Add a phosphate binder in renal bone disease.
CrCl 30–59 mL/min
KDIGO 2017: not for routine use in CKD G3a–G5 not on dialysis; reserve for severe, progressive secondary hyperparathyroidism (mainly G4–G5). If used: SmPC start 1 microgram/day (0.5 microgram in older adults), maintenance 0.25 to 2 micrograms/day, titrated to calcium, phosphate and PTH; stop for hypercalcemia.

Hemodialysis (intermittent)
Daily dosing as above, or intermittent dosing after each dialysis (2 to 3 times weekly): start 0.5 to 2 micrograms, adjust by 0.25 to 0.5 microgram steps by PTH, calcium and phosphate. Converting daily to intermittent: daily dose × 7/3 (three times weekly) or × 7/2 (twice weekly), rounded to 0.25 microgram (SmPC).
Peritoneal dialysis
Daily dosing titrated to calcium, phosphate and PTH (SmPC).
CRRT
No CRRT-specific guidance; usually held in acute illness. Specialist input.
  • Dialyzable? Not stated. In overdose, hemodialysis with low-calcium dialysate can treat hypercalcemia (SmPC 4.9).
  • Monitoring Serum calcium and phosphate regularly; PTH, alkaline phosphatase and calcium × phosphate product as indicated. If hypercalcemia occurs, stop until calcium normalizes (about 1 week), then restart at half the dose. Hypercalcemia can worsen kidney function and cause nephrocalcinosis (SmPC 4.4). In CKD, check calcium and phosphate often during titration and after each dose change, and PTH at the intervals in the KDIGO CKD-MBD guideline; prefer non-calcium phosphate binders if calcium is high-normal.

Dosing: Pediatric

Approved ages
Children and neonates (SmPC).
Usual dose
Over 20 kg: 1 microgram/day. Under 20 kg: 0.05 microgram/kg/day. Neonates and premature infants: 0.05 to 0.1 microgram/kg/day (up to 2 micrograms/kg/day in severe neonatal hypocalcemia) (SmPC).

Kidney dosing in children: see Dosing: Altered kidney function.

Adverse reactions

Common

  • Hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria
  • Hyperphosphatemia
  • Abdominal discomfort
  • Rash, pruritus

Serious

  • Severe hypercalcemia
  • Acute kidney injury, nephrolithiasis, nephrocalcinosis
  • Vascular and soft-tissue calcification
