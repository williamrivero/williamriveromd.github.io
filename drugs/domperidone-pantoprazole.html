    Show full dosing details
    CrCl ≥60 mL/min
    Usual dose: 10 mg up to three times daily before meals; maximum 30 mg/day, usually for no more than 1 week (SmPC).
    CrCl 30–59 mL/min
    No dose change specified (SmPC adjusts only in severe impairment). Keep to 30 mg/day maximum and the shortest course.
    CrCl 15–29 mL/min
    Severe impairment (CrCl <30): reduce dosing to once or twice daily depending on severity; the dose may also need reducing (SmPC).
    CrCl <15 mL/min (not on dialysis)
    Severe impairment: dose once or twice daily; consider a lower dose (SmPC). Check potassium, magnesium and QTc first.
    Hemodialysis (intermittent)
    Dose as for severe impairment: once or twice daily, possibly a lower dose (SmPC). Dialysis removal not stated. Electrolyte shifts around dialysis raise QT risk; check QTc.
    Peritoneal dialysis
    Dose as for severe impairment: once or twice daily, possibly a lower dose (SmPC). No PD data.
    CRRT
    No data. Critically ill patients often have QT risk factors; avoid unless no alternative.
    • Dialyzable? Not stated in the SmPC (91% to 93% protein-bound; removal unlikely to be meaningful).
    • Monitoring QTc and electrolytes (potassium, magnesium) before and during treatment in patients with kidney failure, heart disease or interacting drugs; stop if palpitations, syncope or arrhythmia symptoms. Contraindicated with QT-prolonging drugs and potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (SmPC 4.3, 4.4).

    From the Domperidone monograph (renalcarematters.com, reviewed).

  • Pantoprazole

    Use: gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) and a history of erosive esophagitis (EE) for up to 10 days in adults and up to 7 days in pediatric patients 3 months and older. US FDA label

Note: Fixed-dose combinations may not allow the dose changes advised for each component; check the strength and use the single agents if a component needs adjusting.

Kidney injury risk

Pantoprazole

Acute interstitial nephritis
  • Can occur at any time during therapy. If kidney function falls, stop the drug and evaluate.
Acute Tubulointerstitial Nephritis Acute tubulointerstitial nephritis (TIN) has been observed in patients taking PPIs and may occur at any point during PPI therapy.US label: Protonix I.V. (Wyeth Pharmaceuticals LLC, a subsidiary of Pfizer Inc.), Warnings and precautions

Kidney injury tags come from US label warnings and are reviewed by renalcarematters.com. A drug without this tag is not necessarily safe for the kidneys. Avoiding kidney toxins also protects residual kidney function in patients on dialysis. All drugs with a kidney injury warning.
