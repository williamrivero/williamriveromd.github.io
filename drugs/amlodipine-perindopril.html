Contraindications

  • History of ACE-inhibitor angioedema; hereditary or idiopathic angioedema
  • Second and third trimesters of pregnancy
  • Significant bilateral renal artery stenosis or stenosis to a single functioning kidney
  • Aliskiren with diabetes or GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m2
  • Sacubitril/valsartan within 36 hours
  • Extracorporeal treatments using negatively charged surfaces (some high-flux membranes, LDL apheresis)
  • Severe hypotension, shock, left ventricular outflow obstruction
  • Hemodynamically unstable heart failure after acute myocardial infarction
  • Hypersensitivity to perindopril, other ACE inhibitors or dihydropyridines

Dosing: Altered kidney function

The fixed combination is a substitution product for patients already controlled on the same doses given separately. Perindoprilat, the active ACE-inhibitor metabolite, is cleared by the kidneys and accumulates in kidney impairment, whereas amlodipine needs no kidney adjustment. The European label allows the combination only at creatinine clearance (CrCl) 60 mL/min or above; below that, the components are titrated separately. It has no US FDA label.

Kidney function measure: European summary of product characteristics (SmPC) uses creatinine clearance (CrCl, mL/min).

Hemodialysis (intermittent)
Not suitable (CrCl <60). Perindoprilat is dialyzable (dialysis clearance 70 mL/min); amlodipine is not. Use the separate drugs. Avoid high-flux polyacrylonitrile (AN69) membranes with ACE inhibitors (anaphylactoid reactions) (SmPC 4.3, 4.4).
Peritoneal dialysis
Not suitable (CrCl <60); use the separate drugs.
CRRT
Not suitable; use separate drugs if needed. Extracorporeal circuits with negatively charged surfaces are contraindicated with ACE inhibitors (SmPC 4.3).
  • Dialyzable? Perindoprilat: yes (dialysis clearance 70 mL/min). Amlodipine: no (about 97.5% protein-bound) (SmPC).
  • Monitoring Potassium and creatinine routinely in CKD; blood pressure, kidney function and potassium closely in patients at risk of hypotension or with renal artery stenosis. Hyperkalemia risk higher with CKD, age over 70, diabetes, potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements or salt substitutes (SmPC 4.4).

Adverse reactions

Common

  • Edema (amlodipine)
  • Cough (perindopril)
  • Dizziness, headache, hypotension
  • Palpitations, flushing
  • Gastrointestinal upset

Serious

  • Angioedema
  • Hyperkalemia
  • Acute kidney injury (especially with renal artery stenosis, volume depletion, NSAIDs)
  • Agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia (very rare)
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

  • Potassium and creatinine routinely in CKD; blood pressure, kidney function and potassium closely in patients at risk of hypotension or with renal artery stenosis.
