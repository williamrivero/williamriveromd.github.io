Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to quinolones
  • History of fluoroquinolone tendinopathy
  • Pre-pubertal children
  • Pregnancy
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Dosing: Adult

  • Urinary tract infection: 400 mg twice daily for 7 to 10 days
  • Chronic bacterial prostatitis: 400 mg twice daily for 3 to 7 weeks
  • Acute bacterial gastroenteritis: 400 mg twice daily for 5 days
  • Uncomplicated gonorrhea: 800 mg single dose
renalcarematters.com, reviewed
CKD · dialysis · CRRT

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Norfloxacin is a fluoroquinolone excreted largely unchanged in urine (33% to 48% in 24 hours); its half-life rises from 3–4 hours to about 8 hours at creatinine clearance (CrCl) below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2. The New Zealand data sheet halves the daily dose to 400 mg once daily at CrCl below 30 (not on hemodialysis) and has no data for gonorrhea in that group. Fluoroquinolone class warnings apply. It has no current US FDA label.

Kidney function measure: New Zealand data sheet uses creatinine clearance indexed to body surface area (CrCl, mL/min/1.73 m2); threshold 30.

Adverse reactions

Serious

  • Tendinitis and tendon rupture
  • QT prolongation, torsades de pointes
  • Seizures, peripheral neuropathy, psychiatric reactions
  • Aortic aneurysm and dissection
  • Hypoglycemic coma
  • Myasthenia gravis exacerbation
  • Clostridioides difficile colitis; DRESS; anaphylaxis
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Monitoring parameters

  • Tendinopathy (higher risk with kidney impairment, transplant, corticosteroids, age); QT risk factors; glucose with sulfonylureas or insulin (dysglycemia, hypoglycemic coma); seizures; hydration (crystalluria).
