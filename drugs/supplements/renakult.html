Registered with the Philippine FDA as a FOOD SUPPLEMENT — no approved therapeutic claims. Not a substitute for prescribed treatment.
CKD · dialysis · transplant

Kidney notes

Renakult is a probiotic with the same three organisms as Renadyl (Lactobacillus acidophilus, Bifidobacterium longum, Streptococcus thermophilus); one of its two Philippine registrations also lists Bacillus coagulans. Strain designations and colony counts are not given in the FDA record.

Evidence

  • No clinical trials of Renakult itself were identified on PubMed.
  • Its species overlap with Renadyl, whose small trials showed lower BUN in CKD stages 3 to 4 but no significant change in uremic toxins or quality of life on hemodialysis (Ranganathan 2009; Natarajan 2014). Probiotic effects are strain- and dose-specific, so these results cannot be transferred to Renakult.
  • Class evidence: probiotics alone did not lower indoxyl sulfate or p-cresyl sulfate in a meta-analysis of RCTs (Chen 2022), and biotic supplements did not lower serum creatinine in non-dialysis CKD (Liu 2023).

Cautions in kidney disease

  • Probiotic organisms can enter the bloodstream: whole-genome sequencing has linked Lactobacillus bacteremia in ICU patients to the probiotic capsule they were given. Avoid in critically ill patients, those with a central venous catheter (including a tunneled hemodialysis catheter), recent bowel surgery or gut barrier injury, and in kidney transplant recipients or others on immunosuppression unless the treating team agrees. [Yelin 2019]
  • Trial outcomes were blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine, uremic-toxin levels and quality-of-life scores; no trial reports kidney failure, dialysis start, cardiovascular events or death.
  • Registered as a food supplement with no approved therapeutic claims: viable count, strain identity and storage stability are not verified by the FDA the way drug content is.
  • It does not replace evidence-based CKD care (blood pressure and glucose control, RAAS blockade, SGLT2 inhibitors where indicated, diet).

Bottom line

No product-specific evidence; class evidence is limited to surrogate markers. Same safety limits as any live probiotic, especially in transplant recipients and patients with a dialysis catheter.
