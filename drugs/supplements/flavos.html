Kidney notes

The Flavos food supplement is registered as Phyllanthus niruri (stonebreaker), rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis) and basil (Ocimum basilicum). It is marketed for kidney stones and urinary health. It is a different registration from any Flavos-branded drug product.

Evidence

  • No clinical trials of Flavos itself were identified on PubMed.
  • Phyllanthus niruri: in an RCT of 69 calcium stone formers given 450 mg three times daily or placebo for 3 months, urinary parameters did not change overall; urinary calcium fell only in the hypercalciuric subgroup, and stone passage and pain did not differ (Nishiura 2004).
  • A meta-analysis found two controlled studies, with modest reductions in stone size and number (Dhawan 2020). A 2025 systematic review describes it as safe in the available studies and possibly helpful after shock-wave lithotripsy, with mixed results overall (Iregui-Parra 2025).
  • No clinical trials of rosemary or basil for kidney outcomes were identified. No studies in CKD stages 4 to 5, dialysis or transplant were identified.

Cautions in kidney disease

  • Herbal content is not standardized or verified for a food supplement; extract strength and contaminants are unknown.
  • Not studied in advanced CKD or dialysis; potassium and other mineral content is not declared.
  • Should not delay evaluation of an obstructing stone, infection or a 24-hour urine work-up and proven prevention (fluids, dietary changes, thiazide or citrate where indicated).

Bottom line

Weak, small-trial evidence for stone prevention from one of its three herbs and none for the product. If a stone former insists, it is unlikely to harm a patient with normal kidney function, but it is not a substitute for a metabolic stone work-up.
