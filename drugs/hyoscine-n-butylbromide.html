Dosing: Adult

  • Gastrointestinal and genitourinary spasm (tablets): 10 to 20 mg per dose; for prolonged illness 10 mg three to five times daily; maximum 60 mg/day
  • Acute spasm (injection): 10 to 20 mg by slow IV (1 mL/min), IM or SC injection; maximum 100 mg/day

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Hyoscine butylbromide is a quaternary antispasmodic. Oral absolute bioavailability is under 1%, so tablets reach the circulation very little; after intravenous use about half of the dose is excreted unchanged in urine. The Canadian prescribing information does not address kidney impairment or dialysis. It has no US FDA label.

Adverse reactions

Common

  • Dry mouth
  • Tachycardia (injection)
  • Blurred vision, mydriasis
  • Urinary retention
  • Reduced sweating

Serious

  • Anaphylaxis and anaphylactic shock, including fatal cases (mainly after injection)
  • Hypotension and tachycardia after injection in heart disease
  • Acute angle-closure glaucoma
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Contraindications

  • Myasthenia gravis; megacolon; mechanical gastrointestinal stenosis; paralytic or obstructive ileus
  • Glaucoma (tablets); untreated narrow-angle glaucoma (injection)
  • Prostatic hypertrophy with urinary retention
  • Injection: tachycardia, angina, heart failure
  • Intramuscular injection in anticoagulated patients
  • Hypersensitivity
renalcarematters.com, reviewed
