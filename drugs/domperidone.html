Contraindications

  • QTc or other conduction prolongation, significant electrolyte disturbance, or heart disease such as heart failure
  • Co-administration with QT-prolonging drugs or potent CYP3A4 inhibitors
  • Moderate or severe hepatic impairment
  • Prolactinoma; pheochromocytoma
  • Gastrointestinal bleeding, obstruction or perforation
  • Hypersensitivity
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Dosing: Adult

  • Nausea and vomiting (adults and adolescents 12 years and older, 35 kg or more): 10 mg up to three times daily, before meals; maximum 30 mg/day; usually no longer than 1 week

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Domperidone is cleared by the liver (CYP3A4), but its half-life almost triples in severe kidney impairment (7.4 to 20.8 hours at creatinine clearance [CrCl] below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2; single-dose plasma levels were lower than in healthy volunteers, so the reduction is for accumulation on repeated dosing). The European label reduces dosing to once or twice daily in severe impairment and may lower the dose. Its main risk is QT prolongation and sudden cardiac death, which electrolyte disturbances common in kidney failure make more likely. It has no US FDA label.

Kidney function measure: SmPC section 5.2 defines severe renal impairment as creatinine clearance (CrCl) below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2.

CRRT
No data. Critically ill patients often have QT risk factors; avoid unless no alternative.
  • Dialyzable? Not stated in the SmPC (91% to 93% protein-bound; removal unlikely to be meaningful).
  • Monitoring QTc and electrolytes (potassium, magnesium) before and during treatment in patients with kidney failure, heart disease or interacting drugs; stop if palpitations, syncope or arrhythmia symptoms. Contraindicated with QT-prolonging drugs and potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (SmPC 4.3, 4.4).
