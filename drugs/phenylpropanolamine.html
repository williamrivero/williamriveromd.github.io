Use

  • Historical decongestant and appetite-suppressant use is described for context. FDA removed PPA from US OTC use after hemorrhagic-stroke safety concerns; this is not a recommendation to use it 2.

Clinical evidence and limitations

Study regimens are descriptions of research, not prescribing recommendations.

StudyDesign and populationTreatment and durationMain findingsLimitations
Kernan 2000 3Case-control study: 702 hemorrhagic-stroke cases and 1,376 controls, ages 18–49.Recent PPA-containing product exposure.Among women, appetite-suppressant exposure was associated with hemorrhagic stroke (adjusted odds ratio 16.58; 95% CI 1.51–182.21). First-use association estimate was 3.13 (0.86–11.46).Observational association, sparse exposures and wide confidence intervals; the first-use estimate was not statistically significant. Absence of a demonstrated subgroup association does not establish safety.
Lönnerholm 1984 4Pharmacokinetic study in eight healthy volunteers.Sustained-release PPA at steady state.Mean unchanged urinary recovery was 86%; investigators advised caution with impaired renal function.Healthy volunteers; not a renal dose-finding or dialysis study.

Drug interactions

Drug or classSeverityEffectWhat to do
Urinary alkalinization / sodium bicarbonateAdjust or monitorA small healthy-volunteer study found lower renal PPA clearance, higher postabsorptive exposure and higher diastolic blood pressure with alkalinization 5.Recognize this exposure interaction during medication review; the study does not provide a safe compensating dose 5.

Severity scale (renalcarematters.com): Avoid: do not combine; Adjust or monitor: change the dose or monitor closely; Minor: usually no action.

Adverse reactions

Serious

  • Hemorrhagic stroke is the central regulatory safety concern; FDA history records removal from OTC use following the safety analysis 2,3.
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Mechanism of action

Historically used for oral nasal decongestion and appetite suppression; renal elimination is important to systemic exposure 3,4.

Class: Sympathomimetic decongestant
