    CrCl ≥60 mL/min
    Normal kidney function: 3 mg/kg/day in 3 doses every 8 hours; up to 5 mg/kg/day for life-threatening infection, reduced as soon as possible. Obese patients: dose by lean body mass. Measure peaks (4 to 6 mcg/mL) and troughs (label). Alternative (not in label): extended-interval dosing, e.g., 7 mg/kg with interval by CrCl (≥60: every 24 h; 40–59: every 36 h; 20–39: every 48 h) and a single-level nomogram (Nicolau 1995). Not validated below CrCl 20 or on dialysis: use the label method with levels.
    CrCl 30–59 mL/min
    After the usual first dose, either extend the interval (hours = serum creatinine in mg/dL × 8) or keep 8-hourly doses reduced by the label's serum creatinine table (Table 4) or by dividing the normal dose by serum creatinine. Avoid prolonged peaks above 12 mcg/mL and troughs above 2 mcg/mL; measure levels (label). Table 4 examples: CrCl about 40–45 (SCr 2–2.2): 50%; 30–35 (SCr 2.6–3): 35% of the usual dose every 8 hours. Alternative (not in label): extended-interval dosing, e.g., 7 mg/kg with interval by CrCl (≥60: every 24 h; 40–59: every 36 h; 20–39: every 48 h) and a single-level nomogram (Nicolau 1995). Not validated below CrCl 20 or on dialysis: use the label method with levels. In AKI, creatinine lags; the label warns that deteriorating kidney function needs a greater reduction than these tables.
    CrCl 15–29 mL/min
    After the usual first dose, either extend the interval (hours = serum creatinine in mg/dL × 8) or keep 8-hourly doses reduced by the label's serum creatinine table (Table 4) or by dividing the normal dose by serum creatinine. Avoid prolonged peaks above 12 mcg/mL and troughs above 2 mcg/mL; measure levels (label). Table 4 example: CrCl about 20–25: 25%; 15–20: 20% of the usual dose every 8 hours. Alternative (not in label): extended-interval dosing, e.g., 7 mg/kg with interval by CrCl (≥60: every 24 h; 40–59: every 36 h; 20–39: every 48 h) and a single-level nomogram (Nicolau 1995). Not validated below CrCl 20 or on dialysis: use the label method with levels. In AKI, creatinine lags; the label warns that deteriorating kidney function needs a greater reduction than these tables.
    CrCl <15 mL/min (not on dialysis)
    After the usual first dose, either extend the interval (hours = serum creatinine in mg/dL × 8) or keep 8-hourly doses reduced by the label's serum creatinine table (Table 4) or by dividing the normal dose by serum creatinine. Avoid prolonged peaks above 12 mcg/mL and troughs above 2 mcg/mL; measure levels (label). Table 4: CrCl about 10–15: 15%; under 10: 10% of the usual dose every 8 hours. Specialist input. In AKI, creatinine lags; the label warns that deteriorating kidney function needs a greater reduction than these tables.
    Hemodialysis (intermittent)
    1 to 1.7 mg/kg at the end of each dialysis, depending on severity of infection; an 8-hour hemodialysis lowers levels by about 50% (label). Check levels.
    Peritoneal dialysis
    No PD dose in label; removal by peritoneal dialysis is much slower than by hemodialysis. For PD-associated peritonitis (ISPD 2022): 0.6 mg/kg intraperitoneally once daily, intermittent (in one exchange daily with a dwell of at least 6 hours); continuous dosing is not advised. Check levels; specialist input.
    CRRT
    No data in label, and gentamicin is not in the Li 2020 CRRT table. Level-guided dosing with specialist input.
    • Dialyzable? Yes by hemodialysis (about 50% over 8 hours; label); much less by peritoneal dialysis. Protein binding 0% to 30%.
    • Monitoring Peak and trough levels; serum creatinine before and during therapy; urinalysis; hearing and vestibular function; magnesium, calcium and potassium (wasting, Fanconi-like syndrome reported). Avoid other nephrotoxic drugs and potent diuretics.

    From the Gentamicin monograph (renalcarematters.com, reviewed).
