Kidney notes: Aminoleban

Aminoleban 8% (Otsuka) is an amino acid infusion rich in branched-chain amino acids, indicated for improvement of encephalopathy in chronic liver disease. Philippine registrations: 500 ml LDPE bottle, and 200 ml and 500 ml polypropylene soft bags, made by Otsuka Pharmaceutical Vietnam. It is a hepatic formula, not a kidney formula.

Composition

Total free amino acids (g)15.9839.93
Nitrogen (g)2.446.11
Sodium, approx. (mEq)37
Chloride, approx. (mEq)1947
  • Branched-chain amino acids 35.5% (w/w); Fischer ratio 37.05; osmotic pressure ratio about 3 relative to physiological saline; pH 5.5 to 6.5. Contains sodium bisulfite as an additive.
  • Source is the Japanese package insert (Otsuka Pharmaceutical Factory, revised May 2026), translated; equivalence with the Vietnam-made Philippine product is not confirmed.

Composition from the cited manufacturer SmPC/package insert; values independently re-checked November 17, 2026.

Dose and rate limits

  • Usual adult dose 500 to 1000 ml by intravenous drip; standard rate 180 to 300 minutes per 500 ml (Japanese package insert).

Kidney notes

  • Contraindicated in severe renal impairment, except in patients on dialysis or hemofiltration: urea and other amino acid metabolites may accumulate and worsen symptoms (package insert, sections 2 and 9.2.1).
  • Even on dialysis or hemofiltration, urea and other metabolites may accumulate; removal depends on the dialysis method and the patient's condition. Check blood chemistry, acid-base balance and fluid balance before starting and while continuing (sections 8 and 9.2.2).

How it fits the ESPEN 2021 kidney nutrition guideline

  • ESPEN 2021 Recommendation 24: "No disease-specific enteral nor parenteral formula oriented for patients with reduced kidney function should be routinely utilized in every patient with AKI, AKI on CKD, or CKD with KF in comparison to conventional formulas. Instead, their use is to be individualized (see recommendation 26)." Grade of recommendation B – Consensus (87.5% agreement). (AKI, acute kidney injury; CKD, chronic kidney disease; KF, kidney failure.)
  • ESPEN 2021 Recommendation 31: "Electrolytes abnormalities are common in patients with AKI, AKI on CKD, or CKD with KF receiving KRT and shall be closely monitored." Grade of recommendation A – Strong consensus (100% agreement). (KRT, kidney replacement therapy.)

Bottom line

A hepatic amino acid infusion for encephalopathy in chronic liver disease. In severe renal impairment it is contraindicated unless the patient is on dialysis or hemofiltration, and even then urea and metabolites can accumulate: monitor blood urea, acid-base and fluid balance.

Kidney notes: Aminoven

Aminoven 10% (Fresenius Kabi Austria GmbH) is a standard amino acid solution (100 g amino acids per liter) for parenteral nutrition. Philippine registrations: 500 ml and 1 L bottles. The SmPC lists no electrolytes.

Composition

Per presentationper 1000 ml
Amino acids (g)100
Nitrogen (g)16.2
Energy (kcal)400
  • Theoretical osmolarity 990 mosm/l; titratable acidity 22 mmol NaOH/l; pH 5.5 to 6.3. No sodium, potassium, chloride or phosphate is listed; lysine is present as the acetate salt (SmPC sections 2, 3, 6.1).
  • Source is the Irish SmPC of the 100 g/l product sold in Ireland as Aminoven 16 (its sections 5.3 and 6.3 refer to Aminoven 10%). Confirm against the Philippine package insert.

Composition from the cited manufacturer SmPC/package insert; values independently re-checked November 17, 2026.

Dose and rate limits

  • Maximum daily dose 20 ml/kg body weight (2.0 g amino acids/kg); maximum infusion rate 1.0 ml/kg/hour (0.1 g amino acids/kg/hour) (SmPC 4.2).

Kidney notes

  • Contraindicated in renal insufficiency without hemodialysis or hemofiltration treatment, and in fluid overload (SmPC 4.3).
  • Monitor serum electrolytes, fluid balance and renal function (SmPC 4.4). With hypokalemia or hyponatremia, potassium and/or sodium should be supplied at the same time (SmPC 4.4).

How it fits the ESPEN 2021 kidney nutrition guideline

  • ESPEN 2021 Recommendation 18 (Grade of recommendation 0 – Consensus (82.6% agreement)): protein intakes that may be prescribed are 0.6–0.8 g/kg body weight (BW)/d for a hospitalized patient with CKD without acute/critical illness; 1.2 g/kg BW/d with CKD and KF on conventional intermittent chronic KRT without acute/critical illness; 0.8–1.0 g/kg BW/d with AKI or AKI on CKD without acute/critical illness; start with 1 g/kg BW/day and gradually increase up to 1.3 g/kg BW/d if tolerated with acute/critical illness not on KRT; 1.3–1.5 g/kg/d for critically ill patients on conventional intermittent KRT; and 1.5 g/kg/d up to 1.7 g/kg/d for critically ill patients on continuous KRT (CKRT) or prolonged intermittent KRT (PIKRT). "If available, the pre-hospitalization body weight or usual body weight may be preferred over the ideal BW. Actual BW should not be considered for a protein prescription." (AKI, acute kidney injury.)
  • ESPEN 2021 Recommendation 25: "The choice of the most appropriate EN or PN formula should be made based on the calorie and protein ratio to provide the most accurate dosing in clinical practice." Grade of recommendation B – Strong consensus (91.3% agreement). (PN, parenteral nutrition.)
  • ESPEN 2021 Recommendation 24: "No disease-specific enteral nor parenteral formula oriented for patients with reduced kidney function should be routinely utilized in every patient with AKI, AKI on CKD, or CKD with KF in comparison to conventional formulas. Instead, their use is to be individualized (see recommendation 26)." Grade of recommendation B – Consensus (87.5% agreement).
  • ESPEN 2021 Recommendation 31: "Electrolytes abnormalities are common in patients with AKI, AKI on CKD, or CKD with KF receiving KRT and shall be closely monitored." Grade of recommendation A – Strong consensus (100% agreement).
  • ESPEN 2021 Recommendation 4: "Intradialytic parenteral nutrition (IDPN) shall be applied in malnourished non-critically ill hospitalized patients with CKD and KF on hemodialysis, or the same patients if at risk of malnutrition that fail to respond or do not tolerate ONS or EN." Grade of recommendation A – Strong consensus (91.7% agreement). (CKD, chronic kidney disease; KF, kidney failure; ONS, oral nutritional supplements; EN, enteral nutrition.)

Bottom line

A standard electrolyte-free amino acid solution: in renal insufficiency it is contraindicated unless the patient is on hemodialysis or hemofiltration. Dose protein to the ESPEN targets for the patient's setting and dialysis modality, and add electrolytes only as serum levels require.

Dose and rate limits

  • Acute and chronic renal insufficiency without dialysis: up to 0.5 g amino acids/kg body weight per day (500 ml per day at 70 kg).

Kidney notes
