Contraindications

  • Jaundice
  • Hypersensitivity to rifamycins or isoniazid
  • Saquinavir/ritonavir, lurasidone, cabotegravir, fostemsavir, lenacapavir
  • Manic or hypomanic psychosis
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Dosing: Adult

  • Tuberculosis, continuation phase (adults): Rifampicin about 10 mg/kg (450 to 600 mg) with isoniazid about 5 mg/kg once daily, on an empty stomach

CKD · dialysis · CRRT

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Rifampicin is mainly cleared by the liver, and isoniazid by acetylation (50% to 70% of a dose appears in urine). The Irish label gives no kidney dose adjustment for the fixed combination but warns that isoniazid can cause a cerebellar syndrome, mainly in chronic kidney disease, and peripheral neuropathy. Hemodialysis removes isoniazid (advised for refractory overdose). It has no US FDA label for the combination.

CrCl 15–29 mL/min
No dose change for isoniazid or rifampicin at CrCl <30 (ATS/CDC/IDSA 2016; MSF); the SmPC asks for careful monitoring in severe renal dysfunction. Isoniazid can cause a cerebellar syndrome, mainly in chronic kidney disease, and peripheral neuropathy (slow acetylators, malnutrition); give pyridoxine and interrupt or reduce if neurotoxicity appears (SmPC 4.4). Pyrazinamide and ethambutol (separate products or 4-drug fixed-dose tablets) need thrice-weekly dosing at CrCl <30, so 4-drug fixed-dose tablets are unsuitable in CKD G4–G5 and on dialysis.

Kidney injury risk

Rifampicin

Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA/HUS)
  • Stop the drug if anemia, low platelets and rising creatinine appear together.
Cases of thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA), including thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura and hemolytic uremia syndrome, some fatal, have been reported with rifampin.US label: rifampin (Lupin Pharmaceuticals, Inc.), Warnings and precautions

Kidney injury tags come from US label warnings and are reviewed by renalcarematters.com. A drug without this tag is not necessarily safe for the kidneys. Avoiding kidney toxins also protects residual kidney function in patients on dialysis. All drugs with a kidney injury warning.

Adverse reactions

Common

  • Thrombocytopenia (rifampicin)
  • Gastrointestinal upset
  • Orange discoloration of body fluids

Serious

  • Hepatitis (sometimes fatal; age-related with isoniazid)
  • Peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar syndrome
  • Thrombocytopenic purpura, disseminated intravascular coagulation
  • Acute kidney injury (rifampicin)
  • Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, DRESS, acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis
  • Anaphylaxis with intermittent dosing
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Pharmacokinetics

Key PK parameters

Half-lifeRifampicin about 3 hours (2 to 3 hours with repeated dosing); isoniazid levels halve within about 6 hours (acetylator-dependent)
Protein bindingRifampicin about 80%
Metabolism (CYP, transporters)Rifampicin hepatic (potent inducer); isoniazid by acetylation (genetic)
Renal excretion kidneyRifampicin up to 30% in urine (half unchanged); isoniazid 50% to 70% in urine within 24 hours
Dialyzability kidneyIsoniazid yes; rifampicin not stated
