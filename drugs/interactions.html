Meet the Interaction Checker, in Dosis. Add a patient's medicines, and it shows how they affect each other, and the kidneys. Type a brand or a generic name, tap a favorite, or paste the whole medication list at once. Then set the kidney function, from normal down to dialysis. If you used a Calculo calculator first, the eGFR is already filled in. Every pair is graded: contraindicated, avoid, adjust or monitor, or minor. Each one says what happens, and what to do. For example, clarithromycin raises atorvastatin levels. The monograph says not to exceed twenty milligrams of atorvastatin, and to watch for muscle problems. Kidney checks look further. Ibuprofen with losartan and furosemide is flagged as a triple whammy, and each drug's kidney dosing is shown for the level you chose. No result does not mean no interaction, so check the full prescribing information. Copy the link to share the list, print it, or send the medicines to the Med Scheduler. You will find it in Dosis, the Drug Index. Your favorites and recent medicines stay on your own device.

    How interactions are graded

    • Contraindicated The source says the combination must not be used.
    • Avoid Avoid the combination; use only with specialist reason and close monitoring.
    • Adjust or monitor Usually usable with a dose change, spacing, or monitoring.
    • Minor Small or uncommon effect; awareness is enough for most patients.

    Each interaction is quoted from a drug monograph reviewed by renalcarematters.com, built on US FDA labels and PubMed-verified literature. Reviewed interaction lists now cover 229 drugs (1,012 interactions). A pair is found when either drug's list names the other drug or its class, so no result does not mean no interaction; check the full prescribing information.

    Kidney checks use the reviewed kidney dosing and the kidney injury tags on each drug page. Favorites and recent medicines are saved only on this device.
