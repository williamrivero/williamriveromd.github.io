Contraindications

  • Plasma potassium below 5 mmol/L
  • Hypercalcemia-associated conditions
  • Obstructive bowel disease or reduced gut motility; risk of colonic necrosis
  • Concomitant sorbitol
  • Oral use in neonates
  • Hypersensitivity
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Dosing: Adult

  • Hyperkalemia in acute or chronic kidney insufficiency, including dialysis (oral): 15 g three or four times daily (Calcium Resonium); some products use 20 g 1 to 3 times daily; stop when potassium falls to 5 mmol/L
  • Rectal (retention enema): 30 g in 150 mL water or 10% dextrose (Calcium Resonium); some products use 40 g
renalcarematters.com, reviewed
CKD · dialysis · CRRT

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Calcium polystyrene sulfonate is a non-absorbed resin that exchanges calcium for potassium in the gut (about 0.7 mmol potassium per gram). The Irish label indicates it for hyperkalemia in acute and chronic kidney insufficiency, including dialysis, so no kidney dose change applies; dosing follows serum potassium. It is not effective enough alone for potassium above 6.5 mmol/L or ECG changes. It has no US FDA label.

Kidney function measure: Not renally cleared; dose is titrated to serum potassium. Bands shown on creatinine clearance (CrCl).

Dosing: Pediatric

Approved ages
Children; not for oral use in neonates (SmPC).
Usual dose
Children: 0.5 to 1 g/kg/day orally in at least 3 divided doses; rectal dose at least as large when oral is not possible (SmPC).

Neonates: no oral use; no use by any route with reduced gut motility. Fecal impaction after rectal use in children reported.

Kidney dosing in children: see Dosing: Altered kidney function.

Adverse reactions

Common

  • Hypercalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia
  • Nausea, vomiting

Serious

  • Colonic necrosis and perforation
  • Intestinal obstruction, fecal impaction, ileus
  • Aspiration bronchitis or pneumonia
renalcarematters.com, reviewed
