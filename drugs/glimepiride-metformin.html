    CrCl ≥60 mL/min
    Start 1 or 2 mg once daily with breakfast; increase by 1 to 2 mg every 1 to 2 weeks; maximum 8 mg/day (label). Older adults: start 1 mg.
    CrCl 30–59 mL/min
    Start 1 mg once daily and titrate slowly (label 2.1, 8.6): hypoglycemia risk is higher in kidney impairment, and the M1 and M2 metabolites accumulate (M1 AUC 2.3-fold and M2 AUC 8.6-fold higher at CrCl <20). Studied at CrCl 10–60 mL/min with doses of 1 to 8 mg.
    CrCl 15–29 mL/min
    Start 1 mg once daily and titrate slowly (label 2.1, 8.6): hypoglycemia risk is higher in kidney impairment, and the M1 and M2 metabolites accumulate (M1 AUC 2.3-fold and M2 AUC 8.6-fold higher at CrCl <20). Studied at CrCl 10–60 mL/min with doses of 1 to 8 mg.
    CrCl <15 mL/min (not on dialysis)
    Start 1 mg once daily and titrate slowly (label 2.1, 8.6): hypoglycemia risk is higher in kidney impairment, and the M1 and M2 metabolites accumulate (M1 AUC 2.3-fold and M2 AUC 8.6-fold higher at CrCl <20). Studied at CrCl 10–60 mL/min with doses of 1 to 8 mg. No separate advice for CrCl <15; hypoglycemia can be prolonged. Specialist judgment.
    Hemodialysis (intermittent)
    Not studied in dialysis; the label gives no dialysis dose. Removal by hemodialysis unlikely (over 99.5% protein-bound). Specialist input; prefer agents with lower hypoglycemia risk.
    Peritoneal dialysis
    Not studied; no dose in label. Specialist input. Metabolites accumulate in kidney impairment; risk of prolonged hypoglycemia (label 8.6, 12.3).
    CRRT
    No data. Specialist input; insulin usually preferred in acute illness.
    • Dialyzable? Not stated; unlikely (protein binding over 99.5%; label).
    • Monitoring Blood glucose (hypoglycemia, which can be prolonged), hemoglobin A1c, kidney function. Hold in acute illness with poor intake.

    From the Glimepiride monograph (renalcarematters.com, reviewed).

    Use: Metformin Hydrochloride Tablets are indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults and pediatric patients 10 years of age and older with type 2 diabetes mellitus. US FDA label

    Show full dosing details
    eGFR ≥60
    Usual dosing. Assess kidney function before starting and periodically (label).
    eGFR 30–59
    eGFR 45–59: usual dose; consider dose reduction if at high risk of lactic acidosis. eGFR 30–44: label advises not to start; if continuing, reduce to a maximum of 1,000 mg/day (KDIGO 2022; ADA-KDIGO 2022). Recheck eGFR every 3 to 6 months.
    eGFR 15–29
    Contraindicated (label). Stop if eGFR falls below 30.
    eGFR <15 (not on dialysis)
    Contraindicated (label).
    Hemodialysis (intermittent)
    Contraindicated (eGFR <30 per label).
    Peritoneal dialysis
    Contraindicated (eGFR <30 per label).
    CRRT
    Not appropriate; contraindicated in severe kidney impairment (label).
    • Dialyzable? Yes: dialyzable with clearance up to 170 mL/min under good hemodynamic conditions; hemodialysis may help remove drug in suspected overdose (label).
    • Monitoring eGFR before starting and at least periodically; more often in older adults. Stop before or at the time of iodinated contrast in at-risk patients per label. Hold temporarily during acute illness with dehydration, sepsis, hypoxia or acute heart failure, during procedures with restricted food or fluid intake, and for acute kidney injury; restart when eating and kidney function is stable (label 5.1).

    From the Metformin monograph (renalcarematters.com, reviewed).
