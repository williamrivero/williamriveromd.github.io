Use

  • Chronic constipation, except constipation associated with organic diseases 2
  • The insert notes no clinical experience in drug-induced or disease-induced constipation 2

Contraindications

  • Documented or suspected intestinal obstruction associated with a tumor or hernia (obstruction may worsen) 2
  • History of hypersensitivity to any ingredient 2
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Dosing: Adult

  • Chronic constipation: 10 mg (2 tablets) once daily before a meal; adjust to symptoms; maximum 15 mg (3 tablets) per day 2
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Dosing from the Philippine package insert. If abdominal pain or diarrhea occurs, reduce, interrupt or stop the drug, and reassess the need for continued treatment regularly rather than continuing it indefinitely 2. Regimens used in studies (for example, dosing before bedtime in the Thai hemodialysis trial) are study protocols, not prescribing advice 3.

CKD · dialysis · CRRT

Dosing: Altered kidney function

Elobixibat acts in the gut and is barely absorbed: about 0.01% of a dose appears in urine and protein binding exceeds 99% 2. The Philippine insert gives no kidney-based dose adjustment 2. Small studies in CKD stages G3 to G5 and on hemodialysis used the usual 5 to 15 mg/day and found better bowel function without serious adverse events 3,4,5,6. "No adjustment specified" is not the same as proven safety at every level of kidney function: there are no dedicated kidney-impairment pharmacokinetic studies, and data for peritoneal dialysis, CRRT and acute kidney injury are lacking.

Kidney function measure: The insert sets no kidney function threshold; bands are shown on eGFR for orientation only.

Evidence in CKD, dialysis and chronic constipation

The kidney-specific evidence is small, short and from Japan and Thailand. It supports better bowel function in CKD stages G3 to G5 and on hemodialysis. It does not show slower CKD progression, fewer cardiovascular events or deaths, lower uremic toxins, a phosphate- or potassium-lowering effect, or an advantage over other laxatives in dialysis.

How to read this evidence

  • The Japanese 2023 guideline recommends IBAT inhibitors for chronic constipation (strong recommendation, evidence level A). This is general constipation guidance, not a dialysis-specific grade 10.
  • Magnesium-containing laxatives need care in impaired kidney function, with serum magnesium monitoring 10; the AGA–ACG guideline advises avoiding magnesium oxide in renal insufficiency because of hypermagnesemia 11. That guideline did not evaluate elobixibat; the omission is not a recommendation against it 11.
  • Lipid and phosphorus changes in these studies do not establish cardiovascular benefit or a phosphate-lowering use, and phosphorus results differ between studies 4,5,3.
  • Do not rank effect sizes across these trials: populations, endpoints and durations differ.

Dosing: Hepatic impairment

  • Use with care in serious liver disease; the drug may work less well with biliary obstruction or reduced bile acid secretion 2
  • Abnormal liver tests are listed adverse reactions (1% to <5%) 2

Dosing: Pediatric

  • Safety not established in low-birth-weight infants, neonates, infants or children; no clinical experience 2

Adverse reactions

Common

  • Abdominal pain (23.2%) and diarrhea (14.4%), the only reactions listed at 5% or more 2
  • 1% to <5%: lower or upper abdominal pain, abdominal distension, nausea, abdominal discomfort, soft stools; abnormal liver tests (ALT, AST, gamma-GT, alkaline phosphatase, LAP); dizziness; anemia; raised CK 2
  • In the phase 3 trials: abdominal pain 19% versus 2% with placebo and diarrhea 13% versus 0% over 2 weeks; 24% and 15% over 52 weeks, mostly mild 8

Serious

  • Listed with frequency unknown: ischemic colitis, rectal bleeding 2
  • Swallowing a tablet still in its blister sheet can perforate the esophagus 2
  • No serious adverse events were reported in the small CKD and hemodialysis studies; this does not show that serious risks are absent 3,4,5
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Monitoring parameters

  • Bowel frequency, abdominal pain and diarrhea; if diarrhea develops, volume status, blood pressure and electrolytes (clinical interpretation). Kidney
  • Stool frequency and form, abdominal pain and diarrhea; reduce, pause or stop if needed, and reassess continued use regularly 2
  • Clinical interpretation: low absorption does not remove the volume and electrolyte risk of diarrhea. In CKD and on dialysis, check volume status (interdialytic weight gain on hemodialysis), blood pressure and electrolytes if diarrhea develops
  • Digoxin or dabigatran co-therapy: exposure may rise 2
  • Counsel patients to take tablets out of the blister sheet before swallowing 2
  • Clinical interpretation: give bowel advice within the patient's fluid allowance; do not advise extra fluid for constipation without regard to dialysis fluid limits
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Pregnancy considerations

  • Use only if the expected benefit outweighs the possible risk 2
  • Rat studies: maternal toxicity at 1,000 mg/kg/day and effects on offspring survival, growth and development at 350 mg/kg/day or more 2
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Breastfeeding considerations

  • Avoid during breastfeeding; if treatment is essential, stop breastfeeding during treatment. In rats, radioactivity passed into milk 2
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Overdose

  • No overdose data; do not exceed the labeled dose and monitor the patient's response 2
renalcarematters.com, reviewed

Mechanism of action

Elobixibat blocks the ileal bile acid transporter, so more of the body's own bile acids reach the colon, where they increase colonic secretion and motility 8,6. It acts locally: plasma levels are in the picogram-per-milliliter range, protein binding exceeds 99%, and about 0.01% of a dose appears in urine 2. It also inhibits P-glycoprotein 2.

Class: Ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitor; laxative
