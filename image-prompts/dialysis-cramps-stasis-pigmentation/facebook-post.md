## Option A — The "two signals, one leg" lead (≈170 words, recommended)

> Two leg problems most dialysis patients know — and almost no one connects them.
>
> The painful **muscle cramp** that hits in the last hour of dialysis. The slow **brown discoloration** above the ankles that won't wash off.
>
> They're not two separate problems. They're two signals from one struggling leg — the one that does the hardest work against gravity, holds the most fluid, and loses blood flow first when the machine pulls water out too fast.
>
> Almost everything that helps one also helps the other:
>
> 💧 Tighter fluid control between sessions (less salt → less thirst → less weight gain)
> 🦵 Leg elevation for 20–30 minutes after dialysis (and again before bed)
> 🤸 Gentle ankle pumps and calf stretches every 30 minutes while on the chair
> 🧪 Talk to your nephrologist about L-carnitine, B-vitamins, magnesium, and CoQ10
> 🧦 Compression stockings only AFTER an ABI check (or a toe-brachial index if you have diabetes)
>
> If you also have diabetes, the same problem moves faster — and the brown gaiter on a numb foot can be the early sign of a diabetic-foot ulcer down the line. Worth knowing about now, not later.
>
> Full guide in the first comment ⬇️ — written for both patients and their dialysis team.
>

## Option B — Question-led, shorter (≈120 words, for chattier feeds)

> Cramps during your last hour of dialysis? A slow brown discoloration above the ankles that just won't fade?
>
> These two things travel together more often than chance — and the reason is one underused leg muscle, one over-loaded set of veins, and one set of oxygen-starved cells you can do something about.
>
> A few moves that help **both** at the same time:
>
> ✔️ Keep interdialytic weight gain below 4–5% of dry weight
> ✔️ Elevate your legs after each session
> ✔️ Slow ankle pumps every 30 minutes while on the chair
> ✔️ Ask about L-carnitine, B-vitamins, magnesium, CoQ10
> ✔️ Never start compression stockings without an ABI (and a TBI if you're diabetic)
>
> If you have diabetes too — this is also the earliest stage of the diabetic-foot story.
>

## Option C — Clinician-leaning (≈110 words, for sharing in nephrology groups)

> Quick clinical thought for the dialysis floor: intradialytic muscle cramps and lower-limb stasis pigmentation co-occur far more often than chance — and the cleanest way to think about them is **one continuum, not two diseases**.
>
> Same upstream substrate (volume overload, microvascular compromise, dependent congestion, ATP-cofactor depletion), two outputs at different time scales: the acute axis (cramp during UF) and the chronic axis (gaiter → dermatitis → ulcer).
>
> Diabetes accelerates every node. Don't trust ABI alone in DM-CKD (medial arterial calcification → false-normal ABI) — pair with TBI and add TcPO₂ at CEAP ≥ C4.
>
> New patient + clinician guide with the 5-hit cramp model, the Hypoxic Lower-Limb Spectrum (0–6), and an APA-7 reference set in the first comment.
>
