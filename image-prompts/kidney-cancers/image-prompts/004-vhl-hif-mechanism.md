PURPOSE: Explain, for both patients and clinicians, the signature molecular driver of clear-cell renal cell carcinoma: loss of the VHL tumor-suppressor protein lets HIF-1α/HIF-2α escape degradation and accumulate, switching on VEGF/PDGF and driving the abnormal tumor blood-vessel growth that makes clear-cell RCC a highly vascular cancer — and shows where anti-angiogenic targeted therapy and HIF-2α inhibitors interrupt that axis. Supports the Von Hippel-Lindau and sporadic 3p/VHL-loss content in the hereditary kidney-cancer section.

KEY CONCEPTS:
- Clear-cell RCC arises from VHL loss — inherited (Von Hippel-Lindau syndrome) or sporadic 3p/VHL loss in a kidney cortical tumor.
- Normal cell: VHL protein present → HIF-α is tagged and degraded → low VEGF → normal, orderly vasculature.
- RCC cell: VHL function lost → HIF-1α/HIF-2α not degraded → HIF accumulates in the nucleus → transcription of VEGF and PDGF ↑.
- Excess VEGF/PDGF → abnormal tumor angiogenesis → highly vascular clear-cell tumor.
- Therapeutic point: anti-angiogenic targeted therapy blocks VEGF signaling and HIF-2α inhibitor blocks the upstream driver → reduced tumor angiogenesis and disease control.
