# Module 07 · Water Treatment, Concentrates, and Dialysate

![A dialysis water treatment installation.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 6 hours classroom, 6 hours skills lab, 16 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Module 11  
**Before you start** — Review the two fluid paths in Module 6.

## Learning objectives

1. Follow the water from the incoming supply, through treatment and distribution, to the dialysis station.
2. Tell the water quality checks apart, and name the right sample point, method, and record for each.
3. Explain how concentrates become dialysate, and what the product identity, conductivity, and pH checks can and cannot prove.
4. Read a mock water log, hold the treatment starts that are affected, escalate the exception, and record the authorized release.

## Opening case · First-shift total chlorine result

It is Tuesday, a little before 06:00. The technician on duty, Mr. Paolo Santiago, 26, has just read the total chlorine test at the water system’s designated routine sample point. The log shows the result above the action level for that point. The result recorded on the previous shift was acceptable. A few minutes earlier he noted that the RO product water display was within its usual range. No patient has arrived, and every dialysis station is still empty. The charge nurse, Ms. Liza Domingo, asks whether the first patients can be set up.

- What do you do before any patient starts treatment?
- Does the normal RO display tell you anything about the chlorine result?
- What goes into your report, and who may release the system?

## 1. From incoming water to the station

Think of the water system as the kitchen of a busy carinderia. Ingredients arrive from the market, then pass through washing, cutting, and cooking, one stage after another, before they reach the plate. Each stage removes a different problem, and a problem missed at one stage is not fixed by the next. The picture has a limit. A cook can taste the food before it is served. You cannot judge dialysis water by looking at it, so the only way to know is the right check at the right point.

Dialysis uses treated water to prepare dialysate. Contaminants in that water can enter the dialysate pathway and harm patients. Your facility uses a series of treatment and monitoring steps chosen for its own source water and equipment. Walk the actual installation with the designated water-system lead, so that the diagram below becomes pipes and ports you can point to. [Philippine DOH · Analysis, monitoring and maintenance of dialysis water](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036) · [CDC · Water use in dialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/recommendations-resources/water-use-in-dialysis.html)

![Functional water-treatment path from incoming supply through pretreatment and reverse osmosis to distribution and dialysate preparation.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Simplified water pathway. The table provides a text equivalent. Sample locations and component order vary by installation.*

| Stage | Typical role |
|---|---|
| **Incoming water and pretreatment** | The facility evaluates the water it receives. Filters remove particles. A softener, where one is used, reduces hardness. |
| **Activated carbon** | Reduces the chlorine and chloramine used to disinfect the public supply. Some systems have two carbon stages, with designated sample ports that detect breakthrough and guide the response. Breakthrough means the measured disinfectant has passed a treatment stage at a level that calls for action. |
| **Reverse osmosis (RO)** | A membrane under pressure splits the water into a rejected stream and product water, and reduces many dissolved impurities. The facility watches RO performance and water quality with several different checks. |
| **Product water distribution** | A distribution loop carries the treated water to the stations. The design may include storage or further treatment. The unit follows a validated disinfection and monitoring program for its own loop. |
| **Station** | The dialysis machine mixes treated water with compatible concentrates to make dialysate. Water quality checks and final dialysate checks look at different points in this process. |

## 2. Match the quality check to its purpose

Each check answers one question about one place in the system. The most common mistake is to use the answer to one question as if it answered another.

| Check | What the check tells you | How to use it within your assigned scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Total chlorine** | Measures free chlorine plus combined chlorine, which includes chloramines. A high result can mean breakthrough in the carbon stage. | Use the designated sample point, method, timing, and action level. Write the actual result and its units. |
| **Hardness where applicable** | Helps you follow softener performance at a designated point. | Use the approved kit and compare the result with the local trigger that applies. |
| **RO product water conductivity or related performance reading** | Helps you detect a change in dissolved ions or in RO performance. | Compare with the installation’s settings and with the trend. This reading cannot replace total chlorine testing, and it cannot detect every contaminant. |
| **Laboratory chemical analysis** | Measures specified chemical contaminants under the facility’s monitoring plan and the requirements that apply. | Confirm sampling, labeling, transport, and routing with the authorized team. |
| **Microbiological culture and endotoxin testing** | Culture estimates the number of living organisms by a specified method. Endotoxin testing looks for bacterial endotoxin, which is not the same thing as a count of living organisms. | Follow the sampling plan, the exact point and method, and the laboratory’s instructions. Send results and trends to the designated lead. |

Your facility sets its current sampling schedule, action levels, and maximum limits from the applicable Philippine requirements, locally adopted standards, the design of its system, and verified instructions. An **action level** is a trigger for a defined response, set so that the response starts before, or at the moment, a safety boundary is reached. Apply each requirement at its own test location. A reading from a different port, a different unit, or another shift cannot clear the exception in front of you. CDC and U.S. CMS examples are useful for comparison, but the facility decides which standards govern local practice. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water monitoring and analysis](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036) · [CDC · Dialysis water quality](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/recommendations-resources/water-use-in-dialysis.html) · [CMS · U.S. dialysis interpretive guidance, Appendix H](https://www.cms.gov/Regulations-and-Guidance/Guidance/Manuals/Downloads/som107ap_h_esrd.pdf)

**Before an assigned test,** confirm the sampling point, the timing, the kit or meter and its expiry, calibration or quality control where it applies, and the reporting units. Take the sample by the approved method at the required time. Log the date and time, sample location, system ID, result, the limit or trigger that applies, your name, and any action taken. If a required result is missing or you doubt it, ask the water lead how to proceed. Do not assume the system is ready.

## 3. Verify concentrates and dialysate separately

The machine combines treated dialysis water with compatible concentrate products. Conventional bicarbonate hemodialysis usually uses an acid concentrate and a bicarbonate source. Product families and mixing ratios differ. Check the product identity, formulation, expiry, compatibility, and the authorized prescription through the facility’s verification process. Confirm that the intended concentrate supply is connected, using the applicable instructions for use (IFU). [NIDDK · Hemodialysis and dialysis solution](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/hemodialysis) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Model-specific operator manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

Each check below covers a different risk, so a pass on one does not stand in for another.

| Verification | What it addresses |
|---|---|
| **Concentrate identity and proportioning** | Confirms that the products are compatible with the machine and match the authorized formulation. Read the labels and complete any independent verification the facility’s process requires. |
| **Dialysate conductivity** | Measures how well the fluid conducts electricity, which depends on its dissolved ions. It helps you catch a proportioning problem. A normal value does not prove the intended potassium or calcium formulation, and it says nothing about water quality. |
| **Dialysate pH** | Checks acidity or alkalinity. Use the approved measurement method, with independent verification when the IFU and facility policy require it. |
| **Water system release** | Confirms that the water system has passed the facility’s required checks. A machine’s dialysate display cannot clear a water quality exception upstream. |

**Worked example.** A machine shows an acceptable dialysate conductivity, but the concentrate label does not match the authorized formulation. The practical point is that conductivity cannot see the difference. Hold that machine’s setup, keep what is needed to identify the product, and report the discrepancy. Use the approved verification process for a replacement product, and repeat the required machine checks before the station is released. [Fresenius Medical Care · Model-specific operator manuals and concentrate addenda](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

## 4. Hold, escalate and document water system exceptions

**Case debrief.** *What to do before any patient starts.* Hold the affected treatment starts while the result is assessed. Mr. Santiago’s first words go to the supervising nurse and the designated water-system or biomedical lead, through the facility’s escalation procedure. He reports the actual total chlorine result, the units, the time, the sample point, the system ID, which stations may be affected, and whether anyone is already on treatment.

*Does the normal RO display help?* No. The RO display and the satisfactory result from the earlier shift describe other points and other times. Neither establishes readiness for this shift. Follow the approved response for that sample point, which may include authorized testing of backup components, confirmation testing, and corrective action.

*Who releases the system?* The designated authority documents the decision to release, to keep holding, or to put the unit contingency plan into effect. If anyone is already on treatment, activate the unit’s emergency pathway at once so that the clinical team can act. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water quality requirements](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036) · [CMS · Dialysis water exceptions and corrective action](https://www.cms.gov/Regulations-and-Guidance/Guidance/Manuals/Downloads/som107ap_h_esrd.pdf)

| Mock log entry | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Prior shift · Routine carbon check · Within the trigger | History. Keep it for the trend. |
| 05:40 · RO product water display · Within the usual range | Useful for watching the RO. It does not answer a question about the carbon stage. |
| 05:45 · Designated routine carbon sample · Total chlorine above the action level | Hold the affected starts and notify. Check the approved process for that sample point. |
| 05:48 · Corrective action and authorized release · Pending | The affected system stays on hold until the designated lead documents a decision. |

**Example handoff.** “The 05:45 total chlorine result at the designated carbon sample point is above the action level. The value, units, and point ID are in the water log. We have held first-shift starts on the affected system. No treatment has begun. The water lead and the charge nurse have been told. Release is pending.” Notice what the report contains: the number, where it was taken, what is on hold, who knows, and what is still open. During simulation, ask where the release decision and the notifications are entered.
