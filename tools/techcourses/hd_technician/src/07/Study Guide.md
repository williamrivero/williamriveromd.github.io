# Module 07 · Water Treatment, Concentrates, and Dialysate

![A dialysis water treatment installation.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 6 hours classroom, 6 hours skills lab, 16 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Module 11  
**Before you start** — Review the two fluid paths in Module 6.

## Learning objectives

1. Trace water from the incoming supply through treatment and distribution to the dialysis station.
2. Distinguish water quality checks and identify the right sample point, method and documentation.
3. Explain how concentrates form dialysate and what product identity, conductivity and pH checks verify.
4. Interpret a mock water log, hold affected treatment starts, escalate exceptions and document the authorized release.

## Opening case · First-shift total chlorine result

Before the first treatment, the assigned technician obtains a total chlorine reading at the water system’s designated routine sample point. The value is flagged above that point’s action level in the unit log. The previous recorded result was acceptable. The RO product water display is within its usual range, and the dialysis stations are still empty.

- What is the first step before a patient starts treatment?
- Does a normal RO display resolve the total chlorine exception?
- What information should be included in the handoff, and who may clear the system?

## 1. From incoming water to the station

Dialysis uses treated water to prepare dialysate. Contaminants in dialysis water can enter the dialysate pathway and harm patients. The facility uses a series of treatment and monitoring steps selected for its source water and equipment. Trace the actual installation with the designated water-system lead. [Philippine DOH · Analysis, monitoring and maintenance of dialysis water](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036) · [CDC · Water use in dialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/recommendations-resources/water-use-in-dialysis.html)

![Functional water-treatment path from incoming supply through pretreatment and reverse osmosis to distribution and dialysate preparation.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Simplified water pathway. The table provides a text equivalent. Sample locations and component order vary by installation.*

| Stage | Typical role |
|---|---|
| **Incoming water and pretreatment** | The facility evaluates its feed water. Filters remove particles, and a softener, where used, reduces hardness. |
| **Activated carbon** | Reduces chlorine and chloramine used to disinfect the incoming supply. Some systems use two carbon stages and designated sample ports to detect breakthrough and guide the response. Breakthrough means that the measured disinfectant has passed a treatment stage at a level that requires action. |
| **Reverse osmosis (RO)** | A pressurized membrane separates a rejected stream from product water and reduces many dissolved impurities. The facility monitors RO performance and water quality using several checks. |
| **Product water distribution** | A distribution loop carries treated water to stations. The design may include storage or additional treatment. The unit follows a validated disinfection and monitoring program for its loop. |
| **Station** | The dialysis machine proportions treated water with compatible concentrates to make dialysate. Water quality and final dialysate checks address different points in the process. |

## 2. Match the quality check to its purpose

| Check | What the check tells you | How to use it within your assigned scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Total chlorine** | Measures free chlorine plus combined chlorine, including chloramines. It can signal breakthrough in the carbon treatment pathway. | Follow the designated sample point, method, timing and action level. Record the actual result and units. |
| **Hardness where applicable** | Helps monitor softener performance at a designated point. | Use the approved kit and compare with the applicable local trigger. |
| **RO product water conductivity or related performance reading** | Helps detect changes in dissolved ions or RO performance. | Compare with the installation’s settings and trends. This measurement cannot replace total chlorine testing or detect all contaminants. |
| **Laboratory chemical analysis** | Measures specified chemical contaminants according to the facility monitoring plan and applicable requirements. | Confirm sampling, labeling, transport and routing with the authorized team. |
| **Microbiological culture and endotoxin testing** | Culture estimates viable organisms under specified methods. Endotoxin testing looks for bacterial endotoxin, which is distinct from a viable organism count. | Follow the sampling plan, exact point and method, and applicable lab instructions. Route results and trends to the designated lead. |

Use the facility's current sampling schedule, action levels and maximum limits based on applicable Philippine requirements, locally adopted standards, the system design and verified instructions. An **action level** is a trigger for a specified response before or when a defined safety boundary is reached. Apply the requirement at the correct test location. A reading from a different port, a different unit or another shift cannot clear the exception. CDC and U.S. CMS examples support comparison, while the facility establishes which standards govern local practice. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water monitoring and analysis](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036) · [CDC · Dialysis water quality](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/recommendations-resources/water-use-in-dialysis.html) · [CMS · U.S. dialysis interpretive guidance, Appendix H](https://www.cms.gov/Regulations-and-Guidance/Guidance/Manuals/Downloads/som107ap_h_esrd.pdf)

**Before an assigned test,** verify the sampling point, timing, kit or meter, expiry, calibration or QC as applicable, and reporting units. Follow the approved sampling method and capture the result at the required time. Log date and time, sample location, system ID, result, applicable limit or trigger, operator and any action. When a required result is missing or unreliable, ask the water lead how to proceed before assuming readiness.

## 3. Verify concentrates and dialysate separately

The machine combines treated dialysis water with compatible concentrate products. Conventional bicarbonate hemodialysis commonly uses an acid concentrate and bicarbonate source. Product families and mixing ratios can differ. Check the product identity, formulation, expiration, compatibility and authorized prescription through the facility’s verification process. Verify the intended concentrate supply connection using the applicable instructions for use (IFU). [NIDDK · Hemodialysis and dialysis solution](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/hemodialysis) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Model-specific operator manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

| Verification | What it addresses |
|---|---|
| **Concentrate identity and proportioning** | Confirms that the intended products are compatible with the machine and match the authorized formulation. Check labels and required independent verification under the facility’s process. |
| **Dialysate conductivity** | Reflects the fluid’s ability to conduct electricity, which depends on its dissolved ions. It helps detect proportioning problems. A normal value does not prove the intended potassium or calcium formulation or establish water quality. |
| **Dialysate pH** | Checks acidity or alkalinity. Follow the approved measurement method and ensure independent verification when required by the applicable IFU and facility policy. |
| **Water system release** | Confirms the water system has met the facility’s required checks. A machine’s dialysate display cannot clear an upstream water quality exception. |

**Worked example.** A machine displays an acceptable dialysate conductivity, but the concentrate label does not match the authorized formulation. Hold that machine’s setup, retain the information needed to identify the product and report the discrepancy. Use the approved verification process for a replacement product and repeat the required machine checks before release. [Fresenius Medical Care · Model-specific operator manuals and concentrate addenda](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

## 4. Hold, escalate and document water system exceptions

**Case debrief.** Hold affected treatment starts while the result is assessed. Notify the supervising nurse and designated water-system or biomedical lead through the facility’s escalation procedure. Report the actual total chlorine result, units, time, sample point, system ID, which stations may be affected and whether anyone is already receiving treatment. A normal RO display and a satisfactory earlier sample do not establish readiness for this shift. Follow the approved response for that sample point, including any authorized tracing of backup components, confirmation testing and corrective action. The designated authority documents the decision to release, continue holding or implement the unit contingency plan. If anyone is already receiving treatment, immediately activate the unit’s emergency pathway so the clinical team can act. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water quality requirements](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036) · [CMS · Dialysis water exceptions and corrective action](https://www.cms.gov/Regulations-and-Guidance/Guidance/Manuals/Downloads/som107ap_h_esrd.pdf)

| Mock log entry | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Prior shift · Routine carbon check · Within the trigger | Historical information. Retain it for trending. |
| 05:40 · RO product water display · Within the usual range | Useful for RO monitoring. It does not resolve a carbon-stage finding. |
| 05:45 · Designated routine carbon sample · Total chlorine above the action level | Hold affected starts and notify. Check the approved process for that sample point. |
| 05:48 · Corrective action and authorized release · Pending | Keep the affected system on hold until the designated lead documents the decision. |

**Example handoff.** “The 05:45 total chlorine result at the designated carbon sample point is above the action level. The value, units and point ID are recorded in the water log. We have held first-shift starts on the affected system. No treatment has begun. The water lead and charge nurse have been notified. Release is pending.” During simulation, ask where the release decision and the notifications are entered.
