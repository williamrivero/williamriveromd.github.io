# Module 09 · The Hemodialysis Session

![Two dialysis team members review a checklist near an unoccupied treatment station.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 6 hours classroom, 8 hours skills lab, 18 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Module 9  
**Before you start** — Review fluid calculations, infection prevention, equipment checks and access protection in Modules 4–8.

## Learning objectives

1. Verify patient identity, the current treatment order and pre-treatment measurements, and escalate discrepancies before the affected setup proceeds.
2. Describe assigned intradialytic monitoring and promptly report clinical or equipment changes.
3. Explain the technician’s assigned responsibilities during treatment completion and station turnover.
4. Document observations, changes and notifications and use a structured handoff with check-back.

## Opening case · An unexpected pre-treatment weight

Before dialysis, Ms. Ramos weighs **71.8 kg**. Recent comparable pre-treatment measurements were **68.2–69.0 kg**. The chart shows a prior post-treatment weight of **67.9 kg**. A repeat measurement on the assigned scale again reads **71.8 kg** after identity, scale units and measurement conditions are checked. Ms. Ramos says she has no new symptoms. The current order needs nurse verification before setup continues.

- Which checks does the repeat measurement support, and what remains unresolved?
- How would you report the finding to the dialysis nurse?
- Which decisions belong to authorized clinical staff?

## 1. One session, three phases

The session begins with identification and readiness checks, continues with monitoring during treatment and ends with a completion review, handoff and safe station turnover. Philippine dialysis facility standards and the unit’s job descriptions determine assignment and supervision. The clinical team verifies the order, assesses the patient, decides whether treatment can proceed and addresses changes in the prescription or patient status. The trainee practices the workflow in simulation and performs only verified, authorized tasks under supervision. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [NNCC · CCHT examination specifications for international context](https://www.nncc-exam.org/certification/ccht)

| Phase | Technician’s assigned work | Supervising nurse and clinical team |

## 2. Pre-treatment · Verify before relying on the record

1. **Identify the patient.** Match two facility-approved person-specific identifiers to the record. Ask the patient to state them when possible and use the approved alternative procedure when needed. A chair or station number identifies a location.
2. **Verify readiness.** Check the station, equipment and required release status within your assignment. Confirm that the patient record, current order and approved setup match through the unit’s verification process. Time, dialyzer, dialysate and fluid removal are examples of parameters that may require verification.
3. **Collect assigned observations.** Obtain the current weight and assigned vital signs using approved technique. Ask about new symptoms and note what has changed. For an unexpected measurement, confirm patient identity, units, measurement type, equipment and conditions. Repeat as directed and report the original and repeat results under the unit procedure.
4. **Hand off exceptions.** Notify the supervising nurse promptly of an unresolved order, weight, symptom, access or equipment concern. Pause the affected setup pending direction. Avoid copying a prior treatment’s settings into the current session without verification.

The pre-treatment weight is an observation. Comparison with a previous post-treatment weight can help the clinical team assess fluid gain. The prescribed UF goal reflects the authorized plan for the current session. [WHO · Patient identification](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/patient-safety/patient-safety-solutions/ps-solution2-patient-identification.pdf) · [UK Kidney Association · Fluid assessment and management](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/rationale-for-clinical-practice-guidelines/fluid-in-haemodialysis/)

**Case debrief.** The repeat supports that the current pre-treatment measurement is reproducible under the checked conditions. It does not establish the cause or authorize a fluid removal change. The measured difference from the last post-treatment weight is **3.9 kg**. Communicate the current and repeat readings, timing, earlier weights, patient-reported symptoms and checks performed. Ask the nurse to assess the discrepancy and verify the current order before setup proceeds.

## 3. Intradialytic · Compare readings with what you see

Follow the facility’s observation schedule and authorized role. At assigned checks, note the patient’s reported symptoms and appearance, vital signs, access and line visibility, and machine readings or messages. Compare with prior observations and note the time and treatment context. A concerning change requires a timely verbal report even if charting is due later. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Model-specific operator manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

| Observation | Action within assigned scope |
|---|---|
| New dizziness or nausea, especially with a falling blood pressure | Notify the dialysis nurse promptly, report the symptom and readings, and follow directions. |
| Chest pain, severe breathlessness, unresponsiveness, heavy bleeding or visible dislodgement | Summon help immediately and activate the unit emergency pathway as required. Report the specific signs and location. |
| Newly changed machine reading or alarm | Observe the patient and circuit from your assigned position, notify the nurse and report the exact message. Follow the model and unit procedures. Keep protective functions in place. |
| Unexplained timing, undocumented change or interruption | Bring it to the nurse’s attention and record the actual events and timestamps in the approved record. |

**Example.** If Ms. Ramos later reports dizziness, tell the nurse when it started, what she said, the assigned readings and any visible changes. Avoid interpreting the cause beyond your role. Dialysis pressure alarms may miss some external blood loss, so maintain access visibility as directed and escalate visual concerns independently of alarm status. [UK Kidney Association · Fluid and intradialytic symptoms](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/rationale-for-clinical-practice-guidelines/fluid-in-haemodialysis/) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Model-specific operator manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

## 4. Completion, turnover and handoff

- **Completion.** Follow the nurse’s directions and assigned unit steps. Patient access handling, blood return, disconnection and other clinical tasks are limited to authorized staff with validated competencies. This module rehearses observation, measurement and communication rather than teaching those procedures.
- **Patient status.** Report new symptoms, unresolved bleeding or difficulty standing. Obtain assigned post-treatment observations only when it is appropriate and safe. The nurse confirms the patient’s status and disposition.
- **Turnover.** Follow Module 5 and the approved checklist after the patient leaves. Route used equipment and waste correctly. Verify cleaning, disinfection, contact time and station release before staging the next patient’s clean supplies.
- **Handoff.** Report the treatment status, relevant readings and trends, events or interruptions, actions, who has been notified and any unresolved follow-up. Confirm who will take the next step. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [CDC · Dialysis station disinfection checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/media/pdfs/env-checklist-508.pdf) · [AHRQ · SBAR](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/sbar.html)

### Example pre-treatment handoff

- **Situation.** “Ms. Ramos’s measured pre-treatment weight is higher than her recent trend. Setup is paused.”
- **Background.** “Her last post-treatment weight was 67.9 kg. Recent pre-treatment weights were 68.2 to 69.0 kg.”
- **Assessment within role.** “Today’s measurement and repeat on the assigned scale were both 71.8 kg. I confirmed her identifiers, scale units and measurement conditions. She reports no new symptoms.”
- **Request.** “Please assess the discrepancy and confirm the current order before setup proceeds.”

Use check-back to confirm the nurse’s instructions. Record the measured readings with units and times, notification and confirmed directions. Keep entries factual. Follow the unit’s correction process rather than replacing an original entry without an audit trail. An incident report, when required, follows a separate facility process. [AHRQ · TeamSTEPPS communication tools](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/resources/modules/index.html)
