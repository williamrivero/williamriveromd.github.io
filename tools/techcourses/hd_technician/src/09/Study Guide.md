# Module 09 · The Hemodialysis Session

![Two dialysis team members review a checklist near an unoccupied treatment station.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 6 hours classroom, 8 hours skills lab, 18 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Module 9  
**Before you start** — Review fluid calculations, infection prevention, equipment checks and access protection in Modules 4–8.

## Learning objectives

1. Verify the patient’s identity, the current treatment order, and the pre-treatment measurements, and escalate any discrepancy before the affected setup goes ahead.
2. Describe the monitoring you are assigned during treatment, and report clinical or equipment changes promptly.
3. Explain your assigned responsibilities at the end of treatment and during station turnover.
4. Record observations, changes, and notifications, and give a structured handoff with check-back.

## Opening case · An unexpected pre-treatment weight

It is Friday morning. Ms. Rosario Ramos, 66, steps on the scale before her session, and it reads **71.8 kg**. Her recent comparable pre-treatment weights were **68.2–69.0 kg**, and the chart shows her last post-treatment weight as **67.9 kg**. You check her identity, the scale units, and the measurement conditions, and weigh her again on the assigned scale. It again reads **71.8 kg**. Ms. Ramos says she feels well and has no new symptoms. The current order needs the nurse’s verification before setup continues.

- What does the repeat measurement confirm, and what does it leave unanswered?
- How will you report the finding to the dialysis nurse?
- Which decisions belong to the authorized clinical staff, and not to you?

## 1. One session, three phases

Think of a session as a jeepney trip. Before the jeep moves, the right passengers board and the route is confirmed. During the ride, someone watches the road and passengers call out when something is wrong. At the end, everyone gets off safely, and the seat is cleaned before the next passenger. The picture has a limit. On a jeepney one driver decides everything. In a dialysis unit the decisions are divided, and knowing which ones are yours is half of the work.

The session starts with identification and readiness checks, continues with monitoring during treatment, and ends with a completion review, a handoff, and safe station turnover. Philippine dialysis facility standards and the unit’s job descriptions set who is assigned to what and under whose supervision. The clinical team verifies the order, assesses the patient, decides whether treatment can go ahead, and handles any change in the prescription or in the patient’s condition. As a trainee, you practice the workflow in simulation and perform only verified, authorized tasks under supervision. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [NNCC · CCHT examination specifications for international context](https://www.nncc-exam.org/certification/ccht)

## 2. Pre-treatment · Verify before relying on the record

A record is only as good as its match to the person in the chair. Each step below closes one way the match can fail.

1. **Identify the patient.** Match two facility-approved identifiers that belong only to this person against the record. Ask the patient to state them when possible, and use the approved alternative procedure when they cannot. A chair or station number tells you a location, not a person.
2. **Verify readiness.** Within your assignment, check the station, the equipment, and any required release status. Confirm through the unit’s verification process that the patient record, the current order, and the approved setup agree. Time, dialyzer, dialysate, and fluid removal are examples of settings that may need verification.
3. **Collect assigned observations.** Take the current weight and the assigned vital signs with approved technique. Ask about new symptoms and note what has changed. When a measurement is unexpected, confirm the patient’s identity, the units, the type of measurement, the equipment, and the conditions. Repeat it as directed, and report both the first and the repeat results under the unit procedure.
4. **Hand off exceptions.** Tell the supervising nurse promptly about any unresolved concern with the order, weight, symptoms, access, or equipment. Pause the affected setup until you are directed. Do not copy a previous treatment’s settings into today’s session without verification.

The pre-treatment weight is an observation. Comparing it with the last post-treatment weight helps the clinical team judge how much fluid has been gained. The prescribed UF goal reflects the authorized plan for today’s session. [WHO · Patient identification](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/patient-safety/patient-safety-solutions/ps-solution2-patient-identification.pdf) · [UK Kidney Association · Fluid assessment and management](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/rationale-for-clinical-practice-guidelines/fluid-in-haemodialysis/)

**Case debrief.** *What the repeat confirms.* The repeat shows that today’s pre-treatment weight is reproducible under the conditions you checked. It does not tell you why her weight is higher, and it does not authorize any change to fluid removal. The measured difference from her last post-treatment weight is **3.9 kg**.

*How to report it.* Give the nurse the first and repeat readings, their times, the earlier weights, what Ms. Ramos said about her symptoms, and the checks you made. Then make a clear request: ask the nurse to assess the discrepancy and to verify the current order before setup continues. The cause and the fluid removal plan belong to the clinical team.

## 3. Intradialytic · Compare readings with what you see

Follow the facility’s observation schedule and your authorized role. At each assigned check, note what the patient says and how the patient looks, the vital signs, whether the access and lines are in view, and the machine readings or messages. Compare each with the earlier observations, and note the time and how far into the session you are. A worrying change needs a spoken report now, even if the charting is not yet due. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Model-specific operator manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

| Observation | Action within assigned scope |
|---|---|
| New dizziness or nausea, especially with a falling blood pressure | Tell the dialysis nurse promptly, report the symptom and the readings, and follow the nurse’s directions. |
| Chest pain, severe breathlessness, unresponsiveness, heavy bleeding or visible dislodgement | Call for help at once and activate the unit emergency pathway as required. Report the specific signs and where they are. |
| Newly changed machine reading or alarm | Look at the patient and the circuit from your assigned position, tell the nurse, and report the exact message. Follow the machine model’s and the unit’s procedures. Leave every protective function in place. |
| Unexplained timing, undocumented change or interruption | Bring it to the nurse’s attention, and record the actual events and times in the approved record. |

**Example.** If Ms. Ramos later says she feels dizzy, tell the nurse when it began, her exact words, the assigned readings, and any change you can see. Do not try to explain the cause beyond your role. Dialysis pressure alarms can miss some blood loss outside the circuit, so keep the access in view as directed, and report what you see whether or not an alarm has sounded. [UK Kidney Association · Fluid and intradialytic symptoms](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/rationale-for-clinical-practice-guidelines/fluid-in-haemodialysis/) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Model-specific operator manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

## 4. Completion, turnover and handoff

- **Completion.** Follow the nurse’s directions and your assigned unit steps. Handling the patient’s access, returning blood, disconnecting, and other clinical tasks belong to authorized staff with validated competencies. This module rehearses observation, measurement, and communication. It does not teach those procedures.
- **Patient status.** Report new symptoms, bleeding that has not stopped, or difficulty standing. Take the assigned post-treatment observations only when it is appropriate and safe to do so. The nurse confirms the patient’s condition and whether the patient may leave.
- **Turnover.** After the patient leaves, follow Module 5 and the approved checklist. Send used equipment and waste by the correct route. Confirm cleaning, disinfection, contact time, and station release before you lay out clean supplies for the next patient.
- **Handoff.** Report where the treatment stands, the relevant readings and trends, any events or interruptions, what was done, who has been told, and what follow-up is still open. Confirm who will take the next step. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [CDC · Dialysis station disinfection checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/media/pdfs/env-checklist-508.pdf) · [AHRQ · SBAR](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/sbar.html)

### Example pre-treatment handoff

- **Situation.** “Ms. Ramos’s measured pre-treatment weight is higher than her recent trend. I have paused setup.”
- **Background.** “Her last post-treatment weight was 67.9 kg. Her recent pre-treatment weights were 68.2 to 69.0 kg.”
- **Assessment within role.** “Today’s weight and the repeat on the assigned scale were both 71.8 kg. I confirmed her identifiers, the scale units, and the measurement conditions. She reports no new symptoms.”
- **Request.** “Please assess the discrepancy and confirm the current order before setup continues.”

Notice what the handoff contains: the numbers with units, the checks already made, what has been paused, and one clear request. Use check-back to confirm the nurse’s instructions. Record the measured readings with units and times, the notification, and the directions you confirmed. Keep entries factual. Use the unit’s correction process; never replace an original entry without an audit trail. An incident report, when one is required, follows a separate facility process. [AHRQ · TeamSTEPPS communication tools](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/resources/modules/index.html)
