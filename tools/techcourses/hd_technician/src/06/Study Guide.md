# Module 06 · Machines, Dialyzers, and the Extracorporeal Circuit

![A hemodialysis machine stands beside a seated patient speaking with two people.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 6 hours classroom, 8 hours skills lab, 16 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Module 6  
**Before you start** — Review membrane transport in Module 4 and station turnover in Module 5.

## Learning objectives

1. Trace the blood and dialysate paths and identify the key components in each.
2. Explain what common safeguards monitor and recognize the limits of alarms.
3. Demonstrate assigned preparation and required pre-use checks on a training machine using the applicable instructions for use (IFU) and facility policies.
4. Hold equipment with an unresolved failure, notify the appropriate team members and document the facts.

## Opening case · Failed before the first treatment

The training unit’s hemodialysis machine displays a failed required pre-use self-test before the first patient arrives. The display identifies the failed test, and the machine exterior and disposables appear intact. Another staff member suggests clearing the message because the next patient is due soon. The unit has another machine that may be available.

- Is the first machine ready for use?
- Who should be told? What information will they need?
- If another machine is available, which preparation and verification requirements still apply?

## 1. Trace the two circuits

**Extracorporeal** means outside the body. The blood path extends from the patient’s vascular access, through the blood tubing and dialyzer, and back to the patient. A blood pump moves blood through the circuit. The withdrawal line is often labeled **arterial** and the return line **venous**. These labels refer to flow direction in the HD circuit. With a fistula or graft, both needles may be placed into the same vascular access. Connection to the patient is beyond this module’s simulation. [NIDDK · Hemodialysis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/hemodialysis) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Dialysate** is prepared from treated water and concentrates. It flows through a separate compartment of the dialyzer, outside the hollow fibers that carry blood. Used dialysate flows to the machine’s effluent or drain pathway. The semipermeable membrane is where exchange takes place. Blood and dialysate remain in separate compartments in normal operation. Machine-specific fluid pathways and protective components are shown in the model’s IFU. [NIDDK · Dialyzer cross section](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/news/media-library/17962) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Operator’s manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

| **Blood tubing and pump segment** | Withdrawal and return limbs, their labeled ends, and the section the pump acts on. |
| **Dialyzer** | The hollow-fiber filter and its separate blood and dialysate connections. |

| **Dialysate fluid path** | Treated water and concentrate supply, delivery to the dialyzer, and discharge of used fluid. |
| **Fluid balance and UF system** | The machine system that regulates fluid removal according to the verified plan. |

## 2. Safeguards support direct observation

A safeguard measures a condition at a particular location. Interpret the displayed message and its context instead of assuming the alarm proves a single cause. Some alarms stop the blood pump, close a clamp or divert dialysate, depending on machine design and the event. Keep the patient, access site and circuit under observation according to the facility’s assigned workflow. [Fresenius Medical Care · Operator’s manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/) · [NNCC · CCHT examination specifications](https://www.nncc-exam.org/certification/ccht)

| Safeguard | What it monitors and what to remember |
|---|---|
| **Blood-side pressure monitors** | Sense pressure at their monitoring points. Patient access, tubing restrictions, flow and other factors can affect readings. A venous pressure alarm may miss some dislodgements, so keep the access and connections visible as directed. |
| **Air or level detector and venous clamp** | Monitor for air or a low blood level at the designated point, depending on the design. Correct tubing or chamber placement matters. A functioning detector does not replace a correctly prepared circuit or visual checks. |
| **Blood leak detector** | Checks used dialysate for evidence of blood crossing the dialyzer membrane. It serves a different purpose from visual monitoring for external bleeding or disconnected tubing. |
| **Conductivity and temperature monitors** | Monitor important properties of prepared dialysate. Acceptable conductivity alone cannot establish correct concentrate identity, individual electrolyte composition, or the status of all other required checks. |
| **UF and fluid balance monitoring** | Supports controlled fluid removal. The authorized prescription and recorded settings still need to match before a session begins. |

**When an alarm occurs during treatment,** alert the dialysis nurse and follow the unit’s emergency and equipment procedures. Give the exact message and observable facts. Remain within the assigned role. Protective features and alarm limits must remain in place while the problem is assessed. [Fresenius Medical Care · Operator’s manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

## 3. Verify readiness using the installed model’s IFU

Practice on an unconnected training machine with the preceptor. Sequence, supplies, checks and acceptance criteria depend on the machine, software version and local procedure. Use the following prompts alongside the approved checklist.

1. **Station and machine.** Verify machine identity, service status, cleanliness and any required disinfection or rinse status. Confirm availability of the required utilities and validated dialysis water through the unit’s process.
2. **Supplies.** Match the authorized prescription and equipment requirements against the dialyzer, tubing and concentrate labels. Check package integrity and expiry. Use aseptic technique as applicable when opening or handling sterile components.
3. **Circuit.** Trace the designated paths. Verify assigned placement of tubing, pump segments, monitors, detectors and clamps. Demonstrate the facility-approved priming process in simulation and verify the circuit is ready by the model-specific criteria.
4. **Machine checks.** Confirm successful completion of required self-tests. Complete the assigned dialysate and equipment checks, including independent verification and residual-disinfectant checks when required by the applicable procedure. Record actual results.
5. **Handoff.** Confirm all assigned checks and unresolved issues with the supervising nurse before any patient connection. Ask who has authority to clear support equipment after a fault or service intervention.

A successful built-in test confirms only the functions covered by that test. It does not confirm patient identity, match the disposables to the order, or replace other required checks. [Fresenius Medical Care · Operator’s manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water monitoring](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036)

## 4. Hold, escalate and document an unresolved failure

**Case debrief.** Keep the machine unavailable for patient use and apply the facility’s hold or out-of-service process. Notify the supervising nurse or charge nurse and the designated equipment or biomedical team. Include the machine identifier, time, test name, exact message, whether a patient was connected, and what you did. Perform only authorized operator checks under the applicable procedure. Clearing a display message alone does not establish readiness. Use the facility’s formal clearance process after the issue is resolved.

**Example handoff.** “Machine HD-06 failed its required pre-use test at 06:40. The exact display message is logged. No patient was connected. I have placed it on hold and contacted the equipment lead. Please confirm the treatment assignment.” If a replacement is assigned, verify its identity and complete that machine’s required preparation, checks and documentation before connection.
