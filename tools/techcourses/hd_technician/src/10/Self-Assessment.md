# Module 10 · Self-Assessment

Choose one best answer per question. Score one point per correct response. Suggested mastery is 12 of 15. Review missed safety decisions about urgent escalation, team roles or safe return to service even if you reach the suggested score.

1. **What does intradialytic hypotension describe?**  
   A. Elevated blood pressure before dialysis  
   B. A fall to low blood pressure during dialysis  
   C. A temperature rise after dialysis  
   D. An increase in measured venous line pressure  

2. **Which observation is a vascular access emergency?**  
   A. An unchanged fistula thrill before treatment  
   B. Routine availability of a spare dressing  
   C. A correctly positioned blanket away from the access  
   D. Heavy bleeding from the access  

3. **The machine has no active alarm, but a patient reports new chest pain and difficulty breathing. What does the quiet alarm panel tell you?**  
   A. It does not rule out a patient emergency. The symptoms warrant an immediate response.  
   B. The patient can safely wait until the next scheduled observation.  
   C. The symptoms are likely caused by anxiety.  
   D. You should wait for the pressures to change before calling the nurse.  

4. **A patient becomes lightheaded and develops cramps. An assigned measurement shows a major blood pressure drop from baseline. Which concern should you report?**  
   A. Likely access infection with no need to relay the pressure  
   B. A change that matters only if treatment has already ended  
   C. Possible symptomatic intradialytic hypotension requiring prompt nursing assessment  
   D. A confirmed adverse reaction to a specific drug  

5. **A patient with a dialysis catheter has new chills and drainage near the exit site. Which concern should you raise?**  
   A. The findings rule out an infection if the machine is operating normally.  
   B. Possible infection that needs prompt clinical review.  
   C. The drainage establishes a particular organism.  
   D. The concern can be deferred until a dressing change is scheduled.  

6. **You find that a venous needle has moved and the patient is bleeding. No alarm is sounding. What should you do?**  
   A. Finish recording the latest pressures before speaking.  
   B. Adjust or reinsert the needle before telling anyone.  
   C. Cover the access until the alarm sounds.  
   D. Call for urgent help immediately and activate the facility response within your assigned role.  

7. **What is the purpose of a TeamSTEPPS call-out?**  
   A. To communicate urgent information aloud so the team can respond  
   B. To hand off all records after the incident finishes  
   C. To assign an independent clinical diagnosis  
   D. To debrief after the emergency  

8. **What is a check-back?**  
   A. A private late entry into the chart  
   B. Repeating a measurement with different equipment  
   C. Repeating a received instruction and having the sender confirm it  
   D. Asking a patient to choose which staff member should respond  

9. **Which initial call-out is most useful in the opening case?**  
   A. The treatment has been running for a while and the patient says something feels wrong.  
   B. Urgent help at station 4. The patient has new chest pain and trouble breathing.  
   C. I know what caused it. Someone should check the chart later.  
   D. There is no machine alarm, but please visit this station when free.  

10. **The nurse assigns you to call the emergency team while another colleague stays at the station. What should you do?**  
   A. Reset the machine first.  
   B. Wait until you have a likely diagnosis to share.  
   C. Perform a different intervention from memory.  
   D. Confirm the instruction, activate the emergency response as directed and report back.  

11. **Once immediate care is underway, which documentation entry best supports handoff and review?**  
   A. 10:18 · Patient reported new chest pain and difficulty breathing · Charge nurse notified immediately · Emergency response activated at 10:19 · Assigned task and response documented.  
   B. Probable heart attack. No times entered.  
   C. Machine normal, therefore no event.  
   D. Called someone. Nothing further to record.  

12. **The emergency drill exposed confusion over who calls for help. What should your team contribute during debrief?**

   B. Change the clinical prescription so the scenario cannot recur.  
   C. Describe what happened, clarify the role assignment and agree on one improvement to test.  
   D. Omit the confusion because it occurred in a simulation.  

13. **Several stations on one water loop show an unexpected interruption. Why does the nurse or lead need to know which stations and patients are affected?**  
   A. The number of stations determines the diagnosis of each patient.  
   B. It helps the team identify the shared hazard, prioritize active patients and coordinate the contingency plan.  
   C. It lets each trainee independently decide whether to restart.  
   D. Station locations can replace all status documentation.  

14. **A power interruption occurs while multiple patients are receiving treatment. What should a trainee do?**  
   A. Assume all machines can continue without checking their status.  
   B. Move every patient before the clinical lead can assess the situation.  
   C. Reconnect or restart equipment as soon as a display lights up.  
   D. Notify the team, report current station and patient status, and assist under the site’s power-outage plan and clinical direction.  

15. **Water service returns after a disruption. Before preparing a station for treatment, what should you verify?**

   B. A tap runs clear for a few seconds.  
   C. A single monitor display has returned to normal.  
   D. The next patient agrees to restart.
