# Module 10 · Complications and Emergency Response

![A dialysis team reviews an emergency drill scenario at an unoccupied treatment station.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 6 hours classroom, 6 hours skills lab, 16 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Modules 10 and 12  
**Before you start** — Review equipment alarms, water exceptions, vascular access and session monitoring in Modules 6–9.

## Learning objectives

1. Recognize common dialysis complications and distinguish findings that require immediate emergency escalation.
2. Summon help and give a concise report of patient status, timing, access and equipment observations.
3. Carry out assigned support tasks during a simulation and document and debrief the event.
4. Recognize utility or facility disruptions and follow the hold, communication and authorized release pathway.

## Opening case · Sudden chest pain at the station

During a fictional dialysis session, Mr. Cruz says, “My chest hurts and I can’t catch my breath.” The symptoms started moments ago. He is still speaking but looks distressed. No machine alarm has sounded. Two team members are in the treatment area.

- What do you say and do first?
- What should you tell the responding nurse?
- Which tasks can you perform if assigned, and who coordinates the clinical response?

## 1. Recognize changes that matter

Different complications can produce similar symptoms. Use the symptoms, timing, observed circuit changes and measurements to make a factual report. The clinical team assesses the cause and directs treatment. Follow the unit’s procedure for emergency activation, and summon help before waiting for additional readings or charting when an emergency is apparent. [SA Health · Clinical guideline for hemodialysis complications](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/32dff291-573a-4d24-b3b7-afb5b47c4c00/Management%2Bof%2BComplications%2Bduring%2BHaemodialysis%2BCG%2BV1.pdf?MOD=AJPERES) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

| Finding or suspected complication | What to notice and report |
|---|---|
| **Falling blood pressure or possible intradialytic hypotension** | Report new dizziness, nausea, sweating, weakness or change in alertness and any available blood pressure trend promptly. Escalate immediately for severe symptoms, collapse or rapid deterioration. |
| **Muscle cramps** | Report onset, location, severity, accompanying symptoms and available readings. The nurse reviews the treatment and determines the response. |
| **Chest pain or breathlessness** | Summon help immediately. Give symptom onset and current status. A silent machine and an earlier normal reading leave the clinical concern unresolved. |
| **Fever, chills or suspected infection** | Notify the nurse promptly. Report timing, available temperature, access symptoms and whether other patients have similar findings. Use the emergency pathway for severe illness or deterioration. |
| **Access hemorrhage, disconnected blood line or venous needle dislodgement** | Treat visible dislodgement or heavy bleeding as an immediate emergency. Give the location and what you see. An alarm may miss some dislodgements. |
| **Possible hemolysis or another circuit or dialysate emergency** | Alert the team immediately for a concerning combination such as sudden chest or back pain, breathlessness, nausea or a new unusual bloodline appearance. Report shared water alerts or symptoms at more than one station. |
| **Possible air embolism or other sudden cardiopulmonary event** | Treat abrupt severe breathlessness, chest pain, altered consciousness or a visible air or line concern as an emergency. Report exactly what you observed in the patient and circuit. |
| **Possible severe allergic reaction** | Sudden wheeze, difficulty breathing, facial or throat swelling, widespread hives or collapse requires immediate escalation. Report timing and any recent exposures known to you. |

Machine actions and blood-circuit decisions can differ by event and equipment. The authorized team follows the facility emergency procedure and the machine instructions for use. Follow only the actions assigned to you and for which you are trained. [UK Kidney Association · Hemodialysis clinical practice guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6798406/) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Model-specific operator manuals](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/operators-manuals-hemodialysis-hd/)

## 2. First response and team roles

![Role card showing how to summon help, give the station and patient status, support the assigned team role, and hand off for documentation.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Emergency role card. The sequence below provides a text equivalent. Learn your unit’s activation method and assigned role before each placement.*

1. **Summon help immediately for serious signs.** State where you are, what you observed and that help is needed now. Use the unit’s emergency call process and follow your assignment for emergency medical services activation.
2. **Give a short handoff.** Provide the patient’s location, symptoms, onset, connection status and any findings already available. Identify any visible access or circuit change and quote machine or water messages if known. Gathering measurements should never delay the emergency call.
3. **Stay available for the assigned task.** The team lead directs assessment, treatment changes, circuit decisions, emergency equipment use and coordination with external responders. A technician may summon help, bring equipment, guide responders, relay observations or assist in resuscitation within current training and assignment. If cardiac arrest is suspected, activate the response and follow current BLS training and the unit protocol.
4. **Use closed-loop communication.** Repeat the task you received and report when it is complete or cannot be completed. The team lead tracks what has happened and what still needs action. [AHRQ · TeamSTEPPS communication tools](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/resources/modules/index.html) · [AHA · Adult BLS algorithm for healthcare professionals](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-BLS-Adult-Healthcare-250701.pdf)

**Case debrief.** Call for immediate clinical help at the station and activate the unit response as required. A concise report is “At Station 4, Mr. Cruz suddenly reports chest pain and difficulty breathing. The symptoms started just now. He is connected and speaking but looks distressed. I haven’t seen an alarm. Please come now.” Follow the team lead’s instructions and relay any change. The clinical team determines the cause and treatment.

## 3. Document and debrief

During the response, record only what you are assigned to capture. Take care of immediate actions first. Document observed symptoms, onset or discovery time, patient and connection status, available measurements with times, machine messages, notifications, actions within role and the next handoff. Preserve equipment information, logs or supplies as directed by the unit. Follow the facility process for incident reporting and equipment review. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Example record.** “10:24 · At Station 4, patient said, ‘My chest hurts and I can’t catch my breath.’ Patient connected and speaking. No alarm was observed at that time. Called emergency response and notified charge nurse at 10:24. Remained available for assigned support. At 10:25, directed responders to Station 4.”

A debrief reviews how the event was detected, how quickly help was called, whether roles were understood and what could improve the next response. Record one action, the team member responsible and how the team will check completion. Use approved de-identified data in training.

## 4. Respond to utility or facility disruptions

A patient emergency may affect one station. A shared failure can affect multiple stations at once. Report promptly if several machines alarm together, a water check fails, power is interrupted or an evacuation instruction is given. Learn the incident lead, contact numbers, contingency plans and release authority at your site. [CDC · Water use in dialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/recommendations-resources/water-use-in-dialysis.html) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water monitoring](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036)

| Disruption | What the technician should support |
|---|---|
| **Water outage, water safety alert or suspected contamination** | Hold affected new starts and alert the charge nurse and water-system lead. Report whether patients are already connected and which stations may be affected. Connected patients require immediate coordination through the unit’s clinical contingency process. |
| **Power failure or unstable backup power** | Report the affected areas and observed machine status. Hold affected new starts and follow the site emergency plan under the incident lead. Backup support and alarms vary by equipment and facility. |
| **Fire, earthquake, flood or evacuation** | Use the facility alert and evacuation procedure, identify the affected area and route responders as directed. The incident lead coordinates patient prioritization, safe relocation and transfer. |
| **Proposed return to service** | Verify that equipment, utilities and patient assignments have been cleared by the designated leads according to unit policy. A restored screen or utility alone does not establish readiness. Record the authorized release and who made it. |

**Tabletop variation.** While one patient is connected, water alarms appear at neighboring machines and the next shift is preparing to start. Give the charge nurse and designated technical lead the affected location, number of connected patients if known, exact alert and what is on hold. Identify who directs management of connected patients and who documents water and equipment clearance. [CDC · Emergency water supply planning guide](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/media/pdfs/2024/07/emergency-water-supply-planning-guide-2019-508.pdf)
