# Module 05 · Dialysis Infection Prevention and Waste Management

![A clinician performs hand hygiene beside an unoccupied dialysis station.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 6 hours classroom, 6 hours skills lab, 14 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Module 10  
**Before you start** — Review the technician’s scope and escalation pathway in Module 1.

## Learning objectives

1. Apply Standard Precautions, hand hygiene and task-appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) in dialysis.
2. Separate clean and used items and verify safe station turnover, including wet contact time.
3. Segregate waste at the point of use and respond to an occupational blood exposure.
4. Recognize when additional precautions apply, escalate breaches, and document observations for infection surveillance and quality improvement.

## Opening case · The cuff looks clean

It is Wednesday, 11:20, the changeover between the morning and midday shifts. Mr. Nestor Aquino, 67, a fictional patient, has just left Station 3. The reusable blood pressure cuff used during his treatment lies on the chair. Your coworker, Ms. Grace Domingo, picks it up to use on the next patient at Station 4. The cuff looks clean. Station 3 has been wiped with disinfectant, but its required wet contact time is still running. At Station 4, Mrs. Carmen Flores is ready to sit down.

- What would you do with the cuff?
- When can Station 3 be released?
- What would you report if the cuff had already touched the clean supply cart at Station 4?

## 1. Standard Precautions in the dialysis unit

Think of how a cold spreads through a household. Nobody sees it pass, yet it moves from hand to doorknob to remote control to the next hand. A dialysis unit has the same paths, with one difference that raises the stakes: blood is handled at every station, every shift. Hands, shared equipment, and nearby surfaces can carry blood or microorganisms from one station to the next.

Standard Precautions apply to every patient, whether or not an infection is known. Each one breaks a link in that chain: hand hygiene, PPE chosen for the task, safe sharps practice, equipment disinfection, and keeping clean work apart from contaminated work. Follow your unit’s policies for the specific tasks you are authorized to perform. [CDC · Core infection prevention practices](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/core-practices/index.html) · [CDC · Dialysis infection control](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/infection-control/index.html)

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Before touching the patient or beginning an authorized clean or aseptic task | Perform hand hygiene. For an aseptic task, follow the unit’s procedure and your assigned scope. |
| Handling a patient’s equipment or potentially contaminated items | Wear the gloves the unit requires. Remove them after the task, and perform hand hygiene before you move to a clean task or another station. |
| After touching patient surroundings, after removing gloves, or after contact with blood or body fluids | Perform hand hygiene at the point in the workflow where transfer could happen. Use soap and water when hands are visibly soiled. Use approved alcohol-based hand rub for routine moments when it is appropriate. |
| Task may splash blood or body fluids | Choose gloves, protective clothing, and protection for the eyes, nose, and mouth as the procedure and the splash risk require. Check the disinfectant label and safety data sheet for protection specific to that chemical. |

**Common transition.** After you handle used bloodlines, remove your gloves and perform hand hygiene before you touch clean stock, the charting keyboard outside the station, or the next patient’s equipment. Gloves carry contamination just as hands do. The practical point is that changing gloves without cleaning your hands moves the problem, not removes it. [CDC · Clinical hand hygiene](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/hcp/clinical-safety/) · [CDC · Hand hygiene in hemodialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/media/pdfs/Hemodialysis-Hand-Hygiene-Observations-P.pdf)

## 2. Keep clean supplies separate and turn over stations safely

Clean and used should never meet. Store clean supplies away from used equipment and contaminated surfaces. Take to a station only what that patient needs. Once an item has entered the station, unit policy decides whether it is dedicated to that patient, discarded, or cleaned and disinfected before reuse. Keep clean stock and medication preparation in designated clean areas. Used equipment, used PPE, and specimens leave by the facility’s designated routes. [CDC · Preventing transmission in chronic hemodialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/PREVIEW/MMWRHTML/rr5005a1.htm)

![Turnover sequence showing separation of used items, cleaning and disinfection, wet contact time, and release for the next patient.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Station turnover. The sequence below gives the text version.*

1. **Wait until the patient has left the station.** Keep the next patient’s equipment and supplies outside the used area until then.
2. **Remove and separate used items.** Handle bloodlines, dialyzers, sharps, and other waste by the facility’s procedure. Keep reusable equipment in the designated used area until it is reprocessed.
3. **Clean and disinfect the station and shared equipment.** Follow the facility-approved product label and the equipment instructions. Include the high-touch surfaces that apply, such as the chair, the outside of the machine, the side table, and the controls. Remove visible soil by the approved procedure. A blood spill, or a surface visibly contaminated with blood, may need a separate protocol.
4. **Complete the wet contact time and the release checks.** The disinfectant must stay visibly wet for the full time on the product label. If a surface dries too early or coverage was incomplete, follow the product and unit instructions and confirm completion before release. Let surfaces dry, or meet any other product and unit requirement, before use. Place clean supplies only after release.

**Cleaning** removes soil and organic material. **Disinfection** reduces or inactivates microorganisms when a compatible product is used as directed. The two are different steps, and the second does not work well without the first. A reusable cuff looks exactly the same before and after many contamination events, so how it looks cannot tell you whether it is ready. [CDC · Dialysis station disinfection checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/media/pdfs/env-checklist-508.pdf) · [CDC · Disinfection of healthcare equipment](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/disinfection-sterilization/healthcare-equipment.html)

**Case debrief.**

**What do you do with the cuff?** Stop the transfer, kindly and at once. Ask Ms. Domingo to put the cuff down. It goes to the designated used-equipment area, to be cleaned and disinfected according to the cuff instructions and with a facility-approved product. If Station 4 needs a cuff, offer one that has been released.

**When can Station 3 be released?** Only when the required contact time and every release check are complete. Until then it stays on hold, however ready the next patient is.

**What if the cuff touched the clean cart?** Then the cart is no longer clean. Prevent use of any affected equipment or supplies, tell the supervisor, and follow the unit’s instructions for containing and documenting the breach.

## 3. Separate waste at the point of use and act promptly after exposure

Waste is easiest to sort at the moment you create it. Check the posted waste guide for the correct container, label, and closure procedure. Learn sorting with the unit’s current segregation chart and simulated items. The Philippine Department of Health (DOH) facility rules and health care waste manual give the local framework. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [Philippine DOH · Health Care Waste Management Manual, 4th ed.](https://www.washinhcf.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/DOH-Health-Care-Waste-Management-Manual_4th-Edition_FINAL.pdf)

| Item | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Used needle or other contaminated sharp | Straight into the designated puncture-resistant sharps container at the point of use. Avoid routine recapping, bending, or removing a needle by hand. Replace a container at its marked fill line, as unit procedure states. |
| Blood-contaminated disposable material, such as used bloodlines or dressings | Into the designated infectious waste stream, as facility policy states. A dialyzer approved for facility reprocessing goes through the controlled used-equipment pathway instead. |
| Uncontaminated packaging from a designated clean area | Into the appropriate general or recycling stream, as the posted policy states. Items already taken into a treatment station follow the unit’s rules for that station. |
| Residual chemical product or disinfectant | Follow the product information and the approved hazardous waste procedure. Ask the designated lead if the route is unclear. |

**If a needlestick, cut, or blood splash occurs:** Wash a needlestick or cut promptly with soap and water. Flush splashes to the nose, mouth, or skin with water. Irrigate the eyes with clean water, saline, or a sterile irrigant. Then report at once through the unit’s exposure pathway, and seek urgent medical or occupational health evaluation. Time matters here, because some protective measures depend on how soon they start. The designated clinician assesses the risk of bloodborne infection and decides on testing and any time-sensitive measures. Record the time, the route of exposure, and the notifications in the approved system. [CDC/NIOSH · Bloodborne infectious disease risk factors and exposure response](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/topics/bbp/default.html)

## 4. Additional precautions, surveillance and escalation

Some patients need more than Standard Precautions. Follow the unit’s instructions for any added precautions and dedicated assignments. For a patient with confirmed hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) positivity, CDC dialysis guidance describes a designated dialysis area or room, dedicated equipment and supplies, and staff assignments that do not share personnel with susceptible patients during the same shift. The clinical and infection prevention teams interpret hepatitis results, apply local requirements, and manage vaccination and surveillance. Keep results confidential, and ask the supervising nurse to clarify an assignment before you act on it. [CDC · Preventing hepatitis B transmission in hemodialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/PREVIEW/MMWRHTML/rr5005a1.htm)

A breach is any moment when a barrier fails: a cuff crossing between stations, a sharps container reaching its fill line, a skipped hand hygiene moment, or a patient seated before turnover is complete. Pause the unsafe transition, contain the affected item or area, and tell the supervising nurse or the infection prevention lead as policy states. If a patient reports fever or chills, or you see signs of a possible access infection, alert the nurse promptly. The clinical team evaluates the findings and decides on any surveillance reporting. [CDC · Core interventions for bloodstream infection prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/)

**Record facts and support improvement.** Write down the item, the time, the area affected, what happened, the action taken, and whom you told. Use the facility’s approved incident reporting system. A single report fixes one moment. Many reports, read together, show the unit where its workflow breaks down. Unit surveillance can include bloodstream infection trends, hand hygiene observations, access care audits, and staff competency checks. For practice, count completed hand hygiene actions out of the observed opportunities and discuss where the workflow failed. Use authorized de-identified records for training. [CDC · Core interventions and surveillance](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/) · [CDC · Dialysis assessment and response tool](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/php/dart-toolkit/index.html)
