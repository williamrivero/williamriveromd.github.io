# Module 05 · Dialysis Infection Prevention and Waste Management

![A clinician performs hand hygiene beside an unoccupied dialysis station.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 6 hours classroom, 6 hours skills lab, 14 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Module 10  
**Before you start** — Review the technician’s scope and escalation pathway in Module 1.

## Learning objectives

1. Apply Standard Precautions, hand hygiene and task-appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) in dialysis.
2. Separate clean and used items and verify safe station turnover, including wet contact time.
3. Segregate waste at the point of use and respond to an occupational blood exposure.
4. Recognize when additional precautions apply, escalate breaches, and document observations for infection surveillance and quality improvement.

## Opening case · The cuff looks clean

A patient has left Station 3. A reusable blood pressure cuff used during that treatment is on the chair. A coworker reaches for it to use on the next patient at Station 4. The cuff looks clean. Station 3 has been wiped with disinfectant, but its required wet contact time is still running. The next patient is ready to sit down.

- What would you do with the cuff?
- When can Station 3 be released?
- What would you report if the cuff had already touched the clean supply cart at Station 4?

## 1. Standard Precautions in the dialysis unit

Standard Precautions apply to every patient. During hemodialysis, hands, shared equipment and nearby surfaces can carry blood or microorganisms between stations. Hand hygiene, task-appropriate PPE, safe sharps practices, equipment disinfection and separation of clean and contaminated work interrupt this transfer. Use unit policies for the specific tasks you are authorized to perform. [CDC · Core infection prevention practices](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/core-practices/index.html) · [CDC · Dialysis infection control](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/infection-control/index.html)

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Before touching the patient or beginning an authorized clean or aseptic task | Perform hand hygiene. For an aseptic activity, follow the unit’s procedure and your assigned scope. |
| Handling a patient’s equipment or potentially contaminated items | Wear the gloves required by the unit. Remove them after the task and perform hand hygiene before moving to a clean task or another station. |
| After touching patient surroundings, after removing gloves, or after contact with blood or body fluids | Perform hand hygiene at the point in the workflow where transfer could occur. Use soap and water when hands are visibly soiled. Use approved alcohol-based hand rub for routine indications when appropriate. |
| Task may splash blood or body fluids | Select gloves, protective clothing, and protection for the eyes, nose and mouth as indicated by the procedure and splash risk. Check the disinfectant label and safety data sheet for chemical-specific protection. |

**Common transition.** After handling used bloodlines, remove gloves and perform hand hygiene before handling clean stock, the charting keyboard outside the station, or the next patient’s equipment. Gloves can carry contamination just like hands. [CDC · Clinical hand hygiene](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/hcp/clinical-safety/) · [CDC · Hand hygiene in hemodialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/media/pdfs/Hemodialysis-Hand-Hygiene-Observations-P.pdf)

## 2. Keep clean supplies separate and turn over stations safely

Store clean supplies away from used equipment and contaminated surfaces. Take only what the patient needs to their station. Once an item enters the station, follow unit policy for dedication to that patient, disposal, or cleaning and disinfection before reuse. Keep clean stock and medication preparation in designated clean areas. Used equipment, used PPE and specimens follow the facility’s designated routes. [CDC · Preventing transmission in chronic hemodialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/PREVIEW/MMWRHTML/rr5005a1.htm)

![Turnover sequence showing separation of used items, cleaning and disinfection, wet contact time, and release for the next patient.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Station turnover. The following sequence provides the text version.*

1. **Wait until the patient has left the station.** Keep the next patient’s equipment and supplies outside the used area.
2. **Remove and segregate used items.** Handle bloodlines, dialyzers, sharps and other waste under the facility’s procedure. Keep reusable equipment in the designated used area until reprocessed.
3. **Clean and disinfect the station and shared equipment.** Follow the facility-approved product label and equipment instructions. Include applicable high-touch surfaces such as the chair, machine exterior, side table and controls. Remove visible soil under the approved procedure. A blood spill or a surface visibly contaminated with blood may require a separate protocol.
4. **Complete wet contact time and release checks.** The disinfectant must remain visibly wet for the full time on the product label. If a surface dries too early or coverage is incomplete, follow the product and unit instructions and verify completion before release. Allow surfaces to dry or meet any other product and unit requirements before use. Place clean supplies only after release.

**Cleaning** removes soil and organic material. **Disinfection** reduces or inactivates microorganisms when a compatible product is used as directed. A reusable cuff looks the same before and after many contamination events, so visual appearance alone cannot establish readiness. [CDC · Dialysis station disinfection checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/media/pdfs/env-checklist-508.pdf) · [CDC · Disinfection of healthcare equipment](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/disinfection-sterilization/healthcare-equipment.html)

**Case debrief.** Stop the transfer. Keep the used cuff in the designated used-equipment area for cleaning and disinfection using the cuff instructions and facility-approved product. Offer a released cuff if the next station needs one. Keep Station 3 on hold until the required contact time and all release checks are complete. If the used cuff touched the clean cart, prevent use of affected equipment or supplies, notify the supervisor, and follow the unit’s instructions for containing and documenting the breach.

## 3. Separate waste at the point of use and act promptly after exposure

Check the posted waste guide for the correct container, label and closure procedure. Teach sorting with the unit’s current segregation chart and simulated items. The Philippine Department of Health (DOH) facility rules and health care waste manual provide the local framework. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [Philippine DOH · Health Care Waste Management Manual, 4th ed.](https://www.washinhcf.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/DOH-Health-Care-Waste-Management-Manual_4th-Edition_FINAL.pdf)

| Item | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Used needle or other contaminated sharp | Immediately into the designated puncture-resistant sharps container at the point of use. Avoid routine recapping, bending or manual removal. Replace a container at its marked fill line per unit procedure. |
| Blood-contaminated disposable material, such as used bloodlines or dressings | Into the designated infectious waste stream according to facility policy. Route any dialyzer approved for facility reprocessing through the controlled used-equipment pathway. |
| Uncontaminated packaging from a designated clean area | Into the appropriate general or recycling stream according to the posted policy. Apply the unit’s rules to items already taken into a treatment station. |
| Residual chemical product or disinfectant | Follow the product information and approved hazardous waste procedure. Ask the designated lead if the route is unclear. |

**If a needlestick, cut or blood splash occurs:** Wash a needlestick or cut promptly with soap and water. Flush nose, mouth or skin splashes with water. Irrigate eyes with clean water, saline or sterile irrigant. Report immediately through the unit’s exposure pathway and seek urgent medical or occupational health evaluation. Assessment of bloodborne infection risk and decisions about testing or time-sensitive post-exposure measures belong to the designated clinician. Record the time, exposure route and notifications through the approved system. [CDC/NIOSH · Bloodborne infectious disease risk factors and exposure response](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/topics/bbp/default.html)

## 4. Additional precautions, surveillance and escalation

Follow the unit’s instructions for any added precautions and dedicated assignments. For a patient with confirmed hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) positivity, CDC dialysis guidance describes a designated dialysis area or room, dedicated equipment and supplies, and staff assignments that avoid sharing personnel with susceptible patients during the same shift. The clinical and infection prevention teams interpret hepatitis results, apply local requirements, and handle vaccination and surveillance. Keep results confidential and ask the supervising nurse to clarify assignments before use. [CDC · Preventing hepatitis B transmission in hemodialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/MMWR/PREVIEW/MMWRHTML/rr5005a1.htm)

A breach might involve a cuff crossing stations, a sharps container reaching its fill line, skipped hand hygiene or a patient seating before turnover is complete. Pause the unsafe transition, contain the affected item or area, and notify the supervising nurse or infection prevention lead according to policy. If a patient reports fever or chills or you observe possible access infection, alert the nurse promptly. The clinical team evaluates the findings and determines any surveillance reporting. [CDC · Core interventions for bloodstream infection prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/)

**Record facts and support improvement.** Note the item, time, area affected, what happened, the action taken, and whom you notified. Use the facility’s approved incident reporting system. Unit surveillance can include bloodstream infection trends, hand hygiene observations, access care audits and staff competency checks. For practice, tally completed hand hygiene actions out of observed opportunities and discuss where the workflow broke down. Use authorized de-identified records for training. [CDC · Core interventions and surveillance](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/) · [CDC · Dialysis assessment and response tool](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/php/dart-toolkit/index.html)
