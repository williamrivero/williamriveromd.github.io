# Module 12 · Self-Assessment

Choose one best answer per question. Score one point per correct response. Suggested mastery is 12 of 15. Review missed safety decisions about readiness holds, urgent escalation or supervision even if you reach the suggested score.

1. **Which information should you compare before matching a patient to a dialysis order?**  
   A. Station number and nearest opening  
   B. The current shift and the prior patient’s settings  
   C. Two approved patient identifiers and the current authorized record  
   D. The machine asset number and treatment duration  

2. **What does a dialysis water log record?**  
   A. Assigned measurements, sampling details, results and any required action or release status  
   B. The patient’s fluid restriction alone  
   C. Only the name of the dialysis machine manufacturer  
   D. Treatment completion time in place of results  

3. **The station is clean and the equipment checks passed, but a required water result is marked pending. How should this affect readiness?**  
   A. The station can be cleared because the machine passed.  
   B. The patient may decide to proceed.  
   C. Cleanliness substitutes for the water result.  
   D. The affected station has an unresolved readiness requirement and remains on hold under site policy.  

4. **At shift start, the water check remains unresolved and a patient identifier does not match the order. What is the safest response?**  
   A. Resolve the patient identification issue, then ignore the water result if the patient is waiting.  
   B. Hold the affected treatment start and report both discrepancies to the responsible staff for separate verification.  
   C. Use the dialysis order if the patient recognizes the station.  
   D. Move the patient without confirming whether the alternate station is cleared.  

5. **A machine passes its self-test, but the station disinfectant has not reached the required wet contact time. What should the trainee do?**  
   A. Keep the station on hold until the required turnover procedure is complete and documented.  
   B. Bring the patient in and complete turnover around them.  
   C. Record the station as ready based on the machine test.  
   D. Move clean supplies onto the wet work surface.  

6. **Which communication technique has the receiver repeat an instruction and the sender confirm or correct it?**  
   A. The preventive maintenance check  
   B. A near-miss review  
   C. Teach-back for patient education  
   D. Check-back  

7. **Which report gives the receiving nurse an actionable situation and request?**  
   A. The assignment is confusing. See the record.  
   B. Someone may have checked the patient already.  
   C. Station 2 is on hold because the identifier differs from the order and the water result is pending. Please confirm who will verify each exception.  
   D. Everything is normal because the tests probably passed.  

8. **A preceptor says to hold a station until authorized release. The trainee repeats the instruction, but the preceptor has not confirmed. What remains incomplete?**  
   A. The patient’s post-treatment weight  
   B. The communication loop, which needs confirmation or correction  
   C. The date of the next technical inspection  
   D. The patient’s authorization to change the prescription  

9. **During a mock treatment, the patient reports sudden chest pain and breathlessness. No alarm is active. What should you do first?**  
   A. Call for immediate clinical help using the unit procedure, giving the station and observed symptoms.  
   B. Wait for an alarm to verify an emergency.  
   C. Complete the treatment log before calling.  
   D. Use a previous prescription to adjust the treatment.  

10. **The nurse asks you to notify the water lead while the nurse verifies the patient identifiers. What action best closes the handoff?**  
   A. Assume someone else notified the water lead.  
   B. Record the assignment and leave the area without informing anyone.  
   C. Clear both holds when the water lead replies.  
   D. Confirm the assignment, notify the water lead and report back with the response and any pending action.  

11. **What is a near miss?**  
   A. An event that necessarily caused patient injury  
   B. A risk that can be excluded from discussion  
   C. A safety event intercepted before it reached the patient  
   D. An authorized change made to the prescription  

12. **Which proposed outcome would best show whether a new pre-use water-readiness checklist is being followed?**  
   A. The number of shifts with a completed and verified water-readiness entry before first treatment divided by all shifts audited  
   B. The number of chairs on the treatment floor  
   C. The age of the oldest machine  
   D. The number of people who say the checklist looks helpful  

13. **A learner passed the written quiz but missed a required critical step in the simulated emergency station. What does that mean for skills sign-off?**  
   A. The written score automatically counts as practical competency.  
   B. The learner needs feedback and an observed reassessment of the critical step before sign-off under the course rubric.  
   C. Another learner can sign off in their place.  
   D. The critical step can be waived because the problem was simulated.  

14. **A trainee has observed a procedure but has not been signed off for it. A colleague asks them to perform it without the assigned supervisor. What should the trainee do?**  
   A. Agree if the procedure was demonstrated recently.  
   B. Ask another trainee to watch.  
   C. Perform it if the patient agrees.  
   D. Clarify authority and supervision with the assigned preceptor and remain within the approved role.  

15. **The identifier mismatch is caught before treatment. Which entry and follow-up best support improvement?**  
   A. Record that nothing happened and leave the issue out of the debrief.  
   B. Enter a diagnosis in the chart to justify the delay.  
   C. Record the observed mismatch, time, hold, notification and outcome in the approved system, then contribute facts to the near-miss review.  
   D. Identify a person to blame before gathering the sequence of events.
