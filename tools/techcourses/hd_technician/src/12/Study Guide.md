# Module 12 · Integrated Practice and Competency Assessment

![A preceptor observes a trainee during a simulated dialysis station check.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 2 hours classroom, 4 hours skills lab, 22 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Modules 1, 6 and 9–12  
**Before you start** — Review the case debriefs and critical checklist steps in Modules 1–11.

## Learning objectives

1. Verify patient, station, equipment and utility readiness and hold affected work when an exception remains unresolved.
2. Recognize urgent changes, escalate to the appropriate team and use check-back to confirm instructions.
3. Record objective findings in the right record and use a near-miss review to identify a testable improvement.
4. Demonstrate assigned competencies across five simulation stations and distinguish observation, supervised practice and formal sign-off.

## Opening case · Two holds at shift start

At 05:55, a trainee is assigned to a morning shift. The designated pre-use water check has an out-of-range result, and the log shows no authorized release for the affected system. At one station, the patient states two approved identifiers. One does not match the open treatment record. No patient is connected to the affected system. A colleague offers to reopen the chart while another makes a call about the water result.

- Which activities should be paused? Who needs to know about each exception?
- What facts belong in the water log and the patient identification report?
- If the chart mismatch is resolved first, what still has to happen before this station is ready?

## 1. Verify readiness and close each hold

A station is ready when all applicable checks are complete. A clean chair, a normal machine display or a corrected chart resolves only its own part of readiness. Use the facility’s checklist and report discrepancies before the affected work proceeds. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water monitoring](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036) · [WHO · Patient identification](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/patient-safety/patient-safety-solutions/ps-solution2-patient-identification.pdf)

| Readiness area | Verify within assigned scope | If unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| **Patient and order** | Use two approved identifiers, the authorized record and current treatment information. | Pause patient-specific work and notify the supervising nurse. Confirm the correct record and order through the approved process. |
| **Station and equipment** | Check turnover, contact time, service status, supplies and required pre-use tests. | Withhold the affected station or device and follow the facility’s clearance process. |
| **Water and dialysate** | Verify required system checks and release status and confirm assigned dialysate checks. | Hold affected starts and notify the nurse and designated technical or water-system lead. Report connected-patient status immediately. |
| **Completion and handoff** | Verify assigned measurements and records and identify any open issue. | Give a specific handoff, confirm ownership and document the next action or pending clearance. |

**Case debrief.** Hold starts dependent on the affected water system and hold patient-specific work at the station with the identifier mismatch. Alert the supervising nurse to both issues and notify the designated water-system lead. Report the water reading, units, sample point, time, affected system and connection status. Explain the identifier discrepancy discreetly without using the unverified chart to record patient information. The nurse’s resolution of the patient mismatch still leaves the water hold open. Resume work only when the required clinical and technical verifications and release are documented.

## 2. Recognize urgency and communicate the next action

An unresolved readiness check warrants a hold and timely notification. New severe symptoms, visible dislodgement, heavy bleeding or a shared utility hazard during active treatment may require immediate emergency activation. Follow the unit’s procedure and remain within current training and assigned scope. Summon help before collecting additional data when delay would increase risk. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility emergency and staffing requirements](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Example handoff.** “First-shift starts are on hold for the affected water system. The 05:55 result is above the action level, and the log has the actual result, sample point and units. No patient is connected. I also held Station 2’s patient-specific setup because one approved identifier did not match the open record. Please confirm who will resolve each hold and where clearance will be recorded.” Repeat back assigned actions and identify who owns each decision. [AHRQ · TeamSTEPPS, including SBAR and check-back](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/resources/modules/index.html)

## 3. Document and improve the process

Keep the record factual. Record what happened, when, where, what was held, who was notified and what action or clearance followed. Preserve the original facts using the approved correction and incident procedures. Use confidential reporting channels. A water log captures the actual water result and disposition. An identification issue belongs in the unit’s approved reporting pathway, with a patient record entry only when policy and the verified record permit it. [AHRQ · Reporting patient safety events](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/reporting-patient-safety-events)

**Near-miss review.** If staff intercepted the wrong record before setup and no harm occurred, follow the facility’s near-miss reporting process. The water failure may also require a separate quality or incident entry. Review what helped detect the problems and what made it possible to approach setup while verification remained open. An example improvement is a shift-start checklist that records water release and patient ID as separate completed checks. A designated lead can review the proportion of sampled starts with both recorded before setup or treatment, as applicable, and share the findings with the team. [AHRQ · Responding to patient safety events](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/responding-patient-safety-events) · [CDC · Dialysis infection prevention and surveillance](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/)

## 4. Practical assessment · Five simulation stations

![Five-station capstone assessment map covering turnover, equipment readiness, water quality, patient and access handoff, and emergency response.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Practical assessment map. The scoring grid provides a text equivalent and observable criteria.*

**Setup for assessors** — Use inactive equipment or models, approved local checklists, mock logs, labels and orders, and a scripted patient. Provide the same exercise instructions and test conditions to all trainees. Include one deliberate exception per station. Enter a date, evaluator and scenario version for each attempt.

**Scoring** — Mark each criterion **S** for satisfactory, **P** for needs practice or **N/O** for not observed. Mark a station satisfactory only after all its criteria are demonstrated satisfactorily. If a critical action is missed, stop the unsafe simulation, record the observed behavior, give targeted feedback and retest that station using a parallel scenario. Complete any unobserved criteria before sign-off.

## Clinical logbook and completion review

Complete the log during placement and verify entries with the preceptor. Use only approved de-identified information. Record whether the activity was **observed**, **simulated** or **performed under supervision**. Preceptor verification records what took place. Formal authorization for particular patient-facing tasks follows the facility’s requirements.
