# Module 12 · Integrated Practice and Competency Assessment

![A preceptor observes a trainee during a simulated dialysis station check.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 2 hours classroom, 4 hours skills lab, 22 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Modules 1, 6 and 9–12  
**Before you start** — Review the case debriefs and critical checklist steps in Modules 1–11.

## Learning objectives

1. Confirm that the patient, station, equipment, and utilities are ready, and hold the affected work while any exception stays open.
2. Recognize urgent changes, escalate to the right team, and use check-back to confirm the instructions you receive.
3. Record objective findings in the right record, and use a near-miss review to find an improvement that can be tested.
4. Show your assigned competencies across five simulation stations, and tell observation, supervised practice, and formal sign-off apart.

## Opening case · Two holds at shift start

It is Monday at 05:55. Mr. Mark Villanueva, 24, a trainee, is assigned to the morning shift. The designated pre-use water check has an out-of-range result, and the log shows no authorized release for the affected system. At Station 2, the first patient states two approved identifiers, and one of them does not match the open treatment record. No patient is connected to the affected system. One colleague offers to reopen the chart, while another is on the telephone about the water result.

- Which activities should stop now, and who needs to know about each problem?
- Which facts go into the water log, and which into the patient identification report?
- If the chart mismatch is sorted out first, what still has to happen before Station 2 is ready?

## 1. Verify readiness and close each hold

Think of a passenger plane before departure. Fuel, crew, doors, cargo, and weather are each checked and signed off by someone. A clean cabin tells you nothing about the fuel. The picture has a limit. An airline has a dispatcher who sees every check on one screen. On a dialysis floor the checks are held by different people, so you name each hold, and each owner, out loud.

A station is ready only when every applicable check is complete. A clean chair, a normal machine display, or a corrected chart settles only its own part of readiness. Use the facility’s checklist, and report each discrepancy before the affected work goes on. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water monitoring](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036) · [WHO · Patient identification](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/patient-safety/patient-safety-solutions/ps-solution2-patient-identification.pdf)

| Readiness area | Verify within assigned scope | If unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| **Patient and order** | Use two approved identifiers, the authorized record, and the current treatment information. | Stop patient-specific work and tell the supervising nurse. Confirm the correct record and order through the approved process. |
| **Station and equipment** | Check turnover, contact time, service status, supplies, and the required pre-use tests. | Withhold the affected station or device, and follow the facility’s clearance process. |
| **Water and dialysate** | Confirm the required system checks and release status, and the dialysate checks assigned to you. | Hold the affected starts, and tell the nurse and the designated technical or water-system lead. Report at once whether any patient is connected. |
| **Completion and handoff** | Confirm the assigned measurements and records, and name any open issue. | Give a specific handoff, confirm who owns each next step, and record the next action or the clearance still pending. |

**Case debrief.** *What stops, and who knows.* Two separate holds start at once. Hold every start that depends on the affected water system. Hold patient-specific work at Station 2, where the identifier does not match. Tell the supervising nurse about both problems, and tell the designated water-system lead about the water result.

*What goes where.* The water log gets the reading, units, sample point, time, affected system, and connection status. Explain the identifier problem quietly and privately, and do not use the unverified chart to record anything about the patient.

*If the chart is fixed first.* The nurse resolving the identity mismatch closes one hold only. The water hold stays open. Resume work only when the required clinical and technical checks, and the release, are documented.

## 2. Recognize urgency and communicate the next action

Not every problem is an emergency, and the response should match. An open readiness check calls for a hold and a timely report. New severe symptoms, visible dislodgement, heavy bleeding, or a shared utility hazard during active treatment may call for emergency activation at once. Follow the unit’s procedure and stay within your current training and assigned scope. When waiting would increase the risk, call for help before you collect more information. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility emergency and staffing requirements](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Example handoff.** “First-shift starts are on hold for the affected water system. The 05:55 result is above the action level, and the log has the actual result, sample point and units. No patient is connected. I also held Station 2’s patient-specific setup because one approved identifier did not match the open record. Please confirm who will resolve each hold and where clearance will be recorded.” Notice that each hold has its own sentence and its own question about ownership. Repeat back the actions you are assigned, and name who owns each decision. [AHRQ · TeamSTEPPS, including SBAR and check-back](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/resources/modules/index.html)

## 3. Document and improve the process

Keep the record factual: what happened, when, where, what was held, who was told, and what action or clearance followed. Keep the original facts intact by using the approved correction and incident procedures. Use the confidential reporting channels. The water log holds the actual water result and what was done about it. An identification problem goes through the unit’s approved reporting pathway, with an entry in the patient record only when policy and the verified record allow it. [AHRQ · Reporting patient safety events](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/reporting-patient-safety-events)

**Near-miss review.** A near miss is an error caught before it reached the patient. If staff caught the wrong record before setup and no one was harmed, follow the facility’s near-miss reporting process. The water failure may also need its own quality or incident entry. In the review, ask what helped the team catch both problems, and what made it possible to get close to setup while checks were still open. One example of an improvement is a shift-start checklist that records water release and patient identification as two separate completed checks. A designated lead can then review the proportion of sampled starts that had both recorded before setup or treatment, as applicable, and share the findings with the team. [AHRQ · Responding to patient safety events](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/responding-patient-safety-events) · [CDC · Dialysis infection prevention and surveillance](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/clinical-safety/)

## 4. Practical assessment · Five simulation stations

![Five-station capstone assessment map covering turnover, equipment readiness, water quality, patient and access handoff, and emergency response.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Practical assessment map. The scoring grid provides a text equivalent and observable criteria.*

**Setup for assessors** — Use inactive equipment or models, approved local checklists, mock logs, labels and orders, and a scripted patient. Give every trainee the same instructions and test conditions. Build one deliberate exception into each station. Enter a date, evaluator, and scenario version for each attempt.

**Scoring** — Mark each criterion **S** for satisfactory, **P** for needs practice, or **N/O** for not observed. A station is satisfactory only when every one of its criteria has been shown satisfactorily. If a critical action is missed, stop the unsafe simulation, record what was observed, give focused feedback, and retest that station with a parallel scenario. Complete any criteria not yet observed before sign-off.
