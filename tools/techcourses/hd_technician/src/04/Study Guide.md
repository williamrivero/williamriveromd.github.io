## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish diffusion, ultrafiltration and convection.
2. Describe the dialyzer membrane, opposing flows and dialysate, and recognize what needs pre-use verification.
3. Distinguish measured interdialytic gain, target weight, prescribed ultrafiltration volume and ultrafiltration rate.
4. Explain what the urea reduction ratio (URR) and Kt/V measure and identify sampling or documentation discrepancies.

## Opening case · Which number is the fluid removal goal?

A trainee reviews the fictional record for Mr. dela Cruz before treatment. His last measured post-dialysis weight was **64.8 kg**. Today his pre-dialysis weight is **67.1 kg**, and his chart lists a current target weight of **65.4 kg**. The current session’s ultrafiltration goal has yet to be confirmed. A colleague says, “He gained 2.3 kg. Enter that for fluid removal.”

- Which two weights show what changed between sessions?
- What is the difference between today’s pre-dialysis weight and the charted target weight?
- What must be verified before using either number in setup?

## 1. How the dialyzer works

Blood moves through hollow fibers inside the dialyzer. Dialysate, also called dialysis fluid, passes around the outside of the fibers in a separate compartment. The fiber walls form a **semipermeable membrane**. In normal use, water and selected solutes can cross it while blood cells and most large proteins stay on the blood side. The two streams usually flow in opposite directions. This **countercurrent flow** helps maintain a concentration difference along the fibers, improving exchange. [NIDDK · Hemodialysis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/hemodialysis) · [UK Kidney Association · Membrane flux](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/rationale-for-clinical-practice-guidelines/membrane-flux-and-haemodiafiltration/)

| Process | What drives it | Dialysis example |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffusion** | A difference in solute concentration across the membrane. Net movement is from higher to lower concentration. | Urea normally moves from blood into dialysate. Under typical conditions, bicarbonate can move from dialysate toward blood. |
| **Ultrafiltration (UF)** | A pressure difference across the membrane. The machine controls net fluid removal according to the authorized treatment plan. | Water moves out of the blood compartment. The amount removed is tracked as a volume. |
| **Convection** | Moving water carries dissolved substances that can cross the membrane. | Some solutes move with the water during ultrafiltration. Convection also contributes to clearance in hemodiafiltration. |

**Worked example.** If the blood side contains more urea than the dialysate side, net urea diffusion is toward the dialysate. If that urea concentration difference narrows under otherwise similar conditions, net urea diffusion slows. The prescribed UF goal still describes the fluid volume the treatment is intended to remove. [UK Kidney Association · Membrane flux and convective clearance](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/rationale-for-clinical-practice-guidelines/membrane-flux-and-haemodiafiltration/)

## 2. Dialysate and treatment verification

Dialysate is prepared from treated dialysis water and prescribed concentrate products. Its electrolyte and buffer composition helps establish the gradients for exchange. Sodium, potassium, calcium and bicarbonate are examples of constituents that may matter to the prescription. Blood flow, dialysate flow, membrane characteristics and delivered treatment time can all affect solute clearance. Verify assigned items against the order and unit procedure. [NIDDK · Hemodialysis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/hemodialysis) · [CDC · Water used in dialysis](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/recommendations-resources/water-use-in-dialysis.html)

**Scope in practice.** Checks and machine operation must follow facility policy, manufacturer instructions and the technician’s assigned responsibilities. Confirm the patient record, authorized prescription, applicable dialyzer and concentrate labels, and required machine or dialysate readiness checks. An unexpected conductivity result or concentrate label discrepancy needs review before the affected setup is used. A normal conductivity reading by itself cannot confirm that the concentrate matches the patient’s order. Bring unresolved results to the supervising nurse and the designated technical lead according to local procedure. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water monitoring guidelines](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036)

## 3. Different numbers answer different fluid questions

| Number | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Interdialytic weight gain (kg)** | The current measured pre-dialysis weight minus the previous measured post-dialysis weight. Confirm the dates, units and whether each value was measured before or after treatment. |
| **Target weight (kg)** | The post-treatment weight the clinical team aims for based on the patient’s fluid assessment. The team may revise it as the patient’s condition changes. |
| **Pre-to-target weight difference (kg)** | The current pre-dialysis weight minus the documented target weight. It describes a difference between two weights. |
| **Prescribed UF volume (mL or L)** | The fluid removal goal authorized for the session. The clinical team may account for anticipated fluids given or taken during the session and what the patient is expected to tolerate. Verify how the facility defines and records the machine goal and net removal. |
| **UF rate** | Volume removed per unit time. A machine may display mL/h. A weight-normalized rate uses mL/kg/h and needs the weight basis defined by the applicable protocol. |

**Case debrief.** The measured gain is **67.1 − 64.8 = 2.3 kg**. The pre-to-target difference is **67.1 − 65.4 = 1.7 kg**. The case supplies no confirmed goal for the current treatment. Hold the affected setup and ask the supervising nurse to verify the current order. A concise handoff is “Today’s pre-weight is 67.1 kg. The prior post-weight is 64.8 kg, and today’s charted target is 65.4 kg. The two differences are 2.3 kg and 1.7 kg. Please confirm the authorized UF goal before setup proceeds.” [UK Kidney Association · Fluid assessment and management](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/rationale-for-clinical-practice-guidelines/fluid-in-haemodialysis/)

**Rate practice.** On a separate mock record, an already authorized total UF volume of **1,600 mL** over **4 hours** gives a planned average of **400 mL/h**. This arithmetic describes the example. If the treatment ends early, the clinical team decides how to respond. Excessively rapid removal can contribute to low blood pressure, and symptoms such as new dizziness require prompt escalation under the unit’s procedure. [UK Kidney Association · Fluid assessment and management](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/rationale-for-clinical-practice-guidelines/fluid-in-haemodialysis/)

## 4. Urea clearance and adequacy

**URR** is the percentage reduction in blood urea nitrogen (BUN) from before to after a dialysis session. Use a matched pre/post pair with the same units.

> **URR (%) = (pre-treatment BUN − post-treatment BUN) ÷ pre-treatment BUN × 100**  
> Mock calculation with pre-BUN **60 mg/dL** and post-BUN **18 mg/dL** gives **70%**.

**Kt/V** expresses urea clearance relative to the estimated space in the body in which urea is distributed. **K** represents effective urea clearance, **t** represents treatment time and **V** represents the estimated urea distribution volume. Clinical formulas differ in how they estimate a delivered dose. URR and Kt/V quantify urea removal or clearance and are interpreted alongside other clinical information such as volume status, symptoms and treatment schedule. [UK Kidney Association · Urea clearance and Kt/V](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/appendices/appendix%20a/) · [NIDDK · TiME study protocol on dialysis dose measures](https://repository.niddk.nih.gov/public/study_document/TiME_V1/Documents/TiME_Protocol_V2.20160607_and_Statistical_Analysis_Plan.pdf)

**Why the sampling record matters.** Accurate interpretation depends on matching samples to the patient and session and following unit collection and timing procedures. Review the sample labels and times, delivered duration and any interruptions within your assigned role. If a sample is mislabeled or a result is unexpected, flag the discrepancy for the nurse through the approved process. [UK Kidney Association · Dialysis dose](https://guidelines.ukkidney.org/haemodialysis/rationale-for-clinical-practice-guidelines/dialysis-dose-in-thrice-weekly-dialysis-schedules/) · [NIDDK · Sampling and delivered dose in the ATN operations manual](https://repository.niddk.nih.gov/media/studies/atn/Operations_Manual_Ver_4.0.pdf)
