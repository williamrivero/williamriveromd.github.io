# Module 03 · Treatment Options and Living with Kidney Failure

![A Filipino family discusses kidney treatment options with a clinician in a dialysis unit.](Opener.png)

**Time:** 8 hours classroom, 2 hours skills lab, 10 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment:** MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Modules 3, 4 and 8  
**Before you start:** Review kidney failure in Module 2 and role boundaries in Module 1.

## Learning objectives

1. Compare in-center hemodialysis, home hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, kidney transplant and comprehensive conservative kidney management.
2. Identify the basic equipment and day-to-day responsibilities associated with home dialysis and describe questions for the home program.
3. Elicit patient priorities and barriers involving work, travel, household responsibilities, communication and support.
4. Reinforce approved information with teach-back and refer questions about eligibility, schedules, costs and individualized care.

## Opening case · Making treatment fit everyday life

Ms. Reyes is a fictional patient receiving in-center hemodialysis. She works rotating shifts, travels more than an hour to the clinic, and helps care for her mother. Her daughter offers to handle any home treatment but works nights. Ms. Reyes asks, “Can I do dialysis at home so I can keep working? Would I need my daughter with me? If I can’t get a ride tomorrow, can I wait for the next session?”

- Which concern needs action today?
- What could you explain about home options, and what must the program confirm?
- What would you ask Ms. Reyes to explain back before she leaves?

## 1. Compare the treatment pathways

Kidney replacement therapy includes dialysis and kidney transplantation. Comprehensive conservative kidney management provides active care focused on symptoms, quality of life and the person’s goals without dialysis or transplant. The patient and clinical team discuss medically appropriate options together. Their decision may change with health, preferences and practical circumstances. [NIDDK · Choosing a treatment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment) · [KDIGO · CKD guideline, chapter 5](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

![Five pathways compared by how they work and what patients may need to plan for.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Five treatment pathways. The table provides a text equivalent and additional practical considerations.*

| Option | How it works | What to discuss with the care team |
|---|---|---|
| **In-center hemodialysis (HD)** | Blood passes through an external dialyzer at the center. A trained team operates equipment and monitors treatment. | Prescribed sessions, shift availability, travel, work or school conflicts, and who to call when attendance is at risk. |
| **Home HD** | Uses an external dialyzer and a home HD machine. The patient and any designated care partner receive training and follow a prescribed plan with program support. | Program availability and eligibility, vascular access training, utilities, storage, supply delivery, backup plans, and whether the program requires a care partner. |
| **Peritoneal dialysis (PD)** | Dialysis solution enters the abdomen through a PD catheter. The peritoneal lining serves as the exchange membrane. The fluid is later drained and replaced. | The daily exchange routine, clean setup area, storage, training, infection prevention and how to reach the PD team. |
| **Kidney transplant** | A kidney from a living or deceased donor is placed surgically. A functioning transplant provides kidney function and may remove the need for dialysis. | Referral and evaluation, donor pathways, surgery, follow-up, and ongoing anti-rejection medicines while maintaining the graft. Eligibility and timing require transplant-team review. |
| **Comprehensive conservative kidney management** | The team treats symptoms and supports quality of life through an active care plan without dialysis or transplantation. | Symptom priorities, medicines and supportive measures, psychosocial support, planning ahead, and whom to contact when symptoms change. |

An option discussed in a textbook may vary in availability or requirements across Philippine facilities. Ask the local team about referral routes, program capacity, cost and coverage before giving a patient specific information. [NIDDK · Choosing a treatment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment) · [NIDDK · Treatment comparison chart](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment/comparison-chart) · [KDIGO · CKD guideline, chapter 5](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf) · [NIDDK · Kidney transplant](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/kidney-transplant)

## 2. Recognize home dialysis equipment and responsibilities

- **Home HD.** A machine, dialyzer, bloodlines and access supplies support the prescribed treatment. Water, power and plumbing needs vary by system. The program trains the patient and any required partner in the equipment, access care, monitoring and emergency response. A plan for equipment failure, utility interruptions and urgent symptoms is part of program preparation.
- **Continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD).** Uses prescribed solution bags and a manual exchange system. Learning at this level is about recognizing the components and explaining that exchanges are done by hand.
- **Automated peritoneal dialysis (APD).** Uses a cycler and compatible tubing and solution bags. The machine carries out programmed exchanges, often overnight. Some prescriptions include a daytime exchange.
- **Signs that need referral.** A patient on PD who reports cloudy drained fluid or new abdominal pain should contact their PD team promptly under their program instructions. For a patient with an HD access, reinforce the facility’s approved guidance about access protection and reporting changes. Use the unit’s urgent escalation pathway for urgent symptoms. [NIDDK · Peritoneal dialysis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/peritoneal-dialysis) · [NIDDK · Hemodialysis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/hemodialysis)

## 3. Help the patient plan for daily life

Start with a question such as “What part of the treatment routine is hardest for you right now?” Listen for the specific barrier and ask permission before involving family. Use an interpreter through the facility when needed. Check what the patient wants to keep doing, such as working, attending school, traveling, or caring for relatives. Explore practical barriers such as transport cost, shift timing, storage space, reliable utilities, and who can realistically help. Give the team the patient’s priorities in the patient’s own words where possible. [NIDDK · Choosing a treatment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment) · [NIDDK · Treatment comparison chart](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment/comparison-chart) · [KDIGO · CKD guideline, chapter 5](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

| Patient concern | Technician response within assigned scope |
|---|---|
| **A ride fell through or a session may be missed** | Notify the dialysis nurse promptly. Reinforce the approved contact instructions and help connect the patient with the unit’s scheduling or support pathway. |
| **Food, fluids or medicines are hard to manage** | Ask what makes the plan difficult. Reinforce the approved plan and refer individualized questions to the nurse, dietitian or prescribing team. |
| **Caregiver support is uncertain** | Clarify who the patient wants involved and when help is available. Route questions about care-partner requirements to the home program. |
| **Fatigue, stress or changes in daily function** | Listen, document the reported impact, and refer to the nurse or appropriate support service. New urgent symptoms follow the unit’s escalation procedure. |
| **Interest in home dialysis, transplant or conservative care** | Explain basic differences using approved materials and arrange a handoff to the nurse or nephrology team for individualized discussion. |

## 4. Use teach-back and close the referral

Explain one or two points in plain language. Ask the patient to tell you the plan in their own words. If the response shows a misunderstanding, explain it another way and ask again. Record what was reinforced, what barrier remains, whom you notified and the confirmed next action. Technician education reinforces the care team’s plan. Eligibility, prescription changes and individualized counselling belong to the authorized team. [AHRQ · Teach-back](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool5.html) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Case debrief.** The potential missed session needs a same-day handoff. Alert the dialysis nurse and follow the unit’s instructions for resolving the transport and scheduling concern. Explain that both home HD and PD exist, that they use different equipment and training, and that the local program must confirm suitability, availability and any care-partner rules. Ask Ms. Reyes whether she wants her daughter involved in the later discussion.

**Example teach-back.** “To make sure I explained the next step clearly, what will you do if your ride falls through?” If Ms. Reyes says she will wait until her next slot, clarify the approved contact plan, then ask her to explain it again. **Example handoff:** “Ms. Reyes may miss tomorrow’s session because she may have no ride. She asked about home treatment because of rotating shifts and travel. Her daughter works nights. Please advise on tomorrow’s attendance plan and route her home-treatment questions to the nephrology team.”
