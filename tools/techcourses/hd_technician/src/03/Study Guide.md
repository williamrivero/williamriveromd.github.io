# Module 03 · Treatment Options and Living with Kidney Failure

![A Filipino family discusses kidney treatment options with a clinician in a dialysis unit.](Opener.png)

**Time:** 8 hours classroom, 2 hours skills lab, 10 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment:** MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Modules 3, 4 and 8  
**Before you start:** Review kidney failure in Module 2 and role boundaries in Module 1.

## Learning objectives

1. Compare in-center hemodialysis, home hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, kidney transplant and comprehensive conservative kidney management.
2. Identify the basic equipment and day-to-day responsibilities associated with home dialysis and describe questions for the home program.
3. Elicit patient priorities and barriers involving work, travel, household responsibilities, communication and support.
4. Reinforce approved information with teach-back and refer questions about eligibility, schedules, costs and individualized care.

## Opening case · Making treatment fit everyday life

It is Tuesday afternoon, in the last hour of the session. Ms. Liza Reyes, 46, a fictional patient on in-center hemodialysis, works rotating shifts at a call center. She travels more than an hour each way to the clinic and helps care for her elderly mother. Her daughter has offered to handle any home treatment, but the daughter works nights. As you record her readings, Ms. Reyes asks you three things at once: “Can I do dialysis at home so I can keep working? Would I need my daughter with me? If I can’t get a ride tomorrow, can I just wait for the next session?”

- Which of her concerns needs action today, and from whom?
- What can you explain about home options, and what must the program itself confirm?
- What would you ask Ms. Reyes to explain back to you before she leaves?

## 1. Compare the treatment pathways

Think of the different ways a worker can get from the province to a job in the city: the bus, the train, a motorcycle of his own, or a rented room near the office. Each one costs something different in time, money, effort, and freedom, and the best one depends on the person. Treatment for kidney failure is similar. The picture has a limit: a commuter chooses alone, while a patient chooses together with the clinical team, and some options are open only if the body, the home, and a program allow them.

Kidney replacement therapy includes dialysis and kidney transplantation. Comprehensive conservative kidney management is active care without dialysis or transplant, focused on symptoms, quality of life, and the person’s own goals. The patient and the clinical team discuss the options that are medically appropriate. Their decision may change as health, preferences, and practical circumstances change. [NIDDK · Choosing a treatment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment) · [KDIGO · CKD guideline, chapter 5](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

![Five pathways compared by how they work and what patients may need to plan for.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Five treatment pathways. The table gives a text equivalent and adds practical considerations.*

| Option | How it works | What to discuss with the care team |
|---|---|---|
| **In-center hemodialysis (HD)** | Blood passes through an external dialyzer at the center. A trained team runs the equipment and monitors the treatment. | Prescribed sessions, available shifts, travel, conflicts with work or school, and whom to call when attendance is at risk. |
| **Home HD** | Uses an external dialyzer and a home HD machine. The patient, and any designated care partner, are trained and follow a prescribed plan with program support. | Whether a program is available and the patient is eligible, vascular access training, utilities, storage, supply delivery, backup plans, and whether the program requires a care partner. |
| **Peritoneal dialysis (PD)** | Dialysis solution enters the abdomen through a PD catheter. The lining of the abdomen, the peritoneum, serves as the exchange membrane. The fluid is later drained and replaced. | The daily exchange routine, a clean setup area, storage, training, infection prevention, and how to reach the PD team. |
| **Kidney transplant** | A kidney from a living or deceased donor is placed by surgery. A working transplant provides kidney function and may remove the need for dialysis. | Referral and evaluation, donor pathways, surgery, follow-up, and anti-rejection medicines for as long as the graft is kept. Eligibility and timing need transplant-team review. |
| **Comprehensive conservative kidney management** | The team treats symptoms and supports quality of life through an active care plan, without dialysis or transplantation. | Symptom priorities, medicines and supportive measures, psychosocial support, planning ahead, and whom to contact when symptoms change. |

An option described in a textbook may not be available, or may have different requirements, at a given Philippine facility. Before you give a patient anything specific, ask the local team about referral routes, program capacity, cost, and coverage. [NIDDK · Choosing a treatment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment) · [NIDDK · Treatment comparison chart](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment/comparison-chart) · [KDIGO · CKD guideline, chapter 5](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf) · [NIDDK · Kidney transplant](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/kidney-transplant)

## 2. Recognize home dialysis equipment and responsibilities

At this level you need to recognize the equipment and know who does the work at home. You do not need to train anyone on it.

- **Home HD.** A machine, a dialyzer, bloodlines, and access supplies support the prescribed treatment. Water, power, and plumbing needs differ from one system to another. The program trains the patient, and any required partner, in the equipment, access care, monitoring, and emergency response. Preparing for the program includes a plan for equipment failure, interrupted utilities, and urgent symptoms.
- **Continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD).** Uses prescribed solution bags and a manual exchange system. What you need to know here is the components and the fact that each exchange is done by hand.
- **Automated peritoneal dialysis (APD).** Uses a cycler with compatible tubing and solution bags. The machine performs the programmed exchanges, often overnight. Some prescriptions add a daytime exchange.
- **Signs that need referral.** A patient on PD who reports cloudy drained fluid or new abdominal pain should contact the PD team promptly, as the program instructs. For a patient with an HD access, reinforce your facility’s approved guidance on protecting the access and reporting changes. Urgent symptoms go through the unit’s urgent escalation pathway. [NIDDK · Peritoneal dialysis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/peritoneal-dialysis) · [NIDDK · Hemodialysis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/hemodialysis)

## 3. Help the patient plan for daily life

The treatment is only one part of a patient’s week. Work, school, family, travel, and money shape what a patient can actually do, and a plan that ignores them is a plan the patient will not follow. Start with a question such as “What part of the treatment routine is hardest for you right now?” Listen for the specific barrier, and ask permission before you involve family. Arrange an interpreter through the facility when one is needed. Find out what the patient wants to keep doing: working, studying, traveling, caring for relatives. Then explore the practical barriers, such as the cost of transport, shift timing, storage space, reliable utilities, and who can realistically help. Pass the patient’s priorities to the team in the patient’s own words where you can. [NIDDK · Choosing a treatment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment) · [NIDDK · Treatment comparison chart](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment/comparison-chart) · [KDIGO · CKD guideline, chapter 5](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

| Patient concern | Technician response within assigned scope |
|---|---|
| **A ride fell through or a session may be missed** | Tell the dialysis nurse promptly. Reinforce the approved contact instructions and help connect the patient with the unit’s scheduling or support pathway. |
| **Food, fluids or medicines are hard to manage** | Ask what makes the plan difficult. Reinforce the approved plan, and refer individual questions to the nurse, dietitian, or prescribing team. |
| **Caregiver support is uncertain** | Clarify whom the patient wants involved and when help is available. Send questions about care-partner requirements to the home program. |
| **Fatigue, stress or changes in daily function** | Listen, document the effect the patient describes, and refer to the nurse or the appropriate support service. New urgent symptoms follow the unit’s escalation procedure. |
| **Interest in home dialysis, transplant or conservative care** | Explain the basic differences using approved materials, and arrange a handoff to the nurse or nephrology team for an individual discussion. |

## 4. Use teach-back and close the referral

A patient who nods has not necessarily understood. Teach-back checks your explanation, not the patient. Explain one or two points in plain language. Ask the patient to tell you the plan in his or her own words. If the answer shows a misunderstanding, explain it another way and ask again. Then record what you reinforced, what barrier remains, whom you notified, and the confirmed next step. Your teaching reinforces the care team’s plan. Eligibility, prescription changes, and individual counseling belong to the authorized team. [AHRQ · Teach-back](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool5.html) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Case debrief.**

**What needs action today?** The possible missed session. It needs a same-day handoff. Alert the dialysis nurse and follow the unit’s instructions for sorting out the transport and scheduling problem. Waiting for the next session is not a plan you can approve.

**What can you explain, and what must the program confirm?** You can explain that both home HD and PD exist and that they use different equipment and training. The local program must confirm whether she is suitable, whether a slot is available, and whether a care partner is required. Ask Ms. Reyes whether she wants her daughter involved in that later discussion.

**What do you ask her to explain back?** The contact plan for tomorrow. “To make sure I explained the next step clearly, what will you do if your ride falls through?” If Ms. Reyes says she will wait for her next slot, clarify the approved contact plan and ask her to explain it again. **Example handoff:** “Ms. Reyes may miss tomorrow’s session because she may have no ride. She asked about home treatment because of rotating shifts and travel. Her daughter works nights. Please advise on tomorrow’s attendance plan and route her home-treatment questions to the nephrology team.”
