# Module 02 · Kidneys and Kidney Failure

![A clinician uses a kidney teaching model during a discussion in a dialysis unit.](Opener.png)

**Time:** 8 hours classroom, 2 hours skills lab, 10 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment:** MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Module 2  
**Before you start:** Review Module 1 patient identification, role boundaries and escalation.

## Learning objectives

1. Trace the paths of blood and urine through the kidney and explain the roles of the glomerulus and tubules.
2. Explain how the kidneys handle wastes, fluid, electrolytes and acid, and how they support red blood cell production and mineral balance.
3. Distinguish acute kidney injury (AKI), chronic kidney disease (CKD) and kidney failure using a clinical timeline.
4. Interpret simple mock trends in weight, symptoms and laboratory results, then report the findings within the technician’s role.

## Opening case · Swelling and fatigue before dialysis

It is Thursday morning. Mr. Rodel Santos, 52, a fictional patient on maintenance hemodialysis, steps off the scale at 07:10. His last post-treatment weight was 67.2 kg. Today’s pre-treatment weight is 69.4 kg. He tells you his shoes feel tighter and that he has been more tired than usual. When you ask how he feels right now, he says that he has become short of breath even while sitting, which is new for him. A prior hemoglobin result in his verified chart is flagged low.

- Which finding comes first, and whom do you tell?
- How much has his measured weight changed since the last session?
- Which lost kidney functions could explain what you see, and what belongs in your handoff?

## 1. From blood to urine

Think of each kidney as a large rice mill with many small milling lines working side by side. Grain comes in, the line separates what is kept from what is thrown away, and most of what passed through is gathered back. The picture has a limit: a mill sorts by size alone, while the kidney also decides, moment by moment, how much water and salt the body should keep.

The renal artery brings blood to the kidney. Each kidney contains many nephrons. In each nephron, the **glomerulus** filters water and small solutes out of the blood and into the nephron, and it keeps blood cells and most large proteins in the circulation. The **tubules** then reclaim much of the filtered water and useful solutes, and they secrete selected substances into the tubular fluid. What is left becomes urine. It drains through the collecting ducts into the renal pelvis, then through the ureter to the bladder. Blood leaves the kidney through the renal vein. [NIDDK · How the kidneys work](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidneys-how-they-work)

## 2. What changes when kidney function declines

You will never see a failing kidney at the chair. You will see what its missing work leaves behind. Each row below links one function to what you may notice when it is lost.

| Function | What the kidneys do | Findings you may encounter |
|---|---|---|
| **Waste removal** | Excrete metabolic wastes, including urea and creatinine. | Waste markers may rise. Advanced kidney dysfunction can come with nausea, poor appetite, itching, or fatigue. Each of these symptoms has more than one possible cause. |
| **Fluid and electrolyte balance** | Adjust how much sodium and water leave the body, and help regulate potassium. | Rising weight and swelling may come with fluid retention. Potassium may be high even when the patient feels well. New breathlessness, or a result flagged critical, needs prompt escalation under unit protocol. |
| **Acid–base balance** | Excrete acid, and conserve and regenerate bicarbonate. | A low serum bicarbonate or total CO₂ may point to an acid–base disturbance, which the clinical team evaluates in context. |
| **Hormones and minerals** | Produce erythropoietin (EPO), the signal that tells the bone marrow to make red blood cells, and activate vitamin D. The kidneys also help regulate phosphate. | Less EPO can contribute to anemia. Changes in phosphate handling and vitamin D activation can contribute to CKD-related mineral and bone disorder. |

Dialysis takes over only part of this work. It helps remove wastes and excess fluid and helps manage some electrolyte and acid–base disturbances. Anemia, mineral and bone problems, and other needs are assessed and managed separately by the care team. The practical point is that a patient can have a good session and still be anemic, still itch, and still need the rest of the plan. [NIDDK · Kidney failure](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/what-is-kidney-failure) · [NIDDK · Anemia in CKD](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/anemia) · [NIDDK · Mineral and bone disorder](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/mineral-bone-disorder) · [NIDDK · CKD complications](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/professionals/education-cme/kidney-disease-education-lesson-builder/lesson-3-when-kidney-disease-gets-worse)

## 3. Distinguishing AKI, CKD and kidney failure

The three terms differ mainly in time. Acute injury happens over hours to days. Chronic disease is present for months. Kidney failure is the far end of the chronic course. The clue you can contribute is the timeline: when something started and how it changed.

| Term | What to look for in a teaching case |
|---|---|
| **Acute kidney injury** | A rapid fall in kidney function over hours to days. A rising creatinine or less urine can be a clue. Report the time course and what you observed, so the clinical team can evaluate it. |
| **Chronic kidney disease** | An abnormality of kidney structure or function, with implications for health, present for at least three months. One abnormal creatinine, estimated GFR, or urine albumin result cannot show chronicity by itself. |
| **Kidney failure** | Very advanced loss of kidney function. In CKD, category G5 is an estimated GFR below 15 mL/min/1.73 m². Treatment decisions depend on the whole clinical picture and on the patient’s preferences. Some people with kidney failure still pass urine. |

AKI can happen in a person who already has CKD. Kidney replacement therapy may be needed during severe AKI or for the ongoing care of chronic kidney failure. Your part is to report observations and timelines within your assigned role. Diagnosis, staging, and dialysis decisions belong to the clinical team. [KDIGO · CKD guideline](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf) · [KDIGO · AKI guidance](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/acute-kidney-injury/) · [NIDDK · Kidney failure](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/what-is-kidney-failure)

## 4. Read the trend and tell the team what changed

A number on its own tells you little. For every value, compare the **value, unit, timestamp, baseline and treatment timing**. For a laboratory result, confirm the verified record, the collection time, and the flag. Each test answers a different question. Creatinine and estimated GFR describe filtration in the right context. Urine albumin can show kidney damage. Hemoglobin measures the oxygen-carrying protein in red cells. The clinical team interprets each one alongside symptoms and trends. A result flagged critical goes through your facility’s urgent reporting pathway, at once. [KDIGO · CKD guideline](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf) · [NIDDK · Anemia in CKD](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/anemia) · [DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Case debrief.**

**What comes first?** The new breathlessness at rest. Alert the dialysis nurse immediately and follow the unit’s emergency escalation procedure. The rest of the handoff can follow once help is on the way.

**How much has his weight changed?** Today’s measured weight is **2.2 kg higher** than the previous post-treatment weight (69.4 − 67.2 kg). Give the nurse both source values, not only the difference. The prescribed fluid removal goal is a separate clinical decision.

**What could explain it, and what goes in the handoff?** Swelling and a higher weight can fit fluid retention. Fatigue with a low hemoglobin calls for review for anemia and other causes. You report the findings and their timing. The cause and the treatment plan belong to the clinical team. [NIDDK · Kidney failure](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/what-is-kidney-failure) · [NIDDK · Anemia in CKD](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/anemia) · [DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Example handoff after the urgent alert.** “Mr. Santos reports new shortness of breath while sitting. He also reports tighter shoes and more fatigue than usual. Today’s pre-treatment weight is 69.4 kg, up 2.2 kg from the documented post-treatment weight of 67.2 kg. A prior hemoglobin is flagged low. Please assess him now.” Then record the observation, the time, the notification, and the confirmed directions in the appropriate system within your role. [AHRQ · SBAR](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/sbar.html)
