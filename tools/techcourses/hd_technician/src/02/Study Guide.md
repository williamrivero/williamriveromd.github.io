# Module 02 · Kidneys and Kidney Failure

![A clinician uses a kidney teaching model during a discussion in a dialysis unit.](Opener.png)

**Time:** 8 hours classroom, 2 hours skills lab, 10 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment:** MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Module 2  
**Before you start:** Review Module 1 patient identification, role boundaries and escalation.

## Learning objectives

1. Trace the paths of blood and urine through the kidney and explain the roles of the glomerulus and tubules.
2. Explain how the kidneys handle wastes, fluid, electrolytes and acid, and how they support red blood cell production and mineral balance.
3. Distinguish acute kidney injury (AKI), chronic kidney disease (CKD) and kidney failure using a clinical timeline.
4. Interpret simple mock trends in weight, symptoms and laboratory results, then report the findings within the technician’s role.

## Opening case · Swelling and fatigue before dialysis

Mr. Santos, a fictional patient receiving maintenance hemodialysis, arrives for his next session. His previous post-treatment weight was 67.2 kg. Today’s pre-treatment weight is 69.4 kg. He reports that his shoes feel tighter and that he has been more tired than usual. When asked how he is feeling now, he says he is newly short of breath while sitting. A prior hemoglobin result in the verified chart is flagged low.

- Which finding takes priority, and whom do you contact?
- How much has his measured weight changed since the previous session?
- Which kidney functions might help explain the findings? What information belongs in your handoff?

## 1. From blood to urine

The renal artery brings blood to the kidney. Each kidney contains many nephrons. In each nephron, the **glomerulus** filters water and small solutes from blood into the nephron while retaining blood cells and most large proteins in the circulation. The **tubules** reclaim much of the filtered water and useful solutes and secrete selected substances into the tubular fluid. The resulting urine drains through collecting ducts into the renal pelvis and then through the ureter to the bladder. Blood returns from the kidney through the renal vein. [NIDDK · How the kidneys work](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidneys-how-they-work)

## 2. What changes when kidney function declines

| Function | What the kidneys do | Findings you may encounter |
|---|---|---|
| **Waste removal** | Excrete metabolic waste, including urea and creatinine. | Waste markers may rise. Advanced kidney dysfunction can be accompanied by nausea, reduced appetite, itching or fatigue. Symptoms have multiple possible causes. |
| **Fluid and electrolyte balance** | Adjust sodium and water excretion and help regulate potassium. | Increasing weight and edema may accompany fluid retention. Potassium may be elevated even when a patient feels well. New breathlessness or a result flagged critical needs prompt escalation under unit protocol. |
| **Acid–base balance** | Excrete acid and conserve and regenerate bicarbonate. | A low serum bicarbonate or total CO₂ result may point to an acid–base disturbance that the clinical team evaluates in context. |
| **Hormones and minerals** | Produce erythropoietin (EPO), which signals bone marrow to make red blood cells, and activate vitamin D. The kidneys also help regulate phosphate. | Reduced EPO can contribute to anemia. Changes in phosphate handling and vitamin D activation can contribute to CKD-related mineral and bone disorder. |

Dialysis helps remove wastes and excess fluid and manage some electrolyte and acid–base disturbances. The care team assesses and manages anemia, mineral and bone complications, and other needs separately. [NIDDK · Kidney failure](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/what-is-kidney-failure) · [NIDDK · Anemia in CKD](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/anemia) · [NIDDK · Mineral and bone disorder](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/mineral-bone-disorder) · [NIDDK · CKD complications](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/professionals/education-cme/kidney-disease-education-lesson-builder/lesson-3-when-kidney-disease-gets-worse)

## 3. Distinguishing AKI, CKD and kidney failure

| Term | What to look for in a teaching case |
|---|---|
| **Acute kidney injury** | A rapid decline in kidney function over hours to days. A rising creatinine or reduced urine output can be a clue. Report the time course and observation so the clinical team can evaluate it. |
| **Chronic kidney disease** | An abnormality of kidney structure or function with implications for health that has been present for at least three months. A single abnormal creatinine, estimated GFR or urine albumin result cannot establish chronicity by itself. |
| **Kidney failure** | Very advanced loss of kidney function. In CKD, category G5 is an estimated GFR below 15 mL/min/1.73 m². Treatment decisions depend on the overall clinical picture and patient preferences. Some people with kidney failure still pass urine. |

AKI can occur in a person with CKD. Kidney replacement therapy may be needed during severe AKI or for ongoing care of chronic kidney failure. Report observations and timelines within your assigned role. Diagnosis, staging and dialysis decisions belong to the clinical team. [KDIGO · CKD guideline](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf) · [KDIGO · AKI guidance](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/acute-kidney-injury/) · [NIDDK · Kidney failure](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/what-is-kidney-failure)

## 4. Read the trend and tell the team what changed

Compare the **value, unit, timestamp, baseline and treatment timing**. For a lab result, confirm the verified record, collection time and flag. Creatinine and estimated GFR help describe filtration in the appropriate context. Urine albumin can indicate kidney damage. Hemoglobin measures the oxygen-carrying protein in red cells. Each result answers a different question, and the clinical team interprets it alongside symptoms and trends. Flagged critical values go through your facility’s urgent reporting pathway. [KDIGO · CKD guideline](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf) · [NIDDK · Anemia in CKD](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/anemia) · [DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Case debrief.** Alert the dialysis nurse immediately about the new breathlessness at rest and follow the unit’s emergency escalation procedure. Continue the handoff after help is summoned. Today’s measured weight is **2.2 kg higher** than the prior post-treatment weight (69.4 − 67.2 kg). That comparison is useful for the nurse’s assessment. The prescribed fluid removal goal is a separate clinical decision. Swelling and increased weight can be consistent with fluid retention. Fatigue with low hemoglobin warrants review for anemia and other causes. The technician reports the findings and timing, leaving the cause and treatment plan to the clinical team. [NIDDK · Kidney failure](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/what-is-kidney-failure) · [NIDDK · Anemia in CKD](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/anemia) · [DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Example handoff after the urgent alert.** “Mr. Santos reports new shortness of breath while sitting. He also reports tighter shoes and more fatigue than usual. Today’s pre-treatment weight is 69.4 kg, up 2.2 kg from the documented post-treatment weight of 67.2 kg. A prior hemoglobin is flagged low. Please assess him now.” Record the observation, time, notification and confirmed directions in the appropriate system within your role. [AHRQ · SBAR](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/sbar.html)
