# Module 11 · Dialyzer Reprocessing and Equipment Quality

![An unused dialyzer and blank inventory tags on a training bench.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 4 hours classroom, 4 hours skills lab, 12 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Modules 6, 10 and 11, and the MEI dialyzer reprocessing supplement  
**Before you start** — Review waste segregation, equipment readiness and water quality in Modules 5–7.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish single use from reuse in an authorized, validated dialyzer program.
2. Verify and document the information needed to trace an eligible dialyzer to the same patient and confirm readiness.
3. Hold, reject or escalate a device or record discrepancy through the designated procedure.
4. Describe routine equipment quality checks and distinguish maintenance completion from authorized return to service.

## Opening case · A passed check with an unreadable label

Before treatment, a trainee receives a dialyzer from an authorized reuse program. The reprocessing log shows that device **D-071** passed the previous processing checks. On the device label, one patient identifier is illegible and the other cannot be matched to the current patient. The device is still in the designated storage area. The next shift is about to start.

- Which finding determines whether the device can be used?
- What should the trainee do with the device and record?
- If the unit assigns a replacement, which verification steps still apply?

## 1. Single use and same-patient reuse

A single-use dialyzer is used for one treatment and then handled under the facility’s contaminated waste procedure. An authorized reuse program uses an eligible dialyzer on the **same patient** across treatments under a validated reprocessing program. Product labeling, the instructions for use (IFU), applicable rules and the facility’s written procedure govern whether a particular device can enter that program. Keep the workflow separate from routine station turnover. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [MEI · Dialyzer reprocessing supplement](https://mei.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Dialyzer_Reprocessing.pdf)

An authorized program controls cleaning and disinfection, water quality, device performance, identity, storage, records and pre-use verification. It also defines who may perform each check and make a release decision. For international comparison, U.S. requirements under 42 CFR § 494.50 specify same-patient use and labeling for multiple reuse, among other controls. Apply the local facility’s approved procedure and requirements in Philippine practice. A single-use placement can teach this module through a traceability and disposition simulation. [U.S. CMS · Reuse of hemodialyzers and bloodlines](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-494/subpart-B/section-494.50) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water monitoring](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036)

## 2. Verify a traceable chain of records

**Traceability** is the ability to link the device to the correct patient, previous uses and processing history, current status and staff checks. Check the device label against approved identifiers in the current patient record and the matching processing log. Verify that the use count and all required checks are recorded. An item with a readable label may still be missing a required check. A complete check record may belong to a different device.

![A decision diagram shows identity, processing records and pre-use checks leading to designated release, or a hold and escalation when verification fails.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Traceability and disposition. The following table provides a text equivalent.*

| Verification area | What the approved record should let assigned staff confirm |
|---|---|
| **Identity and eligibility** | Patient match using approved identifiers, device identifier or equivalent traceable record, eligible model and confirmation that the dialyzer belongs to the same patient. |
| **Processing and storage** | Processing date or batch, use count, who performed the checks, and storage or readiness status under facility rules. |
| **Device condition and performance** | The required visual, integrity and performance checks. Total cell volume (TCV), where used, helps indicate whether fibers remain open. The approved protocol sets the applicable acceptance limits. |
| **Disinfectant safeguards** | Documented chemical process verification and a required pre-use residual disinfectant check where applicable. Residual verification addresses chemical remaining after preparation. |
| **Disposition** | Current status, reason for any hold or rejection, designated reviewer, approved replacement or disposal, and any authorized clearance. |

The test methods, chemical concentrations and limits, storage conditions, required exposure times and maximum uses depend on the validated system and applicable requirements. Practice finding these requirements in the written procedure with the preceptor. Verify each required check independently. A satisfactory result in one category does not clear an exception in another. [MEI · Dialyzer reprocessing supplement](https://mei.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Dialyzer_Reprocessing.pdf) · [Philippine DOH · Labeling, records and acceptable reprocessing](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

**Case debrief.** Withhold the device and secure it in the designated hold area. Preserve the label and records so the discrepancy can be reviewed. Notify the supervising nurse and designated reprocessing or quality lead and report exactly which identifiers failed. Do not infer the patient match from the schedule or reconstruct a label from memory. The authorized lead determines whether the issue can be verified and how the device will be handled. Any replacement must match the current order and pass applicable preparation and checks before use. [MEI · Same-patient labeling and verification](https://mei.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Dialyzer_Reprocessing.pdf)

## 3. Hold, reject and report exceptions

- **Hold and escalate.** Remove an uncertain item from ready stock and use the approved hold label or secure area. Examples include unreadable identification, a missing use history, a failed or missing required test, a damaged device, and questionable storage. Record the item, time, discrepancy and whom you notified.
- **Reject or dispose under the approved procedure.** Designated staff apply the documented rejection criteria and record final disposition. Preserve items or records needed for incident investigation as directed. Follow the disposal procedure for contaminated waste or chemical waste.
- **Escalate patient concerns immediately.** During or after treatment, promptly report new symptoms or a suspected device or reprocessing concern. When multiple patients are affected or a shared process is suspected, report that pattern. The clinical and quality teams direct treatment, any quarantine of related items, investigation and external reporting as applicable. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [U.S. CMS · Monitoring and reporting in reuse programs](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-494/subpart-B/section-494.50)

**Example handoff.** “At 06:35 I held D-071. One patient identifier was illegible and the other could not be matched. Previous processing checks were marked passed. Pre-use residual verification is pending, and no patient was connected. The device and records are available for designated review.”

## 4. Equipment quality and authorized release

| Activity | What the technician verifies or records within assigned scope |
|---|---|
| **Routine pre-use checks** | Equipment ID, service status, applicable disinfection or other readiness checks, supply compatibility and required test results. |
| **Preventive maintenance** | Assigned staff perform and document scheduled work using the specific model’s maintenance instructions. The machine’s current maintenance status should be traceable. |
| **Fault response** | Hold affected equipment, document the exact message or observation, and notify the supervising nurse and equipment personnel. Avoid recording a guess as a confirmed fault. |
| **Return to service** | Designated staff confirm the fault is resolved and required post-service checks are documented. Verify formal release under the facility procedure before use. Normal display status or a closed work ticket alone cannot establish release. |
| **Quality review** | Trends such as repeated faults, incomplete checks or repeated labeling exceptions can guide review. Capture objective facts for the designated lead. |

The model’s IFU and service documents define equipment-specific checks. Learn who may perform operator checks, who may service equipment and who can authorize its return. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards and equipment care](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Equipment technical documentation](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/technical-documentation/)
