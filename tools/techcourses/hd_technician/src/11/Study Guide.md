# Module 11 · Dialyzer Reprocessing and Equipment Quality

![An unused dialyzer and blank inventory tags on a training bench.](Opener.png)

**Time** — 4 hours classroom, 4 hours skills lab, 12 hours supervised placement  
**Curriculum alignment** — MEI Core Curriculum for the Dialysis Technician, 7th edition, Modules 6, 10 and 11, and the MEI dialyzer reprocessing supplement  
**Before you start** — Review waste segregation, equipment readiness and water quality in Modules 5–7.

## Learning objectives

1. Tell single use apart from reuse within an authorized, validated dialyzer program.
2. Check and record the information that links an eligible dialyzer to the same patient, and confirm that it is ready.
3. Hold, reject, or escalate a device or record discrepancy through the designated procedure.
4. Describe the routine equipment quality checks, and tell the completion of maintenance apart from authorized return to service.

## Opening case · A passed check with an unreadable label

It is Saturday, just before the first shift. Ms. Joy Navarro, 23, a trainee technician, goes to the designated storage area to collect a dialyzer from the unit’s authorized reuse program. The reprocessing log shows that device **D-071** passed its previous processing checks. On the device label, one patient identifier cannot be read, and the other does not match the patient due this morning. The device is still in storage. The next shift is about to begin.

- Which finding decides whether this device can be used?
- What should Ms. Navarro do with the device, and what should she record?
- If the unit assigns a replacement, which checks still apply to it?

## 1. Single use and same-patient reuse

Think of a balikbayan box shipped home from abroad. However well it was packed, it reaches the right family only if the name and address on the label stay readable all the way. A box with a smudged label is held at the depot until someone proves whose it is. The picture has a limit. A misdelivered box is an inconvenience. A reused dialyzer belongs to one patient only, so in a reuse program the label is a safety check, not a convenience.

A single-use dialyzer is used for one treatment and then handled under the facility’s contaminated waste procedure. An authorized reuse program uses an eligible dialyzer on the **same patient** over several treatments, under a validated reprocessing program. Product labeling, the instructions for use (IFU), the applicable rules, and the facility’s written procedure decide whether a particular device may enter that program. Keep this workflow separate from routine station turnover. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [MEI · Dialyzer reprocessing supplement](https://mei.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Dialyzer_Reprocessing.pdf)

An authorized program controls cleaning and disinfection, water quality, device performance, identity, storage, records, and the checks made before use. It also states who may perform each check and who may make a release decision. For international comparison, U.S. requirements under 42 CFR § 494.50 call for same-patient use and labeling for multiple reuse, among other controls. In Philippine practice, apply the local facility’s approved procedure and requirements. If your placement uses only single-use dialyzers, you can learn this module through a traceability and disposition simulation. [U.S. CMS · Reuse of hemodialyzers and bloodlines](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-494/subpart-B/section-494.50) · [Philippine DOH · Dialysis water monitoring](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/69036)

## 2. Verify a traceable chain of records

**Traceability** means you can link the device to the correct patient, to its previous uses and processing history, to its current status, and to the staff who checked it. Compare the device label with the approved identifiers in the current patient record and with the matching processing log. Confirm that the use count and every required check are recorded. Two traps are common. A device with a readable label may still be missing a required check. A complete check record may belong to a different device.

![A decision diagram shows identity, processing records and pre-use checks leading to designated release, or a hold and escalation when verification fails.](Teaching%20Diagram.png)

*Figure 1. Traceability and disposition. The following table provides a text equivalent.*

| Verification area | What the approved record should let assigned staff confirm |
|---|---|
| **Identity and eligibility** | A patient match using approved identifiers, the device identifier or an equivalent traceable record, an eligible model, and confirmation that the dialyzer belongs to this same patient. |
| **Processing and storage** | The processing date or batch, the use count, who performed the checks, and whether the storage and readiness status meet facility rules. |
| **Device condition and performance** | The required visual, integrity, and performance checks. Total cell volume (TCV), where it is used, helps show whether the fibers are still open. The approved protocol sets the acceptance limits. |
| **Disinfectant safeguards** | A documented check of the chemical process, and a required residual disinfectant check before use where it applies. The residual check looks for chemical left behind after preparation. |
| **Disposition** | The current status, the reason for any hold or rejection, the designated reviewer, the approved replacement or disposal, and any authorized clearance. |

The test methods, chemical concentrations and limits, storage conditions, required exposure times, and maximum number of uses depend on the validated system and the requirements that apply. Practice finding each of them in the written procedure with your preceptor. Check each requirement on its own. A good result in one area does not clear a problem in another. [MEI · Dialyzer reprocessing supplement](https://mei.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Dialyzer_Reprocessing.pdf) · [Philippine DOH · Labeling, records and acceptable reprocessing](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687)

### Mock record · Review before use

All information below is fictional. The incomplete fields are there on purpose.

| Field | Mock entry |
|---|---|
| Current patient record | Training patient TR-204. Two facility-approved identifiers are in the exercise chart. |
| Device | D-071. Labeled for multiple use in this scenario. |
| Patient identifiers on label | One cannot be read. The other does not match the current patient. |
| Previous processing checks | Recorded as passed for D-071. |
| Prior use count | Three recorded uses. Compare with the local approved limit once identity and the other criteria are verified. |
| Pre-use residual check | Pending. |
| Current disposition | Held from use. Nurse and quality lead told. Designated review pending. |

**Case debrief.** *Which finding decides?* Identity. The passed processing checks cannot make up for a label that does not prove whose dialyzer this is. Withhold the device and put it in the designated hold area. Keep the label and records as they are so the discrepancy can be reviewed.

*What to do and record.* Tell the supervising nurse and the designated reprocessing or quality lead, and say exactly which identifiers failed. Do not guess the patient match from the schedule, and do not rebuild a label from memory. The authorized lead decides whether the identity can be verified and what happens to the device.

*If a replacement is assigned.* It must match the current order and pass the applicable preparation and checks before use, exactly as if the first device had never existed. [MEI · Same-patient labeling and verification](https://mei.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Dialyzer_Reprocessing.pdf)

## 3. Hold, reject and report exceptions

- **Hold and escalate.** Take any doubtful item out of ready stock and use the approved hold label or secure area. Examples are an unreadable identification, a missing use history, a failed or missing required test, a damaged device, and storage you are unsure of. Record the item, the time, the discrepancy, and whom you told.
- **Reject or dispose under the approved procedure.** Designated staff apply the written rejection criteria and record the final disposition. Keep items or records needed for an incident investigation as directed. Follow the disposal procedure for contaminated waste or chemical waste.
- **Escalate patient concerns at once.** During or after treatment, report promptly any new symptom or any suspected problem with the device or its reprocessing. When more than one patient is affected, or a shared process is suspected, report that pattern in your first sentence. The clinical and quality teams direct treatment, any quarantine of related items, the investigation, and outside reporting where it applies. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [U.S. CMS · Monitoring and reporting in reuse programs](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-494/subpart-B/section-494.50)

**Example handoff.** “At 06:35 I held D-071. One patient identifier was illegible and the other could not be matched. Previous processing checks were marked passed. Pre-use residual verification is pending, and no patient was connected. The device and records are available for designated review.” Notice that it separates what passed from what failed, and says what is still pending.

## 4. Equipment quality and authorized release

A machine that has been repaired is like a car back from the casa: the mechanic has finished, but someone still has to test it and sign it out before it carries passengers. The table separates the work from the release.

| Activity | What the technician verifies or records within assigned scope |
|---|---|
| **Routine pre-use checks** | The equipment ID, its service status, any disinfection or other readiness checks that apply, supply compatibility, and the required test results. |
| **Preventive maintenance** | Assigned staff perform and record the scheduled work using the maintenance instructions for that model. The machine’s current maintenance status should be traceable. |
| **Fault response** | Hold the affected equipment, record the exact message or what you saw, and tell the supervising nurse and the equipment staff. Do not record a guess as a confirmed fault. |
| **Return to service** | Designated staff confirm that the fault is fixed and that the required checks after service are recorded. Confirm the formal release under the facility procedure before use. A normal display or a closed work ticket is not, by itself, a release. |
| **Quality review** | Patterns such as repeated faults, incomplete checks, or repeated labeling problems can guide a review. Give the designated lead objective facts. |

The model’s IFU and service documents define its specific checks. Learn who may perform operator checks, who may service equipment, and who may authorize its return. [Philippine DOH · Dialysis facility standards and equipment care](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50687) · [Fresenius Medical Care · Equipment technical documentation](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/support/product-support-documents/technical-documentation/)
