# 19. Integrated readiness and improvement capstone

**Core** · 45 minutes study · 120 minutes supervised practice · Source review date: November 24, 2026
**Figures:** FIG-19 (Understand) · FIG-19B (Apply) · FIG-19C (Appreciate) · **Calculator:** all track calculators (CALC-01 to CALC-13)

> **Why this matters.** Real problems never arrive one module at a time. A typhoon warning lands in the same week as a nurse resignation, a returned claims batch and an out-of-range water culture. The capstone tests whether you can hold all of it at once: protect patients first, route each decision to its owner, use numbers to argue for what the unit needs, and leave a written trail that a surveyor, an owner or your successor can follow.

## Learning objectives

1. Lead a simulated 72-hour scenario that combines clinical, technical, staffing, payer and financial pressures.
2. Prioritize actions by patient risk and route each to its authorized owner.
3. Use at least four track calculators to support decisions.
4. Defend a readiness and improvement portfolio before a panel.

## How the capstone works

**Part A: integrated simulation (60 minutes).** The preceptor releases scenario injects every 10 to 15 minutes. You run the response as manager, with colleagues or the preceptor playing the head nurse, Medical Director, water technician, billing officer and owner.

**Part B: portfolio defense (40 minutes).** You present your portfolio (below) and answer questions.

**Part C: independent review (35 minutes).** Thirteen short-answer questions in the trainer guide, self-checked against the model key.

## Sample scenario: "The week before Signal No. 3"

**Day 1, 07:00.** PAGASA forecasts a typhoon landfall in 72 hours. Your unit has 84 patients on a Monday-Wednesday-Friday and Tuesday-Thursday-Saturday schedule.

**Inject 1.** The head nurse reports that two nurses have resigned effective Friday and one is on maternity leave.
**Inject 2.** The monthly loop culture returns at 70 CFU/mL (action level 50, maximum 100 under the facility program).
**Inject 3.** Billing reports that 38 claims from last month were returned for documentation errors.
**Inject 4.** The owner asks whether the unit can close Thursday and Friday to protect staff.
**Inject 5.** A patient on Tuesday-Thursday-Saturday has used 152 of 156 sessions.
**Inject 6.** The generator fuel supplier says delivery may be delayed.

**What a strong response includes**

- **Clinical priority:** Medical Director decides on early dialysis; high-risk list (long-gap, high potassium, catheter, new starts) prepared; no patient left more than three days without treatment.
- **Water:** action-level response with the technician (disinfect and retest) and Medical Director awareness; release status confirmed before the extra sessions; no assumption that "below maximum means fine."
- **Staffing:** CALC-01 run for the compressed schedule; float or agency staff with verified competencies; overtime and holiday premiums computed with CALC-10; nursing lead approves the roster.
- **Continuity:** CALC-12 run with current fuel and water; tanker and fuel alternatives activated; partner units alerted; patient emergency sheets and diet guidance distributed.
- **Owner:** a factual reply proposing schedule compression rather than closure, with staffing and cost implications.
- **Payer:** returned claims assigned for correction after the storm with a deadline; CALC-07 used to counsel the patient near the cap and inform the attending.
- **Records:** decision log with times and owners; CAPA opened for the claims errors and the water result.

## Scoring

The trainer uses the 0–2 rubric in the trainer guide. A score of 0 on any safety-critical item (missed hold, unauthorized clinical decision, clearance bypass, disclosure of patient data) stops the capstone and requires supervised reassessment.

## Question bank

**Q1.** A typhoon is forecast in 72 hours. Who decides on early dialysis?
- [x] The Medical Director and clinical team
- [ ] The owner
- [ ] The billing officer
- [ ] The generator vendor
> Early dialysis is a clinical decision supported by operations.

**Q2.** A loop culture is 70 CFU/mL with action level 50 and maximum 100. The correct response is:
- [x] Action-level response: disinfect, retest and inform the Medical Director
- [ ] No action, since it is below the maximum
- [ ] Permanent closure
- [ ] Copy last month's result
> Action levels exist to prevent reaching the maximum.

**Q3.** The owner proposes closing two days before the storm. The best reply is:
- [x] Propose schedule compression with staffing and cost implications
- [ ] Agree immediately
- [ ] Refuse without explanation
- [ ] Ask patients to decide
> Closure creates dangerous gaps; compression protects patients.

**Q4.** Which calculator tells you whether water or fuel limits operations during an outage?
- [x] CALC-12
- [ ] CALC-09
- [ ] CALC-11
- [ ] CALC-06
> Outage autonomy compares generator runtime and water storage.

**Q5.** A patient has used 152 of 156 sessions in late November. The right action is to:
- [x] Use CALC-07, counsel the patient early and inform the attending
- [ ] Reduce dialysis frequency administratively
- [ ] Ignore it until the cap is reached
- [ ] Charge the patient without notice
> Financial limits must not silently change treatment.

**Q6.** Which is a safety-critical failure that stops the capstone?
- [x] Bypassing water release
- [ ] A typo in a report
- [ ] A slightly late presentation
- [ ] Using a different chart color
> Clearance bypass is a stop item.

**Q7.** Two nurses resign effective Friday. The first quantitative step is:
- [x] Run CALC-01 for the revised schedule
- [ ] Post on social media
- [ ] Reduce ratios to 1:6
- [ ] Wait until Monday
> Numbers define the gap and the options.

**Q8.** Returned claims during a crisis week should be:
- [x] Assigned with a correction deadline after immediate safety work
- [ ] Written off
- [ ] Resubmitted unchanged
- [ ] Ignored permanently
> Prioritize safety, but do not lose the revenue.

**Q9.** Holiday and overtime pay for extra shifts should be computed with:
- [x] CALC-10
- [ ] CALC-02
- [ ] CALC-04
- [ ] CALC-13
> Premium pay rules depend on day type and hours.

**Q10.** What makes a decision log useful afterward?
- [x] Times, decisions, owners and outcomes recorded as they happen
- [ ] Notes written a week later from memory
- [ ] Only the final outcome
- [ ] Photos without captions
> Contemporaneous records support review and accountability.

**Q11.** Which patients are on the high-risk list before a storm?
- [x] Long-gap, high potassium, catheter-dependent and new-start patients
- [ ] Those with the longest commute only
- [ ] Those with private insurance
- [ ] Those who complain most
> Prioritize by physiologic risk.

**Q12.** Portfolio artifacts must use:
- [x] Fictional or de-identified data
- [ ] Full patient names and diagnoses
- [ ] Staff home addresses
- [ ] Real claim numbers with patient identifiers
> Privacy rules apply to training materials.

**Q13.** A float nurse without signed dialysis competencies is offered. You should:
- [x] Use her only for tasks within her competencies
- [ ] Assign four patients
- [ ] Assign the isolation area
- [ ] Refuse all help
> Competency limits hold in emergencies too.

**Q14.** After the storm, treatment resumes only when:
- [x] Power, water quality and staffing are confirmed and released
- [ ] The first patient arrives
- [ ] The owner calls
- [ ] The billing system is online
> Phased reopening follows release.

**Q15.** The central skill the capstone tests is:
- [x] Prioritizing by patient risk and routing each decision to its authorized owner with evidence
- [ ] Memorizing every regulation
- [ ] Doing every task personally
- [ ] Avoiding all documentation
> Integration, routing and evidence define effective management.
