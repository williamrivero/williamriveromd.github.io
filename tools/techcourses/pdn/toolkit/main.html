1. Body surface area and PD fill volume check

Mosteller body surface area (BSA) = √(height in cm × weight in kg ÷ 3,600). The ordered fill volume is shown in mL/m² and mL/kg. Reference bands from Fischbach and Warady (2009): from age 2, peritoneal recruitment rises up to about 1,400 mL/m²; under age 2, about 800 mL/m² (30–50 mL/kg) is often suggested as an upper limit, with tolerance deciding. A flag means “confirm with the team”, never “safe” or “unsafe” (Modules 7 and 10).

2. Extracorporeal volume check

Estimated blood volume uses a teaching approximation of 80 mL/kg in children (85 mL/kg under 1 year). The ESPN practical guidelines (Fischbach 2005) state that total extracorporeal volume (lines, needles and dialyzer) should be approximately less than 10% of blood volume. Enter the printed volumes for your lines and dialyzer. Above 10%, confirm the priming plan with the pediatric nephrologist (Module 13).

3. Blood flow check

The ordered blood flow (QB) is shown in mL/min/kg and mL/min/m² beside the ranges described in the ESPN practical guidelines (2005): 5–7 mL/min/kg or 150–200 mL/min/m² often sufficient; about 3 mL/kg/min for a first session; and the small-child rule of thumb (weight + 10) × 2.5 mL/min. These are expert descriptions, not targets; the order decides (Modules 12 and 14).

4. Fluid removal check

Hemodialysis: ultrafiltration rate = volume ÷ hours ÷ weight, in mL/kg/h and as % of body weight per hour. PD: net removal for one exchange = drained volume − filled volume. Arithmetic verifies the order; whether the child tolerates the rate is a clinical judgment (Modules 4, 9 and 14).

5. Percent fluid overload

%FO = (fluid in − fluid out, in liters) ÷ intensive care admission weight (kg) × 100, the definition used by the prospective pediatric CRRT registry (Sutherland 2010). In that observational registry, mortality was 29.4% below 10%, 43.1% at 10–20% and 65.6% at 20% or more; the bands are context, not treatment thresholds (Module 16).

6. Infection rates

PD peritonitis (or exit-site infection) rate = episodes ÷ (patient-months at risk ÷ 12), per patient-year. The ISPD 2024 pediatric benchmark for overall peritonitis is 0.4 or fewer episodes per year at risk (not graded). Hemodialysis catheter-associated bloodstream infection (CA-BSI) = episodes ÷ catheter patient-months × 100, the unit the SCOPE collaborative reported. Small programs have wide uncertainty; read trends and causes (Modules 11 and 20).

Important: These tools verify arithmetic against a written order and display published reference ranges. They are not prescribing tools and do not say whether a value is safe for a particular child; that decision belongs to the pediatric nephrologist. Estimated blood volume is a teaching approximation. Use no patient names or identifiers; nothing you enter is stored or sent.
