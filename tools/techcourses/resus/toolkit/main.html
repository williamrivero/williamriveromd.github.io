Teaching and drill tools for the Renal Care Academy Resuscitation Lab. They do arithmetic and display, not clinical decisions. They are not BLS, ACLS or PALS certification, and they do not replace your unit’s protocol, order set, length-based tape, dose card or the attending physician. Nothing you enter leaves this page.

1. CPR interruption and compression-fraction analyzer

Chest compression fraction (CCF) is the share of the arrest observation time spent compressing: CCF = (observation time − total pause time) ÷ observation time × 100. The 2025 AHA adult BLS guideline says a fraction of at least 60% may be reasonable (COR 2b, LOE C-LD) and that pulse checks should take no more than 10 seconds (COR 1). Pauses over 10 seconds are flagged. State the data source: browser timing measures time only, never compression depth or ventilation quality.

2. Megacode timeline analyzer

Paste a drill log, one event per line, as mm:ss event. Recognized events: recognition, compressions start, compressions stop, pads on, shock, epinephrine, amiodarone, rhythm check, circuit stopped, blood returned, blood clamped, airway, ROSC, handover, end. The Practice Lab facilitator recorder exports this format. Times are measured from recognition (or 00:00). Compression fraction is computed from compressions start/stop pairs between the first compression and ROSC or end. This is an educational result, not a predicted patient outcome.

3. Pediatric resuscitation dose-and-volume trainer

A trainer for the arithmetic of weight-based doses, using the doses printed on the 2025 AHA/AAP pediatric algorithms: epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg of 0.1 mg/mL (maximum 1 mg); amiodarone 5 mg/kg (maximum 300 mg); lidocaine 1 mg/kg; adenosine 0.1 mg/kg (maximum 6 mg), repeat 0.2 mg/kg (maximum 12 mg); defibrillation 2 J/kg, then 4 J/kg, later up to 10 J/kg or the adult maximum; synchronized cardioversion 0.5 to 1 J/kg, then 2 J/kg; fluid bolus 10 or 20 mL/kg (septic shock, with reassessment). Every step is shown. After puberty, adult guidance applies. Use your unit’s length-based tape or approved dose card at the bedside; a second person checks every dose.

4. Oxygen cylinder duration planner

Estimates how long a cylinder lasts at a given flow, for transfer planning: usable liters = full content × (current pressure − reserve pressure) ÷ full pressure; minutes = usable liters ÷ flow. Use the content and full pressure printed on your cylinder (any pressure unit, used consistently). The estimate assumes the cylinder’s stated capacity and a steady flow.

5. Drill coverage and role-rotation planner

Builds a rotation so that, over a series of drills, each staff member practices different code roles. Enter initials only (no full names needed). The watcher role is included so it is practiced too. Nothing is stored; print or copy the table.
