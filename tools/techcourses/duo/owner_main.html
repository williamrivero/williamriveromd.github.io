These tools do arithmetic, not decisions: a result never overrides a clinical or technical hold, a staffing rule, or the attending nephrologist. Nothing you enter leaves this page.

1. Contribution margin and break-even volume

Contribution per session = net facility revenue per session − variable cost per session. Break-even sessions per month = fixed cost ÷ contribution. If contribution is zero or negative, no volume breaks even, and the calculator says so instead of giving a number (Course Modules 7 and 16).

2. Cost per treatment across volumes, with a step cost

Fixed cost spreads over more sessions as volume rises, so cost per treatment falls, until a step (a new nurse team, a third shift) adds fixed cost at once. Test the step before you promise the volume (Course Module 16).

3. Physical capacity versus safely staffed capacity

Weekly station-sessions = usable stations × shifts per day × operating days. The safe number is the smaller of that and what your cleared staff can cover. Patients supported = safe station-sessions ÷ sessions each patient needs per week. Never plan from physical capacity alone (Course Module 8).

4. Staffing: full-time equivalents with a relief factor

FTE = posts per shift × shifts per week × hours per shift ÷ contracted hours per week. The relief factor adds cover for leave, training, and sickness. The ratio of patients to staff comes from your licensing rule and your clinical head, not from this tool (Course Module 10).

5. Cash tied in receivables, days sales outstanding, and runway

A profitable unit can still run out of cash. Cash tied up = daily net revenue × days to collect. Days sales outstanding (DSO) = receivables ÷ daily net revenue. Runway = cash on hand ÷ weekly cash costs (Course Module 17).

6. Loan payment and debt service coverage ratio

Level monthly payment on an amortizing loan, and the debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) = annual cash available for debt service ÷ annual debt payments. Lenders often look for a cushion above 1; confirm your lender’s covenant (Course Module 7).

7. Capital project: simple payback and net present value

Payback = capital cost ÷ yearly net cash. Net present value (NPV) discounts each year’s cash back to today. A positive NPV at an honest discount rate is a better test than payback alone. Use it for a new water system, extra stations, or a generator (Course Modules 7 and 11).

8. What the patient pays in a year

Under PhilHealth Circular 2024-0023 the package (default 6,350 pesos per session, facility and professional fees included, up to 156 sessions a year) covers the minimum standard of care with no co-payment. A patient pays only for optional additional or premium services beyond that minimum, explained in advance, with an additional professional fee capped at 450 pesos, and for sessions beyond the yearly limit. Confirm the current circular before quoting a patient (Course Modules 14 and 16).

Formula, sources & limitations

The formulas are standard managerial-accounting and finance arithmetic: contribution margin, break-even, the level-payment loan formula, discounted cash flow, and days sales outstanding. They describe money; they do not show that any spending level is safe. Staffing and capacity limits come from the Department of Health (DOH) licensing rules, the Philippine Society of Nephrology (PSN) guidelines, and your clinical and technical heads. The PhilHealth defaults follow PhilHealth Circular No. 2024-0023, the hemodialysis benefits agreement form, and PhilHealth’s October 2024 release (6,350 pesos per session, inclusive of facility and professional fees; up to 156 sessions per calendar year; no co-payment for the minimum standard of care; additional professional fees capped at 450 pesos), checked November 25, 2026. Payer rules change: confirm the current circular before you rely on a figure.
