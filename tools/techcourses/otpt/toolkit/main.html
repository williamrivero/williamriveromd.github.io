1. Six-minute walk: percent predicted

Compares a six-minute walk distance (6MWD) with the Enright–Sherrill reference equations for healthy adults aged 40 to 80 (men: 7.57 × height cm − 5.02 × age − 1.76 × weight kg − 309; women: 2.11 × height cm − 2.29 × weight kg − 5.78 × age + 667). A reference point, not a Filipino norm: the person’s own trend matters most (Module 5).

2. Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) scorer

The SPPB scores balance, 4-meter usual gait and five chair rises, 0 to 4 each, total 0 to 12, using the widely used 4-meter scoring of the battery developed by Guralnik and colleagues. Guard closely; stop if the person becomes dizzy (Modules 3 and 5).

3. Gait speed and Timed Up and Go log

Gait speed is distance ÷ time. In a pooled analysis of older adults, each 0.1 m/s faster was associated with better survival, and in CKD each 0.1 m/s slower was associated with about 26 percent higher risk of death; both are observational. Enter a baseline and a follow-up walk time, and optionally the Timed Up and Go (TUG), to see the change (Modules 5 and 8).

4. Sit-to-stand change tracker

Tracks the 60-second sit-to-stand (STS60; repetitions, higher is better) or the five-times sit-to-stand (seconds; lower is better) between two tests under the same conditions. For context, the Dialysis Training Therapy (DiaTT) trial’s between-group difference after 12 months of intradialytic exercise was 3.85 STS60 repetitions; that is a group average, not an individual target (Module 10).

5. Weekly activity minutes

Adds up a week of planned activity. Minutes at moderate effort (rating of perceived exertion, RPE, 12 to 14 on the 6 to 20 Borg scale) count toward the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) 2024 aim of at least 150 minutes a week, or to a level compatible with cardiovascular and physical tolerance (graded 1D). Light minutes are shown separately (Modules 6 and 9).

6. Session screen log

Records a pre-session screen against the limits your unit’s physician has set. The limit fields are blank on purpose: this track sets no universal exercise-clearance numbers. The tool only compares what you enter with the limits you enter and prints a log line; the decision and any call are yours and the team’s (Module 3).

Important: These tools do arithmetic and record-keeping, not clinical decisions. They do not clear anyone for exercise, change any dialysis setting or diagnose anything. Exercise parameters come from the referring physician and your unit’s agreed sheet; per unit protocol, but always ask the attending nephrologist’s guidance. Use no patient names or identifiers; nothing you enter is stored or sent. Reviewed November 25, 2026.
