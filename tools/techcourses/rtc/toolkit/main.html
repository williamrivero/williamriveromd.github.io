1. Four-gate readiness board

Readiness means all four gates (medical, legal, ethical, insurance) are closed for both the recipient and the living donor by the people who own them. This board only displays what you enter; the gate owners close the gates (Modules 1 and 14).

2. Barrier register aging

A barrier row closes only on evidence from the service that owns it. This tool turns the dates on an open row into days open, days to or past the due date, and an escalation tier (Modules 1 and 19).

3. Pre-authorization validity timer

Under PhilHealth’s kidney transplant package (PC 2024-0035; Revision 1, PC 2026-0019, published November 21, 2026), an approved pre-authorization is valid for 180 calendar days. For deceased donors the request is due within two working days after the procedure. Confirm the circular in force for your case (Module 10).

4. Claim filing window

The contracted facility files the kidney transplant claim within 60 days after discharge; late filing is a stated ground for denial. Enter the discharge date to see the deadline and the days left (Module 11).

5. Out-of-pocket gap estimator

Estimated gap = facility estimate − package rate − assistance confirmed in writing. Package rates are those listed in PC 2024-0035 and unchanged in PC 2026-0019. The package rate is not the bill; the result is an estimate. Donation-related care is never charged to the donor (Modules 10 and 11).

6. Medicine supply runway

Days of supply = tablets on hand ÷ tablets per day. The refill must start before the run-out date minus the pharmacy’s lead time, counted in working days and around closures. The team decides doses; this tool only counts (Module 15).

7. Living donor follow-up planner

An example schedule from the donation date: 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, then yearly. KDIGO 2017 and DOH AO 2021-0059 support lifelong donor follow-up; the 6-month, 1-year, and 2-year points mirror United States reporting and are shown for comparison. Align every date with your program’s protocol (Modules 7 and 17).

Important: These tools do arithmetic and display, not decisions. Medical, ethical, legal, and benefit decisions belong to the transplant team, the ethics committee, the surgeon, and PhilHealth under the current issuances. Use no patient names or identifiers; nothing you enter is stored or sent. Rules and rates change: confirm the circular in force for each case. Reviewed November 25, 2026.
