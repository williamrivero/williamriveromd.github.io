Meet the Med Scheduler, in Dosis. It turns a long prescription into one clear daily timetable, for the clinic and for home.
Start with the patient's day: when they wake, eat and sleep. For hemodialysis, pick the dialysis days and the session times.
Then add the medicines. Search them one by one, or paste the whole prescription, and the scheduler reads how often each one is taken.
Each dose is placed by rules: meal and time-of-day rules from the drug label, spacing between medicines that block each other, and the fewest dose times possible.
Dialysis days get their own timetable. Doses that fall during the session move to after it, and blood pressure medicines can wait until dialysis is done.
Every placement shows why: the label, the evidence, or a practice default. If a rule cannot be met, like levothyroxine four hours away from a phosphate binder, it says so at the top.
When the schedule looks right, copy it into the prescription remarks, print a tick-box sheet for the patient, and check the pill count for the month.
The sample prescriptions are fictional, so always review the schedule before you give it to a patient. You will find it in Dosis, the Drug Index.
