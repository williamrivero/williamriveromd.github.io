## Section Blueprint

### §1 — Who Qualifies? `#who-qualifies`
**Section tag:** Eligibility
**Format:** Eligibility card + inclusion/exclusion table

**Key legal basis:**
- RA 7277 (Magna Carta for Disabled Persons, 1992) + RA 10754 (2016 amendments expanding benefits)
- NCDA (National Council on Disability Affairs) administrative guidance: chronic kidney disease causing functional limitation qualifies as a disability under "chronic illness" category
- HB 05136 (19th Congress): specifically proposes formal codification of CKD Stage 5 as a qualifying disability — currently under deliberation but existing law already supports qualification under RA 10754

**Who qualifies (CKD):**
✅ CKD Stage 5 (eGFR <15) — whether or not on dialysis
✅ On regular hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis
✅ Post-kidney transplant patients with significant functional limitation (case-by-case per LGU)
✅ CKD with documented functional impairment affecting activities of daily living (nephrologist certification required)

**Who likely does NOT qualify based on CKD alone:**
❌ CKD Stage 1–3 without significant functional limitation
❌ CKD Stage 4 without documented functional impairment

**Important note:** "There is no single national list of CKD stages that automatically qualify. The key is your doctor's certification that your kidney disease causes substantial limitation in daily activities. A nephrologist who documents this clearly makes the difference between approval and rejection."

**Amber callout:** "Even if your LGU has previously told you that CKD does not qualify — bring this guide and your nephrologist's certification. RA 10754 does not exclude CKD. Many LGU rejections result from a clerk's misunderstanding, not the law."

### §2 — What Benefits Does a PWD Card Give You? `#benefits`
**Section tag:** Your Rights
**Format:** Benefit cards with peso-savings calculator context

**Core benefits under RA 10754:**

| Benefit | What it means for CKD/dialysis patients |
|---|---|
| **20% discount on dialysis sessions** | On the service fee charged by dialysis centers — applies on top of PhilHealth coverage |
| **20% discount on prescription medicines** | Immunosuppressants, EPO, phosphate binders, antihypertensives, etc. |
| **20% discount on medical/lab services** | Creatinine, eGFR, CBC, PTH, dialysis-related labs |
| **20% discount on medical equipment** | BP monitors, weighing scales, dialysis consumables in some settings |
| **VAT exemption** | Purchases of medicines and medical goods are VAT-free |
| **Priority lane** | At government offices, hospitals, dialysis centers, pharmacies |
| **5% tax deduction** | For establishments giving the discount (benefit to provider, not patient) |

**Savings illustration (amber callout):**
"At ₱3,000 out-of-pocket per session after PhilHealth, a 20% PWD discount saves ₱600 per session — or ₱93,600 per year on 3×/week dialysis. That is more than one month of dialysis sessions saved."

**Important limitation:** The 20% discount applies to the remaining balance after PhilHealth — it does NOT stack as 20% on the full session price. Calculate from your actual out-of-pocket, not from the gross fee.

**Also note:** Senior Citizens (60+) with CKD can use BOTH the Senior Citizen discount and PWD discount — apply whichever is higher for each transaction. They cannot be combined on a single transaction.

### §3 — Documents to Prepare `#documents`
**Section tag:** Document Checklist
**Format:** Numbered checklist with notes on each item

**Primary documents (ALL required):**
1. **Accomplished PWD Registration Form** — available at your City/Municipal Social Welfare and Development Office (C/MSWDO) or the LGU health office; also sometimes downloadable from LGU website
2. **Medical Certificate from a Licensed Physician** — specifically your nephrologist; must state:
   - Diagnosis (CKD Stage 5 or ESRD; or dialysis-dependent CKD)
   - That the condition causes "substantial limitation in one or more major life activities"
   - Physician's PRC license number + clinic address + contact
   - Date of certificate (usually accepted within 6 months of application)
3. **1 recent 1×1 ID photo** (white background)
4. **PSA Birth Certificate** (original or certified true copy) — for identity verification
5. **Any government-issued ID with photo** — PhilHealth card, UMID, Voter's ID, passport, driver's license
6. **Proof of residence** — barangay certification or utility bill in your name

**Optional but helpful:**
- PhilHealth ID or MDR (Member Data Record) — shows PhilHealth number for coordinating benefits
- Previous hospital discharge abstract or laboratory results showing CKD Stage 5/dialysis-dependence — supports the medical certificate

**Common rejection reasons:**
❌ Medical certificate does not mention "functional limitation" or "disability"
❌ Medical certificate from a GP only (no nephrologist signature); LGU may require specialist
❌ Photo does not meet specifications
❌ Birth certificate not PSA-certified

**Green callout:** "Ask your nephrologist specifically: 'Please include in my medical certificate that my kidney disease substantially limits my daily activities.' This exact phrase aligns with RA 10754 language and reduces rejection risk."

### §4 — Where and How to Apply: Step by Step `#how-to-apply`
**Section tag:** Application Steps
**Format:** Numbered steps with sub-notes; HowTo schema

**Step 1: Gather all documents** (§3 checklist above)
→ Get your medical certificate first — it is the longest step if your nephrologist needs to prepare it.

**Step 2: Go to your City/Municipal Social Welfare and Development Office (C/MSWDO)**
→ This is the correct office for most LGUs. In some cities (e.g., Manila, Quezon City, Davao), the Health Office processes PWD registration instead.
→ Call your barangay hall first to confirm which office handles PWD registration in your specific LGU.
→ Bring originals AND one photocopy of each document.

**Step 3: Fill out the PWD Registration Form**
→ Form is provided at the office (free of charge — do NOT pay anyone who charges a form fee).
→ Disability category: select "Chronic Illness" — kidney disease falls here.
→ Disability cause: write "Chronic Kidney Disease, Stage 5 / Dialysis-Dependent"

**Step 4: Submit documents; photo is taken at the office (usually)**
→ Some LGUs take a new photo on-site; others use your submitted 1×1 photo.
→ Keep all original documents — they should be returned to you after photocopying.

**Step 5: Receive your PWD ID**
→ Processing time varies: same day in some LGUs; 1–4 weeks in others.
→ Ask the officer for a receiving slip/acknowledgment receipt while waiting.
→ The ID is free of charge.

**Step 6: Register with the NCDA National Registry (optional but recommended)**
→ Go to ncda.gov.ph or ask your LGU officer — national registry gives your PWD ID broader recognition if you travel or use the discount in other cities.

**Map note:** "PWD registration is done at your LGU where you are registered — not necessarily where your dialysis center is located. Bring your barangay certificate of residency."

### §5 — Using Your PWD Card at Your Dialysis Center `#using-the-card`
**Section tag:** At Your Dialysis Center
**Format:** Practical tips + FAQ callouts

**At a dialysis center:**
- Present your PWD ID at registration before each session
- The 20% discount applies to the portion YOU pay (after PhilHealth benefit deduction)
- Ask the billing staff to show you the computation — you have the right to see how the discount was applied
- If refused: ask for the name and position of the person refusing; cite RA 10754; escalate to the center administrator

**At a pharmacy:**
- Present PWD ID + prescription
- Discount applies to brand-name AND generic medicines
- VAT exemption applies simultaneously (you should not pay 12% VAT on medicines)
- Keep your Official Receipt — you may need it for reimbursement claims

**At a laboratory:**
- Present PWD ID before the lab request is processed (not after)
- 20% applies to most diagnostic lab services ordered by your nephrologist

**Refusal of discount:**
- Establishments that refuse the PWD discount violate RA 10754 and may be reported to the DTI (Department of Trade and Industry) or NCDA
- You can also report to your LGU's PWD Affairs Office (PDAO)

**Red callout:** "Some private dialysis centers attempt to apply the PWD discount only to the 'facility fee' and exclude the 'professional fee.' This is incorrect — RA 10754 applies to the full service charge. Politely clarify and escalate if needed."

### §6 — Renewals and Replacements `#renewals`
**Section tag:** Keeping Your Card Valid
**Format:** Simple checklist

- **Validity:** PWD ID cards do not have a fixed national expiration — but some LGUs set 1–3 year validity. Check your card.
- **Renewal:** Return to the same C/MSWDO with updated medical certificate (some LGUs only require a valid government ID + updated photo for renewal)
- **Lost card:** Report to LGU; bring same documents as original application + an affidavit of loss (₱100–200 at a notary)
- **Moving to a new LGU:** Re-register at your new LGU. Your old card may still work in many establishments but formal transfer is recommended.
- **If your condition improves (e.g., post-transplant with near-normal eGFR):** Technically the PWD status may be re-evaluated at renewal. Discuss with your nephrologist whether continued certification is appropriate.

### §7 — FAQ `#faq` (10 questions)
1. "My nephrologist is in Manila but I live in Cebu. Where do I apply?" → Apply at your LGU of residence (Cebu), not where your doctor practices. Your doctor's medical certificate is valid regardless of where issued.
2. "Can my family member apply on my behalf?" → Yes — with a Special Power of Attorney (SPA) signed by the patient and notarized.
3. "I'm on dialysis but my eGFR is sometimes above 15 after a session. Do I still qualify?" → Post-dialysis eGFR values are not representative. The basis is your clinical status (dialysis-dependent), not a single post-session lab. Your nephrologist's certificate covers this.
4. "I have a Senior Citizen card already. Do I still need a PWD card?" → If you are 60+ and have CKD, you may already have a 20% senior discount. Compare which discount applies in each specific setting. Some PhilHealth-covered services may benefit more from the PWD card. Having both is not prohibited.
5. "My LGU said CKD doesn't qualify. Is that correct?" → This is a common misunderstanding by some LGU staff. RA 10754 does not enumerate specific diseases — it uses functional limitation as the standard. Request to see the specific provision they are citing, or ask to speak with the PDAO (PWD Affairs Office) officer.
6. "Can my child with CKD get a PWD card?" → Yes — the same process applies. A parent or guardian signs the application. Pediatric nephrologist certification is required.
7. "Does the PWD card cover dialysis consumables I buy myself (e.g., PD bags)?" → Yes — medicines and medical equipment purchases qualify. Confirm with the supplier that they accept PWD discount.
8. "I'm undocumented or don't have a birth certificate. Can I still apply?" → PSA birth certificate is standard, but you can pursue late registration at PSA or use other documents. Contact your barangay for guidance on late birth registration.
9. "Does the discount apply at PhilHealth-accredited government hospitals?" → Yes — the 20% applies to your remaining balance after PhilHealth deduction at any establishment, government or private.
10. "How long does the application process take?" → Same-day to 4 weeks depending on LGU. Processing time is not governed by a national standard. Bring all documents complete on first visit to avoid multiple trips.
