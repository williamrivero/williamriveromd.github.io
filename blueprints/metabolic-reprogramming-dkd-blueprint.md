# Metabolic Reprogramming in Diabetic Kidney Disease: From Fuel Selection to Ferroptosis

**RenalCareMatters guide blueprint for Claude Code · Evidence checked 8 November 2026**

### Suggested opening copy

> The kidney uses energy to reclaim salt, water and nutrients from the fluid it filters. In diabetes, some kidney cells face a heavier workload just as their ability to use fuel and repair damage changes. One result can be damage to the fats that make up cell membranes. In experiments, this damage can trigger an iron-dependent form of cell death called ferroptosis. For people with diabetes today, urine albumin and estimated kidney function are still the tests that guide treatment.

Add three takeaways near the top. Fuel use varies across kidney cells and stages of disease. Human kidney studies show changes consistent with ferroptosis, while experiments test whether those changes cause cell death. Kidney-protective treatment is selected using the clinical phenotype and trial evidence.

## Page outline and copy direction

### 1. A kidney uses different fuels in different places

Use a nephron schematic with three selectable regions and a companion text table. Use qualitative labels for fuel dependence and show oxygen gradients as context without numerical oxygen values.

| Region | What to teach | What changes in DKD |
|---|---|---|
| Proximal tubule | High ATP demand for sodium-coupled transport. Fatty acid oxidation (FAO) and mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation support much of the workload. The cortex can also produce glucose through gluconeogenesis. Glucose reabsorption through SGLT2 and downstream SGLT1 is transport, while glucose oxidation and glucose production are separate processes. | Early diabetes may increase transport work and substrate flux. Injured or fibrotic tubules can lose oxidative capacity and show glycolytic and lipid-handling changes. Separate cell state, stage, and experimental model. |
| Thick ascending limb and more distal segments | The thick ascending limb still spends considerable ATP on transport. Segments differ in glycolytic capacity and oxygen supply. Show the low oxygen reserve of the outer medulla without treating the entire medulla as one cell type. | Transport redistribution can change local oxygen demand. Use dashed arrows for proposed regional oxygen effects of therapy and distinguish oxygen delivery from oxygen consumption. |
| Glomerulus and podocytes | Podocytes maintain the filtration barrier and can draw on glycolytic and mitochondrial pathways. Energy use differs by context. | Lipid remodeling, oxidant stress and podocyte injury may contribute to albumin leakage. Podocyte and tubular injury can coexist. |

### 2. From higher transport work to loss of metabolic flexibility

Use a two-state illustration with a branching path and overlapping states. On the left, show excess filtered glucose and sodium, increased proximal transport, and higher ATP and oxygen demand in early disease. On the right, show how mitochondrial stress, altered FAO and impaired repair can coexist with glycolytic pathway activation and fibrosis. Draw a feedback loop between mitochondrial injury and reduced oxidative capacity. State that some patients lose eGFR without marked albuminuria and that the diagram does not assign a universal temporal sequence.

In the clinician detail, distinguish PPARα and PGC-1α regulation, CPT1-mediated mitochondrial entry of long-chain fatty acids, β-oxidation, the TCA cycle and the respiratory chain. Explain that abundance of an acylcarnitine may reflect several processes, including entry, incomplete oxidation and clearance. Separate lipid uptake, synthesis, neutral storage and membrane incorporation. Triglyceride droplets may buffer fatty acids in some settings. Oxidation-prone membrane lipids belong in the ferroptosis branch. Gene expression, static metabolite pools and tracer flux should each have distinct visual labels.

Reconcile the early db/db isotope data showing increased cortical nutrient flux with the human fibrotic-tubule data showing lower FAO pathway expression. Species, stage, compartment and measurement differ. Avoid inferring impaired FAO from every lipid signal or imposing a kidney-wide switch to glycolysis. [Sas et al.](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/86976) · [Kang et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/nm.3762) · [Sharma et al. human urine metabolomics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23949796/)

### 3. How a vulnerable membrane becomes a site of injury

Design an original three-lane schematic. Use numbered steps and the same reading order in its text alternative.

1. **Substrate.** ACSL4 activates susceptible polyunsaturated fatty acids. Remodeling enzymes including LPCAT3 can incorporate them into membrane phospholipids.
2. **Oxidation.** Redox-active intracellular iron and radical reactions promote propagation of phospholipid peroxides. Excess oxidized membrane phospholipids can compromise membrane integrity and lead to ferroptotic death.
3. **Defense.** System x₍c₎⁻, whose light chain is SLC7A11, imports cystine in exchange for glutamate. Cysteine contributes to glutathione (GSH) synthesis. GPX4 uses GSH to reduce phospholipid hydroperoxides. FSP1–CoQ is a parallel defense and can sit in the clinician detail.

Define ferroptosis as regulated cell death driven by iron-dependent phospholipid peroxidation. Explain that susceptibility can rise when oxidation increases, when susceptible membrane lipids accumulate, or when defenses weaken. Show AGE/carbonyl stress and impaired autophagy/AMPK as examples of experimentally supported upstream stresses. Keep ferritinophagy optional and context-dependent. Autophagy can preserve organelle quality, while selective ferritin degradation can increase labile iron under some conditions. Serum ferritin, TSAT and prescribed iron therapy are separate clinical topics.

The clinician evidence box should require convergent assays for experimental attribution. A credible design combines cell death measurements, phospholipid peroxidation, pathway perturbation, and rescue with orthogonal interventions such as a lipid radical-trapping agent plus genetic or iron-dependent evidence. 4-HNE, MDA, GPX4 staining, a transcriptomic enrichment score, or mitochondrial morphology alone have limited specificity. [Dixon et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2012.03.042) · [Doll et al. ACSL4](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchembio.2239) · [Friedmann Angeli et al. kidney Gpx4 model](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb3064) · [Doll et al. FSP1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1707-0)

### P2 · Kidney treatment response timeline

Extend or compose the current UACR trend and eGFR slope widgets with dated treatment events. Accept paired UACR and eGFR time series, drug start or change dates, and flags for AKI, acute illness, sample confounders and lab qualifiers. Let clinicians choose a documented baseline and show an accessible table next to the plot. Compute signed change as `100 × (followUp − baseline) / baseline` for positive quantitative baselines. Convert UACR mg/mmol to mg/g by multiplying by approximately 8.84. Keep guideline category cut points tied to the chosen source and original unit, since tabulated mg/mmol equivalents are rounded. Keep unrounded values for threshold logic. Mark assay-censored and unsuitable samples, suppress affected comparisons and retain the original entered values and units for interpretation.

KDIGO 2024 flags a subsequent eGFR change >20%, an eGFR reduction >30% after initiation of a hemodynamically active therapy, and a doubling of ACR as warranting evaluation. Show which context applied. Treat exactly 2× as doubling and exactly 30% as below the strict >30% flag. In people with CKD and baseline UACR ≥300 mg/g, show the ADA ≥30% UACR reduction goal in a separate context badge. Avoid conflating CKD progression, medication response and AKI. A trajectory crossing AKI or an acute medication dip should require review before any chronic slope is displayed. [KDIGO 2024 §2.1](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/ckd-evaluation-and-management/kdigo-2024-ckd-guideline/) · [ADA 2026 §11](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-s011)

Acceptance cases include UACR 400→280 mg/g (−30%), 150→300 (2×), eGFR 50→35 (−30%) and 50→34.9 (−30.2%), mixed units, censored values such as <5, zero baseline, duplicate dates, out-of-order entries, missing baseline and an AKI interval. For dual thresholds, a hemodynamic-start scenario uses the specific drop rule with an explicit context label.

### P3 · SGLT2 perioperative hold-date helper

Add a small module to the existing SGLT2 eligibility tool. Inputs are agent including combination products, elective procedure date/time and local zone, last known dose and urgent/elective status. U.S. guidance uses at least three days for canagliflozin, dapagliflozin and empagliflozin and at least four for ertugliflozin. Make the minimum interval explicit and show the facility’s instructions when supplied. For date-only inputs, show a conservative hold window and request exact timing when needed to check an actual dose. Unknown agents, emergent procedures or inadequate hold time route to the perioperative team. The restart message is conditional on clinical stability, resumed oral intake and resolution of ketoacidosis risk, per clinical team and product labeling. [ADA 2026 §16](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-s016) · [2026 U.S. perioperative cardiovascular guideline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2026.06.017) · [U.S. ertugliflozin label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e6f3e718-bb99-48f1-ab94-b9f0af05fed6)

Include a clinician link to the existing DKA protocol and verify it against current diagnostic criteria. In adults, prior diabetes or glucose ≥200 mg/dL, ketosis (blood β-hydroxybutyrate ≥3.0 mmol/L or urine ketones ≥2+) and acidosis (pH <7.3 and/or bicarbonate <18 mmol/L) are the three criteria. In a patient taking an SGLT2i, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain or rapid breathing should trigger urgent assessment even with near-normal glucose. Present this as escalation copy and avoid a standalone home DKA scoring tool. [Hyperglycemic crises consensus](https://doi.org/10.2337/dci24-0032)
