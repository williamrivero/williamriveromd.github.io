FOR ADULTS ON HEMODIALYSIS · DISASTER PLAN

Three-Day Emergency Kidney Diet

For adults on hemodialysis whose dialysis is delayed or missed because of a typhoon, flood, earthquake, ashfall, power or water failure, or blocked roads. For patients and the family members who help them.

This is a temporary bridge to dialysis, not a replacement for it.

Try to get dialysis within 3 days of your last session. Call your dialysis unit or the backup unit below as soon as you know your session may be missed. Start this diet as soon as the emergency is announced, before you miss a session. Starting early does no harm.

Pansamantalang gabay lamang ito habang naghahanap ng dialysis. Hindi nito napapalitan ang dialysis.

Do not use this plan for: peritoneal dialysis, children, pregnancy, kidney transplant, kidney disease not yet on dialysis, sudden (acute) kidney injury, or tube feeding. Ask your team for your own plan.

Go to the nearest hospital now, or call 911, if you have:

  • Severe shortness of breath, can't lie flat, or frothy spit
  • Chest pain or pressure
  • Racing, pounding or irregular heartbeat
  • Marked weakness: heavy arms or legs, trouble standing, chewing or breathing
  • Confusion, unusual sleepiness, or a seizure
  • Fainting
  • Fast weight gain with swelling or breathlessness
  • Fever, chills, or pus or redness at your access or catheter

Feeling fine does not mean you are safe. Dangerously high potassium can cause no warning signs before it stops the heart. If you have missed dialysis and cannot get a session, go to a hospital that can dialyze even if you feel well.

Bring: this sheet, your medicine list, your last dialysis record, PhilHealth and ID cards.

The three rules

  1. Less meat, enough food. Eat small, measured portions of meat and fish, but do not skip meals. Bread, crackers, sugar, jam and oil give you energy so your body does not break down muscle, which releases potassium.
  2. Measure everything. Use a ½-cup measure and a tablespoon. Count every drink, soup and ice cube.
  3. No added salt, and never "lite salt." Salt makes you thirsty. Potassium salt substitutes ("lite," "low-sodium" salt) can raise potassium to a dangerous level.

Fluid — every drop counts

These all count as fluid: water, coffee, tea, juice, soft drinks, milk, sabaw (soup), lugaw and arroz caldo, ice and ice candy, gulaman and gelatin, fruit syrup, very juicy fruit like watermelon, and water taken with medicines.

For thirst: suck hard candy (sugar-free if you have diabetes), chew gum, rinse your mouth and spit, and eat less salt. Weigh yourself each day if you have a scale.

Plan A — no cooking, no refrigerator

Food safety when the power is out

  • A closed refrigerator keeps food safe for about 4 hours. A full freezer keeps it safe for about 48 hours (half-full: 24 hours). Eat refrigerator food first.
  • At room temperature, cooked or opened food lasts 2 hours, or only 1 hour above 32 °C.
  • Never taste food to check it. When in doubt, throw it out.

Safe drinking water

  • Use sealed bottled water first.
  • Or let cloudy water settle and filter it through clean cloth, then boil it at a rolling boil for 1 minute.
  • If you cannot boil it: add 8 drops of unscented bleach (5–9%) per gallon (3.8 L), or 16 drops if cloudy. Mix and wait 30 minutes.
  • Do not add salt to improve the taste.

If you have diabetes

  • Keep checking your blood sugar, more often if you are eating less.
  • Do not change or stop your insulin or diabetes pills on your own. Call your unit or doctor. Eating less while taking them can make your sugar drop.
  • Low sugar (below 70 mg/dL): take 15 g of sugar, such as 1 Tbsp sugar, glucose tablets or hard candy (check the pack), then recheck in 15 minutes. Repeat if still low. Juice or regular soft drink (½ cup) also works but counts as fluid. Do not use orange juice.
  • If the person cannot be woken or cannot swallow: call 911.

Medicines

  • Keep taking your medicines as prescribed, including phosphate binders with meals, unless your team tells you otherwise.
  • Keep at least a 1-week supply on hand.

My 3-day kit — Plan A, for one person

  • 1 large loaf white bread (about 20 slices; buy fresh when a storm is forecast)
  • 8 packs plain low-salt crackers
  • 3 of the smallest cans of plain tuna (in water or brine)
  • 1 small jar peanut butter (lowest-salt brand)
  • 1 small jar jam (sugar-free if you have diabetes)
  • 3 mayonnaise sachets
  • Margarine, small tub or sachets
  • Small pack of white sugar
  • 1 bag hard candy
  • Powdered milk for three ½-cup servings
  • 6 single-serve cups (or smallest cans) of pineapple or fruit cocktail
  • At least 2 liters of sealed drinking water for drinks and milk, plus separate water for washing
  • ½-cup measure and tablespoon
  • Manual can opener; disposable plates and utensils
  • Unscented bleach and a medicine dropper
  • Flashlight, radio, extra batteries
  • 1-week supply of all medicines; diabetes supplies
  • This sheet, in a plastic sleeve
