Vaccination is part of the infection-prevention & control domain because unimmunized patients and a bloodstream-access-heavy population are exactly where a preventable outbreak does the most damage — hepatitis B virus (HBV) transmission in a shared dialysis unit remains a real risk if a single patient's status is unknown.

2Quick-Reference Schedule
VaccineScheduleRevaccination / recheck
Hepatitis B (HBV)Unit safetyDouble-dose series — either 3 doses (0-1-6 mo, dialysis-formulation vaccine) or 4 doses (0-1-2-6 mo, standard vaccine at double concentration)Anti-HBs titer 1–2 months after the final dose; responders (≥ 10 mIU/mL) rechecked annually; non-responders offered a repeat series
Influenza1 dose, annuallyEvery season; re-offer to anyone declining previously
PneumococcalPCV (conjugate) once, followed by PPSV23 (polysaccharide) ≥ 8 weeks laterOne-time revaccination with PPSV23 5 years after the first dose, per current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) guidance
COVID-19Primary series, then updated/booster dosesPer current CDC/DOH seasonal recommendation — check before each campaign, the schedule changes

Dialysis patients mount a weaker antibody response than the general population, which is why the series is doubled in dose for hepatitis B and why titers — not just "series complete" — are what confirm protection.

Educational tracking aid. Not a validated accreditation instrument. Confirm every schedule against the current CDC/ACIP and DOH Expanded Program on Immunization guidance in effect at the time of vaccination. renalcarematters.com
