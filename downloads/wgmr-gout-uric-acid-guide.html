1What Is Uric Acid — and Why Does It Build Up?

Uric acid is the normal end product of purine metabolism. Purines are broken down in the liver by an enzyme called xanthine oxidase. About 70% of uric acid is excreted by the kidneys in urine; the remainder goes through the gut. When production exceeds excretion — due to diet, genetics, or reduced kidney function — serum uric acid rises (hyperuricemia). Above 6.8 mg/dL, uric acid is supersaturated in blood and begins forming needle-shaped monosodium urate (MSU) crystals in cooler peripheral joints, tendon sheaths, and renal tubules.

Uric Acid Targets (on treatment)
Men: < 6.0 mg/dL (general); < 5.0 mg/dL if tophi present
Women: < 5.0 mg/dL (post-menopausal = same as men)
CKD patients: < 6.0 mg/dL — KDIGO 2024
Saturation point: 6.8 mg/dL — crystals form above this
Do NOT target zero — uric acid is a natural antioxidant
The Gout–CKD Vicious Cycle
High uric acid → deposits crystals in renal tubules (urate nephropathy) ↓ Kidney function declines → less uric acid excreted in urine ↓ Serum uric acid rises further → more crystal deposition → more CKD ↓ Breaking this cycle requires uric acid lowering therapy early
2Gout Spectrum — Acute Flare vs Chronic Tophaceous Gout
🔥 Acute Gout Flare
Sudden onset of severe joint pain — usually at night
Typical joint: First metatarsophalangeal joint (big toe) — "podagra." Also: ankle, knee, wrist, elbow.
Onset: Maximal pain within 6–12 hours. Skin red, hot, swollen, exquisitely tender — patient cannot bear bedsheet contact.
Duration: Untreated: 7–14 days. Treated early: 3–5 days.
Triggers: Dehydration, alcohol (especially beer), high-purine meal, rapid uric acid change (starting allopurinol), infection, surgery, diuretics.
💠 Chronic Tophaceous Gout
Years of untreated hyperuricemia → crystal deposits in soft tissue
Tophi: Chalky white deposits visible under skin — ear helix, knuckles, Achilles tendon, elbow bursae. Can ulcerate and become infected.
Joint damage: Chronic erosive arthropathy — permanent joint destruction resembling RA on X-ray. Punched-out erosions with overhanging edges.
Kidney impact: Urate nephropathy (tubular crystal deposits), uric acid kidney stones, CKD progression, hypertension.
Treatment: Requires long-term uric acid lowering therapy (ULT) — allopurinol or febuxostat — for life.

Gout-Friendly Filipino Diet — What to Eat and What to Avoid
Fig. 3 — Gout-friendly Filipino diet: emphasize rice, eggs, low-fat protein, fruits, and water. Avoid organ meats (atay, goto, dinuguan), dried fish (dilis, tuyo, sardinas), beer, and high-fructose drinks (Coke, Pepsi, sweetened juices). 🦶 What to Do During an Acute Gout Flare — 6-Step Protocol
①
Rest the joint
Do NOT walk on a swollen foot. Elevate the affected limb above heart level. Avoid all pressure on the joint for 24–48 hours.
②
Ice, not heat
Apply ice pack 20 min ON, 20 min OFF — repeat 4–6×/day. Never apply heat — it worsens inflammation. Wrap ice in towel to prevent frostbite.
③
Medicate early
Take colchicine or NSAIDs as prescribed — START WITHIN 12–24 HOURS of first pain. Early treatment = shorter, less severe flare.
④
Hydrate
Increase water to 2–3 L/day (if not fluid-restricted by your nephrologist). Good hydration improves uric acid excretion.
⑤
Stop alcohol
Stop all alcohol immediately — beer especially. Even 1 bottle prolongs the flare significantly. No alcohol until uric acid is controlled.
⑥
Keep allopurinol
Do NOT stop allopurinol if already prescribed — sudden withdrawal makes flares worse. Continue dose as prescribed through the flare.

⚠️ CKD Warning — NSAIDs (Mefenamic Acid, Ibuprofen, Diclofenac) Are Dangerous in Kidney Disease

Do NOT take NSAIDs if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m². NSAIDs cause acute kidney injury (AKI) by reducing renal blood flow — a single dose can cause a significant and sometimes permanent drop in kidney function. Ask your nephrologist before taking any pain medication during a flare. Use colchicine with dose reduction instead (see medication table on next page). If colchicine is contraindicated, prednisolone 20–40 mg/day for 3–5 days is the alternative.

🚨 When to Go to the Emergency Room (ER) Immediately
Warning sign Why it is an emergency
Fever > 38.5°C with joint swellingThis may be septic arthritis (joint infection) — not gout — and requires emergency joint aspiration and IV antibiotics. Septic arthritis can destroy a joint in 24 hours.
Tophi breaking through skinOpen tophi are an entry point for infection. Requires wound care, possible IV antibiotics, and urgent uric acid control.
Multiple joints swollen simultaneouslyPolyarticular gout flare — may require IV colchicine or systemic steroids. Also rule out other arthritis (RA, reactive arthritis).
No improvement after 5 days of treatmentConsider alternative diagnosis: septic arthritis, pseudogout (calcium pyrophosphate), or inadequate medication dosing.

⏱ How Long Does a Flare Last?

  • Untreated: 7–14 days before spontaneous resolution
  • Treated within 12 hours: 3–5 days
  • Treated within 24 hours: 5–7 days
  • Key principle: The earlier the treatment, the shorter and milder the flare. Do not wait hoping it will pass — treat immediately.

💊 Correct Colchicine Dosing in CKD

  • eGFR > 50: Standard dose — 1.2 mg at first sign, then 0.6 mg 1 hour later
  • eGFR 30–50: Reduce dose — 0.6 mg once at onset; do not repeat within 24h
  • eGFR < 30: Use with extreme caution — 0.3 mg once; avoid repeated dosing
  • On dialysis: Colchicine generally avoided — use prednisolone instead
  • Colchicine interacts with clarithromycin, cyclosporine, and some statins — alert your doctor

💊 Medications for Gout — Uric Acid Lowering Therapy (ULT) & Flare Management
Medication Dose Use Key Notes for CKD Patients
Allopurinol50–300 mg/dayLong-term ULT (first-line)Start at 50 mg/day in CKD — titrate slowly by 50 mg every 4 weeks to target. Monitor uric acid monthly. DRESS syndrome risk — stop immediately if rash develops (can be life-threatening). HLA-B*5801 testing recommended before starting in Filipino patients. Do not start during an acute flare.
Febuxostat40–80 mg/dayLong-term ULT (alternative)Preferred over allopurinol in some CKD patients — does not require renal dose adjustment for mild–moderate CKD. 🎯 Long-Term Management Goals

✅ Treatment Targets

  • Serum uric acid < 6.0 mg/dL on treatment (general)
  • Serum uric acid < 5.0 mg/dL if tophi present or frequent flares
  • Zero gout flares per year once uric acid is controlled
  • eGFR stabilization — halt CKD progression
  • Allopurinol/febuxostat: lifelong therapy (stopping causes flares to return)

💡 Non-Drug Strategies (Adjuncts — Not Substitutes for ULT)

  • Hydration: 2–2.5 L water/day (if not fluid-restricted)
  • Eliminate beer and spirits completely
  • Avoid organ meats, dilis, sardinas, bagoong
  • Replace softdrinks with water, plain coffee, unsweetened tea
  • Weight loss if overweight — reduces uric acid by 0.02 mg/dL per kg lost
  • Coffee 1–2 cups/day — evidence for uric acid lowering
  • Low-fat dairy daily — orotic acid promotes uric acid excretion
